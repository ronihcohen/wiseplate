# Ethiopian and Israeli-Ethiopian recipe verification — 2026-09-19

## Scope and cultural framing

Ten Hebrew recipes were added under `/recipes/ethiopia/`: injera, doro wat/sanbat wat, misir wat, shiro wat, beef tibs, fully cooked kitfo, gomen, atkilt wat, injera firfir and dabo. The collection distinguishes broad Ethiopian cuisine from Beta Israel practice and does not treat Ethiopian Orthodox fasting food as Jewish merely because it is vegan.

Doro wat and dabo are explicitly documented in Ethiopian-Israeli Jewish family archives as Shabbat/holiday foods. Kitfo is identified as a Gurage dish, not a Beta Israel dish; the published version is `yebesele` (well done), because Beta Israel historically avoided raw meat and raw minced beef presents a documented food-safety hazard. Shiro is described as northern Ethiopian/Eritrean rather than exclusively Ethiopian. Atkilt and gomen are presented as broad household preparations with regional/family variation.

## Injera method verification

The injera page uses 100% teff, an active ersho/sourdough inoculum, 48–72 hours of primary fermentation, `absit` (a cooked portion of batter), and a short secondary fermentation. This reflects published reviews describing spontaneous/backslopped lactic-acid and yeast fermentation, 1–3 day variation with temperature, decanting separated liquid, and cooking roughly 10% of the batter with water before returning it. It does not claim that baking powder creates authentic fermentation.

The page gives spoilage signs and avoids sealing active fermentation. It identifies wheat starter as a practical but gluten-containing option and requires labeled gluten-free teff/starter for people with celiac disease.

## Nutrition method

Calories were calculated from listed raw ingredient weights, divided by declared yield and rounded to the nearest 5–10 kcal. Commodity values use USDA FoodData Central; Israeli package labels take precedence for branded teff flour, berbere, shiro powder and injera. Water, salt and small spice quantities are negligible. All oil retained in a stew is counted. Injera is excluded from stew nutrition unless the page says otherwise.

Estimates are not laboratory measurements; meat trim, injera moisture/diameter and package formulations can shift results by 10%–20%.

## Israeli availability, kosher adaptation and safety

Teff flour, injera, berbere, mitmita and seasoned shiro are sold in Ethiopian groceries in Israeli cities and are also available through spice/natural-food retailers. Korarima can be harder to find; the recipes label black cardamom plus warm spices as an approximation, not an identical substitute. Kale, mangold, cabbage, lentils, chickpea flour and standard beef/chicken cuts are mainstream supermarket items.

Meat recipes use kosher meat and spiced vegetable oil rather than dairy niter kibbeh. Ayib cheese is omitted from kitfo service to avoid meat/dairy mixing. This is an Israeli kosher adaptation and not a claim that all Ethiopian religious communities follow the same rules.

Safety controls include 74°C for chicken, 71°C for ground beef, 63°C plus a three-minute rest for intact-cut beef, rapid chilling, and explicit warnings that lemon/spice does not sanitize raw meat. The collection intentionally does not publish raw or rare kitfo. Injera fermentation includes mold/off-odor discard guidance.
