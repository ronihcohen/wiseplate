# Tunisian recipe verification — 2026-09-19

## Scope and selection

Ten Hebrew recipes were added under `/recipes/tunisia/`. The selection deliberately separates Tunisian identity from the existing Tripolitan collection: Tunisian fried street foods (fricassé, brik, bambalouni), Tunisian-Jewish dishes (pkaila and Passover msoki), coastal red fish couscous, and everyday dishes built around harissa, tabel/caraway and grilled vegetables.

Dish identity and core technique were checked against community-preservation sources, the Tunisian tourism authority's culinary brochure, FOODISH at ANU Museum, Jewish Food Society, UNESCO's harissa and couscous records, and Tunisian culinary references. Family and regional variants exist; the pages state that rather than claiming one exclusive version.

## Nutrition method

Calories were calculated from the listed raw ingredient weights, divided by the declared yield, and rounded to the nearest 5–10 kcal. Generic commodity values use USDA FoodData Central. Israeli package labels should replace the generic assumption for branded leaf pastry, harissa, tuna, matzah, bread, merguez and powdered mloukhia.

Oil that remains in a sauce or stew is counted in full. Fried recipes state the assumed absorbed oil. Water, salt and spices are negligible unless a material amount is used. These are recipe estimates, not laboratory measurements; oil uptake, drained weight, meat fat and serving size can shift results by 10%–20%.

Ingredient-brand labels take precedence.

## Israeli availability and safety check

All core ingredients are available in ordinary Israeli supermarkets, butcher shops, spice shops or common North African groceries. Round cigar wrappers are specified as the practical substitute for malsouka. Frozen artichoke bottoms, broad beans, peas and spinach are standard supermarket products. Powdered Tunisian mloukhia may require a spice or specialty shop and is explicitly distinguished from frozen whole leaves and okra. Kosher beef/lamb merguez is available from butcher counters, but its nutrition label varies substantially.

Safety notes are included where they materially matter: 175°C frying guidance and no unattended oil; 63°C for fish; 71°C for sausage/braising meat and eggs served to at-risk diners; rapid chilling and short refrigerated storage for cooked fish, egg and tuna; and warnings about hot oil and sugar.
