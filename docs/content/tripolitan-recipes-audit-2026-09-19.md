# Tripolitan recipe verification — 2026-09-19

## Scope

Ten Hebrew recipes were added under `/recipes/tripoli/`. Dish identity and technique were checked against the ANU Museum FOODISH Libyan community archive, the World Organization of Libyan Jews heritage center, Libyan Heritage House, and the Libya Observer's traditional bazin method. The collection uses Jewish-Libyan practice where relevant and labels broader Libyan dishes accordingly.

## Nutrition method

Calories were calculated from the listed raw ingredient weights, divided by the declared yield, then rounded to the nearest 5–10 kcal. Commodity values use USDA FoodData Central; Israeli package labels should be used when a branded ingredient differs. Oil remaining in a soup or stew is counted in full. For frying, the estimate explicitly states the assumed absorbed oil. Water, salt, spices, herbs and lemon are included where material but do not drive the totals.

The values are recipe estimates, not laboratory measurements. Meat trim, drained weight, frying temperature and serving size can reasonably change an individual result by 10%–20%.

## Israeli availability check

All core ingredients are available from ordinary Israeli supermarkets or common local specialty shops: couscous, whole wheat for hamin, semolina, date paste, canned chickpeas and beans, mangold, harissa, orzo/"leshonot", caraway, frozen white fish, chicken legs, and standard Israeli beef cuts 5 and 8. Barley flour may require a natural-food aisle or shop; the recipe says so. Traditional usban was intentionally excluded because kosher casing and the required offal are not reliably available in an ordinary grocery shop.
