# Bukharian Jewish recipe verification — 2026-09-19

## Scope and cultural framing

Ten Hebrew recipes were created under `/recipes/bukhara/`: oshpelo, bakhsh, osh savo, manti, dushpara, bichak, goshtgizhda, lagman, non and Bukharian salad. The collection treats “Bukharian Jews” as a Central Asian Jewish community spanning Bukhara, Samarkand, Tashkent, the Fergana Valley and other places, not as a claim that every dish originated inside the city of Bukhara.

Oshpelo, manti, lagman and non are explicitly identified as foods shared with broader Uzbek/Tajik/Central Asian cuisines. Osh savo is identified as a Jewish Sabbath adaptation; bakhsh and the community's versions of dumplings and pastries are presented in their documented Jewish-Bukharian context. The selection and techniques were checked against the ANU Museum FOODISH Bukhara archive, its museum recipes for bakhsh and oshpelo, its Jewish-food dictionary entry on osh savo, community documentation, an academic Bezalel article on the Samarkand table, and current Israeli Bukharian menus as an availability/recognition check.

## Nutrition method

Calories were calculated from raw ingredient weights, divided by declared yield and rounded. Typical USDA FoodData Central assumptions: dry white rice 365 kcal/100 g; wheat flour 364; medium-fat beef about 220; chicken liver 119; vegetable/olive oil 884; rendered lamb fat about 900; cooked chickpeas 164. Herbs, vegetables and fruit are counted where material. Water, salt and dry spices are negligible. Israeli package labels should replace generic values where they differ.

## Israeli availability and safety

All core ingredients are available in ordinary Israeli supermarkets, produce markets or butcher shops: basmati/Persian rice, flour, beef cuts 5/9, chicken liver, pumpkin, cilantro, parsley, chickpeas and dried fruit. Lamb fat is optional and may require a butcher. Dumplings use a domestic steamer or metal steaming basket; a dedicated mantovarka is not required. Ground and diced beef fillings reach at least 71°C. Bakhsh uses only chicken liver that was already koshered by broiling under appropriate kashrut supervision (or is broiled separately before the recipe), and the liver is then cooked fully to 74°C. Reheated rice reaches steaming hot throughout. Cooked rice is cooled quickly in shallow containers and refrigerated within two hours.

## SEO image production

Eleven original images were generated with the built-in image generator and saved as optimized 1200×630 WebP. The shared `photorealistic-natural` prompt set specified a recipe/collection SEO asset, wide editorial framing, warm side-window light, plausible Central Asian Jewish tableware, realistic homemade textures, no people/text/logos/watermarks, and dish-specific exclusions. Defining visual details were specified for every asset: separate carrot and beef layers in oshpelo, deeply green herb rice in bakhsh, overnight dark fruit-studded rice in osh savo, pleated steamed manti, tiny dushpara in broth, crescent pumpkin bichak, round sealed meat goshtgizhda, hand-pulled noodles for lagman, stamped-center non and finely diced fresh salad.
