The Tunisian cooking that became familiar in Israel arrived with families from Tunis, Djerba, Sfax, Sousse and other cities. It is spicy, lemony and rich in harissa, but it is not just about heat: it has fried or paper-thin dough, legumes, fish, roasted vegetables and Jewish holiday stews. [Fricassé](/en/recipes/tunisia/fricasse/) and [brik](/en/recipes/tunisia/brik/) are street food; [pkaila](/en/recipes/tunisia/pkaila/) and [msoki](/en/recipes/tunisia/msoki/) tell the story of Jewish-Tunisian cooking.

All the recipes are adapted to products sold in Israel. Malsouka sheets are replaced with cigar pastry sheets, mloukhia powder is found in spice shops and North African grocery stores, and merguez comes from a kosher butcher. Where a dish is shared across North Africa, such as couscous, a distinctly Tunisian version was chosen here — red, spicy and made with fish.

### What defines Tunisian flavor

* **Harissa is a raw ingredient, not just a table sauce.** It is bloomed in oil or broth and balanced with lemon. Saltiness and heat vary between Israeli brands, so taste before salting.
* **Tabil and caraway.** In Tunisia a blend of dried coriander and caraway is common. At home you can grind a teaspoon of each; don't replace both with cumin.
* **Sour, spicy and salty together.** Preserved lemon, capers, olives and tuna give fricassé and brik their signature.
* **Roast before chopping.** In [slata mechouia](/en/recipes/tunisia/slata-mechouia/) the peppers, tomatoes and garlic are charred whole and only then chopped — they are not cooked down into a sauce.
* **A local Jewish tradition.** Pkaila is built on spinach fried until very dark; msoki is a Passover stew loaded with spring vegetables and matzah. These are distinct Tunisian versions within a North African continuum, and they are not identical to the closely related Libyan stews.

### A note on the calories

Calories were calculated from the weight of the raw ingredients before cooking and divided by the stated yield. Oil that stays in a stew was counted in full; for frying, an estimated absorption was counted, detailed in each recipe. The manufacturer's label takes precedence over the estimate when you use branded merguez, cigar sheets, harissa or tuna. Frying, the fat percentage of the meat and portion size can change the result by 10%–20%.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Is all Tunisian food spicy?**
Answer: No. You can cut back on the harissa and hot pepper in fricassé, msoki and couscous without changing the method. Mloukhia and pkaila are deep and well spiced, but they don't have to be hot.

**Question: How is Tunisian cuisine different from Tripolitan (Libyan) cuisine?**
Answer: They share ingredients, but the techniques differ. Tunisian cooking features harissa, tabil, brik, fricassé and Jewish stews such as pkaila and msoki; the Libyan collection features caraway, fried tomato paste, mafroum and bazeen.

**Question: Which dishes can be made ahead?**
Answer: Pkaila, mloukhia and msoki improve after a night in the fridge. The fillings for fricassé and brik can be prepared in advance, but the dough and pastry sheets are fried shortly before serving.

**Question: Are the ingredients kosher and available in Israel?**
Answer: Yes, based on the products listed. Choose kosher fish, and tuna and merguez with kosher certification, and avoid combining dairy with the meat dishes.
