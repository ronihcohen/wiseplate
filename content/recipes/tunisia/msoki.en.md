## Tunisian Msoki for Passover

Msoki is the spring stew Tunisian Jews make for the Seder night: lamb, green and bitter vegetables, artichokes, fava beans and matzah that soaks up the liquid at the end. There is no single list of vegetables; the idea is seasonal abundance, not the precision of any one vegetable.

**Prep time:** 45 minutes · **Cooking time:** 150 minutes · **Yield:** 10 servings · **Difficulty:** Medium

### Add the vegetables according to how long they take to soften

The lamb and fava beans go in first; peas, spinach and herbs go in late. If everything goes in together, the tender vegetables disappear before the meat softens.

### Ingredients (for 10 servings)

* 1 kg boneless [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) shoulder, cubed
* 4 tablespoons olive oil
* 2 leeks and 1 onion, chopped
* 4 garlic cloves
* 4 carrots, 3 turnips and 4 celery stalks, cut up
* 400 g frozen artichoke bottoms, quartered
* 400 g frozen green fava beans and 300 g frozen peas
* 300 g spinach, 1/2 small cabbage, sliced
* A bunch of cilantro, a bunch of parsley and a handful of mint, chopped
* 1 tablespoon harissa, 1 teaspoon ground coriander, 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon, salt and pepper
* 2 liters hot water
* 250 g matzah, coarsely broken, to serve in the stew

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Israeli frozen vegetables work well and save work. You can replace the lamb with beef from Israeli cut 5 (chuck), though the cooking time may run longer. On Passover, use harissa and spices with a kosher-for-Passover certification that suits your family's custom.

### Method

1. **Brown the lamb.** Heat the oil in a large pot and brown the meat in three batches. Remove to a plate.
2. **Build the base.** Fry the leeks and onion 8 minutes, then add the garlic, harissa and spices for half a minute.
3. **Start cooking.** Return the meat and add the carrots, turnips, celery, cabbage and water. Bring to a boil and skim the foam.
4. **Cook slowly.** Partially cover and cook 75 minutes at a gentle simmer.
5. **Add the spring vegetables.** Add the artichokes and fava beans for 35 minutes; add the peas, spinach and herbs for the last 15 minutes. The lamb should reach at least 71°C (160°F) and be tender.
6. **Finish with matzah.** Adjust the salt and heat. Break matzah into bowls and ladle the stew over it, or stir it into the pot just before serving so it doesn't turn to mush.

### Variations and tips

* Add fennel or zucchini if they are in season, but don't give up the variety of greens.
* To make ahead, cook without the matzah and add it only at the final reheating.
* Keep up to 4 days in the fridge or freeze up to two months without the matzah.

### Summary

Msoki is a Passover stew of abundance: tender meat, spring vegetables and herbs, with the matzah soaking up the broth at the last moment. A full serving is about 500 calories. For more Jewish-Tunisian tradition, see [pkaila](/en/recipes/tunisia/pkaila/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in msoki?**
Answer: A serving with 25 g of matzah is about 500 calories; without matzah, about 400.

**Question: Does msoki have to contain lamb?**
Answer: Lamb is traditional and meaningful in the Passover story, but many households also use beef.

**Question: Can I use frozen vegetables?**
Answer: Yes. Frozen artichoke bottoms, fava beans and peas are available in Israel and work very well.

**Question: When is the matzah added?**
Answer: Right before serving, into the bowls or the pot. With long cooking it falls apart and thickens the stew too much.
