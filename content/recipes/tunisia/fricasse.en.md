## Tunisian Fricassé

Fricassé is a Tunisian sandwich in which the roll itself is part of the flavor: an oval yeast dough fried until golden, split open and filled with tuna, [potato](/en/food/baked-potato/), [egg](/en/food/eggs/), olives, harissa and preserved lemon. It is associated with street stalls and with the tables of Tunisian Jews in Israel.

**Prep time:** 45 minutes · **Rising:** 75 minutes · **Frying and assembly:** 30 minutes · **Yield:** 10 pieces · **Difficulty:** Medium

### Soft dough, but not runny

The dough should stick slightly to the bottom of the bowl and pull away from the sides. Too much flour makes a heavy roll; cold oil causes absorption. Fry at 175°C (347°F), two or three pieces at a time.

### Ingredients (for 10 pieces)

* 500 g white flour
* 14 g instant dry yeast, 1 teaspoon sugar, 1 teaspoon salt
* 1 egg, 2 tablespoons oil and 240–270 ml lukewarm water
* Neutral oil for frying; about 120 g absorbed was counted in the calculation
* 300 g tuna in water, drained weight
* 400 g cooked, sliced potatoes
* 5 hard-boiled eggs, sliced
* 100 g pitted green olives
* 80 g harissa, thinned with 2 tablespoons water
* 50 g chopped preserved lemon peel

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Bread flour gives an elastic chew, but regular Israeli white flour works. You can use drained tuna in oil; it adds about 20–30 calories per piece, depending on the label. Store-bought preserved lemon is very salty, so taste before adding salt to the filling.

### Method

1. **Knead.** Mix the flour, yeast and sugar. Add the egg, oil and most of the water, then the salt. Knead 10 minutes, adding water only until the dough is soft.
2. **Let it rise.** Cover and let rise 60–75 minutes, until doubled in volume.
3. **Shape.** Divide into ten pieces of about 85–90 g, form ovals 10 cm long and place them on squares of baking paper. Cover for 25 minutes.
4. **Fry safely.** Heat oil 4 cm deep to 175°C. Remove the paper and fry 3–4 minutes per side. Transfer to a rack; never leave a pot of oil unattended.
5. **Assemble.** Cool 10 minutes, cut a slit without cutting all the way through and spread with harissa. Fill with potato, tuna, egg, olives and preserved lemon.

### Variations and tips

* Prepare all the fillings ahead, but fry and assemble close to serving.
* For a milder version, use one tablespoon of harissa for all the sandwiches and thin it with water and lemon.
* Leftovers keep a day in the fridge; it's best to store the rolls and the filling separately.

### Summary

A successful fricassé is an airy roll with a golden shell and a sour-spicy filling. One filled piece is about 440 calories. For more Tunisian street food, see [brik](/en/recipes/tunisia/brik/) and [bambalouni](/en/recipes/tunisia/bambalouni/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a fricassé?**
Answer: A medium roll with all the filling is about 440 calories, assuming 12 g of absorbed oil per roll.

**Question: Why did my fricassé come out heavy and greasy?**
Answer: The oil was too cold or the dough didn't rise enough. Keep the oil at 170–180°C and fry in small batches.

**Question: Can I bake it instead?**
Answer: You can bake at 200°C for about 15 minutes, but you'll get a bread roll rather than the traditional flavor and texture of fricassé.

**Question: Can I make it ahead?**
Answer: The fillings, yes. Fry the rolls the same day and fill them once they've cooled slightly.
