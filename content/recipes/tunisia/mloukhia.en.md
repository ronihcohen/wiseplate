## Tunisian Mloukhia

Tunisian mloukhia is different from the Middle Eastern soup of chopped leaves: it uses powder made from dried mloukhia (jute mallow) leaves, mixed with oil and cooked for hours with beef until it becomes a dark green, smooth, thick sauce. The flavor is herbal and deep, and the dish is eaten with bread.

**Prep time:** 25 minutes · **Cooking time:** about 5 hours · **Yield:** 8 servings · **Difficulty:** Medium

### Mix the powder with oil before the water

Powder poured straight into water forms lumps. Stir it into cold oil to make a smooth paste, and only then add hot water gradually while whisking.

### Ingredients (for 8 servings)

* 150 g finely ground mloukhia powder
* 200 ml olive oil
* 1 kg beef for long cooking, cubed, from Israeli cut 5 or 8 (chuck/shoulder)
* 1 large onion, chopped, and 8 garlic cloves, crushed
* 1 tablespoon tomato paste
* 1 tablespoon ground coriander, 1 teaspoon caraway, 1 teaspoon paprika, 1/2 teaspoon dried mint
* 2.5 liters hot water, plus more as needed
* Salt, pepper and one whole hot pepper (optional)

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Look in a spice shop for "Tunisian mloukhia powder" that is green and smells fresh. Frozen leaves give a different texture and are not a substitute. You can cut the oil to 150 ml, but the sauce will be less silky and the dish will drop by about 55 calories.

### Method

1. **Make a paste.** In a heavy, cold pot, whisk the mloukhia powder with 160 ml of the oil until there are no lumps.
2. **Loosen with water.** Add hot water one cup at a time, whisking. Bring to a very gentle simmer; the mixture tends to foam up.
3. **Cook the base.** Cook uncovered for 2.5 hours on the lowest heat, stirring from the bottom every 20 minutes. Add water if it sticks.
4. **Season the meat.** Mix the beef with the onion, garlic, tomato paste, remaining oil and spices. Add to the pot and stir.
5. **Cook until glossy.** Cook another 2–2.5 hours, until the beef is tender, the sauce is dark and glossy and a thin layer of oil appears on top. The meat should reach at least 71°C (160°F).
6. **Adjust.** Salt at the end and remove the hot pepper. If the sauce is too thick, add a little boiling water.

### Variations and tips

* Use a pot twice the volume of the liquid to prevent boil-overs early in the cooking.
* If the stew is bitter, the powder is old or the pot scorched; low heat and stirring prevent this.
* The flavor improves the next day. Keep up to 4 days in the fridge or two months in the freezer.

### Summary

Tunisian mloukhia takes time but little active work: a smooth paste, low heat and meat added halfway through. A serving without bread is about 570 calories. For another green Jewish-Tunisian stew, see [pkaila](/en/recipes/tunisia/pkaila/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in Tunisian mloukhia?**
Answer: One of eight servings is about 570 calories without bread, including the oil that stays in the sauce.

**Question: Are mloukhia powder and frozen leaves the same thing?**
Answer: No. The Tunisian version is based on dried, ground leaves; frozen leaves are meant for a soup with a different texture.

**Question: Why is the sauce lumpy?**
Answer: The powder met water before it was mixed with oil. Start in a cold pot and whisk to a smooth paste.

**Question: Can the cooking time be shortened?**
Answer: Not significantly. The color, flavor and texture develop over hours on low heat.
