## Tunisian Ojja Merguez

Ojja is a quick Tunisian skillet of tomatoes, peppers, harissa, merguez and [eggs](/en/food/eggs/). It resembles shakshuka, but its identity here comes from the merguez sausage and the caraway seasoning; the common Tunisian version doesn't need onion.

**Prep time:** 15 minutes · **Cooking time:** 35 minutes · **Yield:** 4 servings · **Difficulty:** Easy

### Cook the merguez before the eggs

A sausage browns on the outside before it is safe in the center. Slice it only after a quick sear and cook it in the sauce to 71°C (160°F); the eggs go in once the meat is done.

### Ingredients (for 4 servings)

* 500 g kosher beef or lamb merguez sausages
* 3 tablespoons olive oil
* 2 sweet green peppers and 1 hot pepper, sliced
* 5 garlic cloves, chopped
* 800 g crushed tomatoes
* 2 tablespoons tomato paste and 1 tablespoon harissa
* 1 teaspoon ground caraway, 1 teaspoon paprika, salt and pepper
* 6 eggs
* Parsley and lemon for serving

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Kosher merguez is sold at butcher shops and supermarket chains in Israel, but the fat content varies widely; the label is the accurate source for calories. You can use beef meatballs seasoned with harissa and caraway, but then it's no longer merguez sausage.

### Method

1. **Sear the sausages.** Heat 1 tablespoon of oil, brown the whole merguez for 5 minutes and remove. Cool slightly and slice into pieces.
2. **Fry the peppers.** Add the remaining oil and the peppers and fry 7 minutes. Add the garlic for half a minute.
3. **Reduce the sauce.** Add the tomatoes, tomato paste, harissa and spices. Cook uncovered 12–15 minutes, until the sauce is thick.
4. **Finish the merguez.** Return the slices and cook 6–8 minutes, to 71°C in the center.
5. **Add the eggs.** Make six wells, crack in the eggs, cover and cook 5–8 minutes. For at-risk groups, cook until the yolk is firm. Serve without dairy to keep it a kosher meat dish.

### Variations and tips

* The heat also comes from the sausage; taste before adding all the harissa.
* You can make the sauce a day ahead, reheat it, and then cook the merguez and eggs in it.
* Leftovers with egg keep up to two days in the fridge and should be reheated until steaming.

### Summary

Ojja merguez isn't just shakshuka with an extra: the caraway, peppers and sausage fat melt into the sauce. A serving is about 630 calories before bread. For another hot Tunisian dish with harissa, see [lablabi](/en/recipes/tunisia/lablabi/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in ojja merguez?**
Answer: A quarter of the skillet is about 630 calories without bread, based on merguez at 280 calories per 100 g.

**Question: Are ojja and shakshuka the same thing?**
Answer: They are close, but Tunisian ojja leans on harissa and caraway and often includes merguez or seafood.

**Question: How do I know the merguez is done?**
Answer: Measure 71°C in the center of a thick piece; color alone is not a safe indicator.

**Question: Can I make it less spicy?**
Answer: Yes. Leave out the hot pepper and use 1 teaspoon of harissa, after checking how the sausage is seasoned.
