## Khinkali

Khinkali (in Hebrew "חינקלי") are large Georgian dumplings whose filling contains cold water. As they cook, the meat releases its juices and the water turns into a broth trapped inside the dough. They are eaten by hand: hold the knot, bite carefully, drink the broth and then eat the rest.

**Prep time:** 75 minutes · **Resting:** 30 minutes · **Cooking:** 20–25 minutes · **Yield:** about 32 pieces, 8 servings · **Difficulty:** challenging

### The filling should be almost as soft as porridge

Without liquid you get a meatball inside dough. Add cold water gradually until the meat absorbs it but can still be set down with a spoon. Work cold; warm fat separates and the filling turns firm.

### Ingredients (for about 32 pieces)

* 700 grams white flour, plus a little for rolling
* 330–360 ml cold water
* 14 grams salt, half for the dough and half for the filling
* 600 grams ground beef, 15% fat
* 300 grams ground lamb
* 2 medium onions, chopped very finely
* 1 teaspoon black pepper, 1 teaspoon cumin
* A handful of chopped cilantro and a handful of chopped parsley
* 300–350 ml cold water for the filling

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

The city version with herbs is known as kalakuri; mountain versions are simpler. Instead of lamb you can use only 15%–20% beef, but not very lean meat. Elsewhere there are pork fillings; the version here is a meat dish and kosher in its ingredients, with no milk and no butter.

### Method

1. **Knead a stiff dough.** Mix the flour, 7 grams of salt and 330 ml of water. Knead for 10 minutes; add a little water only if dry flour remains. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes.
2. **Mix the filling.** Combine the meat, onion, herbs and spices. Add cold water in several rounds and knead until each addition is absorbed. Chill.
3. **Roll out.** Divide into 32 balls of about 32 grams. Roll each into a circle 14–15 cm in diameter, thin at the edges and slightly thicker in the center.
4. **Pleat.** Place about 45 grams of filling in the center. Lift the edge and form pleats all the way around; pinch and twist the knot. There is no need to chase a particular number of pleats — sealing matters more.
5. **Cook in batches.** Bring a wide pot of salted water to a boil. Stir the water gently, add 6–8 pieces and loosen them from the bottom. Cook for 7–9 minutes from the moment they float.
6. **Serve.** Lift out with a slotted spoon, sprinkle with black pepper and eat carefully while the broth is hot.

### Variations and tips

* Freeze uncooked in a single layer, then transfer to a bag; cook from frozen and add 2–3 minutes.
* Khinkali that leak were torn during rolling, not sealed, or stuck to the bottom. A wide pot and cooking in batches help.
* Ground meat should reach 71°C (160°F) in the center; if you have no thermometer, open a test piece from the first batch.

### Summary

The secret of khinkali is not just the pleats but a cold, liquid-saturated filling inside a strong, thin dough. Four pieces are about 600 calories. See also [kharcho](/en/recipes/georgia/kharcho/), and for more dishes, [Georgian cuisine](/en/recipes/georgia/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in khinkali?**
Answer: A medium piece is about 150 calories and a serving of four about 600.

**Question: Why is there no broth inside?**
Answer: The filling was too dry or the dumpling leaked. Add water until the filling is very soft and seal well.

**Question: Do you eat the knot?**
Answer: You can, but in Georgia the thick knot is often left on the plate and used as a handle.

**Question: Can you make them ahead?**
Answer: Yes, but don't leave them filled in the refrigerator for long, because the dough gets soggy. Freeze them right away and cook from frozen.
