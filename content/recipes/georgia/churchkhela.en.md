## Churchkhela

Churchkhela looks like a candle, but it is a sweet made of walnut halves threaded on a string, dipped in a thickened grape juice and flour mixture and hung to dry. In Georgia it is made after the grape harvest; in an Israeli home you can use 100% grape juice and set aside a ventilated spot for drying.

**Prep time:** 45 minutes · **Cooking:** 25 minutes · **Drying:** 5–7 days · **Yield:** 20 small strands · **Difficulty:** challenging

### The batter should coat a spoon in a thick layer

A thin batter slides off the walnuts and one that is too thick forms lumps. Cook while whisking to a soft pudding texture, dip, let dry a little and dip again. The coating shrinks as it dries.

### Ingredients (for 20 small strands)

* 500 grams whole walnut halves
* 2 liters 100% red grape juice, no added sugar
* 250 grams white flour
* Strong cotton thread and a clean needle

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Very sweet grape juice yields a sweet confection; no sugar is needed. You can reduce strained fresh grape juice. Use cotton thread suitable for food contact, not dyed synthetic thread. Walnuts are a major allergen.

### Method

1. **Thread.** Cut 20 strings 35 cm long, tie a loop at one end and thread 25 grams of walnuts onto each string. Leave a loop for hanging.
2. **Make a cold mixture.** Whisk the flour with 500 ml of the juice until there are no lumps.
3. **Reduce.** Cook the rest of the juice in a wide pot for 10 minutes. Pour in the flour mixture in a thin stream while whisking.
4. **Cook thoroughly.** Cook for 12–15 minutes over low heat, stirring, until the floury taste disappears and the batter coats a spoon. If it is too thick, add a little juice.
5. **Dip twice.** Dip each strand, hang it over a tray for 20–30 minutes and dip again. Use a spoon to cover bare spots.
6. **Dry safely.** Hang in a clean, dry, ventilated spot, protected from insects and direct sun, for 5–7 days. The coating should be dry to the touch but slightly pliable. If a sour smell, sticky moisture or fuzzy mold appears — throw it out.

### Variations and tips

* An even white powder on aged churchkhela may be sugar crystallization; mold is fuzzy, patchy and comes with a smell. When in doubt, throw it out.
* In a humid climate, use an air-conditioned, ventilated room, not a closed cupboard.
* After drying, wrap in baking paper and keep in a box in a cool place for up to two weeks.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in churchkhela?**
Answer: A small strand of about 25 grams of walnuts plus coating is about 250 calories; a quarter strand about 60.

**Question: Do I need fresh grape juice?**
Answer: No. 100% grape juice with no added sugar works and is available in Israel.

**Question: How long does it dry?**
Answer: Usually 5–7 days in a dry, ventilated spot. High humidity lengthens the time and increases the risk of mold.

**Question: Is the white coating mold?**
Answer: An even layer of sugar can appear, but fuzzy spots or an unusual smell are a sign to throw it out. Never taste a suspicious product.
