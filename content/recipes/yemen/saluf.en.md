## Yemenite Saluf

Saluf (in Hebrew "סלוף") is a flatbread traditionally baked on the wall of a taboon oven. In an Israeli home, a baking stone or an upside-down heavy baking sheet provides strong bottom heat. It is thicker and chewier than lahuh and is not supposed to puff into an even pocket like pita.

**Prep time:** 25 minutes · **Rising:** 90 minutes · **Baking:** 20 minutes · **Yield:** 10 · **Difficulty:** medium

### A home substitute for the taboon

Preheat a stone or an upside-down baking sheet for at least 35 minutes at 250 degrees Celsius (480°F). The dough goes onto a blazing-hot surface and bakes fast; don't use parchment paper that isn't rated for this temperature.

### Ingredients (for 10 pieces)

* 600 grams white flour
* 200 grams whole wheat flour
* 10 grams dry yeast
* 12 grams sugar
* 16 grams salt
* 520–560 ml lukewarm water
* 30 grams olive oil
* 2 teaspoons nigella seeds, optional

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Whole wheat flour adds flavor, but too much of it weighs the bread down; you can replace it with white. Nigella is the black seed sprinkled on top and is not cumin or caraway. A pizza stone is not essential — a thick metal baking sheet is enough.

### Method

1. **Knead.** Knead the flours, yeast, sugar, salt, water and oil for 10 minutes into a soft, slightly sticky dough.
2. **Rise.** Cover and let rise for about an hour, until it grows by about 70%.
3. **Heat and divide.** Heat the oven and stone to 250 degrees Celsius (480°F). Divide into 10 balls and rest for 20 minutes.
4. **Shape.** With wet hands, open each ball into a circle about 8 mm thick, make dimples with your fingertips and sprinkle with nigella.
5. **Bake.** Bake 2–3 pieces at a time for 5–7 minutes, until browned spots appear. Wrap in a clean towel to keep them soft.

### Variations and tips

* For sticky dough, use wet hands and don't add much flour.
* If the bread is pale, the surface didn't heat up enough.
* Freeze after cooling completely and reheat straight in the toaster or oven.

### Summary

Strong bottom heat and a soft dough create a pliable saluf with charred spots. A piece is about 320 calories. For a spongy pan bread, see [lahuh](/en/recipes/yemen/lahuh/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in saluf?**
Answer: One piece out of ten is about 320 calories.

**Question: Is saluf a pita?**
Answer: It is a flatbread, but it doesn't have to form a pocket and it is usually thicker and more rustic.

**Question: Can you make it without a baking stone?**
Answer: Yes. Preheat an upside-down heavy metal baking sheet and carefully slide the dough onto it.

**Question: Are nigella seeds a must?**
Answer: No. They are common in some versions and add aroma, but the bread works without them.
