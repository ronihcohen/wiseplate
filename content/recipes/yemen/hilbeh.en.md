## Whipped Yemenite Hilbeh

Hilbeh (in Hebrew "חילבה", the word for fenugreek) is ground fenugreek seeds that, after soaking and whipping, become a light, airy spread. It is strongly identified with the table of Yemenite Jews in Israel, but it also belongs to older and wider traditions in the Middle East.

**Working time:** 20 minutes · **Soaking:** 8 hours · **Yield:** about 20 tablespoons · **Difficulty:** easy

### Soaking removes the bitterness

Cover the powder with plenty of water, refrigerate, and pour off the water on top without stirring up the sediment. Repeating the rinse once gives a milder flavor. Use cold water to preserve the texture.

### Ingredients (for about 20 tablespoons)

* 100 grams fenugreek seed powder
* 500 ml water for soaking, plus 150–250 ml for whipping
* Juice of one lemon
* 1/2 teaspoon salt
* 1/2 teaspoon cumin
* 1–2 tablespoons [zhug](/en/recipes/yemen/zhug/), optional and not included in the calories

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Buy ground fenugreek from a spice shop with high turnover. A yellowish, fragrant powder is better than a gray, old one. There is no direct substitute for the seed itself; fenugreek leaves are not suitable for this recipe.

### Method

1. **Soak.** Mix the powder with 500 ml of water, cover and soak in the fridge for 8 hours.
2. **Pour off the water.** Gently pour off the dark water on top. For a milder flavor, add water, wait 20 minutes and pour it off again.
3. **Whip.** Transfer to a bowl and whip with an electric mixer for 7–10 minutes. Add cold water a tablespoon at a time until it becomes airy and voluminous.
4. **Season.** Fold in the lemon, salt and cumin; add zhug only if you want.
5. **Chill.** Refrigerate for an hour. The spread firms up slightly in the cold.

### Variations and tips

* Hilbeh that is too runny firms up as it chills; don't add more unsoaked powder.
* The characteristic smell can linger in the body — this is a natural property of fenugreek.
* Keeps up to 3 days in the fridge; freezing is not recommended because the foam breaks down.

### Summary

Cold soaking and long whipping turn a bitter powder into an airy spread. A tablespoon is about 15 calories. It goes well alongside [Yemenite soup](/en/recipes/yemen/yemenite-soup/) and kubaneh.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in hilbeh?**
Answer: A tablespoon of whipped hilbeh is about 15 calories; the result depends on the amount of water.

**Question: Why is the hilbeh bitter?**
Answer: The powder wasn't soaked and rinsed enough, or it is old. Soak it in the fridge and pour off the soaking water.

**Question: Do you need a mixer?**
Answer: No, but a hand whisk takes 12–15 minutes of vigorous work.

**Question: Are hilbeh and zhug the same thing?**
Answer: No. Hilbeh is a delicate seed foam; zhug is a hot spread of peppers and herbs.
