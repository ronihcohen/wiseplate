## Yemenite Chicken Soup

Yemenite soup (in Hebrew "מרק תימני") is the name of a family of soups, not a single formula. This home version uses chicken leg quarters, [potatoes](/en/food/baked-potato/) and soup hawaij. It is close to the versions that took hold in Israel; in Yemen and in various families people also cooked lamb or beef, and sometimes a clearer soup.

**Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Cooking time:** 1 hour and 40 minutes · **Yield:** 8 bowls · **Difficulty:** easy

### Hawaij and the right texture

Soup hawaij usually includes [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), cumin and coriander and sometimes cardamom and black pepper. Store-bought blends vary a lot in salt and heat, so start with two tablespoons and taste only after cooking. This is not coffee hawaij.

### Ingredients (for 8 servings)

* 1.4 kg bone-in, skin-on chicken leg quarters, separated
* 2 tablespoons oil
* 2 large [onions](/en/food/onion/), chopped
* 4 cloves [garlic](/en/food/garlic/), crushed
* 2 tablespoons soup hawaij, plus more to taste
* 1 teaspoon cumin, 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
* 800 grams potatoes, peeled and coarsely cut
* 2 carrots and 2 celery stalks, cut
* 2.2 liters water
* 2 teaspoons salt, depending on the blend
* A handful of chopped cilantro to serve

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Leg quarters make a rich soup; without the skin the calorie value goes down. You can use 1.2 kg of cubed beef shoulder and extend the cooking to about two and a half hours. Soup powder is a common Israeli addition but isn't necessary; if you use it, reduce the salt.

### Method

1. **Build the base.** Heat the oil in a wide pot, brown the onion for 8 minutes and add the garlic and hawaij for 30 seconds.
2. **Add the chicken.** Add the chicken, water, carrot and celery. Bring to a boil and skim off the foam.
3. **Simmer.** Lower the heat, partially cover and cook for 50 minutes at a gentle simmer.
4. **Add the potatoes.** Add the potatoes and cook for 35–45 minutes, until they are soft and the chicken reaches at least 74 degrees Celsius (165°F) in the thickest part, without touching the bone.
5. **Season and serve.** Taste, adjust the salt and hawaij, and serve with cilantro, [lahuh](/en/recipes/yemen/lahuh/) and zhug on the side. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours.

### Variations and tips

* For a thicker soup, mash one potato into the liquid; there is no need for flour.
* Add whipped hilbeh to the plate, not to the whole pot.
* The soup keeps 4 days in the fridge or two months in the freezer.

### Summary

The base is browned onion, hawaij bloomed briefly in oil, and gentle cooking of chicken and potatoes. A generous bowl is about 345 calories. For more accompaniments, see [zhug](/en/recipes/yemen/zhug/) and [hilbeh](/en/recipes/yemen/hilbeh/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in Yemenite soup?**
Answer: In this recipe, a bowl with chicken and potato is about 345 calories.

**Question: Can you use chicken breast?**
Answer: You can, but it dries out. Add it only in the last 25 minutes and make sure it reaches 74 degrees Celsius (165°F).

**Question: Why is the soup bitter?**
Answer: The hawaij may have burned in the oil. Bloom it for only half a minute and add liquid immediately.

**Question: Is the traditional soup made with chicken or beef?**
Answer: Both exist, along with lamb and foot soups. This is a home chicken version common in Israel.
