## Yemenite Zalabia

In Israel, zalabia (in Hebrew "זלביה") is sometimes associated with Hanukkah or with sweets, but in Yemenite Jewish families it was also an everyday breakfast: fried yeast dough served hot with tomato, zhug and egg. Powdered sugar is an option, not the only default.

**Prep time:** 25 minutes · **Rising:** 30 minutes · **Frying:** 35 minutes · **Yield:** 18 · **Difficulty:** medium

### Hot oil, not smoking

A temperature of 170–175 degrees Celsius (340–350°F) lets the dough cook through before it browns. Cold oil gets absorbed; oil that is too hot burns the outside. Fry 3–4 pieces per batch and don't fill the pot more than halfway with oil.

### Ingredients (for 18 pieces)

* 750 grams white flour
* 10 grams dry yeast
* 30 grams sugar
* 14 grams salt
* 470–500 ml lukewarm water
* Neutral oil for frying; the calculation counts 120 grams absorbed

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Canola or sunflower oil works. There is no need for olive oil for this frying. Regular white flour gives a pliable dough; don't add flour to get rid of all stickiness, because dry dough comes out heavy.

### Method

1. **Knead.** Knead the flour, yeast, sugar, salt and 470 ml of water for 10 minutes into a soft, slightly sticky dough.
2. **Rise.** Cover and let rise for 30–40 minutes, until noticeably puffed.
3. **Divide.** With wet hands, divide into 18 balls and rest for 10 minutes.
4. **Heat the oil and shape.** Heat oil 3 cm deep to 170–175 degrees Celsius (340–350°F). Open each ball by hand into a circle about 1 cm thick.
5. **Fry.** Fry 3–4 at a time, 3–4 minutes on each side, until deep golden. Remove to a rack. Keep children and water away from the pot of oil.

### Variations and tips

* To serve savory: grated tomato, [zhug](/en/recipes/yemen/zhug/) and a hard-boiled egg.
* To serve sweet: a little powdered sugar or honey; these are not included in the calories.
* Zalabia is best right away. Reheat leftovers for 5 minutes at 190 degrees Celsius (375°F), not in the microwave.

### Summary

A soft dough, a short rest and oil at a steady temperature give puffy, not greasy, zalabia. A piece is about 215 calories. For another pan bread, see [malawach](/en/recipes/yemen/malawach/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in Yemenite zalabia?**
Answer: A medium piece is about 215 calories, based on about 120 grams of oil absorbed over the whole batch.

**Question: Is Yemenite zalabia sweet or savory?**
Answer: Both exist, but in family accounts it is also described as a savory breakfast with tomato and zhug.

**Question: Why did it soak up oil?**
Answer: The oil was too cold or too many pieces went in at once.

**Question: Can you bake it?**
Answer: You can bake it at 210 degrees Celsius (410°F), but you get a flat bun rather than the traditional fried texture.
