## Yemenite Jachnun

Jachnun (in Hebrew "ג'חנון") gets its layers from stretching a thin dough, greasing it and folding it — not from store-bought puff pastry. Overnight baking suited the Sabbath. Margarine is associated with the pareve (dairy-free) Israeli version; samneh or clarified butter reflect dairy traditions, but are not suitable for a meat meal.

**Working time:** about 90 minutes · **Resting:** 2 hours · **Baking:** 10–12 hours · **Yield:** 12 rolls · **Difficulty:** advanced

### The layers start with resting

Dough that springs straight back when stretched needs more rest. Don't tear it by force: grease it, wait ten minutes and continue. A nearly transparent sheet gives thin layers; rolling too tightly leaves a doughy center.

### Ingredients (for 12 rolls)

* 1 kg white flour
* 80 grams sugar
* 20 grams salt
* 40 grams [honey](/en/food/honey/)
* 600–650 ml lukewarm water
* 300 grams soft [margarine](/en/food/margarine/), or clarified butter for a dairy version
* 2 slices of bread or 2 pitas to line the bottom of the pot
* Parchment paper

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Bread flour makes stretching easier, but regular Israeli white flour works with good kneading. Choose a baking margarine with no aftertaste and check the calorie label. There is no need for date syrup for color; the browning comes from the long bake.

### Method

1. **Knead.** Knead the flour, sugar, salt, honey and 600 ml of water for 10 minutes. Add a little water only if the dough is stiff. Cover and rest for an hour.
2. **Divide.** Divide into 12 balls, grease lightly and rest for another 45 minutes.
3. **Stretch and roll.** On a greased surface, stretch a ball into a very thin sheet. Spread about 25 grams of margarine, fold a third from the right and a third from the left into a strip, and roll up without squeezing.
4. **Fill the pot.** Line an ovenproof jachnun pot with the bread and parchment paper. Arrange the rolls crosswise in layers and cover tightly.
5. **Bake overnight.** Bake for 10–12 hours at 100 degrees Celsius (210°F). In the morning, check that it is amber-brown and the center is baked through; if it is pale, bake another hour at 120 degrees Celsius (250°F).

### Variations and tips

* Unbaked rolls can be frozen, separated with paper, for up to two months and thawed in the fridge.
* A thin pot can scorch the bottom; the bread layer is insulation and not part of the dish.
* An oven is better than an unstable hot plate; use only a pot and lid designed for prolonged heat.

### Summary

Successful jachnun requires kneading, resting, a thin sheet and steady low heat. A roll without toppings is about 520 calories. For a fluffier Sabbath bake, see [kubaneh](/en/recipes/yemen/kubaneh/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in jachnun?**
Answer: A homemade roll from this recipe is about 520 calories, before the egg and grated tomato.

**Question: Why did the jachnun come out hard?**
Answer: Usually the dough was dry, didn't rest enough, or was baked without a tight seal. Add water gradually and close the pot well.

**Question: Do you have to use margarine?**
Answer: No. Clarified butter suits a dairy version; oil alone creates less distinct layers.

**Question: Can you bake it for less time at a higher heat?**
Answer: You can get a pastry, but not the texture and browning typical of overnight baking.
