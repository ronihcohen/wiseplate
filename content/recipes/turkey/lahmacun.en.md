## Lahmacun

**In Turkish: *Lahmacun*, from the Arabic *lahm bi'ajin* — "meat in dough."** It is also spelled lahmajun, and is often misleadingly called "Turkish pizza"; in Hebrew it is written "לחמג'ון" or "לאחמג'ון". It isn't pizza: there's no cheese on it, the dough is paper-thin, and it's eaten rolled up by hand with parsley and lemon inside.

Lahmacun is the main street food of southeastern Turkey, especially Gaziantep, Şanlıurfa and Adana. In a professional stone oven it bakes for just 90 seconds at 400°C. A home kitchen can't reproduce that heat exactly, but you can get very close with a pizza stone or an upside-down baking sheet that has been preheated.

**Prep time:** 30 minutes · **Rising time:** 1 hour · **Baking time:** 6 minutes per piece · **Yield:** 12 pieces · **Difficulty:** Medium

### The economics of street food

Lahmacun is what happens when a meat dish needs to feed many people cheaply. The layer of meat on one flatbread weighs only about 35-40 g — less than a quarter of what's on a [kebab](/en/recipes/turkey/adana-kebab/) skewer — and the dough, vegetables and parsley do the rest of the work. That's why lahmacun has always been the cheapest dish on the menu in southeastern Turkey, and also why it's more balanced than it seems: its meat-to-vegetable ratio is the reverse of most meat dishes.

In Gaziantep and Urfa it's customary to order it as a *dürüm* — rolled around a generous handful of parsley, onion and lemon — and to drink ayran or *şalgam*, a fermented, spicy purple-turnip juice, alongside it. This isn't accidental either: a cold, tart drink cleanses the fat from the palate between bites.

### Ingredients (for 12 pieces)

**For the dough:**
* 500 g bread flour
* 300 ml lukewarm water
* 7 g instant dry yeast
* 1 teaspoon sugar, 1.5 teaspoons salt
* 2 tablespoons [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)

**For the meat mixture:**
* 300 g ground [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) (or beef; see [red meat](/en/food/red-meat/))
* 1 large [onion](/en/food/onion/)
* 2 ripe [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/)
* 1 [red pepper](/en/food/red-pepper/) and 1 long green pepper
* 4 [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) cloves
* A large handful of parsley
* 1 tablespoon Turkish pepper paste + 1 tablespoon tomato paste — or 2 tablespoons tomato paste and half a teaspoon sweet paprika
* 1 teaspoon pul biber, 1 teaspoon cumin, 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon, salt and pepper
* 2 tablespoons [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)

**To serve:** parsley, red onion, sumac and [lemon](/en/food/lemon/) wedges.

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Instead of a tablespoon of Turkish pepper paste, use an extra tablespoon of tomato paste and half a teaspoon of sweet paprika. Instead of a teaspoon of pul biber, use half a teaspoon of sweet paprika and a pinch of chili. A regular sweet green pepper works for the topping. You can use regular white flour instead of bread flour, adding the water gradually until the dough is supple.

### Method

1. **The dough.** Mix the yeast, sugar and lukewarm water and wait 10 minutes until foamy. Add the flour, salt and oil and knead 8 minutes until the dough is smooth and soft. Let rise about an hour until doubled in volume.
2. **The mixture — most important: purée, don't chop.** Process the onion, peppers, garlic, tomatoes and parsley in a food processor into a very fine purée. Mix with the meat, the pastes, the spices and the olive oil until spreadable. If the mixture is watery, press it lightly with a spoon in a sieve — excess liquid makes the dough soggy.
3. **Heat the oven to maximum** (250-280°C / 482-536°F) with a pizza stone or an upside-down baking sheet inside, for at least 40 minutes. This is the one condition that really determines the result.
4. **Roll paper-thin.** Divide the dough into 12 balls (about 65 g each) and roll each into a circle 25-28 cm in diameter. The dough should be so thin you can almost see through it.
5. **Spread thinly.** Spread about 4 tablespoons (about 75 g) of the mixture in a thin, even layer all the way to the edges. A thick layer makes a heavy flatbread that won't bake properly.
6. **Bake 5-7 minutes** on the hot stone, until the edges brown and the meat is cooked. Lahmacun should stay pliable, not crisp — it's meant to be rolled.
7. **Serve** immediately: scatter parsley and red onion with sumac, squeeze lemon generously, roll up and eat by hand.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is one lahmacun, about 150 g.

| Component | Per piece (about 150 g) |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **About 280** |
| Protein | About 9 g |
| Carbohydrates | About 35 g |
| Of which fiber | About 2 g |
| Fat | About 11 g |

For comparison, a slice of pizza of similar weight is about 400-450 calories. The entire difference comes from two things: the dough is much thinner, and there's no cheese. That said, one lahmacun isn't a meal in the eyes of most Turks — a restaurant serving is usually two or three, meaning 560-840 calories, plus ayran and salad.

The weak point of the dish is the white flour — about 35 g of carbohydrate and only 2 g of fiber. A generous addition of parsley, onion and lemon isn't garnish: it adds fiber and [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/), which improves absorption of [iron](/en/food/iron/) from the meat, and balances the dish.

### Variations and tips

* **A lighter version:** Replace 30% of the flour with whole-wheat flour and use lean ground meat (10% fat) — about 30 fewer calories per piece and more fiber.
* **A vegetarian version:** Replace the meat with mashed cooked [lentils](/en/food/lentil/) or finely chopped [mushrooms](/en/food/mushrooms/) with walnuts — common in modern versions in Istanbul.
* **Freezing:** Bake for only 3 minutes, cool, freeze, and finish baking straight from frozen. That's how bakeries do it.
* **Without a pizza stone:** Turn a baking sheet upside down, heat it for 40 minutes and slide the lahmacun onto it on baking paper.

### Summary

Lahmacun is proof that thinness isn't just a matter of texture but also of numbers: exactly the same ingredients, on paper-thin dough, give a 280-calorie flatbread instead of 450. It needs a blazing hot oven and a finely puréed meat mixture, and not much more. It's usually served with [cacık](/en/recipes/turkey/cacik/) or ayran; another Turkish flatbread, thicker and richer, is [pide](/en/recipes/turkey/pide/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in lahmacun?**
Answer: A standard lahmacun weighing about 145 g is about 280 calories, with about 9 g of protein. A restaurant serving usually includes two or three, meaning 560-840 calories before sides and a drink.

**Question: What's the difference between lahmacun and pizza?**
Answer: There's no cheese on lahmacun, the dough is several times thinner, and the topping is a thin layer of puréed ground meat with vegetables. The result is about 30% lighter than pizza of the same weight, and lahmacun is eaten rolled up by hand with lemon and parsley rather than in slices.

**Question: Can lahmacun be made in a home oven?**
Answer: Yes, on one condition — maximum heat (250°C and up) on a pizza stone or an upside-down baking sheet preheated for at least 40 minutes. In a lukewarm oven the dough dries out before it browns, and the result is hard rather than pliable.

**Question: Why does my lahmacun come out wet in the middle?**
Answer: Usually because of a watery meat mixture or too thick a layer. Press the mixture lightly in a sieve before spreading, and spread a thin layer — about 4 tablespoons, about 75 g, for a 26 cm circle.

**Question: Which meat works for lahmacun?**
Answer: The traditional choice is ground [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) with about 15%-20% fat, which gives juiciness and a characteristic flavor. Ground beef works too, but with very lean meat (under 10% fat) the mixture tends to dry out in baking.
