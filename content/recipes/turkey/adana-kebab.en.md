## Adana Kebab (Adana Kebabı)

**In Turkish: *Adana Kebabı*, named after the city of Adana in southern Turkey.** It is the only kebab in Turkey protected by an official standard: the Adana Chamber of Commerce registered the recipe, and for a dish to be called "Adana kebab" it must meet several defined conditions.

The three main conditions are: [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) from a young ram, **chopped with a knife, not ground in a grinder**; added sheep's tail fat in a defined proportion; and a local dried red pepper called *isot* — a pepper that is harvested, sun-dried and sweated under cloth, so its color is almost black and its flavor smoky and fruity, not just hot. The knife chopping isn't a whim: the coarse texture creates pockets of fat that melt on the grill, which is why the kebab is juicy without any extra fuss.

**Prep time:** 30 minutes · **Chilling time:** 1 hour · **Grilling time:** 8 minutes · **Yield:** 6 skewers · **Difficulty:** Medium

### The ritual around the skewer

At a real *kebapçı*, the kebab doesn't arrive alone, and the sides around it aren't decoration but a fixed structure: *ezme* — a finely chopped, spicy tomato and pepper salad; thinly sliced red onion with sumac and parsley; peppers and [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) roasted over the same coals; lavash bread laid under the skewer; and to drink — cold ayran or *şalgam*, fermented turnip juice. The meal opens with [lentil soup](/en/recipes/turkey/mercimek-corbasi/) and closes with black tea, not dessert.

This arrangement has a logic that isn't only culinary. The red onion, sumac, parsley and roasted vegetables add [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) and [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) to the meal; vitamin C improves iron absorption from the meat, and studies on meat cooked at high heat suggest that plant antioxidants in the meal reduce part of the oxidative load it creates. A plate of meat alone loses all of this.

### Ingredients (for 6 skewers)

* 800 g [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) shoulder or neck, coarsely chopped with a knife
* 120 g sheep's tail fat (alya) — or replace both the meat and fat with 920 g ground lamb at 20%–25% fat
* 2 long hot green peppers, very finely chopped
* 4 [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) cloves, crushed
* 1.5 tablespoons pul biber, or a paprika and chili blend in the amounts given below
* 1 tablespoon coarse salt, 1 teaspoon black pepper
In this recipe the onion is served alongside the kebab and does not go into the mixture.

**To serve:** lavash bread, roasted [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) and peppers, red onion with sumac and parsley, [cacık](/en/recipes/turkey/cacik/) or ayran, and [lemon](/en/food/lemon/) wedges.

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Sheep's tail fat is called "alya" in Israel, and you can ask the butcher for it. If you can't get it, replace the meat and the added fat together with 920 g of ground lamb at 20%–25% fat; don't add the 120 g of fat on top. Pul biber is Turkish pepper flakes, and isot is a different, darker spice. A home substitute for the pepper quantity in the recipe: 1 tablespoon sweet paprika, half a teaspoon smoked paprika and a quarter to half a teaspoon ground chili. Serve in laffa or pita instead of lavash.

### Method

1. **Chop with a knife.** On a large board, chop the meat and fat together with two heavy knives to a coarse, even texture. You can also ask the butcher for a coarse grind with the largest plate, but not a fine grind — then it's no longer Adana.
2. **Add and knead.** Add the hot peppers, garlic, isot, salt and pepper, and knead by hand 5-8 minutes until the mixture becomes sticky and holds together. Kneading develops the meat proteins and makes the kebab cling to the skewer.
3. **Chill.** Refrigerate for at least an hour. A cold mixture sticks better to the skewer and doesn't fall onto the coals.
4. **Shape.** Wet your hands with cold water and spread about 160 g of the mixture on a wide, flat skewer, about 25 cm long. A round skewer won't hold — the width is what keeps the meat from spinning.
5. **Grill over coals.** Grill over glowing coals but not in flames, about 4 minutes per side, turning often. Dripping fat will ignite flames — move the skewer away for a moment rather than leaving it in the flame.
6. **Rest on lavash.** Lay the skewers on lavash bread for a few minutes: the bread soaks up the juices and becomes part of the dish. This is the traditional way to serve it, not a garnish.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is one skewer — about 160 g of mixture, which yields about 140 g of cooked meat.

| Component | Per skewer (140 g cooked) |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **About 450** |
| Protein | About 24 g |
| Fat | About 39 g |
| Of which saturated fat | About 17 g |
| Carbohydrates | Less than 2 g |
| Iron | About 3 mg |

**The restaurant plate is much bigger than the skewer alone:** a skewer on lavash (about 200 calories for the bread), with roasted vegetables, rice and cacık — about 800-950 calories in total. The skewer alone is only part of the number.

The strong point is the protein: 24 g in one skewer, along with heme [iron](/en/food/iron/), which is absorbed much better than plant iron, plus [zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) and vitamin B12. The weak point is the amount of saturated fat — about 17 g per skewer, close to the entire recommended daily limit — and the kebab's classification as [red meat](/en/food/red-meat/) cooked at high heat, which forms heterocyclic amines (HCA) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAH). Grilling over coals without direct flame, without black charring, and alongside antioxidant-rich vegetables significantly reduces exposure.

### Variations and tips

* **A leaner version:** Replace some of the lamb with turkey or lean beef and cut the tail fat in half. The serving drops to about 300 calories, at the cost of juiciness.
* **Urfa vs. Adana:** *Urfa Kebabı* is exactly the same recipe without hot pepper — the version for kids and the heat-sensitive.
* **Without a grill:** A very heavy grill pan or an oven on top broil at 250°C (482°F), 5 minutes per side. The result is good, but the smoky flavor won't be reproduced.
* **Check the seasoning:** Fry a small ball of the mixture in a pan and taste before spreading it on the skewers.

### Summary

Adana kebab is defined by three details — knife-chopped lamb, sheep fat and isot pepper — and without them it's just a good kebab. About 450 calories and 24 g of protein per skewer, with high saturated fat that justifies saving it for a special-occasion meal. Serve it with [cacık](/en/recipes/turkey/cacik/) or a salad, and [lentil soup](/en/recipes/turkey/mercimek-corbasi/) makes a fitting first course. The parallel kebab in Persian cuisine, finely pounded and served over rice, is [chelo kabab koobideh](/en/recipes/iran/chelo-kabab-koobideh/). More recipes in [Turkish cuisine](/en/recipes/turkey/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in Adana kebab?**
Answer: One skewer of about 140 g cooked is about 450 calories, with about 24 g of protein and about 39 g of fat. A full restaurant plate — a skewer on lavash with rice, roasted vegetables and cacık — reaches about 800-950 calories.

**Question: What's the difference between Adana kebab and regular kebab?**
Answer: Adana kebab is defined by a standard from the Adana Chamber of Commerce — lamb chopped with a knife rather than ground, sheep's tail fat in a fixed proportion, and local dried isot pepper. Regular kebab is finely ground in a grinder, usually contains onion, and is sometimes made from beef.

**Question: Can I make Adana kebab from regular ground meat?**
Answer: You can, but the texture will change. Finely ground meat releases its fat quickly and dries out, whereas coarse knife chopping keeps pockets of fat that melt during grilling. If there's no choice, ask for a coarse grind with the largest plate and grind only once.

**Question: Why does the kebab fall off the skewer?**
Answer: Usually because of a round skewer instead of a wide, flat one, a mixture that wasn't kneaded enough, or meat that wasn't chilled before shaping. Kneading for 5-8 minutes and an hour in the fridge almost always solve it.

**Question: What is isot pepper and what can replace it?**
Answer: Isot is a red pepper from the Urfa region that is sun-dried and sweated under cloth, so its color is very dark and its flavor smoky and fruity alongside the heat. A reasonable substitute is a combination of smoked paprika with Turkish pul biber or with chili flakes.
