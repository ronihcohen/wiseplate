## Kleicha

**In Iraqi Arabic: *كليجة*.** In Hebrew it is written "קליצ'ה" and also "כליצ'ה" or "קלֵיצ'ה". This is Iraq's holiday cookie — a dough rich in cardamom and rose water, filled with [dates](/en/food/dates/) or with walnuts and sugar, and baked until lightly golden.

Kleicha is considered Iraq's national cookie, and it is made for Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, for Purim and the holidays of Iraqi Jews, and for every family occasion. Food historians link it to date-filled pastries documented in ancient Mesopotamia under the name *qullupu* — a connection that is hard to prove directly, but the Tigris and Euphrates region is the oldest date-growing area in the world, and the combination of dough and date filling has been documented there for a very long time.

**Prep time:** 45 minutes · **Rest time:** 1 hour · **Bake time:** 20 minutes · **Yield:** about 40 cookies · **Difficulty:** medium

### Dates: the filling that needs no sugar

What sets kleicha apart from most holiday cookies in the region is that its classic filling — [date](/en/food/dates/) paste — contains no added sugar at all. The dates provide all the sweetness themselves, and also the texture: pectin and soluble fiber that make the paste pliable and let you spread it on the dough without it cracking.

This changes the nutritional profile in an interesting way. The sugar in dates is natural sugar in a fiber matrix — about 7 grams of fiber per 100 grams of dates — so it is absorbed more slowly than free sugar, and it comes along with [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/), [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) and [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/). That does not make kleicha a health food: the dough itself is rich in butter or oil, and it remains a cookie. But compared with [baklava](/en/recipes/turkey/baklava/) soaked in sugar syrup or [sholeh zard](/en/recipes/iran/shole-zard/) with 27 grams of added sugar per serving, the difference is real.

More on dates themselves — including why they are calorie-dense despite their benefits — on the [dates](/en/food/dates/) page.

### Ingredients (for about 40 cookies)

**For the dough:**
* 500 grams flour
* 200 grams soft butter or 180 ml oil
* 7 grams dry yeast + 1 teaspoon sugar
* 150-180 ml lukewarm [milk](/en/food/milk/) or water
* 2 teaspoons ground cardamom — the spice most associated with kleicha
* 1/2 teaspoon ground mahlab — optional; you can leave it out or replace it with half a teaspoon of fennel for a different flavor
* 1 tablespoon rose water
* 1/2 teaspoon salt

**For the date filling:**
* 500 grams good-quality [date](/en/food/dates/) paste
* 2 tablespoons butter or oil
* 1 teaspoon cardamom, 1/2 teaspoon [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/), a pinch of nutmeg

**For the walnut filling (alternative):**
* 250 grams chopped [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/), 100 grams sugar, 1 teaspoon cardamom, 2 tablespoons rose water

**For the topping:** 1 beaten [egg](/en/food/eggs/) + sesame seeds or sliced [almonds](/en/food/almonds/).

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Mahlab is a spice made from the kernels of the mahaleb cherry, and it is not fennel. You can leave it out or use half a teaspoon of ground fennel for a different, anise-like flavor. Store-bought baking date paste works for the filling; liquid silan (date syrup) is not a substitute for paste. Use culinary rose water, and a concentrated extract only at the manufacturer's dosage. You can skip the rose water and add a tablespoon of water to the dough instead.

### Method

1. **Activate the yeast.** Mix the yeast into the lukewarm milk with the sugar and leave for about 10 minutes until foamy.
2. **Knead a rich dough.** Mix the flour, cardamom, mahlab or fennel if you chose to add it, salt and butter to a crumbly texture, add the yeast mixture and rose water, and knead for just 2-3 minutes, until the dough comes together into a soft ball that doesn't stick. **Don't over-knead** — this is a rich dough, and long kneading develops gluten and makes the cookie hard.
3. **Rest.** Cover and leave for an hour in a warm place.
4. **Make the filling.** Warm the date paste with the butter and spices over low heat for 3-4 minutes until pliable and spreadable. Cool to lukewarm.
5. **Roll up.** Divide the dough into three parts, roll each into a rectangle 3-4 mm thick, spread a thin layer of dates, and roll lengthwise into a tight log.
6. **Slice.** Cut the log into slices 1.5-2 cm thick and place them in the pan cut side up — this shows the spiral.
7. **Brush and bake.** Brush with egg, sprinkle with sesame, and bake at 180 degrees Celsius (350°F) for 18-22 minutes until **only lightly** golden. Over-browned kleicha dries out.
8. **Cool completely** before storing — it keeps setting as it cools.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is 2 cookies, about 70 grams.

Comparing the desserts in the three cuisines on the site is instructive: [baklava](/en/recipes/turkey/baklava/) is about 205 calories and 14 grams of sugar for a 45-gram square, [sholeh zard](/en/recipes/iran/shole-zard/) about 290 calories and 31 grams of added sugar for a 150-gram serving, and kleicha — about 250 calories and 18 grams of sugar for two cookies weighing 70 grams. Kleicha is not the lightest of them by equal weight, but it is the only one whose sugar comes almost entirely from fruit rather than white sugar.

The usual caveat applies here too: the serving is defined as two cookies, and kleicha is often eaten in larger quantities — six cookies are about 750 calories. The walnut-filled version contains added sugar and comes to about 280 calories for two cookies.

### Variations and tips

* **Traditional shapes:** besides the spiral, kleicha is traditionally shaped into filled discs (*khfaifiyat*) or crescents filled with walnuts.
* **Without butter:** oil works well and makes a slightly crisper cookie. The calorie difference is negligible.
* **Homemade date paste:** soak pitted dates in hot water for 20 minutes and blend. The quality is much better than industrial pastes that contain added sugar.
* **Storage:** in an airtight container at room temperature for up to two weeks; frozen for up to 3 months.

### Summary

Kleicha is a holiday cookie sweetened mostly with fruit rather than sugar: date paste inside a cardamom and rose-water dough. About 250 calories for two 70-gram cookies, with most of the sugar coming from dates. ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in kleicha?**
Answer: Two cookies (about 70 grams) are about 250 calories with about 18 grams of sugar, most of it from the dates rather than added sugar. The walnut-filled version, which contains white sugar, comes to about 280 calories for the same amount.

**Question: Is kleicha healthier than other cookies?**
Answer: It is sweetened mostly with fruit, so its sugar comes in a fiber matrix along with potassium and magnesium. Still, the dough is rich in butter, and it is still a cookie — the difference compared with syrup-soaked desserts like [baklava](/en/recipes/turkey/baklava/) is real, but it doesn't make it an everyday food.

**Question: Why did my kleicha come out hard?**
Answer: Two common reasons — kneading too long, which develops gluten in a dough that is meant to stay crumbly, and baking too long. Kleicha should come out of the oven only lightly golden; it sets as it cools.

**Question: What is cardamom and why is it essential here?**
Answer: Cardamom is an aromatic seed pod that is kleicha's signature — its aroma is identified with cardamom more than with the dates. It has no real substitute; leaving it out gives a tasty date cookie, but not kleicha.

**Question: How long does kleicha keep?**
Answer: In an airtight container at room temperature for up to two weeks — it is baked specifically to last through the holiday. Frozen, it keeps for up to 3 months and thaws at room temperature without losing texture.
