## Kubba Hamusta

**In Iraqi Arabic: *كبة حامض*, literally "sour kubba."** In Hebrew the name "חמוסטה" (hamusta) has stuck, and sometimes "קובה חמוצה" (sour kubba). It is one of the two sour soups most associated with Iraqi-Jerusalem cooking, alongside red [beet](/en/food/beets/) kubba.

The kubba itself — a thin semolina shell filled with ground meat — is the product of a whole tradition. In Iraq it had dozens of versions: fried, baked, cigar- or disc-shaped, and in various soups. The version in a sour green soup, with [celery](/en/food/celery/), chard or [spinach](/en/food/spinach/) and lemon, is the one that took root in Israel and became identified with the kubba restaurants of Jerusalem.

**Prep time:** 1 hour · **Cook time:** 40 minutes · **Total:** about 1 hour 40 minutes · **Yield:** 6 servings (about 18 dumplings) · **Difficulty:** high

### The dough with no flour: why semolina

The kubba shell is made of fine semolina, water and a little salt — no [eggs](/en/food/eggs/), no yeast and usually no flour at all. Semolina is coarsely ground wheat endosperm, and on contact with hot water it absorbs it and becomes a pliable paste that can be stretched between your palms without tearing.

That is why the ratio of water to semolina is critical, and why the dough must rest: absorbing the water takes time. Dough that is kneaded and used immediately will crumble and crack; the same dough after 20-30 minutes of rest stretches into an exceptionally thin wall. Among veteran home cooks it is a skill measured by the thickness of the wall — the thinner it is, the greater the expertise.

During cooking, some of the starch in the semolina is released into the soup and thickens it slightly. That is why hamusta soup gets richer after the dumplings cook in it, and why it is worth cooking them in the soup itself rather than separately.

### Ingredients (for 6 servings, about 18 dumplings)

**For the shell:**
* 2 cups fine semolina
* 1.25-1.5 cups hot water
* 1/2 teaspoon salt
* 1 tablespoon oil

**For the filling:**
* 400 grams ground [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) or beef
* 1 very finely chopped [onion](/en/food/onion/)
* 1 tablespoon oil for frying the filling
* 1/2 teaspoon baharat, 1/2 teaspoon allspice, salt and pepper
* A handful of chopped parsley

**For the soup:**
* 4 [celery](/en/food/celery/) stalks with leaves, sliced
* 400 grams chard or [spinach](/en/food/spinach/), chopped
* 1 chopped [onion](/en/food/onion/), 3 cloves [garlic](/en/food/garlic/)
* Juice of 2 [lemons](/en/food/lemon/)
* 1 teaspoon dried mint, 1/2 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/)
* 1.5 liters water or stock, salt and pepper
* 2 tablespoons [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Regular wheat semolina from the supermarket works for the shell; do not use instant couscous or cornmeal. Add the water gradually until the dough is pliable. You can replace the 400 grams of chard with 400 grams of spinach, or with thawed and squeezed frozen spinach of the same weight. Fresh lemon juice is the easy default for sourness.

### Method

1. **Make the filling.** Fry the onion until golden, add the meat and spices and cook until the meat loses its pink color. Add the parsley and cool completely — a hot filling tears the shell.
2. **Knead the semolina.** Mix the semolina, salt and oil, add hot water gradually and knead into a soft dough that doesn't stick. **Let rest 20-30 minutes** — this step is mandatory.
3. **Shape.** Wet your hands, take a walnut-sized ball of dough, press a hollow into it with your thumb and widen it into a thin cup. Fill with a teaspoon of filling and close by gently rolling into a smooth ball.
4. **Make the soup.** Fry the onion and garlic in olive oil, add the celery and fry for 5 minutes, add the turmeric and the water or stock, and cook for 15 minutes.
5. **Add the greens and lemon.** Add the chard or spinach, the lemon juice and the dried mint, and cook for another 5 minutes. Adjust the sourness — the soup should be distinctly sour.
6. **Cook the kubba in the soup.** Gently lower the dumplings into the simmering soup, reduce the heat and cook for 20-25 minutes without stirring vigorously. They float when they are done.
7. **Serve** immediately, with an extra lemon wedge on the side.

Nutritionally this is a far more balanced dish than it is perceived to be. Two-thirds of the volume in the bowl is vegetable soup — celery, chard or spinach, onion and garlic — so it provides fiber and [vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/) alongside protein. Most of the calories in the serving — about 200 out of 490 — actually come from the semolina shell, not from the meat or the soup.

Two points for anyone counting: the first is that the shell is made from white wheat semolina, meaning refined carbohydrate — you can replace up to a quarter of the semolina with whole-wheat semolina without hurting its pliability. The second is [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/), which is reasonable in a homemade serving but much higher in commercial kubba restaurants.

### Variations and tips

* **Vegetarian version:** fill with cooked [lentils](/en/food/lentil/) with fried onion and pine nuts. Common mainly in modern versions.
* **Beet kubba:** exactly the same dumplings, in a sweet-and-sour red beet soup. It is hamusta's famous sister.
* **Freezing:** freeze the uncooked dumplings in a single layer on a tray, then pack them. Cook straight from frozen, adding 5 minutes.
* **Dumplings that fall apart:** almost always a hot filling, dough that didn't rest, or a rolling boil. All three are preventable.

### Summary

Kubba hamusta is a dish that splits the effort between two parts that are each simple on their own — a semolina dough and a sour vegetable soup — and needs practice in only one step: stretching the shell. About 490 calories for a serving of three dumplings and soup, with 22 grams of protein and 6 grams of fiber. ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in kubba hamusta?**
Answer: A serving of three dumplings with about 280 ml of soup is about 490 calories, with about 22 grams of protein and 6 grams of fiber. A serving at a kubba restaurant tends to be larger and saltier.

**Question: Why do kubba dumplings fall apart during cooking?**
Answer: Three main reasons — a filling added hot rather than cooled, semolina dough that didn't rest for 20-30 minutes before shaping, and soup boiling hard. Cook at a gentle simmer and don't stir the pot vigorously once the dumplings are in.

**Question: What is the difference between kubba hamusta and beet kubba?**
Answer: The dumplings are identical; the soup is different. Hamusta is a sour green soup based on celery, chard or spinach and lemon, and beet kubba is a sweet-and-sour red soup based on beets and sugar or silan (date syrup).

**Question: Can you use flour instead of semolina?**
Answer: Not really. Semolina absorbs water and forms a pliable dough that stretches into a thin wall, whereas flour forms an elastic dough that shrinks back. You can add a tablespoon or two of flour for stability, but the base must be semolina.

**Question: Can you make kubba ahead of time?**
Answer: Yes, and that is the usual way. Freeze the uncooked dumplings in a single layer on a tray until hard, then transfer them to a bag. They keep for about 3 months and are cooked straight from frozen, adding about 5 minutes of cooking.
