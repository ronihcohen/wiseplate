## T'bit

**In Iraqi Arabic: *تبيت*, from a root meaning "to stay the night" — to pass the night.** In Hebrew it is written "תבית" and also "תביט" or "טבית". The name describes the method: the pot goes onto low heat on Friday evening and comes off at midday the next day, after about 12 hours.

T'bit is the Shabbat dish of Iraqi Jews, the Baghdadi counterpart of the Ashkenazi hamin (cholent) and the Moroccan skhina. All three were born of exactly the same constraint — the prohibition on cooking on Shabbat — and the same solution: a dish set on low heat before Shabbat begins that keeps cooking on its own. What sets the Iraqi version apart is that it is built around a whole [chicken](/en/food/chicken-breast/) stuffed with [rice](/en/food/rice/), rather than around beef and legumes, and that [amba](/en/recipes/iraq/amba/) is almost always served alongside it.

**Prep time:** 40 minutes · **Cook time:** 10-12 hours · **Total:** a whole night · **Yield:** 6 servings · **Difficulty:** medium

### What happens to food after 12 hours on low heat

Long overnight cooking isn't just "more of the same." At a temperature of 90-100 degrees Celsius over hours, processes take place that don't happen in an hour: the [collagen](/en/food/collagen/) in the chicken's connective tissue breaks down into gelatin and gives the sauce body; the grains of rice absorb the fat and juices instead of water; and slow Maillard reactions darken the color and deepen the flavor — especially in the bottom layer touching the pot.

That layer is the heart of the matter. In rice that stays in contact with the bottom of the pot for hours, a hard brown crust forms that is considered the best part of the dish, just as *tahdig* is regarded in Persian cuisine — see [tahchin](/en/recipes/iran/tahchin/).

There is also a safety point worth knowing. Cooked rice is an especially sensitive food, because *Bacillus cereus* spores survive cooking and multiply at room temperature. T'bit that stays on **continuous** low heat is safe in this respect — the temperature is too high for bacteria to multiply. The risk starts after the meal, when the pot sits on the counter for hours. Full details on the page [the danger of reheated rice](/en/food/reheated_rice_dangers/).

### Ingredients (for 6 servings)

**For the chicken:**
* 1 whole chicken (about 1.6 kg)
* 1 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), 1 teaspoon paprika, 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom, 1/2 teaspoon [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/), salt and pepper
* 3 tablespoons oil (1 for the rice mixture, 2 for searing the chicken)

**For the stuffing and rice:**
* 2.5 cups basmati [rice](/en/food/rice/), rinsed and soaked
* 1 large chopped [onion](/en/food/onion/)
* 2 grated [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) (or 2 tablespoons tomato paste)
* 1 teaspoon baharat, or a homemade blend in the same amount per the note below
* 1 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), salt and pepper
* 4-6 [eggs](/en/food/eggs/) in their shells — to cook along with the dish
* 800-1,000 ml boiling water — enough to cover the rice by about 2 cm

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Basmati rice and a regular supermarket baharat blend work fine. For a substitute blend in the amount of one teaspoon, mix a quarter teaspoon of cumin, a quarter teaspoon of cinnamon, a quarter teaspoon of allspice, an eighth of a teaspoon of cardamom and an eighth of a teaspoon of cloves. There is no need to double the amount of spices when substituting the blend.

### Method

1. **Make the rice mixture.** Fry the onion in oil until golden, add the grated tomatoes and spices, and cook for 5 minutes. Stir in the soaked and drained rice.
2. **Stuff the chicken.** Season the chicken inside and out, and fill the body cavity with about a third of the rice mixture — only up to two-thirds of the volume, because the rice swells. Close the opening with toothpicks or string.
3. **Sear.** Heat oil in a heavy pot and sear the chicken on all sides until browned — about 10 minutes. This step sets the color and flavor of the whole pot.
4. **Assemble the pot.** Remove the chicken, line the bottom of the pot with the rest of the rice mixture, set the chicken on top, and arrange the eggs in their shells around it.
5. **Add liquid.** Pour in boiling water to cover the rice by about 2 cm (don't cover the chicken), adjust the salt, and bring briefly to a boil.
6. **Cook overnight.** Cover tightly, reduce to the lowest possible heat (or transfer to the oven at 100 degrees Celsius, about 210°F), and leave for 10-12 hours.
7. **Serve.** Remove the chicken, take it apart, and serve with the rice, the resulting brown eggs, [amba](/en/recipes/iraq/amba/), salad and [lemon](/en/food/lemon/).

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is one-sixth of the pot — about 180 grams of chicken with about 250 grams of rice and an egg.

| Component | Per serving |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **about 820** |
| Protein | about 46 grams |
| Carbohydrates | about 69 grams |
| Fat | about 38 grams |
| Iron | about 3 mg |

The strong side is clear: 46 grams of protein per serving, from the chicken and the egg together. The weak side is structural and can't be fixed without changing the dish — the white rice that absorbs the chicken fat overnight. This combination is exactly what makes t'bit so delicious, and also what raises the calories: the same rice without the chicken is about 300 calories per serving, and here it reaches about 380.

What you can improve: remove the chicken skin before serving (a saving of about 110 calories per serving, without giving up its contribution to the flavor during cooking), and serve alongside a large vegetable salad that brings in the fiber the dish almost entirely lacks.

### Variations and tips

* **Without a whole chicken:** you can use bone-in thighs and spread the rice around them. The result is very similar and easier to serve.
* **The bottom crust:** if you want a stronger crust, add a tablespoon of oil to the bottom and raise the heat for the last 10 minutes.
* **Oven instead of stovetop:** 100-110 degrees in the oven gives better control and prevents burning on the bottom.
* **Leftovers:** cool within an hour and refrigerate for up to two days — cooked rice left at room temperature is a real risk, not excessive caution.

### Summary

T'bit is the Baghdadi solution to the same problem that gave rise to hamin and skhina: a dish that cooks by itself all night. About 820 calories per serving with 46 grams of protein, and a rice crust at the bottom of the pot that is considered the best part. It is served with [amba](/en/recipes/iraq/amba/), and the more festive Iraqi dish from the same family is [quzi](/en/recipes/iraq/quzi/). More recipes in [Iraqi cuisine](/en/recipes/iraq/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in t'bit?**
Answer: One-sixth of the pot — about 180 grams of chicken with 250 grams of rice and an egg — is about 820 calories, with about 46 grams of protein. It is a generous Shabbat dish; removing the chicken skin before serving cuts about 110 calories from it.

**Question: What is the difference between t'bit and hamin (cholent)?**
Answer: Both dishes were born of the same need — a hot meal on Shabbat without cooking — but their makeup is different. Iraqi t'bit is built around a whole chicken stuffed with rice, and Ashkenazi hamin around beef, beans, potatoes and barley. What they share are the brown eggs that cook in the pot.

**Question: Is it safe to leave rice on the heat all night?**
Answer: Yes, as long as the heat stays on continuously. The risk in cooked rice comes from *Bacillus cereus* spores that multiply at room temperature, whereas a pot that stays on low heat is at a temperature too high for them to multiply. The risk actually starts after the meal — see [reheated rice](/en/food/reheated_rice_dangers/).

**Question: Can you make t'bit in the oven?**
Answer: Yes, and many people prefer it that way. Put the covered pot in the oven at 100-110 degrees for 10-12 hours. The oven distributes the heat more evenly and reduces the risk of the bottom burning.

**Question: Why did my rice come out mushy?**
Answer: Usually too much liquid or rice that wasn't soaked and rinsed. Rinse until the water runs clear, soak for about an hour, drain well, and cover with liquid by only about 2 cm — the rice will also absorb the chicken juices overnight.
