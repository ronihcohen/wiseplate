## Masgouf

**In Iraqi Arabic: *مسكوف*, from a root meaning "propped up" or "set upright."** In Hebrew it is written "מסגוף" and also "מסגוּף" or "מסקוף". The name describes the technique exactly: the fish is split open, stretched on two wooden stakes, and set **upright** around an open fire — not over it.

This is Iraq's national dish, and it is inseparably tied to the Tigris River and its banks in Baghdad, where a whole row of masgouf restaurants operated for generations. The traditional fish is river carp (*shabout* or *binni*), and the seasoning is deliberately minimal: [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), salt, [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/) and sometimes a little tamarind. Everything else is smoke, time and patience.

**Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Grilling time:** 1.5-2.5 hours · **Total:** 2-3 hours · **Yield:** 4 servings · **Difficulty:** medium

### Why grill a fish for two hours instead of ten minutes

The masgouf technique looks inefficient until you understand what it does. The fish stands 40-60 cm from the fire, so it cooks mainly by radiant heat rather than by contact — at a relatively low temperature for an hour and a half to two hours.

This has two results. The first is texture: fish flesh grilled slowly doesn't contract sharply or lose liquid like fish thrown on a scorching grill, so it stays completely moist. The second is smoke: apricot or palm wood burns slowly and releases aromatic compounds that bind to the fish's fat over time, and that's impossible in ten minutes. Only at the end, when the fish is almost done, is it laid on the coals for a few minutes — and that is the stage that gives it its crust.

From a health standpoint there's an easy-to-miss advantage here: meat grilled at high heat in direct contact develops larger amounts of heterocyclic amines and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. Grilling with moderate radiant heat, without the fat dripping directly onto the coals, reduces their formation — the opposite of [Adana kebab](/en/recipes/turkey/adana-kebab/), which is grilled quickly and directly over the fire.

### Ingredients (for 4 servings)

* 1 whole carp (about 2 kg), cleaned and butterflied — or 2 tilapia or sea bass of about 1 kg each
* 3 tablespoons [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)
* 1 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/)
* 1 tablespoon [lemon](/en/food/lemon/) juice, as an easy-to-find substitute for tamarind
* 1 teaspoon coarse salt, 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
* 2 cloves [garlic](/en/food/garlic/), crushed

**To serve:**
* 3 sliced [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/), 2 sliced [onions](/en/food/onion/)
* [Amba](/en/recipes/iraq/amba/), [samoon](/en/recipes/iraq/samoon/) or laffa, raw onion and lemon

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Ask the fishmonger for a whole carp of about 2 kg, cleaned and butterflied from the back, masgouf style. Alternatively, use two tilapia or sea bass of about 1 kg each; they will need less grilling time, depending on thickness. A tablespoon of lemon juice works in place of tamarind. Laffa or pita are easy-to-find alternatives to samoon for serving.

### Method

1. **Butterfly the fish.** Cut along the back (not the belly), open the fish into one wide flat piece, remove the innards and rinse well. Leave the scales and skin on — they are the shield that prevents drying out.
2. **Season lightly.** Mix the olive oil, turmeric, garlic, salt, pepper and lemon juice, and spread on **the flesh side only**. The outer side stays dry.
3. **Set it upright.** Stretch the fish between two skewers or a metal frame, and set it at an angle facing the fire at a distance of 40-60 cm, with the flesh side toward the fire.
4. **Grill slowly.** 60-90 minutes, turning the frame now and then so the heat is distributed. The fish is done when the flesh is opaque and flakes easily with a fork.
5. **Finish on the coals.** Lay the fish skin side down directly on dying coals for 5-10 minutes, until the skin chars and the flesh absorbs the last of the smoke.
6. **Serve** on a platter with tomatoes and onion, alongside bread, [amba](/en/recipes/iraq/amba/), lemon and raw onion.

**Oven version:** preheat the oven to 200 degrees Celsius (400°F), bake the butterflied fish on a rack for 35-45 minutes, and finish for 4 minutes under the top broiler. There's no smoke — but the texture holds up, and you can add a teaspoon of smoked paprika to the seasoning as a partial substitute.

This is one of the best-value dishes across all three recipe categories on the site: 50 grams of complete protein for about 470 calories, almost no carbohydrates, and [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) in an amount that depends on the type of fish. River carp is a moderately fatty fish, so it provides less omega-3 than [salmon](/en/food/salmon/) or [sardines](/en/food/sardines/), but more than lean fish such as tilapia.

Another point in its favor: freshwater fish of this kind sit low in the food chain and therefore accumulate less mercury than large predatory fish such as [tuna](/en/food/tuna/). On the other hand, fish from polluted rivers can accumulate other contaminants, so the source of the fish matters.

**What raises the number** is not the fish but what surrounds it: a whole [samoon](/en/recipes/iraq/samoon/) (about 250 calories), rice, and [amba](/en/recipes/iraq/amba/). A full masgouf meal in an Iraqi restaurant is 800-1,000 calories.

### Variations and tips

* **Choosing the fish:** any moderately fatty fish weighing 1.5-2.5 kg works. It is important that it be whole with skin — a fillet will dry out.
* **The grilling wood:** in Iraq they use apricot, palm or willow wood. Fruit woods in general give a gentle smoke; conifers (pine) are unsuitable and give a resinous taste.
* **Tamarind:** adds a rounded acidity that suits a fatty fish. Lemon juice is a reasonable substitute but sharper.
* **No early salting:** salt applied hours in advance draws liquid out. Season shortly before grilling.

### Summary

Masgouf is a dish whose whole secret lies in the technique rather than the ingredients: four seasonings, two hours of gentle radiant heat, and a short finish on the coals. About 470 calories and 50 grams of protein per serving — the best protein-to-calorie ratio among the Iraqi dishes here. It is almost always served with [amba](/en/recipes/iraq/amba/) and [samoon](/en/recipes/iraq/samoon/). More recipes in [Iraqi cuisine](/en/recipes/iraq/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in masgouf?**
Answer: A serving of 250 grams of grilled fish is about 470 calories and contains about 50 grams of protein. A full masgouf meal at a restaurant — with samoon bread, rice and amba — comes to 800-1,000 calories.

**Question: Why is the fish grilled standing up rather than on the grill?**
Answer: Standing at a distance from the fire, the fish cooks with moderate radiant heat for an hour and a half to two hours, so it doesn't shrink, doesn't lose liquid, and absorbs smoke over time. A direct grill cooks it in ten minutes — too fast for the smoke to penetrate.

**Question: Which fish is suitable for masgouf in Israel?**
Answer: The original fish is river carp, and in Israel carp, tilapia (St. Peter's fish), a large sea bass or sea bream all work. The criterion is a whole fish weighing 1.5-2.5 kg with skin and scales — a fillet dries out during long grilling.

**Question: Can you make masgouf in the oven?**
Answer: You can, with a good result in terms of texture: 35-45 minutes at 200 degrees on a rack, then 4 minutes under the top broiler. What can't be reproduced is the smoke; a teaspoon of smoked paprika in the seasoning mix partly makes up for it.

**Question: Is masgouf healthier than grilled fish?**
Answer: In terms of the process, yes, to some extent. Grilling with moderate radiant heat, without fat dripping onto the coals and sending up acrid smoke, reduces the formation of heterocyclic amines and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons compared with direct high-heat grilling.
