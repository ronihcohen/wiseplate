## Iraqi Bamia

**In Arabic: *بامية*.** In Hebrew it is "במיה" — exactly the same word, and in English the vegetable is okra. This is one of the basic stews of Iraqi cuisine: whole [okra](/en/food/okra/), [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) on the bone or cubed, [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/), garlic and sourness — lemon or tamarind — cooked together until the okra is soft and the sauce is thick.

Okra is a vegetable that draws more polarized reactions than any other, and for one reason only: the sliminess. These are mucilaginous compounds (*mucilage*) — soluble polysaccharides that are released from the cut and thicken the liquid around them. Iraqi cuisine sees them not as a problem but as a feature, yet even there techniques are used to tame them, and that is exactly what separates a successful okra stew from a failed one.

**Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Cooking time:** an hour and a half · **Total:** about an hour and fifty minutes · **Yield:** 6 servings · **Difficulty:** easy

### Sliminess: what it is, and how to control it

Okra's sliminess is a mixture of polysaccharides and glycoproteins locked inside the cells and released as soon as a cell is cut and meets water. Three factors affect how much of it ends up in the pot:

* **Cutting.** Cut okra releases several times more than whole okra. In the Iraqi version, only the hard "cap" is removed at an angle, without cutting into the pod.
* **Acidity.** [Lemon](/en/food/lemon/) juice, tamarind or [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) break down some of the polysaccharides and reduce the sliminess. That is why almost every cuisine that cooks okra does so with acid — no coincidence.
* **Dry searing.** Briefly frying or roasting the okra in oil before adding the liquids seals the surface.

Nutritionally, those very mucilaginous compounds are soluble dietary fiber — the same family linked to slowing sugar absorption and binding bile acids. In other words, what is considered a culinary drawback is exactly the vegetable's nutritional advantage. More on the [okra](/en/food/okra/) page.

### Ingredients (for 6 servings)

* 800 grams small fresh [okra](/en/food/okra/) (or frozen)
* 600 grams [lamb](/en/food/lamb/), cubed or on the bone
* 1 large [onion](/en/food/onion/), chopped
* 6 [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) cloves — some left whole
* 4 grated [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) + 2 tablespoons tomato paste
* Juice of 1.5 [lemons](/en/food/lemon/), or diluted tamarind concentrate as per the note below
* 1 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), 1 teaspoon baharat, salt and pepper
* 4 tablespoons oil
* 700 ml hot water
* A handful of chopped cilantro

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Small frozen okra works in the same amount and goes into the pot without thawing; there is no need to chop it. The readily available option for sourness is lemon juice. Tamarind is sold as a paste or concentrate in Asian and spice shops, in varying strengths; if you choose it, start with a teaspoon of concentrate mixed with two tablespoons of water and add more to taste. Instead of a teaspoon of baharat, you can mix half a teaspoon of cumin, a quarter teaspoon of allspice and a quarter teaspoon of cinnamon.

### Method

1. **Clean fresh okra.** With frozen okra, skip the cleaning and searing and add it directly at step 6. With fresh okra: remove only the hard cap with a knife, in a diagonal cut that does not enter the pod. Rinse and dry well.
2. **Sear the okra.** Fry it in 2 tablespoons of oil over high heat for 4-5 minutes until it firms up and browns lightly, then remove. You can also roast it in the oven at 220°C (430°F) for 12 minutes.
3. **Sear the meat.** In the same pot, fry the onion until golden, add the meat and sear on all sides. Add the turmeric and baharat.
4. **Cook the meat.** Add the hot water, bring to a boil, lower the heat and cook covered for an hour until the meat softens.
5. **Add the tomatoes and acid.** Add the grated tomatoes, tomato paste, garlic and lemon juice or tamarind, and cook for 10 minutes.
6. **Return the okra.** Add the seared okra, or the frozen okra without thawing, and cook for another 20-25 minutes without stirring hard — aggressive stirring breaks the pods and releases sliminess.
7. **Serve** with fresh cilantro on top, alongside white [rice](/en/food/rice/) or [samoon](/en/recipes/iraq/samoon/).

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is one sixth of the pot — about 350 grams of stew — alongside rice.

| Nutrient | Serving of stew (350 grams) |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **about 410** |
| Protein | about 21 g |
| Fat | about 28 g |
| Carbohydrates | about 18 g |
| of which fiber | about 6 g |
| Magnesium | about 60 mg |

With 180 grams of white [rice](/en/food/rice/), as the dish is actually served, the plate reaches about 630 calories.

[Okra](/en/food/okra/) itself is one of the lowest-calorie vegetables — about 33 calories per 100 grams — and yet it provides soluble fiber, [vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/), [folate](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b9/) and [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/). In practice, this means the roughly 130 grams of okra in each serving add volume and fiber at a cost of only about 45 calories.

As with most meat stews, saturated fat is the side that calls for consideration. Using lean lamb shoulder or beef instead of fatty cuts brings the serving down to about 330 calories, and if you cook a day ahead you can remove the layer of fat that solidifies on top.

### Variations and tips

* **Vegan version:** leave out the meat, double the okra and add cooked [chickpeas](/en/food/chickpeas/). The serving drops to about 190 calories and also works as a cold dish.
* **Frozen okra:** works well and even releases less sliminess than fresh. Don't thaw — put it into the pan frozen for searing.
* **Pod size:** small, young okra is always better. Large pods become fibrous and tough, and that does not change with cooking.
* **Making ahead:** the stew improves on the second day. The okra softens further, so cook it a little less the first time.

### Summary

Iraqi bamia shows that what is considered the vegetable's drawback — the sliminess — has a simple fix: don't cut, sear, and cook with acid. About 410 calories per serving of stew with 21 grams of protein and 6 grams of fiber. The more elaborate Iraqi dish with the same tamarind sourness is [dolma](/en/recipes/iraq/dolma/). ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in Iraqi bamia?**
Answer: A serving of stew of about 350 grams, one sixth of the pot, is about 410 calories with about 21 grams of protein. With a serving of white rice, as it is actually served, the plate reaches about 630 calories.

**Question: How do you keep okra from being slimy?**
Answer: Three things together — don't cut into the pod but remove only the cap at an angle; sear the okra in oil or in the oven before adding the liquids; and cook it in an acidic environment of lemon, tamarind or tomatoes. It is also important not to stir the pot hard.

**Question: Is frozen okra as good as fresh?**
Answer: For a stew — yes, and sometimes even better, because it releases less sliminess. The only condition is not to thaw it: put it frozen directly into the pan for searing, otherwise it releases water and softens too much.

**Question: What is so healthy about okra?**
Answer: It is very low in calories (about 33 per 100 grams) and yet provides soluble fiber, vitamin K, folate and magnesium. Its soluble fiber is exactly the compounds responsible for the sliminess — what is considered a culinary drawback is the nutritional advantage. Details on the [okra](/en/food/okra/) page.

**Question: Can you make bamia without meat?**
Answer: Yes. Double the amount of okra, add cooked chickpeas for protein, and cook for only 30 minutes instead of an hour and a half. In Iraq this version is also eaten cold, at room temperature, alongside bread.
