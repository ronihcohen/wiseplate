## Ghormeh Sabzi

**In Persian: *قورمه سبزی*, literally "fried herbs."** In Hebrew it is written "קורמה סבזי" and also appears as "גורמה סבזי" or "חורמה סבזי". Two words that describe the recipe precisely: *ghormeh* is a method of long frying originally meant to preserve meat, and *sabzi* is the collective name for fresh herbs.

This is the dish most Iranians would pick if they had to choose just one. It is built on an amount of herbs that sounds excessive the first time — almost a kilogram of parsley, cilantro, dill and leek for one pot — which shrinks during long frying into a dark, aromatic mass. Two more ingredients give the stew its identity: *limoo amani*, lemons dried in the sun until they are light as paper and dark inside, and dried fenugreek — an herb with a strong aroma, a small amount of which determines the whole smell of the dish.

**Prep time:** 30 minutes · **Cooking time:** 2.5-3 hours · **Total:** about 3.5 hours · **Yield:** 6 servings · **Difficulty:** medium

### Why frying the herbs takes 40 minutes

The step that separates a good ghormeh sabzi from an average one is not the meat but the frying of the herbs. Fresh herbs contain about 90% water, and as long as the water is inside them the herbs stew rather than fry — and the result is a greenish stew that tastes of grass. Frying for 30-40 minutes over medium heat evaporates the water, and once it is gone, browning reactions begin that create the dark color and deep flavor associated with the dish.

It is the same principle that explains why caramelized [onion](/en/food/onion/) differs from boiled onion, and why it matters to reduce the [tomato](/en/food/tomato/) juices before adding eggs to Turkish [menemen](/en/recipes/turkey/menemen/). Water in the pot is a ceiling of 100 degrees; only after it evaporates does the temperature rise and the flavor change.

### Ingredients (for 6 servings)

* 600 grams [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) or beef stew meat, cubed (shoulder, neck or shank)
* 2 large [onions](/en/food/onion/), chopped
* 300 grams parsley, 200 grams cilantro, 100 grams dill, 200 grams leek or scallions — finely chopped (or 800 grams of a frozen mix of the same herbs, thawed and squeezed)
* 2 tablespoons dried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi, *shanbalileh*) — no more, it dominates the aroma
* 1 cup dried red kidney beans, soaked overnight — or 450–500 grams cooked and drained beans, added in the last 20 minutes
* 4–6 Persian dried lemons (loomi) (*limoo amani*), pierced — or 3 tablespoons [lemon](/en/food/lemon/) juice
* 1 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), salt and black pepper
* 4-5 tablespoons oil or [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)
* 1.2 liters hot water

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

This recipe uses dried fenugreek leaves, also sold as kasuri methi, not ground fenugreek seeds or fenugreek paste. If you have no leaves, you can skip the two tablespoons; you will get an herb stew with a milder aroma. Weigh the herbs after cleaning, and frozen ones after thawing and squeezing. Persian dried lemon is also called loomi; with the juice substitute, add 3 tablespoons only at the end and taste.

### Method

1. **Fry the onion and meat.** Heat 2 tablespoons of oil, fry the onion until golden, add the turmeric for half a minute and then the meat cubes. Sear on all sides.
2. **Fry the herbs separately.** In a large pan, in 3 tablespoons of oil, fry all the herbs and the fenugreek over medium heat for 30-40 minutes, stirring, until the volume shrinks dramatically and the color turns dark green, almost black. This is the heart of the recipe.
3. **Combine.** Transfer the herbs to the meat pot, add the hot water, salt and pepper, and bring to a boil. Lower the heat and cook covered for 1.5 hours.
4. **Add the beans and lemons.** Boil the soaked kidney beans in separate water at a hard boil for 10 minutes and drain — red kidney beans contain a lectin that breaks down only at high heat, and slow cooking alone is not enough for that. Add them to the pot together with the pierced dried lemons, if using, and continue cooking for another hour until the meat and beans are tender. With canned beans, skip the boiling and add them drained only in the last 20 minutes.
5. **Check the acidity.** If using lemon juice instead of dried lemons, add it now, gradually. Dried lemons release acidity gradually, and if they burst they also add bitterness. Remove them as soon as the acidity is sufficient.
6. **Rest.** The stew is ready when a dark film of oil rises to the surface. Serve with steamed white [rice](/en/food/rice/).

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is one sixth of the pot — about 320 grams of stew — alongside rice.

With 180 grams of cooked white rice, as the dish is always served, the plate reaches about 740 calories.

This is one of the more balanced meat stews there is: the amount of herbs and beans brings about 9 grams of fiber into the dish — far beyond a regular meat stew — and the beans add plant protein that complements the meat's. The [iron](/en/food/iron/) here comes from two sources — heme from the meat and non-heme from the beans and herbs — and the acidity of the dried lemon improves absorption of the latter. The other side is the oil: four to five tablespoons in the pot are about 570 calories divided among six servings, that is, about 95 per serving.

### Variations and tips

* **Vegan version:** leave out the meat, double the beans and add seared [mushrooms](/en/food/mushrooms/) for depth of flavor. The serving drops to about 330 calories and rises in fiber.
* **Frozen herbs:** work excellently and even save chopping time, but require thawing and squeezing before frying — otherwise the water brings us back to the problem the frying is meant to solve.
* **Fenugreek:** if you have none you can skip it, but the dish will lose its signature aroma. Do not replace it with a larger amount of another herb.
* **Making ahead:** the stew improves significantly on the second day and keeps 4 days in the refrigerator and 3 months in the freezer.

### Summary

Ghormeh sabzi is a dish that demands time, not skill: 40 minutes of frying herbs and two and a half hours of slow cooking, without a single step that requires precision. About 500 calories per serving of stew with 29 grams of protein and 9 grams of fiber — an especially good fiber profile for a meat stew. The other stew every Persian household knows is [fesenjan](/en/recipes/iran/fesenjan/), and the dish almost always served alongside it is [mast-o-khiar](/en/recipes/iran/mast-o-khiar/), yogurt with cucumber. ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in ghormeh sabzi?**
Answer: A serving of stew of about 320 grams, one sixth of the pot, is about 500 calories with about 29 grams of protein and 9 grams of fiber. With a serving of white rice, as it is actually served, the full plate reaches about 740 calories.

**Question: Why is it important to fry the herbs for so long?**
Answer: Fresh herbs are about 90% water, and as long as the water is inside them they stew rather than fry. Only after the water evaporates does the temperature in the pan rise above 100°C and browning reactions begin — and that is where the dark color and deep flavor of the dish come from.

**Question: What is dried lemon (limoo amani) and what can you replace it with?**
Answer: Small lemons that were boiled and dried in the sun until they are light and black inside. They add a deep acidity and a slight bitterness that you can't get from fresh lemon. A reasonable substitute is 3 tablespoons of lemon juice added at the end of cooking, but the flavor will be sharper and less rounded.

**Question: Can you make ghormeh sabzi without meat?**
Answer: Yes. Double the amount of kidney beans, add seared mushrooms, and cook for just an hour instead of three. Nutritionally, the vegan version is richer in fiber and lower in saturated fat, but it will need more generous seasoning.

**Question: The stew came out bitter — what happened?**
Answer: Almost always the dried lemons burst in the pot or stayed in it too long. Pierce them with a knife instead of cutting them, add them only in the last hour, and remove them as soon as the acidity is sufficient.
