## Sholeh Zard

**In Persian: *شله زرد*, literally "the soft yellow thing."** In Hebrew it is written "שולה זרד" and also appears as "שולה זארד" or "שלה זרד". This is the yellow dessert most closely identified with saffron: a [rice](/en/food/rice/) pudding cooked for an hour and a half in water and sugar, flavored with rose water and cardamom, and decorated with patterns of [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/) and [almonds](/en/food/almonds/).

The main difference between it and other rice desserts — like Turkish [sütlaç](/en/recipes/turkey/sutlac/) or European rice pudding made with [milk](/en/food/milk/) — is that it contains no milk at all. The base is water, and the thickness comes entirely from the starch of the rice as it breaks down completely. The result: a dessert that is vegan from the start, lighter than it looks, but also significantly sweet.

**Prep time:** 15 minutes · **Cooking time:** 90 minutes · **Chilling time:** 3 hours · **Yield:** 8 servings · **Difficulty:** easy

### A dessert meant for sharing, not for the plate

Sholeh zard has a social role most desserts don't: it is the quintessential *nazri* in Iran — food made in large quantities and handed out to neighbors, passersby and the needy in fulfillment of a vow, especially in the month of Muharram. Families cook dozens of servings in huge pots, pour them into small disposable plates, and hand them out around the neighborhood.

This way of sharing has a consequence for the recipe itself. The dessert is meant to be made ahead, to cool, to stand for hours and stay stable — so it is poured into thin, flat plates rather than cups, and decorated with cinnamon patterns on the surface. The decoration is not only ornamental: it turns the disposable dish into something that looks like a gift.

### Ingredients (for 8 servings)

* 1 cup short-grain [rice](/en/food/rice/) or broken basmati
* 1.5 liters water
* 250 grams sugar (can be reduced to 180)
* 1/4 teaspoon ground saffron, dissolved in 3 tablespoons hot water
* 3 tablespoons butter or [coconut](/en/food/coconut/) oil (for the vegan version)
* 2 tablespoons rose water
* 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom
* 50 grams sliced almonds

**For garnish:** ground [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/), chopped [pistachios](/en/food/pistachios/), sliced almonds.

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Short-grain rice sold for sushi or risotto works; there is no need for broken Persian rice. Rose water is scented water for cooking, not a concentrated extract: with an extract, follow the manufacturer's dosage. If you have no rose water, you can skip it and rely on the cardamom. Saffron is a central ingredient here; without it you get a different rice pudding, and you can leave it white rather than substituting turmeric.

### Method

1. **Soak and cook the rice.** Rinse and soak the rice for an hour. Cook in the water over low heat for about an hour, stirring occasionally, until the grains break down completely and the mixture becomes pasty. This is the long part, and it should not be cut short.
2. **Add the sugar.** Add the sugar only when the rice is completely soft. Sugar added early firms up the grain and lengthens the cooking significantly.
3. **Add the saffron and butter.** Add the saffron water and the butter, and cook for another 20 minutes until the color is an even golden yellow and the mixture thickens.
4. **Flavor at the end.** Add the rose water, cardamom and almonds and cook for a final 5 minutes. Rose water and cardamom compounds are volatile — adding them early loses most of the aroma.
5. **Pour and chill.** Pour into flat plates in a layer of about 1.5 cm, cool to room temperature and then refrigerate for at least 3 hours.
6. **Decorate** with cinnamon — traditionally in stripes or geometric patterns — and scatter pistachios and almonds.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is a plate of about 150 grams, one eighth of the recipe.

| Nutrient | Per serving (150 grams) |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **about 290** |
| Carbohydrates | about 52 g |
| of which added sugar | about 31 g |
| Fat | about 8 g |
| Protein | about 3 g |

This needs to be said directly: 31 grams of added sugar per serving is above the recommended daily limit for a woman (about 25 grams) and almost the entire limit for a man (about 36 grams). That is more than in a square of [baklava](/en/recipes/turkey/baklava/) (about 14 grams) or a cup of [sütlaç](/en/recipes/turkey/sutlac/) (about 19 grams). Reducing the sugar in the recipe to 180 grams works well, brings the serving down to about 255 calories and about 22 grams of sugar, and does not hurt the texture — the sugar here is not structural.

The difference from sütlaç reveals something else too: because there is no milk here, there is also none of the protein and [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) that offset the sugar in the dairy dessert. In fat the two desserts are similar (8 grams versus 4), but nutritionally sholeh zard is almost entirely carbohydrate. It is a dessert for an occasion, not an everyday end to a meal. For background on added sugar, see [sugar industry tactics](/en/food/sugar-industry-tactics/).

### Variations and tips

* **Fully vegan version:** replace the butter with coconut oil or a neutral oil. The rest of the recipe is vegan anyway.
* **Less sweet:** 180 grams of sugar instead of 250. This is the only adjustment that significantly changes the nutritional profile.
* **Texture:** if the dessert comes out runny, keep cooking over low heat — it thickens significantly as it cools, but it won't thicken if the rice hasn't broken down completely.
* **Rose water:** a delicate product that loses its aroma in heat. Always at the end, and never at a boil.

### Summary

Sholeh zard is a dairy-free rice dessert, vegan from the start, whose whole character rests on saffron, rose water and cardamom. About 290 calories per serving — but 31 grams of sugar, which are its real drawback and worth reducing. This Persian dessert parallels Turkish [sütlaç](/en/recipes/turkey/sutlac/) in its role, but is completely different in composition. More recipes in [Persian cuisine](/en/recipes/iran/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in sholeh zard?**
Answer: A 150-gram serving is about 290 calories, including about 31 grams of added sugar. Reducing the sugar in the recipe from 250 to 180 grams brings the serving down to about 255 calories and about 22 grams of sugar.

**Question: What is the difference between sholeh zard and rice pudding made with milk?**
Answer: Sholeh zard contains no milk at all — it is cooked in water, and its thickness comes from the rice starch alone. So it is vegan from the start, but it also lacks the protein and calcium found in Turkish [sütlaç](/en/recipes/turkey/sutlac/) or European milk rice pudding.

**Question: Why is the sugar added only at the end?**
Answer: Sugar firms up the rice grain and slows its breakdown. If it is added at the start of cooking, the rice will need much longer to reach the required pasty texture, and sometimes won't reach it at all.

**Question: Can you make sholeh zard without saffron?**
Answer: You can use [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/) for color, but it is not the same dish — saffron is the main flavor, not just the colorant. A quarter teaspoon of ground saffron dissolved in hot water is enough for eight servings, so the cost per serving is lower than it seems.

**Question: How long does the dessert keep?**
Answer: In the refrigerator, covered, up to 4 days. It is always eaten cold or at room temperature and is not meant to be reheated. If liquid forms on it, stir gently before serving.
