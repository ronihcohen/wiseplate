## Tahchin

**In Persian: *ته چین*, literally "what is arranged at the bottom."** In Hebrew it is written "תהצ'ין" and also appears as "טהצ'ין" or "תחצ'ין". The name describes the method: the yellow mixture — [rice](/en/food/rice/), [yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/), egg yolks and saffron — is layered at the bottom of the pan, baked until it forms a firm crust, and then the pan is flipped like a cake.

Tahchin is what happens when *tahdig*, the coveted rice crust that forms at the bottom of a Persian rice pot, goes from an extra to a dish in its own right. Instead of one piece of crust everyone fights over, you get a dish that is all crust. On the outside it is golden-orange and crispy, on the inside soft and moist, with a layer of shredded [chicken](/en/food/chicken-breast/) or tart barberries inside.

**Prep time:** 40 minutes · **Baking time:** 75-90 minutes · **Total:** about two hours · **Yield:** 8 servings · **Difficulty:** medium

### Saffron: the world's most expensive spice, and why a tiny amount is enough

Tahchin is one of the dishes in which saffron is not just color but the main flavor, which makes it a good opportunity to explain how to use it properly. Saffron is the dried stigmas of the saffron crocus flower (*Crocus sativus*), which blooms in autumn, and hand-picking one kilogram requires about 150,000 flowers — hence the price. Iran today produces most of the world's saffron.

The common mistake is to add saffron threads directly to the pot. Its active compounds — crocin (the color), picrocrocin (the bitterness) and safranal (the aroma) — are water-soluble, so the right way is to grind the threads to a powder with a grain of sugar or salt, dissolve in about 3 tablespoons of hot (not boiling) water, and let it stand for 20 minutes until the liquid is a deep red-orange. That way a quarter teaspoon is enough for a dish for eight diners. Compare this with [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), which also colors intensely but is hundreds of times cheaper — which is why in Iran it is used for everyday coloring, while saffron is saved for dishes for guests.

### Ingredients (for 8 servings)

* 3 cups basmati [rice](/en/food/rice/)
* 400 grams thick [yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/) (5% or more)
* 3 [egg](/en/food/eggs/) yolks
* 1/4 teaspoon ground saffron, dissolved in 3 tablespoons hot water
* 5 tablespoons oil or melted butter
* 600 grams [chicken breast](/en/food/chicken-breast/) or boneless thigh
* 1 large [onion](/en/food/onion/), 1/2 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), salt and pepper
* 3 tablespoons zereshk (barberries) — optional; chopped dried cranberries work for a sweeter taste
* 3 tablespoons salt for the cooking water

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Thick plain yogurt and supermarket basmati work. Zereshk (barberries) is sold in spice shops; you can leave it out or replace it with three tablespoons of chopped dried cranberries, for a sweeter taste. Saffron determines the aroma and color: you can bake without it, but it will be a white, milder version. There is no need to add turmeric to the yogurt mixture as a substitute.

### Method

1. **Soak and partly cook the rice.** Rinse and soak the rice for an hour in salted water, cook in boiling water for only 5-6 minutes — the grain should break under pressure but stay hard in the center — and drain well.
2. **Cook the chicken.** Cook the chicken with the onion, turmeric, salt and pepper for about 25 minutes, cool and shred into strips. Reserve a little broth.
3. **Make the tahchin mixture.** Whisk the yogurt, yolks, saffron water, salt and 2 tablespoons of oil. Add about a third of the drained rice and mix gently until every grain is yellow.
4. **Assemble.** Grease a pan (or a heavy pot) well, line it with the yellow mixture in an even layer and press lightly. Scatter the chicken and barberries on top, and cover with the rest of the white rice. Drizzle with the remaining oil and 3 tablespoons of broth.
5. **Seal and bake.** Cover tightly with aluminum foil and bake at 180°C (350°F) for 75-90 minutes. Sealing is important — the steam is what cooks the upper rice.
6. **Flip.** Take it out, let it rest for 10 minutes, run a knife along the sides and flip onto a tray. The crust should come out whole and golden-orange.
7. **Cut** with a serrated knife into slices like a cake.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is a slice of about 200 grams, one eighth of the pan.

| Nutrient | Per slice (200 grams) |

Tahchin is an example of a rice dish that becomes a complete meal: the yogurt, yolks and chicken add about 34 grams of protein, compared with only about 4 grams in a regular serving of white rice. This also changes the glycemic response — protein and fat alongside starch slow its absorption.

The weak side remains the dish's empty starch — the white rice itself, and the amount of oil needed to create the crust. You can drop a tablespoon of oil, but no more: the crispy crust is the whole idea.

### Variations and tips

* **Vegetarian version:** replace the chicken with roasted [eggplant](/en/food/eggplant/) or [spinach](/en/food/spinach/) sautéed with [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) — both are traditional versions.
* **Lighter version:** 3% yogurt and 2 yolks instead of 3, and 4 tablespoons of oil instead of 5. A saving of about 55 calories per slice.
* **Sticking crust:** a well-greased pan and 10 minutes of cooling before flipping almost always solve this. A silicone or nonstick pan makes it much easier.
* **Leftovers:** tahchin is cooked rice, and as such requires quick cooling and refrigerated storage. See [the danger in reheated rice](/en/food/reheated_rice_dangers/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in tahchin?**
Answer: A 200-gram slice is about 550 calories with about 34 grams of protein. It is a complete dish, not a side, so there is no need to add extra rice as is customary with stews.

**Question: How do you use saffron properly?**
Answer: Grind the threads to a powder together with a grain of sugar or salt, dissolve in about 3 tablespoons of hot, not boiling, water, and let it stand for about 20 minutes. The active compounds are water-soluble, so threads thrown directly into the pot give only partial color and a weak flavor.

**Question: Why didn't my tahchin come out crispy?**
Answer: Three common reasons — too little oil at the bottom of the pan, too low a temperature, or rice that was rinsed and not drained well, adding excess water. The pan should be greased generously, and the baking done at 180°C and no lower.

**Question: What is the difference between tahchin and tahdig?**
Answer: *Tahdig* is the rice crust that forms at the bottom of a regular rice pot, and it is served as part of the rice dish. Tahchin is a dish built from the start around the crust: a layer of rice mixed with yogurt, yolks and saffron lines the bottom of the pan, bakes until it sets into a crust, and the pan is flipped like a cake.

**Question: Can you make tahchin ahead?**
Answer: Yes, you can bake it a few hours ahead and reheat for 20 minutes at 160°C before serving. Do not leave it at room temperature for long — cooked rice is a convenient medium for bacterial growth, so leftovers are cooled quickly and kept in the refrigerator for up to two days.
