## Abgoosht / Dizi

**In Persian: *آبگوشت*, literally "water-meat," and by its common nickname *dizi* — after the stone pot in which it is served.** In Hebrew it is written "אבגושת" and also appears as "אבגושט" or "אבגוש". This is Iran's people's stew: [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) on the bone, [chickpeas](/en/food/chickpeas/), white beans, potatoes and dried lemon, all cooked together for long hours.

What makes it special is not the recipe but the way it is served, which is a ritual in itself: the dish is split in two. First the liquid is strained into a bowl and eaten as a soup with pieces of soaked bread (*tilit*), and only afterwards come the solids — pounded on the spot with a special pestle (*gooshtkoob*) into a thick mash eaten with bread, raw onion and herbs. One meal, two textures, two courses.

**Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Cooking time:** 3-4 hours · **Total:** about 4 hours · **Yield:** 4 servings · **Difficulty:** easy (but long)

### The stew that invented the Iranian eatery

Abgoosht is the basis of an entire culinary institution in Iran — the *dizi sara*, an eatery whose whole menu is a single dish. The pots stand for hours over low heat, and each diner gets a personal stone pot with a pestle. This format has survived for hundreds of years precisely because of its economics: a small amount of meat on the bone, which gives the liquid flavor and gelatin, is rounded out with legumes and potatoes that fill out the quantity.

It is the same principle that explains Turkish [manti](/en/recipes/turkey/manti/) and [lahmacun](/en/recipes/turkey/lahmacun/): traditional cuisines were not built around a large portion of meat, but around methods for turning a little meat into a filling meal. The difference between 100 grams of meat per serving and 250 grams is not only economic — it is also the difference between a diet in which red meat appears in moderation and one in which it is the center, a difference that studies on red meat intake consider meaningful. See [red meat](/en/food/red-meat/).

### Ingredients (for 4 servings)

* 600 grams [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) on the bone (neck, shank or ribs)
* 1/2 cup dried [chickpeas](/en/food/chickpeas/), soaked overnight
* 1/2 cup dried white beans, soaked overnight
* 2 medium potatoes, halved
* 1 large [onion](/en/food/onion/), quartered
* 2 [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) or 1 tablespoon tomato paste
* 3 Persian dried lemons (loomi), pierced — or 2–3 tablespoons lemon juice at the end of cooking
* 1 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), salt and black pepper
* 1.5 liters water

**To serve:** flatbread, red onion, fresh herbs (*sabzi khordan*), pickles.

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Persian dried lemon is sold in spice shops, also under the name loomi. If you have none, replace the three lemons with 2–3 tablespoons of lemon juice added gradually at the end of cooking; the sourness is similar but the aroma is different. For serving you can use pita or laffa, alongside parsley, mint and scallions.

### Method

1. **Start with cold water.** Place the meat, onion, chickpeas and beans in a pot with cold water and bring slowly to a boil. Starting with cold water draws more flavor and gelatin from the bone.
2. **Skim the foam.** Skim off the foam that rises at the start of boiling with a spoon — it contains proteins that coagulate and cloud the soup.
3. **Cook slowly.** Add the turmeric, lower the heat to the minimum and cook covered for 2.5-3 hours, until the meat falls apart and the legumes are completely soft.
4. **Add the potatoes, tomatoes and lemons.** About an hour before the end of cooking, that is, during the previous step and not after it, add the potatoes, tomatoes and dried lemons, if using, to the pot. With the lemon juice substitute, add it only at the end of cooking and taste. A potato that cooks for four hours falls apart completely, and the dried lemons can turn bitter.
5. **Separate.** Strain the liquid into a separate bowl. This is the first course: crumble dry flatbread into it and eat it as soup.
6. **Pound.** Remove the bones, and pound all the solids with a pestle or a potato masher into a thick, even mash. This is the second course, eaten with bread, raw onion and herbs.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is a quarter of the pot — about 350 ml of soup and about 250 grams of pounded mash, without the bread.

| Nutrient | Per full serving (both courses) |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **about 630** |
| Protein | about 38 g |
| Fat | about 32 g |
| Carbohydrates | about 48 g |
| of which fiber | about 11 g |
| Iron | about 5 mg |

The bread is not included in this figure, and that is not a minor note: in practice 2-3 pieces of flatbread are eaten with the dish, adding 200-300 calories and bringing the meal to about 850-930 calories.

In terms of composition, this is a far more balanced dish than "lamb stew" sounds: 11 grams of fiber from the chickpeas and beans, 38 grams of protein from two sources, and a high amount of [iron](/en/food/iron/). The weak side is the saturated fat from the lamb — the traditional way to deal with it is to remove the layer of fat that solidifies on the surface of the soup after chilling, a technique that cuts 80-120 calories per serving without hurting the flavor.

### Variations and tips

* **Removing fat:** cook a day ahead, chill, remove the solidified fat, and reheat. The flavor improves too.
* **In a pressure cooker:** 45 minutes instead of three hours. The flavor is less deep, but the result is good.
* **Lower-meat version:** 350 grams of meat instead of 600, with an extra cup of legumes. The serving drops to about 510 calories and gains more fiber.
* **The lemons:** pierce them rather than cut them, and remove them as soon as the sourness is sufficient — otherwise they turn the whole pot bitter.

### Summary

Abgoosht is a stew of patience, not skill: cold water, low heat, three hours, and then a split into two courses that makes it a complete meal. About 630 calories per full serving with 38 grams of protein and 11 grams of fiber, without the bread. The Iraqi dish built on exactly the same idea — a stew poured over torn bread — is [tashreeb](/en/recipes/iraq/tashreeb/); another Persian stew with a completely different profile is [fesenjan](/en/recipes/iran/fesenjan/); and for a light dish that balances a meal like this — [yogurt and cucumber salad](/en/recipes/iran/mast-o-khiar/). ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in abgoosht?**
Answer: A full serving of both courses, a quarter of the pot, is about 630 calories. The bread eaten with it adds another 200-300 calories, so the meal in practice reaches about 850-930.

**Question: Why separate the soup from the solids?**
Answer: It is the traditional way of serving, and it turns one dish into two courses with different textures: soup with crumbled bread first, followed by a thick mash of meat and legumes. Beyond the experience, the separation also makes it easy to remove the layer of fat from the top of the soup.

**Question: What is dizi, and is it the same thing?**
Answer: Yes. *Dizi* is the stone or clay pot in which the dish is cooked and served, and the name stuck to the dish itself. In the Iranian eateries devoted to it (*dizi sara*), each diner gets a personal pot with a pestle for pounding.

**Question: Can you make abgoosht without meat on the bone?**
Answer: You can, but the bone is what gives the soup its gelatin and body. Boneless meat will give a thinner soup, so it is worth adding at least one bone to the pot even if the meat itself is boneless.

**Question: How do you reduce the fat in the dish?**
Answer: Cook a day ahead and chill in the refrigerator — the layer of fat solidifies on the surface of the soup and can be removed with a spoon. This cuts 80-120 calories per serving without hurting the flavor, and even deepens it — a stew like this improves overnight in the refrigerator.
