## Fesenjan

**In Persian: *فسنجان*, and in full *khoresh-e fesenjan* — "fesenjan stew."** In Hebrew it is written "פסנג'אן" and also appears as "פסנג'ון" (fesenjoon). This is the festive stew of Persian cuisine, the dish served at weddings, on *Yalda* night (the long winter night) and at occasions when you want to honor your guests.

Fesenjan is built on just two ingredients that determine its whole character: finely ground [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/) and [pomegranate](/en/food/pomegranate/) molasses. The walnuts cook for hours until their oil separates, thickening the sauce and giving it a dark brown, almost chocolate color, and the molasses adds a sweet-tart note that keeps the dish from being heavy. In Gilan province in northern Iran, where the dish comes from, it is made with duck; in most of Iran it is made with chicken.

**Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Cooking time:** 2-3 hours · **Total:** about 3 hours · **Yield:** 6 servings · **Difficulty:** medium

### The stew built entirely on walnuts

Almost every culture knows nuts as an addition — scattered on a salad, ground into a dessert, mixed into a dough. Fesenjan is one of the few dishes in the world in which nuts are the main ingredient by quantity and mass: about 400 grams of [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/) in one pot, that is, about 67 grams per serving.

This has two opposing consequences. On one hand, the dish provides an unusual amount of alpha-linolenic acid — plant [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) — and of [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) from both the walnuts and the pomegranate. On the other hand, those same walnuts are 650 calories per 100 grams, so fesenjan is the most calorie-dense stew in Persian cuisine. Both things are true at the same time, and that is exactly why the dish is kept for special occasions rather than the everyday table.

### Ingredients (for 6 servings)

* 400 grams [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/), ground very finely
* 1 kg boneless chicken thighs or drumsticks (or duck in the northern version)
* 1 large [onion](/en/food/onion/), finely chopped
* 150–250 ml good-quality [pomegranate](/en/food/pomegranate/) molasses, with no added sugar
* 1–3 tablespoons sugar as needed, or [honey](/en/food/honey/) — depending on the acidity of the molasses
* 1/2 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), a pinch of dissolved saffron, salt and pepper
* 2 tablespoons oil
* 800 ml hot water
* 1–2 tablespoons lemon juice — only if needed to balance a sweet molasses

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Pomegranate molasses is found on the sauce shelf and in spice shops. Choose a molasses meant for cooking, not pomegranate juice for drinking; the degree of sweetness and sourness varies between products. Start with 150 ml and add to taste up to the amount in the recipe. If the molasses is sweetened, skip the sugar at first. Chicken thighs or drumsticks work and there is no need to look for duck; the saffron can be left out.

### Method

1. **Grind the walnuts finely.** Grind in a food processor to a sandy, oily texture, almost like marzipan. A coarse grain will stay coarse even after three hours of cooking.
2. **Toast gently.** Fry the ground walnuts in a dry pot over low heat for 5-7 minutes, stirring constantly, until a toasted aroma rises. This step deepens the flavor — but a burnt walnut turns bitter and there is no going back.
3. **Cook the walnuts in water.** Add the hot water and cook over a very low flame for 1.5-2 hours, stirring occasionally. The sauce gradually darkens and the walnut oil separates and rises to the surface — that is the sign the stew is at the right stage.
4. **Fry the chicken and onion separately.** Meanwhile, fry the onion until golden, add the turmeric and the chicken pieces and sear on all sides.
5. **Combine and balance.** Add the chicken to the walnut pot, first pour in 150 ml of pomegranate molasses and the saffron water, if using, and cook together for 45-60 minutes. Taste and balance: a very sour molasses needs sugar; for a sweet molasses you can gradually add 1–2 tablespoons of lemon juice. Good fesenjan sits exactly in the middle.
6. **Serve** with steamed white [rice](/en/food/rice/), and scatter fresh [pomegranate](/en/food/pomegranate/) seeds on top.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is one sixth of the pot — about 350 grams of stew — alongside rice.

| Nutrient | Serving of stew (350 grams) |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **about 900** |
| Fat | about 70 g (mostly polyunsaturated) |
| Protein | about 40 g |
| Carbohydrates | about 42 g |
| of which sugar | about 27 g |

With a serving of [rice](/en/food/rice/), the plate reaches about 1,140 calories — the heaviest dish across the three cuisine categories on the site, and by a wide margin.

It is important to distinguish between calorie density and nutritional quality, because here they part ways. The 70 grams of fat in the dish are almost all unsaturated fat from walnuts, one of the richest plant sources of ALA [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/), together with [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) and [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/). This is fat that studies link to an improved blood lipid profile — and still, 900 calories per serving are 900 calories. The Persian way of dealing with this is not to shrink the serving but to serve it rarely.

### Variations and tips

* **Lighter version:** cut the walnuts to 250 grams and cook longer to get the thickness. The serving drops to about 730 calories and loses a little of the velvety texture.
* **Vegan version:** replace the chicken with cubes of [pumpkin](/en/food/pumpkin/) or seared [eggplant](/en/food/eggplant/). The pumpkin version is common in Gilan and highlights the sweet side of the stew.
* **Pomegranate molasses:** check the ingredients — cheap molasses contains sugar or corn syrup, and then the dish comes out too sweet and can't be fixed.
* **Avoiding separation:** if the oil separates too much, add boiling water and stir hard. Some separation is desirable; a puddle of oil is not.

### Summary

Fesenjan is a stew built entirely around walnuts, and that is both its advantage and its price: an excellent fat profile, plant omega-3 and polyphenols on one side, and about 900 calories per serving on the other. A dish for holidays, not weekdays. A more everyday Persian stew is [ghormeh sabzi](/en/recipes/iran/ghormeh-sabzi/), and for the nutritional values of the main ingredient see [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/). ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in fesenjan?**
Answer: One sixth of the pot, about 350 grams of stew, is about 900 calories — including about 70 grams of fat that comes almost entirely from the walnuts (about 67 grams of walnuts per serving). With a serving of rice, the plate reaches about 1,140 calories, making it the heaviest dish on the site.

**Question: Why didn't my fesenjan darken?**
Answer: Cooking that was too short. The dark brown color develops over an hour and a half to two hours of slow cooking, as the walnuts release their oil and undergo browning. Toasting the walnuts in a dry pan before adding the water speeds up the process.

**Question: The stew came out too sour or too sweet — how do you fix it?**
Answer: This is the central balance of the dish, and it depends entirely on the pomegranate molasses you used. A sour molasses is corrected with a tablespoon of sugar or [honey](/en/food/honey/) at a time until the flavor balances; a sweet molasses is corrected with a tablespoon of lemon juice. Adjust gradually and taste in between.

**Question: Can you make vegan fesenjan?**
Answer: Yes, and it is a traditional version, not a modern invention: cubes of pumpkin or seared eggplant instead of the chicken. The base — walnuts, pomegranate molasses and onion — is vegan to begin with, and the stew gets its full thickness from the walnuts, not the meat.

**Question: Can you use other nuts?**
Answer: Not really. Walnuts are the only ones that release oil with the right texture and provide the slightly bitter flavor that balances the molasses. Pecans or almonds will give a sweet, oily stew that is not fesenjan. See the differences between the nuts in [the Brazil nut, walnut and almond comparison](/en/compare/brazil-walnut-almond/).
