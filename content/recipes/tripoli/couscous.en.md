## Tripolitan Couscous

Libyan couscous is served with a red, spiced soup, usually with lamb or beef, [chickpeas](/en/food/chickpeas/), potatoes, pumpkin and zucchini. Unlike the more delicate and aromatic Moroccan couscous, here the tomato paste and paprika are the center of the flavor.

**Prep time:** 30 minutes · **Cook time:** about two hours · **Total:** two and a half hours · **Yield:** 8 servings · **Difficulty:** medium

### Separate grains without a couscoussier

Israeli instant couscous is not a fake but semolina that has already been steamed and dried. Prepare it by weight: an amount of water close to the amount of couscous, a covered rest and then fluffing with a fork and a little oil. Steaming it again over the soup improves the texture, but it isn't essential.

### Ingredients (for 8 servings)

* 800 grams [lamb](/en/food/lamb/) or beef shoulder, cubed
* 3 tablespoons [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)
* 2 [onions](/en/food/onion/), chopped
* 100 grams [tomato](/en/food/tomato/) paste
* 1 tablespoon sweet paprika, 1/2 teaspoon hot paprika, 1 teaspoon ground caraway, 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
* 250 grams cooked chickpeas, drained
* 4 medium potatoes, halved
* 500 grams [pumpkin](/en/food/pumpkin/) in large pieces
* 3 thick [zucchini](/en/food/zucchini/), halved
* 3 carrots, coarsely cut
* 2–2.5 liters hot water, salt and pepper
* 500 grams medium quick-cooking couscous
* 600 ml boiling water, 1 tablespoon oil and 1 teaspoon salt for the couscous

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Lamb shoulder gives the traditional taste; beef cut no. 5 or no. 8 are more available and sometimes cheaper. Whole-wheat couscous works in the same amount but requires following the water ratio on the package. Drained canned chickpeas save soaking and go in only in the last hour.

### Method

1. **Brown the meat.** Heat oil in a large pot and sear the meat cubes in two batches. Transfer to a plate.
2. **Fry the base.** Fry the onion until golden, add the paste and spices and stir for 2 minutes until the paste is dark and fragrant.
3. **Cook the meat.** Return the meat, add 2 liters of water and salt, partially cover and cook for 60–75 minutes.
4. **Add the vegetables by firmness.** Put in the potatoes and carrots for 30 minutes; then the chickpeas, pumpkin and zucchini for another 25–30 minutes. Add water only if needed to keep a generous soup.
5. **Make the couscous.** Mix the couscous, salt and a tablespoon of oil, pour in boiling water, cover for 10 minutes and fluff well with a fork. If it is dry, sprinkle on a little more soup and fluff again.
6. **Serve in the right structure.** Put down about 200 grams of prepared couscous, top with meat and vegetables, and ladle the soup on gradually so the grains don't turn into porridge.

### Variations and tips

* For a vegetarian version, double the chickpeas and add cabbage; the serving is about 460 calories.
* Pumpkin and zucchini go in last so they don't fall apart into the soup.
* The soup keeps for 4 days and freezes well. Store the couscous separately.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in Tripolitan couscous?**
Answer: A serving of about 200 grams of prepared couscous with lamb, vegetables and soup is about 690 calories. With lean beef it is about 630; the number doesn't include mafrum on the side.

**Question: Do you need a couscous pot?**
Answer: No. Quick-cooking couscous gives a good result if you measure the water, let it rest and fluff it well with a fork.

**Question: Why did the couscous come out sticky?**
Answer: Too much water or stirring with a spoon. Add water by weight, wait covered and separate the grains with a fork without mashing.

**Question: When do you add the pumpkin?**
Answer: Only in the last half hour. Large pieces keep their shape and absorb the sauce without disappearing into it.
