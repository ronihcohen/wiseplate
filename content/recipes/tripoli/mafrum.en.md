## Mafrum

Mafrum is the dish most identified with Libyan Jews: a slice of [potato](/en/food/baked-potato/) opened almost all the way through, stuffed with meat, coated in flour and egg, fried and then cooked in tomato sauce. The frying is not the end of the preparation but a way of setting the coating so it doesn't open during cooking.

**Prep time:** 50 minutes · **Cook time:** 90 minutes · **Total:** about 2 hours and 20 minutes · **Yield:** 16 pieces, 8 servings · **Difficulty:** medium

### The cut that decides whether the mafrum stays closed

Slice a potato crosswise into a slice about 1.5 cm thick, then cut it through the middle almost to the edge, leaving a "hinge" of 4–5 mm. A cold, firm potato is easier to cut. If the pocket is too thin it tears; if it is too thick the meat is done before the potato softens.

### Ingredients (for 16 pieces)

* 1.5 kg medium potatoes, peeled
* 600 grams ground [beef](/en/food/red-meat/), 15% fat
* 1 large [onion](/en/food/onion/), grated and squeezed
* 80 grams breadcrumbs or 2 slices of bread soaked and squeezed
* A handful of chopped parsley, 1 teaspoon baharat, 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon, salt and pepper
* 2 [eggs](/en/food/eggs/)
* 80 grams flour for coating
* Neutral oil for frying; the nutrition calculation counts about 100 grams absorbed
* 2 tablespoons oil for the sauce
* 1 medium onion, sliced
* 200 grams [tomato](/en/food/tomato/) paste
* 1 teaspoon sweet paprika, 1/2 teaspoon hot paprika, 1/2 teaspoon ground caraway
* 750 ml hot water, salt and the juice of half a [lemon](/en/food/lemon/)

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Firm white or red potatoes work; avoid very small potatoes. Regular Israeli baharat works. If you want less frying, you can spray with oil and bake the coated pieces for 25 minutes at 210°C (410°F), but the coating will be less even.

### Method

1. **Cut the pockets.** Make 16 "sandwiches" of even thickness and soak them for 15 minutes in salted water. Drain and dry well.
2. **Make the filling.** Knead the meat, squeezed onion, breadcrumbs, parsley and spices only until uniform. Divide into 16 balls.
3. **Stuff.** Gently open each pocket, flatten a ball of meat inside it and press the edges. The meat should reach the rim but not stick out of it.
4. **Coat and fry.** Dip in flour and then in beaten egg. Fry in oil 2 cm deep, 2–3 minutes on each side, just until golden. Transfer to a rack; there is no need to cook the meat at this stage.
5. **Build the sauce.** Fry the onion in two tablespoons of oil, add the paste and spices and fry for 2 minutes. Add the water, salt and lemon and bring to a boil.
6. **Arrange snugly.** Lay the mafrums in one layer or two snug layers, with the opening facing up. The liquid should reach about two thirds of their height.
7. **Cook slowly.** Cover and cook for 60–75 minutes over low heat, until a knife goes into the potato without resistance. Let rest for 15 minutes before serving.

### Variations and tips

* You can also stuff [eggplant](/en/food/eggplant/) or cauliflower; the cooking time changes.
* If the pieces open, the pocket was cut all the way through or the sauce boiled hard. Cook at a gentle simmer.
* Mafrum keeps for 4 days in the fridge and freezes well for up to two months in its sauce.

### Summary

Good mafrum relies on geometry more than on a family secret: a pocket of even thickness, a filling that doesn't stick out, brief frying and gentle cooking. Two pieces with sauce are about 580 calories. ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in mafrum?**
Answer: Two medium pieces with sauce are about 580 calories. Baked instead of fried they are about 470, depending on the amount of oil.

**Question: Why did the mafrum fall apart?**
Answer: The pocket was cut through to the edge, the filling stuck out, or the sauce boiled hard. Leave a hinge, press firmly, fry to set and cook at a low simmer.

**Question: Can you make it a day ahead?**
Answer: Yes. The taste even improves. Chill it in the sauce and reheat covered over low heat or in the oven.

**Question: Do you have to fry it?**
Answer: No. You can bake the coated pieces at 210°C for about 25 minutes, turning halfway, and then cook them in the sauce as usual.
