## Misir Wat

Misir wat is a thick, spicy red [lentil](/en/food/lentil/) stew seasoned with [berbere](/en/recipes/ethiopia/awaze/), and one of the most recognizable components of a mixed Ethiopian platter. It is common as a vegan fasting dish among Ethiopian Christians, but that does not make it a "Jewish dish"; in Israel it is simply an accessible pareve dish that goes well with injera.

**Prep time:** 15 minutes · **Cooking time:** 55 minutes · **Yield:** 6 servings · **Difficulty:** easy

### Rinse the lentils, don't soak them

Hulled red lentils cook quickly and don't need soaking. Rinse until the water is almost clear to reduce foam; add salt toward the end and keep a gentle simmer so they don't stick.

### Ingredients (for 6 servings)

* 500 grams red lentils, rinsed and drained
* 500 grams finely chopped onion
* 75 ml olive or canola oil
* 5 garlic cloves and 30 grams ginger, chopped
* 2 tablespoons berbere, plus more to taste
* 100 grams [tomato](/en/food/tomato/) paste
* 1/2 teaspoon ground fenugreek and 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
* 1.5 liters hot water, salt

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

The orange lentils sold in Israel are hulled red lentils and are exactly right. Ready-made berbere may contain salt, so salt at the end. For a mild version, use one tablespoon of berbere and add a teaspoon of sweet paprika.

### Method

1. **Soften the onion.** Cook the onion in a heavy pot without oil for 15–20 minutes, stirring, until soft and almost dry.
2. **Add the fat and spices.** Add the oil, garlic and ginger for 2 minutes; add the berbere, tomato paste, fenugreek and turmeric and fry for 3 minutes.
3. **Cook the lentils.** Add the lentils and 1.2 liters of water. Bring to a boil, lower the heat and cook uncovered for 25–35 minutes. Stir from the bottom and add water as needed.
4. **Finish.** When the lentils are soft and the sauce thick, salt and cook for another 5 minutes. The wat should hold on a spoon, not be a runny soup.

### Variations and tips

* If the bottom of the pot starts to catch, add boiling water and lower the heat; don't scrape a burnt layer into the stew.
* The stew thickens when chilled. When reheating, add 2–3 tablespoons of water per serving.
* Keeps for 4 days in the fridge and freezes for up to 3 months.

### Summary

Good misir wat is thick and partly broken down but not mashed, with heat built on sweet onion. A serving is about 450 calories without injera. For a colorful platter, add [gomen](/en/recipes/ethiopia/gomen/) and [atkilt](/en/recipes/ethiopia/atkilt-wat/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in misir wat?**
Answer: A serving out of six is about 450 calories without injera; a medium injera adds about 200.

**Question: Do red lentils need soaking?**
Answer: No. Rinse well and cook directly; soaking can make them too soft.

**Question: Does misir wat have to be spicy?**
Answer: Berbere is the signature of the dish, but you can cut it by half and make up the difference with sweet paprika.

**Question: Why did the stew set firm in the fridge?**
Answer: The starch keeps absorbing liquid. Add a little water when reheating and stir until it returns to its original texture.
