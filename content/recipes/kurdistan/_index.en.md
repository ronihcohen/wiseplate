Kurdish cuisine doesn't belong to a single country: historical Kurdistan stretches across parts of Turkey, Iraq, Iran and Syria. The Jews of Zakho, Amadiya, Erbil and Sulaymaniyah brought to Israel traditions that are close but not identical. So a dish can be Kurdish, Iraqi and Jerusalemite all at once; this page notes the overlaps rather than ruling on ownership.

In Israel, kubbeh became the most prominent symbol. The five versions here are not the same dumpling in a different color: [hamusta](/en/recipes/kurdistan/kubbeh-hamusta/) is semolina kubbeh in a sour green soup; [selek](/en/recipes/kurdistan/kubbeh-selek/) is in beet soup; [bamia](/en/recipes/kurdistan/kubbeh-bamia/) is in tomato sauce; [fried bulgur kubbeh](/en/recipes/kurdistan/fried-bulgur-kubbeh/) gets a different shell and is fried; and [kotla](/en/recipes/kurdistan/kubbeh-kotla/) is meant for overnight Shabbat cooking.

### What's important to know

* **Kubbeh shells are sensitive to water.** Give the semolina or bulgur time to absorb, and only then adjust. A dry dough cracks; a soft one melts.
* **Siska is cooked, shredded meat.** Many families use it for filling; for accessibility, the recipes also give a route with ground beef and mark it as an Israeli adaptation.
* **Sourness isn't just citric acid.** Fresh lemon gives a fuller aroma and flavor. A little citric acid ("lemon salt") can stabilize the sourness in a large pot, but it isn't required.
* **Kurdish yaprakh is sometimes folded and wide.** This is a familiar family distinction, not an absolute rule across all of Kurdistan.

### Kashrut and shopping in Israel

All the basic ingredients are available in the supermarket: semolina, bulgur, chard, celery, beets, frozen okra and jarred grape leaves. Rinse preserved grape leaves to reduce the salt. The other nine dishes are meat or pareve; [cheese kada](/en/recipes/kurdistan/cheese-kadeh/) is dairy and is prepared and served with separate dishes. Ground meat should reach 71 degrees Celsius and chicken 74 degrees.

### About the calories

The values were calculated from raw weights and divided by the number of servings. For fried kubbeh, absorbed oil is counted; for soups, all the oil in the pot is counted. The actual amount of meat inside each kubbeh and the fat percentage can shift the result by 10%–20%.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Is kubbeh Kurdish or Iraqi?**
Answer: Kubbeh is a broad regional family of dishes. Jews from Kurdistan and from Iraq preserved different versions, and in Israel the traditions met.

**Question: What is the difference between hamusta and selek?**
Answer: The shell and filling can be similar, but hamusta is green and tangy from chard and celery; selek is red, sweet and sour.

**Question: Can you freeze kubbeh?**
Answer: Yes. Freeze uncooked in a single layer, transfer to a bag, and add frozen to gently boiling soup.

**Question: Which dish is dairy?**
Answer: Only cheese and chard kada. Meat kada and all the kubbeh soups are meat dishes according to the recipes here.
