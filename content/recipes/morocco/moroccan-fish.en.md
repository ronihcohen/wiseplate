## Moroccan Fish (*الحوت بالشرمولة*)

**In Moroccan Arabic the dish is named after its seasoning: *chermoula*, a blend of cilantro, [garlic](/en/food/garlic/), cumin, paprika and lemon.** In Hebrew the name that stuck is "דג מרוקאי" (Moroccan fish), and sometimes "דג בחריף" (spicy fish) or "דג ברוטב אדום" (fish in red sauce).

This is a [white fish](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) fillet that cooks in a shallow pot in a sauce of [red peppers](/en/food/red-pepper/), [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/), garlic and paprika, with no frying first. The fish is neither grilled nor fried but gently cooked inside the sauce, which is why it stays moist. In Israel it is one of the standard starters on the Shabbat table of Moroccan-born families, and it is usually eaten cold or lukewarm the next day — not hot from the pot.

**Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Cook time:** 30 minutes · **Total:** about 50 minutes · **Yield:** 4 servings · **Difficulty:** easy

### Moroccan fish, chraime and chermoula: three dishes that get confused

All three names show up in Israeli cooking almost interchangeably, but they actually describe three different things.

**Chermoula** is not a dish but a seasoning blend — fresh cilantro, garlic, cumin, paprika, olive oil and lemon — used in Morocco as a marinade for fish, chicken and vegetables. It is the base of this recipe, but it is also the base of dozens of other dishes.

**Chraime** is a Tripolitan-Libyan dish that came to Israel with Libyan Jews, and its name comes from the Arabic word for "hot." It is based on a hot pepper sauce and a little tomato, is much spicier than the Moroccan version, and the fish in it is usually lightly fried before cooking in the sauce.

**Moroccan fish**, in the sense that took hold in Israel, is the Moroccan-Jewish version: a sauce based on **sweet** red peppers, tomato, a large amount of fresh cilantro, and moderate heat that comes from one pepper rather than six. It is eaten cold or lukewarm, unlike chraime, which is usually served hot. In practice the three families have blended in Israel, so "Moroccan fish" in many homes is something in between.

### Ingredients (for 4 servings)

* 800 grams white fish fillet in thick slices — tilapia, sea bream, grouper, cod or sea bass
* 3 large [red peppers](/en/food/red-pepper/) (about 350 grams), in strips
* 500 grams grated [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) (or 500 grams canned crushed tomatoes)
* 1 whole head of [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) (about 10 cloves), sliced
* 60 ml [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)
* 1 tablespoon sweet paprika
* 1 teaspoon ground cumin
* 1 hot pepper, whole or sliced
* A large bunch of fresh cilantro
* Juice of half a [lemon](/en/food/lemon/)
* Salt and black pepper
* 100 ml water

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Supermarket tilapia fillet works fine in the sauce too. Thaw frozen fish in the fridge, drain and pat dry; 800 grams is the weight of the fish after thawing. With canned tomatoes use 500 grams, which isn't necessarily one can. The fish's cooking time depends on its thickness and type, so check that the flesh is opaque and flakes easily.

### Method

1. **Season the fish ahead.** Sprinkle the fish slices with salt, half of the cumin and a little lemon, and refrigerate for 20 minutes while you make the sauce. Seasoning early gets flavor into the flesh, not just around it.
2. **Start the garlic in cold oil.** Put the garlic slices in a wide pot **with the cold oil** and raise the heat gradually. Garlic that goes into hot oil browns and turns bitter in seconds; garlic that heats up together with the oil releases its aroma without browning.
3. **Add the peppers.** Add the red pepper strips and fry for 8-10 minutes over medium heat until they soften and sweeten.
4. **Build the sauce.** Add the grated tomatoes, paprika, the rest of the cumin, the hot pepper, salt, black pepper and water, and cook for 12-15 minutes until the sauce thickens and turns a deep red.
5. **Add the fish.** Lay the fish slices **on** the sauce, don't stir, and cover. Cook over low heat for about 10–12 minutes, depending on the fish's thickness, until the flesh is opaque and flakes easily. **This is the step that determines the result:** overcooked fish falls apart into dry fibers, and there is no bringing it back.
6. **Finish with cilantro.** Scatter half the chopped cilantro over the top, turn off the heat, and cover for another 5 minutes. Add the rest of the cilantro and the lemon just before serving.
7. **Serve** at room temperature or cold, with bread. The flavor settles after a few hours in the fridge, which is why the dish is made a day ahead.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is about 200 grams of fish with sauce — a quarter of the pot.

| Component | Per serving |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **about 390** |
| Protein | about 42 grams |
| Fat | about 19 grams |
| Carbohydrates | about 14 grams |
| Of which fiber | about 3 grams |

This is the dish with the best protein-to-calorie ratio on this page: 42 grams of protein in 390 calories, with almost no carbohydrates. The fish itself contributes only about 210 calories per serving; the olive oil contributes about 135, and the vegetables the rest.

There is also a gain that doesn't show up in the table. The sauce is [tomatoes](/en/food/tomato/) and [red peppers](/en/food/red-pepper/) cooked in olive oil — exactly the conditions that raise the bioavailability of lycopene and [beta-carotene](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-a/), both fat-soluble carotenoids. Red pepper also provides a very large amount of [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/), although some of it is destroyed during the long cooking.

As for [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/): white fish like sea bream and cod provide only a modest amount compared with [salmon](/en/food/salmon/), [mackerel](/en/food/mackerel/) or [sardines](/en/food/sardines/). Anyone who wants this dish mainly for omega-3 should make it with salmon fillet, at a cost of about 120 extra calories per serving.

### Variations and tips

* **Less oil:** 40 ml instead of 60 cuts about 45 calories per serving. The sauce comes out less glossy; the flavor is almost identical.
* **With [chickpeas](/en/food/chickpeas/):** Add a drained can to the sauce 10 minutes before the fish. Adds about 100 calories and 4 grams of fiber per serving, and turns it into a main course.
* **Salmon instead of white fish:** About 510 calories per serving, but several times the [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/). The cooking time shortens by about 2 minutes.
* **Making ahead:** The dish improves on the second day and keeps in the fridge for 3 days. **Don't freeze it** — the fish loses its texture completely when thawed.
* **Too salty:** Add a cut-up potato and cook for 15 minutes before adding the fish.

### Summary

Moroccan fish is a recipe of twenty minutes' work and two decisions: don't brown the garlic, and don't overcook the fish. The result is a dish with 42 grams of protein in 390 calories, almost no carbohydrates, and a sauce that improves the availability of its carotenoids. It is served on the Shabbat table alongside [matbucha](/en/recipes/morocco/matbucha/) and [zaalouk](/en/recipes/morocco/zaalouk/), and is usually made a day ahead. A parallel fish dish from a neighboring cuisine: Iraqi [masgouf](/en/recipes/iraq/masgouf/). More recipes in [Moroccan cuisine](/en/recipes/morocco/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in Moroccan fish?**
Answer: A serving of about 200 grams of fish with sauce is about 390 calories, with about 42 grams of protein. The fish itself contributes about 210 of them and the olive oil about 135. In the salmon version the serving rises to about 510 calories, and with chickpeas added to about 490.

**Question: What is the difference between Moroccan fish and chraime?**
Answer: Chraime is a Tripolitan-Libyan dish built on a **hot** pepper sauce and a little tomato, and the fish in it is usually lightly fried first and served hot. Moroccan fish is built on sweet red peppers, tomato and a large amount of cilantro, its heat is moderate, and it is eaten cold or lukewarm.

**Question: Which fish is best for this recipe?**
Answer: A white fish with firm flesh that doesn't fall apart during cooking — sea bream, grouper, sea bass or cod. Thick slices are better than thin fillets, which cook too fast. Oily fish like [salmon](/en/food/salmon/) work too, give more [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/), and need about 2 minutes less.

**Question: Why did the fish come out dry and crumbly?**
Answer: Almost always overcooking. A white fish fillet 2-3 cm thick needs 10-12 minutes **at most** in gently bubbling sauce, and no more. The sign it's done is that the flesh flakes easily at the tip of a fork but is still opaque in the center. The fish keeps cooking even after the heat is turned off.

**Question: Can you make the dish ahead?**
Answer: Not only can you — that's how it's eaten. Moroccan fish improves after a night in the fridge, is served cold or at room temperature, and keeps for 3 days. Freezing is not recommended: the fish releases water when thawed and loses its texture.
