## Pastilla (*بسطيلة*)

**In Moroccan Arabic: *بسطيلة* (bastilla), from the Spanish word *pastilla*.** In Hebrew it appears as "בסטילה," "פסטייה," "בסטיה" or "פסטילה." The name itself gives away the dish's origin — it came to Morocco with the exiles from al-Andalus, and that also explains its strangest feature.

Pastilla is a round layered pastry filled with chicken (originally pigeon) cooked in saffron, beaten eggs set in the sauce, and fried, ground [almonds](/en/food/almonds/) — and it is served dusted with powdered sugar and cinnamon. In other words: a savory main course covered in sugar. In Morocco it is the dish of celebrations — weddings, honored guests and holiday meals.

**Prep time:** 60 minutes · **Cooking and baking time:** 90 minutes · **Total:** about two and a half hours · **Yield:** 8 servings · **Difficulty:** high

### Sweet on savory: a 500-year-old Andalusian relic

Combining meat, sugar and cinnamon on the same plate sounds odd today, but until the early modern era it was completely normal in the cuisines of Europe and the Middle East. Medieval cooking had no sharp divide between "savory" and "sweet"; sugar was an expensive spice, like saffron, and those who could afford it sprinkled it on meat dishes precisely to show that they could.

In Europe the separation between main course and dessert took shape in the 17th-18th centuries and the habit disappeared; in Morocco it survived. Pastilla, which arrived with the exiles from Spain after 1492, has stayed almost as it was — and so have *tfaya* (onion and raisins with cinnamon on [couscous](/en/recipes/morocco/couscous/)) and tagines with prunes and dates. A similar relic in Persian cuisine is [fesenjan](/en/recipes/iran/fesenjan/).

The second detail that gives away its origin is the wrapper. Originally it is *warqa*, "leaf" — a liquid dough dabbed by hand onto an upturned hot pan to form paper-thin, translucent sheets. Making warqa by hand is a profession, which is why almost every pastilla outside Morocco — and many inside it — is made today with phyllo sheets. The difference is real but not critical: warqa is more pliable and less brittle, while phyllo is drier and needs a more generous hand with the brush.

### Ingredients (for 8 servings)

**For the chicken filling:**
* 1.1 kg **bone-in** chicken pieces (for example thighs and drumsticks totaling this weight; see [chicken breast](/en/food/chicken-breast/))
* 3 large [onions](/en/food/onion/) (about 400 grams), finely chopped
* 40 ml [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)
* A generous pinch of saffron, soaked in hot water
* 1 teaspoon ground [ginger](/en/food/ginger/), 1/2 teaspoon [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/), 1/2 teaspoon turmeric, black pepper
* A bunch of cilantro and a bunch of parsley, chopped
* 4 [eggs](/en/food/eggs/), beaten
* Salt

**For the almond layer:**
* 150 grams whole blanched [almonds](/en/food/almonds/)
* Oil for a quick fry
* 2 tablespoons sugar, 1/2 teaspoon [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/)

**For assembly:**
* 10-12 phyllo sheets (or warqa)
* 80 grams melted butter
* Powdered sugar and cinnamon for dusting

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Frozen supermarket phyllo sheets work in place of warqa: thaw them in the fridge according to the package and keep them covered while you work. Use chicken pieces in the total weight given, with no need for pigeon or special pastry sheets. The saffron can be skipped; the turmeric already in the recipe will add color. Use regular powdered sugar for the topping.

### Method

1. **Cook the chicken.** Heat oil in a wide pot, add the onion, spices and half of the chopped herbs, and fry for 8 minutes. Add the chicken pieces, season with salt, cover halfway with water, and cook for 45 minutes over low heat until the meat falls off the bone.
2. **Shred and reduce.** Remove the chicken, shred it and discard the bones and skin. Return the pot to the heat **uncovered** and reduce the liquid until only a thick sauce and a little onion remain — about 15 minutes. If you want a lighter dish, skim off the layer of fat floating on top at this stage, before reducing — about 40 calories less per serving.
3. **Set the eggs.** Lower the heat, pour the beaten eggs and the rest of the herbs into the sauce, and stir continuously until the eggs set completely into moist crumbs. **This is the critical step in the recipe:** an egg mixture that is too wet will soak the phyllo from below, and the pastry will never be crisp. It should look almost dry.
4. **Prepare the almonds.** Fry the almonds in deep oil for a minute or two until golden, drain on paper towels, then grind coarsely with the sugar and cinnamon. You can toast them in the oven instead of frying — about 20 calories less per serving.
5. **Assemble.** Brush a round pan (26-28 cm) with butter, and lay in 5-6 phyllo sheets with their edges hanging over the pan, each brushed with butter. Spread the egg and chicken mixture, and the almonds over it.
6. **Close.** Fold the overhanging edges inward, cover with 4-5 more brushed sheets, and tuck the edges firmly underneath. Brush the whole surface with butter.
7. **Bake** at 180 degrees Celsius (356°F) for 30-35 minutes until deep and evenly golden.
8. **Dust and serve.** Immediately dust with a generous layer of powdered sugar and mark lines of [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/) on it in a traditional pattern. Serve hot.

### Nutrition facts and calorie estimate

The recommended serving is one-eighth of the pastry — one wedge.

| Component | Per serving (one-eighth) |
|---|---|
| **Calories** | **about 600** |
| Protein | about 27 grams |
| Fat | about 40 grams |
| Carbohydrates | about 32 grams |
| Of which sugar | about 7 grams |

Pastilla is a festive dish, and the number reflects that. The calorie sources break down roughly like this: the chicken about 175 calories per serving, the [almonds](/en/food/almonds/) and the oil they absorbed about 130, the phyllo sheets about 94, the butter between the sheets about 72, the cooking oil and onion about 60, the eggs about 36, and the sugar — the most visually striking component — only about 27.

Two points emerge from this breakdown. The first is that the sugar is misleading: the layer of powdered sugar leads many people to assume this is a high-sugar dish, but in practice 7 grams of sugar per serving is about half the amount in a [square of baklava](/en/recipes/turkey/baklava/) — in a serving three times its size. The second is that the three real sources of calorie density are the almonds, the butter between the sheets and the chicken fat, and each can be reduced separately: skimming the fat off the sauce before reducing cuts about 40 calories per serving, 60 grams of butter instead of 80 cuts about 18 more, and toasting the almonds instead of frying them another 20 or so.

On the other hand, 27 grams of protein per serving, along with [vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/) and [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) from the [almonds](/en/food/almonds/), make it a complete main course rather than a pastry eaten alongside something else.

### Variations and tips

* **Skimming the fat from the sauce:** About 560 calories per serving instead of 600 — the biggest lever, with no change in flavor.
* **Fish or vegetable pastilla:** Common versions without sugar and cinnamon — seafood and glass noodles, or vegetables and almonds. About 390-420 calories per serving.
* **Individual pastillas:** Small squares instead of one large pastry. Shortens baking to 20 minutes and makes portion control easier.
* **Making ahead:** Assemble and freeze **unbaked** for up to two months, and bake straight from the freezer, adding 15 minutes. A baked pastilla that has cooled loses its crispness and doesn't recover when reheated.

### Summary

Pastilla is a dish that explains half a millennium of history in one bite: saffron chicken and powdered sugar on the same plate, a relic of a medieval cuisine that vanished everywhere else. About 600 calories per serving, with 40 grams of fat and about 27 grams of protein — a festive main course, not an everyday pastry. The step that decides whether it succeeds isn't the folding but drying out the egg mixture. More recipes in [Moroccan cuisine](/en/recipes/morocco/).

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in pastilla?**
Answer: A wedge of one-eighth of the pastry is about 600 calories, with 40 grams of fat and 27 grams of protein. Skimming the fat off the sauce before reducing brings it down to about 560. Fish or vegetable pastillas range from 390 to 420 calories per serving. Note that 1.1 kg of bone-in chicken yields only about 790 grams of edible meat.

**Question: Why sprinkle sugar on a savory dish?**
Answer: It is a relic of medieval Andalusian cuisine, in which sugar was an expensive spice rather than a separate dessert category. The separation of savory and sweet took shape in Europe only in the 17th-18th centuries, and in Morocco the original habit was preserved. In calorie terms the sugar layer contributes only about 27 calories per serving.

**Question: What is the difference between warqa and phyllo?**
Answer: *Warqa* is a liquid dough dabbed by hand onto an upturned pan, and the result is pliable and less brittle. Phyllo sheets are stretched and dried, so they are more brittle and need more generous brushing with butter. Phyllo is a legitimate substitute, and most pastillas outside Morocco are made with it.

**Question: My pastilla came out soft instead of crisp — why?**
Answer: Almost always the egg and chicken mixture was too wet when it went in. The sauce needs to reduce until there is almost no liquid, and the eggs need to set into crumbs that look dry. A second common cause is stingy buttering between the sheets.

**Question: Can you make pastilla ahead?**
Answer: Yes, but only **before** baking: assemble, wrap well and freeze for up to two months, and bake straight from the freezer, adding about 15 minutes. A pastry that has already been baked and cooled loses its crispness, and reheating doesn't bring it back.
