## Pumpkin Soup

Pumpkin soup is the most common pureed soup of autumn and winter, and it is also one of the clearest examples that "vegetable soup" and "low-calorie soup" are not the same thing. The pumpkin itself is about 40 calories per 100 grams; what happens to it on the way to the bowl — oil, coconut milk or cream — is what decides whether the bowl comes to 130 calories or to 300.

In Israel the soup is usually made from chestnut [pumpkin](/en/food/pumpkin/) (a kabocha-type squash) or from butternut squash, and sometimes combined with [sweet potato](/en/food/sweet-potato/). The recipe here is built on roasting rather than boiling, and that is the single big difference between a decent pumpkin soup and an excellent one.

**Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Roasting and cooking time:** 50 minutes · **Total:** about an hour and a quarter · **Yield:** 6 servings · **Difficulty:** easy

### Roasting vs. boiling: the biggest difference in the recipe

Pumpkin cooked in water loses sugars into the liquid and never reaches the temperature at which caramelization happens — water does not rise above 100°C (212°F). Pumpkin roasted at 200°C (392°F) loses water, concentrates its sugars, and browns at the edges. The result is much sweeter and deeper, without a single gram of sugar added.

That difference in flavor is also a calorie decision: soup from roasted pumpkin hardly needs cream to taste rich, whereas soup from boiled pumpkin "demands" it — and every 100 ml of heavy cream is about 350 calories going into the pot.

### Ingredients (for 6 servings)

**For roasting:**
* 1.2 kg regular [pumpkin](/en/food/pumpkin/) or butternut squash, peeled and seeded, in large cubes
* 1 medium [sweet potato](/en/food/sweet-potato/) (about 200 grams), cubed — optional, adds sweetness and body
* 1 large [onion](/en/food/onion/), quartered
* 4 whole [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) cloves in their skins
* 2 tablespoons [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)
* Salt and black pepper

**For the soup:**
* 1 medium [carrot](/en/food/carrot/), chopped
* 2 cm fresh [ginger](/en/food/ginger/), grated
* 1/2 teaspoon [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/), 1/2 teaspoon cumin, a pinch of nutmeg
* 800 ml vegetable stock or water
* 200 ml canned coconut milk (full fat)
* Salt, and the juice of half a [lemon](/en/food/lemon/) at the end

**For serving:** toasted [pumpkin seeds](/en/food/pumpkin-seeds/), a drizzle of olive oil, a little freshly ground black pepper.

### Choosing ingredients and substitutes

Regular pumpkin sold in pieces or butternut squash both work; you need 1.2 kg after peeling and removing the seeds. For coconut milk, choose a cooking product in a can or small carton, not a thin coconut drink. A dairy alternative is 200 ml of 15% cooking cream, added with gentle heating at the end in place of the coconut milk.

### Method

1. **Preheat the oven to 200°C (392°F).** Spread the cubes of pumpkin, sweet potato, onion and the garlic on a baking tray, and toss with olive oil, salt and pepper.
2. **Roast for 30-35 minutes**, until the edges are truly browned. **This is the step that decides the result** — pumpkin that is "soft but pale" gives a flat soup. Spread it in a single layer: an overcrowded tray makes the vegetables steam instead of browning.
3. **Make the base.** While roasting, heat a little olive oil in a pot, fry the carrot for 5 minutes, add the ginger and spices and stir for half a minute until fragrant.
4. **Combine.** Put all the roasted vegetables into the pot (squeeze the garlic out of its skin), add the stock and bring to a gentle boil. Cook for 15 minutes.
5. **Blend.** Puree with an immersion blender until completely smooth. For full velvetiness, transfer to a countertop blender and blend for 60 seconds — a difference you notice immediately.
6. **Add the coconut milk** and return to low heat for 3-4 minutes without boiling.
7. **Adjust at the end.** Salt, and lemon juice — that is what keeps the soup from tasting too sweet. Serve with toasted pumpkin seeds.

The recommended serving is a bowl of about 300 ml, a sixth of the pot.

The breakdown reveals the real structure of the dish: all the vegetables — 1.2 kg of pumpkin, sweet potato, onion and carrot — come to about 130 calories per bowl. The two tablespoons of olive oil and 200 ml of coconut milk are about 105 more calories. In other words, **almost half of this soup is the fat added to it**, and the same is true of the versions with heavy cream.

This is not necessarily a downside. The carotenoids that give pumpkin its color — beta-carotene, from which the body makes [vitamin A](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-a/) — are fat-soluble, and their absorption from a fat-free soup is much lower. One bowl easily provides more than the daily reference intake of vitamin A.

### Variations and tips

* **Light version (about 170 calories):** skip the coconut milk and make do with a tablespoon of olive oil. Roasting provides enough depth that the soup won't taste lacking.
* **Protein version:** half a cup of red [lentils](/en/food/lentil/) cooked with the pumpkin adds about 60 calories and 4 grams of protein per serving, and is barely noticeable in the taste. It solves this soup's main weakness.
* **Pumpkin vs. butternut:** butternut squash is sweeter and less watery, so it gives a thicker soup. Chestnut pumpkin is slightly lower in calories. Both work.
* **A spice that changes the soup completely:** a teaspoon of curry powder or a teaspoon of harissa instead of the cumin and turmeric — a complete change of direction without a single extra calorie.
* **Making ahead:** 4 days in the fridge, 3 months in the freezer. Pumpkin soup freezes especially well, as long as the coconut milk is added after thawing and not before freezing.

### Summary

Pumpkin soup rests on one technical decision — roast, don't boil — which doubles the depth of flavor and makes much of the cream unnecessary. About 235 calories per bowl in the version with coconut milk, about 170 without it, and a full daily dose of vitamin A in both versions. ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a bowl of pumpkin soup?**
Answer: About 235 per 300 ml bowl in the version with coconut milk, and about 170 without it. The pumpkin itself accounts for only about 130 of them — almost half the soup is the added oil and coconut milk.

**Question: Is it better to roast the pumpkin or boil it?**
Answer: Roast it. Water doesn't rise above 100°C, so boiled pumpkin never caramelizes, and it also loses sugars into the liquid. Roasting at 200°C concentrates the sugars and browns the edges, and that is the biggest difference between a decent pumpkin soup and an excellent one.

**Question: How do you thicken pumpkin soup without cream?**
Answer: Add a starchy vegetable — [sweet potato](/en/food/sweet-potato/), potato or half a cup of red [lentils](/en/food/lentil/) — and blend everything together in a countertop blender rather than with an immersion blender. A countertop blender breaks down the pumpkin fibers better and gives a velvety texture an immersion blender can't reach.

**Question: Which is better — coconut milk or cream?**
Answer: Calorie-wise they are fairly similar (full-fat coconut milk about 200 calories per 100 ml, heavy cream about 350). The real difference is in taste and kashrut — coconut milk makes the soup vegan and pareve, and adds a sweet note. To save about 40 calories per serving you can choose light coconut milk.

**Question: The soup came out too sweet — what do you do?**
Answer: Add acid, not salt. [Lemon](/en/food/lemon/) juice or a teaspoon of apple cider vinegar balances the sweetness of the pumpkin and sweet potato immediately. If the sweetness is still dominant, a pinch of chili pepper or coarsely ground black pepper cuts it from another direction.

**Question: Is pumpkin soup suitable for vegans?**
Answer: Yes, the recipe here is completely vegan — it relies on olive oil and coconut milk instead of butter and cream. Just make sure the stock is vegetable stock and not chicken stock.
