When looking at natural sugar substitutes, the three leading candidates are [honey](/en/food/honey/), silan ([date](/en/food/dates/) "[honey](/en/food/honey/)" — date syrup, called "סילאן" in Hebrew) and maple syrup. Although all of them are seen as "healthy" alternatives to white sugar, their chemical composition and metabolic effects differ substantially.

### 1. [Honey](/en/food/honey/)

[Honey](/en/food/honey/) is produced by bees from flower nectar and goes through an enzymatic process in the hive.

* **Composition:** It contains about 80% sugar (mainly fructose and glucose) and 17% water.
* **Benefits:**
    * **Antioxidants:** Rich in [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) and flavonoids that help reduce oxidative stress.
    * **Antibacterial properties:** It contains enzymes that produce hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), which gives it disinfecting ability (especially in manuka [honey](/en/food/honey/)).
    * **Glycemic index:** Around 58 (medium), slightly lower than white sugar.
* **Downsides:**
    * **Botulism:** It must not be given to babies under one year old because of the risk of Clostridium botulinum spores.
    * **Calories:** Very calorie-dense (about 64 calories per tablespoon).

### 2. Silan (Date Syrup)

An extract of Medjool or Hayani dates that are cooked and strained into a thick syrup.

* **Composition:** It contains mainly glucose and fructose, but is richer in minerals than the other sweeteners.
* **Benefits:**
    * **Rich in minerals:** An excellent source of [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/), [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) and [iron](/en/food/iron/).
    * **Dietary fiber:** If it hasn't been fully strained, it contains residual fiber that aids digestion.
    * **[Polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/):** A high concentration of antioxidants that protect the blood vessels.
* **Downsides:**
    * **Sugar content:** Some brands add processed white sugar, so you should choose "100% natural silan."
    * **Glycemic load:** Relatively high, which can lead to a sharp rise in blood sugar.

### 3. Maple Syrup

Produced from the sap of the maple tree (mainly in Canada) by boiling and reduction.

* **Composition:** It contains mainly sucrose (unlike [honey](/en/food/honey/) and silan, which are rich in free fructose).
* **Benefits:**
    * **[Manganese](/en/food/supplements/manganese/) and riboflavin:** One tablespoon provides about 25% of the recommended daily intake of [manganese](/en/food/supplements/manganese/), which is essential for bone health and metabolism.
    * **Unique antioxidants:** It contains compounds such as quebecol that have been found to have anti-inflammatory properties in preliminary studies.
    * **Glycemic index:** The lowest of the three (about 54).
* **Downsides:**
    * **Sucrose:** Its molecular composition is closer to table sugar.
    * **Fakes:** The market for "maple-flavored syrup" is large and contains high-fructose [corn syrup](/en/food/corn-syrup/) and food coloring.

### Summary and conclusions

From a scientific standpoint, **maple syrup** wins on glycemic index and rare minerals, **silan** stands out for its [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) and fiber content, and **[honey](/en/food/honey/)** offers unique medicinal and antibacterial properties. However, it's important to remember that all three count as "added sugars," and they should be consumed in moderation as part of a balanced diet.
