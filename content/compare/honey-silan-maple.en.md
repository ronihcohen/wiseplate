## A Scientific Comparison: Honey, Date Syrup and Maple Syrup

**All three are mostly sugar, and the health differences between them are small. Pure maple syrup is the lowest in calories (about 52 per tablespoon) and supplies manganese; natural date syrup keeps a little of the date's minerals and polyphenols; honey is the one to choose to soothe a cough in a child over one year old. For any other purpose, the healthy choice is simply to use less.**

[Honey](/en/food/honey/), date syrup (in Hebrew "סילאן", silan, made from [dates](/en/food/dates/)) and maple syrup are seen as "natural" alternatives to white sugar. The World Health Organization classes all of them as "free sugars", together with table sugar and fruit juices, and recommends keeping total free sugars below 10% of daily energy, ideally below 5%, which is about 25 g, or 6 teaspoons, a day for an adult. [WHO guideline on sugars intake](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549028). This comparison helps you choose among the three, not turn any of them into a health food.

Glycemic index values are an order of magnitude: they vary between varieties and between laboratories. [International glycemic index tables, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34258626/).

### Honey

Honey is made by bees from flower nectar. It is about 80% sugar, mainly fructose and glucose, and about 17% water, with very small amounts of acids, enzymes and [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/). Dark honeys (such as buckwheat or manuka) contain more polyphenols than light ones.

**Benefits:**
* **Cough in children:** a Cochrane review of six trials (899 children) found that honey probably relieves cough frequency better than placebo or no treatment, when used for up to three days. [Cochrane review, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29633783/).
* **High sweetness:** because of its fructose it tastes sweeter than sugar, so a smaller amount can do the job.
* **Polyphenols:** present, but not in meaningful amounts in a tablespoon. Honey's antiseptic properties matter for medical use on wounds, not for eating.

**Downsides:**
* **Not for babies under one year:** honey can contain spores of *Clostridium botulinum*, and an infant's gut cannot yet stop them from growing. That is the reason for the blanket ban, which includes honey in cooked or baked foods. [Review on infant botulism and honey](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41964486/).
* **The most calories of the three:** about 64 kcal per tablespoon, because it is dense and contains less water.
* **Adulteration:** honey diluted with sugar syrups does reach the market. A brand with a recognized quality mark lowers the risk.

### Date syrup (silan)

Date syrup is made from dates that are cooked in water, pressed and reduced to a thick syrup. Natural date syrup contains only dates; some cheaper products also contain sugar or glucose syrup.

**Benefits:**
* **A little of the date survives:** date syrup keeps some of the potassium, magnesium and polyphenols of [dates](/en/food/dates/), more than white sugar does. In a tablespoon, the contribution is modest.
* **Rich flavor:** the deep caramel taste lets you use less, in tahini, yogurt and baking.
* **Local and affordable:** a common Middle Eastern product, cheaper than maple syrup.

**Downsides:**
* **Still sugar:** about 70–75% sugars. Claims of "mineral richness" hold only relative to white sugar, not relative to the fruit.
* **Fiber:** filtering removes most of the fiber, so date syrup raises blood sugar like other sweeteners. If you want the date's fiber, eat a date.
* **Variable quality:** check that the ingredient list says "100% dates" only.

### Maple syrup

Maple syrup is made from the sap of maple trees (mainly in Canada and the north-eastern United States), boiled until most of the water evaporates.

**Benefits:**
* **The fewest calories:** about 52 kcal per tablespoon, because it contains more water.
* **Manganese and riboflavin:** a tablespoon provides about 0.6 mg of [manganese](/en/food/supplements/manganese/), roughly a quarter of the daily value, and a meaningful amount of riboflavin (B2).
* **Moderate glycemic index:** about 54, slightly lower than white sugar, though the difference is not large.

**Downsides:**
* **Sucrose:** its main sugar is the same as table sugar.
* **Price:** the most expensive of the three.
* **"Maple-flavored syrup":** many "pancake syrups" are [corn syrup](/en/food/corn-syrup/) with coloring and flavoring. Only "100% maple syrup" is maple.
* **Anti-inflammatory claims:** compounds such as quebecol have been studied only in the lab, and there is no evidence of benefit in people at normal servings.

### Which to choose, and for whom?

* **For babies under one:** better to give none of them, and honey is strictly off-limits.
* **For a cough in a child over one:** a teaspoon of honey before bed is the only option of the three with evidence behind it.
* **If you count calories:** pure maple, in a small amount.
* **For diabetes or blood-sugar control:** the differences are small. Count each as carbohydrate (about 13–17 g per tablespoon) and prefer whole fruit for sweetness.
* **For vegans:** date syrup or maple; honey is not vegan.
* **For baking:** maple and date syrup give a deep flavor; honey browns faster in the oven, so lower the temperature slightly.
* **For tahini, yogurt and porridge:** date syrup, by the teaspoon.

### Summary

Honey, date syrup and maple syrup are three versions of sugar with different flavors and very little extra nutrition. Maple is slightly lighter in calories and supplies manganese, date syrup keeps a trace of the date, and honey helps with coughs in children but is forbidden for infants. Choose by taste, and keep the amount down: together with all other sugars, it is best not to go above about 6 teaspoons a day.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Which is healthier, honey or date syrup?**
Answer: The difference is small. Natural date syrup contains slightly more potassium and magnesium and slightly fewer calories; honey helps with coughs in children. Both are free sugars, and the amount matters more than the choice between them.

**Question: Is maple syrup healthier than sugar?**
Answer: Slightly. A tablespoon of maple has fewer calories than a tablespoon of sugar and adds manganese, but its main sugar is the same sucrose. It has no advantage that justifies using it in large amounts.

**Question: Why can't babies have honey?**
Answer: Honey can contain spores of the botulism bacterium. In babies under one year, the spores can grow in the gut and produce a toxin that causes paralysis. The ban also covers cooked or baked honey.

**Question: What is the glycemic index of honey, date syrup and maple syrup?**
Answer: About 50–60 for honey (depending on the variety), about 60 for date syrup and about 54 for maple, compared with about 65 for white sugar. The differences are small, and at a normal serving the glycemic load of all three is similar.

**Question: Can people with diabetes eat date syrup or honey?**
Answer: In small amounts and within their carbohydrate count, yes, but they are not "free foods". A tablespoon contains about 13–17 g of carbohydrate, and the effect on blood sugar is similar to that of ordinary sugar.

**Question: How can I tell real maple syrup?**
Answer: The ingredient list will say only "100% maple syrup". Products labeled "maple-flavored" or "pancake syrup" are usually corn syrup or glucose syrup with added flavoring and coloring.
