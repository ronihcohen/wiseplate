The question "which is healthier" is almost always asked between foods from the same family — which rice, which lentil, which oil, which sugar. This section puts the options side by side in a single table and ends with a practical conclusion, not with "they're both good."

### What usually decides these comparisons

* **The difference is smaller than it seems, except in one place.** Between [white, brown and coconut sugar](/en/compare/white-brown-coconut-sugar/) or between [table, Himalayan and sea salt](/en/compare/table-himalayan-sea-salt/), the nutritional gap is almost entirely negligible. By contrast, between [white and brown rice](/en/compare/white-brown-wild-rice/) or between [wheat and rye flour](/en/compare/wheat-spelt-rye-flour/), the difference in fiber and glycemic load is real.
* **Color is a hint about polyphenols.** In lentils, quinoa, rice and grapes, the darker version usually carries more [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) and antioxidants.
* **What suits the recipe beats a difference of a few percent.** [Red lentils](/en/compare/red-green-black-lentils/) fall apart into soup, green ones hold their shape in a salad. That is a genuine practical consideration, not a compromise.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Which rice is the healthiest?**
Answer: Wild rice and brown rice, thanks to their fiber, magnesium and lower glycemic load than white rice. The caveat is arsenic, which accumulates in the bran and is therefore actually higher in brown rice — rinsing before cooking and cooking in plenty of water reduce it. The details are in the [comparison of white, brown and wild rice](/en/compare/white-brown-wild-rice/).

**Question: What is the difference between red and green lentils?**
Answer: Red lentils are lentils that have been hulled, so they cook quickly, break down into a soft texture and contain less fiber. Green and black lentils keep their skin, and with it more fiber and polyphenols, and stay whole when cooked. The details are in the [lentil comparison](/en/compare/red-green-black-lentils/).

**Question: Is Himalayan salt healthier than table salt?**
Answer: Not meaningfully. The extra minerals in it are present in tiny amounts that don't contribute to daily intake. What's more, iodized table salt provides iodine — an essential nutrient that most sea and Himalayan salts don't contain.

**Question: Which is healthier — brown sugar or white?**
Answer: They are almost identical. Brown sugar is white sugar with residual molasses, which adds negligible amounts of minerals. The body digests both the same way, and the calorie difference is nil.

**Question: Which plant milk is best?**
Answer: It depends on your goal. Soy milk is the only one with a protein profile similar to cow's milk (about 7 grams per cup), almond milk is the lowest in calories, and oat milk is the richest in carbohydrates and has the highest glycemic index. The full comparison is in [soy, almond and oat milk](/en/compare/soy-almond-oat-milk/).
