We all know we should cut down on sugar, but when we do sweeten, does the type of sugar matter? The answer may surprise you.

### 1. White Sugar

The classic table sugar, produced from sugar cane or sugar [beets](/en/food/beets/).

* **Composition:** 99.9% sucrose (a disaccharide made of glucose and fructose).
* **Benefits:**
    * **Taste and texture:** A clean, neutral taste that doesn't take over. Essential to the chemistry of baking (it provides airiness and crispness).
    * **Price:** The cheapest sweetener on the market.
* **Downsides:**
    * **Nutritionally empty:** It provides only "empty calories" – energy without any vitamins, minerals or dietary fiber.
    * **Blood sugar spikes:** It causes a fast, sharp rise in blood sugar and [insulin](/en/food/supplements/insulin/) levels (glycemic index ~65).

### 2. Brown Sugar

Usually this is white sugar cooked with molasses, or raw sugar that hasn't been fully refined.

* **Composition:** About 95% sucrose, with about 5% molasses.
* **Benefits:**
    * **Moisture and flavor:** The molasses gives baked goods moisture (cookies come out softer) and a rich caramel flavor.
    * **Minerals:** It contains tiny amounts of [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/), [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) and [iron](/en/food/iron/) that come from the molasses.
* **Downsides:**
    * **A health illusion:** The amount of minerals is so negligible that it has no real health significance at all. The body treats it almost exactly like white sugar.

### 3. [Coconut](/en/food/coconut/) Sugar

A natural sweetener produced from the sap of the [coconut](/en/food/coconut/) palm's flowers (not from the fruit itself).

* **Composition:** It contains mainly sucrose (about 70-80%), but also contains dietary fiber (inulin).
* **Benefits:**
    * **Glycemic index:** Significantly lower than white sugar (about 35-54 compared with 65), probably thanks to the inulin fiber, which slows absorption.
    * **Minerals:** It contains a nice concentration of [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/), [zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) and [iron](/en/food/iron/), plus antioxidants.
    * **Processing:** Produced by a natural evaporation process, without chemicals.
* **Downsides:**
    * **Price:** Significantly more expensive than regular sugar.
    * **Still sugar:** Despite its advantages, it is still sugar and high in calories, and should be consumed in moderation.

### Summary and conclusions

If you're looking for a **healthier substitute** that will spike your blood sugar less, **[coconut](/en/food/coconut/) sugar** is a small step in the right direction. If you're torn between **white and brown sugar** for health reasons – don't bother. The difference is only in taste and texture. Bottom line: your body sees them all as sugar, so enjoy the flavor you prefer, but in moderation.
