## A Scientific Comparison: Olive, Coconut and Avocado Oil

**For everyday use and heart health, pick extra virgin olive oil, the most studied oil and one that suits most home cooking. For very high heat or when you want a neutral taste, pick refined avocado oil. Keep coconut oil for baking and dishes that need its flavor or texture: it raises LDL cholesterol more than other plant oils.**

All three oils provide about 120 kcal per tablespoon. The difference lies in the type of fat: olive oil and [avocado](/en/food/avocado/) oil are rich in monounsaturated fat (oleic acid), while [coconut](/en/food/coconut/) oil is mostly saturated fat, which is why it is solid at room temperature. In Hebrew they are "שמן זית", "שמן קוקוס" and "שמן אבוקדו".

### Extra virgin olive oil

[Olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/) is the main fat of the Mediterranean diet. "Extra virgin" is oil extracted by mechanical pressing without refining, so it keeps its [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) and flavor.

**Benefits:**
* **A controlled trial:** in the PREDIMED trial, a Mediterranean diet supplemented with about a liter of extra virgin olive oil a week per household reduced cardiovascular events compared with a low-fat diet. [Estruch et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29897866/).
* **Population studies:** in 28 years of follow-up of about 92,000 Americans, people who used more than half a tablespoon of olive oil a day had about a 19% lower risk of dying from cardiovascular disease than those who rarely used it. Replacing butter and margarine with olive oil was linked to lower risk. [Guasch-Ferré et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35027106/).
* **Stable in cooking:** despite a moderate smoke point, the monounsaturated fat and antioxidants in extra virgin olive oil protect it during normal home cooking, including sautéing and oven roasting.

**Downsides:**
* **Not for prolonged deep-frying:** at very high heat over a long time, the flavor and polyphenols suffer.
* **Sensitive to light and heat:** keep it in a dark, closed bottle away from the stove.
* **Adulteration:** "olive" oil blended with cheaper oils does reach the market. Buy from a known brand with a harvest date.

### Coconut oil

[Coconut](/en/food/coconut/) oil is extracted from the flesh of the coconut. Virgin coconut oil keeps the coconut aroma and taste; refined coconut oil is neutral.

**Benefits:**
* **Heat-stable:** its saturated fat barely oxidizes, so it suits baking and stir-frying.
* **Texture:** solid at room temperature, so it works as a vegan butter substitute in baking and desserts.
* **HDL:** it raises "good" cholesterol slightly, but that does not offset the rise in LDL.

**Downsides:**
* **Raises LDL:** a meta-analysis of 16 trials found that coconut oil raised LDL cholesterol by about 10 mg/dL compared with non-tropical vegetable oils, with no benefit for weight, blood sugar or inflammation. [Neelakantan et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31928080/).
* **"MCT" isn't quite right:** most of the fat in coconut oil is lauric acid, which behaves in the body mostly like a long-chain fat. Studies on "quick energy" were mostly done on special MCT oil, not on coconut oil.
* **Taste:** virgin coconut oil adds a coconut flavor that doesn't suit every dish.

### Avocado oil

Avocado oil is pressed from the flesh of the fruit. Its fat composition is very similar to olive oil.

**Benefits:**
* **High heat:** refined avocado oil holds up at searing and frying temperatures, which makes it the convenient choice for a steak in a smoking-hot pan or for frying.
* **Neutral taste:** suits homemade mayonnaise, baking and dishes where the taste of olive oil gets in the way.
* **A good fat profile:** about 70% monounsaturated, like olive oil.

**Downsides:**
* **Little research:** there are no large clinical trials on avocado oil as there are on olive oil. Its advantage rests mainly on its fat composition.
* **Price:** usually much more expensive than olive oil.
* **Variable quality:** avocado oil has no mandatory quality grade like "extra virgin" for olive oil, so buy from a reliable brand and pay attention to smell and taste.

### Which oil should you choose?

* **For salads, dressing and everyday cooking:** extra virgin olive oil.
* **For heart health and high cholesterol:** olive oil, and cut back on coconut oil.
* **For high-heat frying and searing in a very hot pan:** refined avocado oil. For deep-frying, canola oil or regular (not extra virgin) olive oil also work.
* **For vegan baking and desserts that need a solid fat:** coconut oil, in moderation.
* **For a neutral taste (mayonnaise, cakes):** avocado oil, or a mild olive oil.
* **On a budget:** regular olive oil or canola for cooking, and a small bottle of extra virgin for salads.

### Summary

Extra virgin olive oil is the best-supported choice for heart health and suits most uses in the kitchen. Avocado oil is a good alternative for high heat and neutral flavor, but it is expensive and far less studied. Coconut oil raises LDL, so its place is in baking and occasional use, not as your everyday oil. Whichever you use, a tablespoon is about 120 kcal, and measuring is worth it.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: What is the healthiest oil for cooking?**
Answer: Extra virgin olive oil, for most home cooking. It is more heat-stable than its smoke point suggests, thanks to its monounsaturated fat and antioxidants. For very high heat, refined avocado oil.

**Question: Can I fry with olive oil?**
Answer: Yes, for sautéing, roasting and shallow frying. For long deep-frying at high heat, regular olive oil or an oil with a higher smoke point is better, because extra virgin loses its flavor and polyphenols.

**Question: Is coconut oil healthy?**
Answer: Not as an everyday oil. A meta-analysis of 16 trials found that it raises LDL by about 10 mg/dL compared with other plant oils, with no benefit for weight or blood sugar. A small amount in baking is fine.

**Question: Is avocado oil better than olive oil?**
Answer: For very high heat and neutral flavor, yes. For health, there is no evidence that it is better: the fat composition is similar, but olive oil has far more research behind it and more polyphenols.

**Question: What's the difference between extra virgin and regular olive oil?**
Answer: Extra virgin is mechanically pressed without refining and keeps its polyphenols and flavor. Regular olive oil ("pure" or "light") is mostly refined oil, more neutral and with a higher smoke point, but with fewer antioxidants.

**Question: How much olive oil should I have a day?**
Answer: There is no single official recommendation. In studies, a benefit appears from half a tablespoon a day, and in PREDIMED participants used about 4 tablespoons a day. The amount should replace other fats, not be added on top of them.
