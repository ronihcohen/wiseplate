## A Scientific Comparison: Soy, Almond and Oat Milk

**Unsweetened, calcium-fortified soy milk is the plant milk closest to cow's milk nutritionally, with about 7 g of protein per cup, and fortified pea milk comes close. Almond milk is the lowest in calories — and in protein — and oat milk is the best in coffee. Rice and coconut drinks have almost no protein, and rice drinks are not suitable for young children because of arsenic.**

In Hebrew, plant milks are called "חלב צמחי", and soy, almond and oat milk are "חלב סויה", "חלב שקדים" and "חלב שיבולת שועל". The shelf of [milk](/en/food/milk/) alternatives has grown enormously, and the products differ far more than their cartons suggest. A review comparing the most common plant drinks with cow's milk found that only soy comes close in protein, and that most of these drinks cannot be treated as a direct replacement for milk without checking their fortification and composition. [Vanga & Raghavan, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29358791/).

*\* Oats themselves are [gluten-free](/en/food/gluten/), but they are often processed alongside wheat. People with celiac disease should choose a product labeled gluten-free.*

**About "if fortified":** Without fortification, plant drinks are very low in calcium. And even when calcium is added, absorption is not necessarily the same: in a study of calcium-fortified soy milk, its calcium was absorbed at about 75% of the efficiency of calcium from cow's milk. [Heaney et al., 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10799379/). The added calcium also settles to the bottom — shake the carton before pouring.

### 1. Soy Milk

The oldest and most studied option, and the only one close to cow's milk in composition.

**Benefits:**
* **Complete protein:** About 7 g per cup, with all the essential amino acids.
* **Fortification:** Most brands add calcium, vitamin B12 and vitamin D.
* **In cooking:** Behaves well in baking and sauces, and froths well.

**Downsides:**
* **Allergen:** Soy is a common allergen, especially in children.
* **Taste:** A bean-like flavor that not everyone likes.
* **Hormones — a fear not borne out:** A meta-analysis of clinical studies in men found that soy or isoflavones do not change testosterone or estrogen levels. [Reed et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33383165/).

### 2. Almond Milk

Popular for its low calories and mild taste.

**Benefits:**
* **Calories:** About 30–40 per cup in the unsweetened version — the lowest of all.
* **Taste:** Mild and nutty, good in smoothies and porridge.

**Downsides:**
* **Almost no protein:** About 1 g per cup. The drink is mostly water, and almonds usually make up only about 2–7% of it.
* **Environment:** Almond growing uses a lot of water, mainly in dry regions.

### 3. Oat Milk

The winner in coffee and foam.

**Benefits:**
* **Texture:** Thick, creamy and naturally sweet. "Barista" versions froth stably and split less in acidic coffee.
* **Beta-glucan:** The soluble fiber in oats lowers LDL cholesterol at intakes of at least 3 g a day. [Whitehead et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25411276/). A cup of oat drink usually contains far less than that, so it shouldn't be seen as a cholesterol treatment.

**Downsides:**
* **Carbohydrates and sugar:** During production, enzymes break the starch down into sugars, so even a "no added sugar" oat drink is sweet and relatively high in carbohydrates.
* **Little protein and added oil:** 2–3 g of protein per cup, and usually sunflower or rapeseed oil is added for creaminess.

### 4. Pea, Rice and Coconut Drinks

* **Pea:** Made from isolated [pea](/en/food/peas/) protein. About 6–8 g of protein per cup, usually fortified, free of soy and nuts. A good option for people allergic to soy, though less studied.
* **Rice:** Sweet, thin and almost protein-free, with a lot of available carbohydrate. Rice also takes up inorganic arsenic from the soil, so rice drinks are not recommended for children under 5. [NHS, drinks for babies and young children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/drinks-and-cups-for-babies-and-young-children/).
* **Coconut drink:** The carton drink (not canned coconut milk for cooking) is low in calories, almost protein-free and contains saturated fat. Choose it for taste, not nutrition.

### Children and babies

* **Under 12 months:** Plant drinks — soy included — are **not a substitute** for breast milk or infant formula. The North American Society for Pediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition states that in the first year only commercial infant formula is an appropriate alternative to breast milk, and it documents poor growth and severe deficiencies when plant drinks were used instead. [NASPGHAN, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32732790/). Soy-based infant formula is an entirely different product from soy drink, and is given only on a health professional's advice.
* **Rice drinks:** Not for children under 5, because of their arsenic levels (see NHS above).
* **Toddlers from age 1:** The UK's National Health Service allows unsweetened, calcium-fortified plant drinks as part of a varied diet. In practice, fortified unsweetened soy milk is the usual plant option, because it is the only one with protein close to cow's milk. Almond, oat and coconut drinks have too little protein for a toddler for whom the drink is a large part of the diet. [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/drinks-and-cups-for-babies-and-young-children/).
* **In any case:** If a toddler doesn't drink cow's milk (allergy, vegan family), plan the diet with a pediatric dietitian to make sure of protein, calcium, vitamin D, B12, iodine and iron.

### How to read the label

* **Ingredient percentage:** The ingredient list shows the percentage of almonds, oats or soy. In almond drinks it is usually just 2–7%.
* **Sugar:** Look for "unsweetened". "Original" and "vanilla" versions usually contain added sugar — sometimes 2–3 teaspoons per cup.
* **Fortification:** Check for calcium (about 120 mg per 100 ml is similar to milk), vitamin B12 and vitamin D. Organic versions are often **not fortified**.
* **Protein:** At least 3 g per 100 ml if the drink replaces milk in your diet.

### Which plant milk should you choose?

* **A nutritional replacement for cow's milk (vegans, children from age 1, athletes):** fortified unsweetened soy, or fortified pea.
* **Fewer calories:** unsweetened almond — knowing it has no protein to speak of.
* **Coffee:** a barista-style oat drink.
* **Allergic to soy and nuts:** pea or oat (with celiac disease, labeled gluten-free).
* **Vegans:** make sure the drink is fortified with B12, or take a supplement. B12 is not naturally present in plant foods. [NIH ODS, Vitamin B12](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/).
* **Diabetes:** unsweetened soy, almond or pea before oat and rice.

### Summary

There is no single "healthiest plant milk", but there is a clear choice for each goal. **Fortified unsweetened soy** is closest to cow's milk, **pea** is a high-protein soy-free alternative, **almond** is the lowest in calories, and **oat** wins in coffee. **Rice and coconut** are mostly a matter of taste, and rice is not for young children. Either way, the label matters more than the name: unsweetened, fortified with calcium and B12.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Which plant milk is the healthiest?**
Answer: As a replacement for cow's milk, unsweetened soy milk fortified with calcium and B12. It provides about 7 g of protein per cup, close to the 8 g in cow's milk. Fortified pea milk is a good alternative. Almond is the lowest in calories, and oat is the best in coffee.

**Question: Which plant milk has the fewest calories?**
Answer: Unsweetened almond milk, with about 30–40 calories per cup. It is mostly water with a few percent almonds, so it also has almost no protein.

**Question: Which plant milk is best for coffee?**
Answer: A barista-style oat drink. It froths stably and splits less in acidic coffee. Soy froths well but can split in espresso, and almond froths the least well.

**Question: Can I give my baby plant milk?**
Answer: Not under 12 months. Until age 1, only breast milk or infant formula. From age 1, an unsweetened fortified plant drink can be part of a varied diet, and fortified soy is usually the choice, with a dietitian's guidance. Rice drinks are not suitable under age 5.

**Question: Why is rice milk not suitable for children?**
Answer: Rice takes up inorganic arsenic from the soil, so the UK's National Health Service advises against rice drinks for children under 5. It also contains almost no protein.

**Question: Does plant milk contain calcium?**
Answer: Only if it is fortified. Fortified versions have about 120 mg per 100 ml, similar to cow's milk, though absorption may be somewhat lower. Shake the carton, because the calcium settles. Organic versions are often not fortified.

**Question: Does soy milk affect hormones?**
Answer: According to a meta-analysis of clinical studies, soy and isoflavone intake does not change testosterone or estrogen levels in men. Isoflavones bind estrogen receptors far more weakly than estrogen itself.
