The [milk](/en/food/milk/) alternatives shelf at the supermarket has exploded in recent years, presenting us with an everyday dilemma: what's best for [coffee](/en/food/coffee/), for cereal and for our health?

### 1. [Soy Milk](/en/food/soy-milk/)

The oldest and most researched, and the only one that comes close to cow's [milk](/en/food/milk/) in its nutritional composition.

* **Composition:** Made from soybeans and water. Rich in complete, high-quality protein.
* **Benefits:**
    * **Protein:** It contains about 7-8 grams of protein per cup (similar to cow's [milk](/en/food/milk/)), which is filling and contributes to building muscle.
    * **Phytoestrogens:** It contains isoflavones that may help reduce menopause symptoms and protect the heart.
    * **Cooking:** It performs very well in cooking and baking, thickens and creates a stable foam for coffee.
* **Downsides:**
    * **Allergenic:** Soy is a common allergen.
    * **Taste:** It has a noticeable "beany" taste that not everyone takes to (though it has improved a lot in newer products).

### 2. [Almond Milk](/en/food/almond-milk/)

Loved for its low calories and delicate flavor.

* **Composition:** Made mainly from water and a few [almonds](/en/food/almonds/) (usually about 2% to 7%), so the commercial version is nutritionally thin.
* **Benefits:**
    * **Calories:** Especially low in calories (in the unsweetened version), about 30-40 calories per cup.
    * **Taste:** Delicate, nutty and pleasant; it blends wonderfully into smoothies and porridges.
    * **[Vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/):** Many brands fortify it with [vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/) and [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/).
* **Downsides:**
    * **Low in protein:** It contains a tiny amount of protein (about 1 gram per cup). It is not a nutritional substitute for [milk](/en/food/milk/).
    * **Environment:** Growing [almonds](/en/food/almonds/) uses enormous amounts of water (mainly in California).

### 3. [Oat](/en/food/oatmeal/) [Milk](/en/food/milk/)

The new trend, which wins the taste and foam test.

* **Composition:** Made from [oats](/en/food/oatmeal/) and water, rich in carbohydrates and soluble fiber (beta-glucan).
* **Benefits:**
    * **Texture and taste:** Thick, creamy and naturally slightly sweet. The big winner in coffee, with perfect frothing ("barista style").
    * **Environment:** Considered one of the most environmentally friendly (it requires little water and growing area).
    * **Beta-glucan:** Fiber that helps lower cholesterol (although the amount in the drink is lower than in porridge).
* **Downsides:**
    * **Carbohydrates and sugar:** Rich in carbohydrates and has a higher glycemic index than the other alternatives. It sometimes contains sugar created during the enzymatic processing.
    * **Oil:** To achieve the creamy texture, many manufacturers add oil (usually sunflower or canola oil).

### Summary and conclusions

If you're looking for a full **nutritional substitute** for cow's [milk](/en/food/milk/) (especially for children and vegans), **[soy milk](/en/food/soy-milk/)** is the only choice that provides high-quality protein. If the goal is **weight loss**, (unsweetened) **[almond milk](/en/food/almond-milk/)** will save you calories. For the most indulgent coffee and for anyone sensitive to soy's aftertaste, **[oat](/en/food/oatmeal/) [milk](/en/food/milk/)** is the new king of coffee shops, but watch the amount of carbohydrates and oil.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Which plant milk is the healthiest?**
Answer: It depends on the goal, but if you're looking for a true nutritional substitute for cow's milk — soy milk. It's the only one that provides a similar protein profile (about 7 grams per cup compared with 8 in cow's milk) of complete quality. Almond milk is the lowest in calories, and oat milk is the most coffee-friendly but the richest in carbohydrates.

**Question: Which plant milk is the lowest in calories?**
Answer: Unsweetened almond milk — about 30-40 calories per cup, compared with about 80-100 in soy milk and about 120 in oat milk. The reason is simple: it's mostly water with a small percentage of almonds, so it's also very low in protein (about one gram per cup).

**Question: Which plant milk is best for coffee?**
Answer: Oat milk, especially the "barista" versions. Its fat content and structure allow stable frothing, and it's less likely to curdle on contact with coffee's acidity. Soy milk froths well but can separate in an acidic espresso, and almond milk froths the worst.

**Question: How much protein is in plant milk?**
Answer: There are big gaps. Soy milk — about 7 grams per cup, close to cow's milk. Oat milk — about 2-3 grams. Almond milk — only about one gram. Anyone relying on plant milk as a protein source should choose soy.

**Question: Does plant milk contain calcium?**
Answer: Only if it's fortified, and this is a critical point. Naturally, plant milks are very low in calcium. Fortified versions contain 120-300 mg per cup — similar to cow's milk. It's important to shake the carton before pouring, since the added calcium settles to the bottom.

**Question: Which plant milk is the most environmentally friendly?**
Answer: Oat milk, thanks to its relatively low water and land use. Almond milk is the most problematic in terms of water — growing almonds uses a lot of water, and most of it is concentrated in drought-stricken California. All plant milks still pollute less than cow's milk.

**Question: Does soy milk affect hormones?**
Answer: The phytoestrogens in soy (isoflavones) bind to estrogen receptors much more weakly than estrogen itself. Meta-analyses in men found no effect on testosterone or fertility at normal dietary amounts, and in women after breast cancer the evidence doesn't point to harm either.
