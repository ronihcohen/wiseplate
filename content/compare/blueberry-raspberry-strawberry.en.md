## A Scientific Comparison: Blueberries, Raspberries and Strawberries

**For fiber and the least sugar, pick raspberries: about 6.5 g of fiber and only about 4.4 g of sugar per 100 g. For vitamin C, pick strawberries: one cup covers the whole daily requirement. For anthocyanin antioxidants and the research on memory, pick blueberries. All three are low in calories, and a mix beats choosing just one.**

Berries are among the lowest-sugar fruits, and they are rich in pigments from the polyphenol family that give them their color. In a follow-up study of about 93,000 young women, a high intake of anthocyanins, the blue-red pigment in which blueberries and strawberries lead, was linked to about a 32% lower risk of heart attack. [Cassidy et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23319811/). It is an observational study, which shows an association rather than proof, but it is one of the best in the field. In Hebrew they are "אוכמניות", "פטל" and "תות שדה".

### Blueberries: the brain research

[Blueberries](/en/food/blueberries/) get their deep blue color from anthocyanins, and of the three fruits they are the richest in them.

**Benefits:**
* **Memory in older age:** a meta-analysis of nine controlled trials (513 participants) found that regular blueberry intake improved episodic memory in older adults with mild cognitive decline, but not processing speed or working memory. The effect is modest, and the authors call for larger trials. [da Silva et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40856863/).
* **Heart and blood vessels:** anthocyanins are linked in population studies to a lower risk of heart attack.
* **Convenience:** frozen blueberries are available all year, are considerably cheaper and are nearly identical nutritionally.

**Downsides:**
* **The sweetest:** about 10 g of sugar per 100 g, twice as much as raspberries and strawberries. Still little compared with most fruit.
* **Price:** fresh blueberries are expensive, especially out of season.
* **Less vitamin C and fiber** than the other two.

### Raspberries: the fiber champion

A [raspberry](/en/food/raspberry/) is an aggregate of dozens of tiny "drupelets", each with its own seed, which is why it is so rich in fiber.

**Benefits:**
* **The most fiber:** about 8 g per cup, nearly a third of the daily requirement; very few fruits come close.
* **The least sugar:** about 5 g per cup, an unusual fiber-to-sugar ratio that suits low-carb diets and anyone managing blood sugar.
* **Ellagitannins:** polyphenols studied mostly in the lab and in animals. There is no clinical evidence yet that they prevent cancer in humans, so it's important not to overstate them.

**Downsides:**
* **Spoils fast:** fresh raspberries keep a day or two in the fridge before they go moldy. Frozen is better for most uses.
* **Price:** fresh raspberries are usually the most expensive of the three.
* **Seeds:** the tiny seeds bother some people; in sauces and purées you can strain them out.

### Strawberries: the most vitamin C

[Strawberries](/en/food/strawberry/) are the most popular berry in Israel, where their local season is winter and spring.

**Benefits:**
* **Vitamin C:** a cup of strawberries provides about 89 mg of vitamin C, nearly the full recommended intake for men (90 mg) and more than that for women (75 mg). Vitamin C is needed to make [collagen](/en/food/collagen/) and boosts the absorption of plant iron. [Vitamin C — NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/).
* **The fewest calories:** about 32 kcal per 100 g, and a whole cup is under 50 kcal.
* **Anthocyanins:** fewer than blueberries, but strawberries and blueberries together were a main source of anthocyanins in the heart-attack study in women.

**Downsides:**
* **Pesticide residues:** strawberries often top lists of fruit with pesticide residues. Rinsing removes part of them, and if you prefer, you can choose organic.
* **Allergy:** strawberries are a relatively common cause of skin reactions, mainly in children.
* **Seasonal:** fresh strawberries out of season are less tasty and more expensive.

A popular claim that strawberries "whiten teeth" thanks to malic acid is not supported by research, and we have removed it.

### Which berry should you choose?

* **For fullness, constipation and blood-sugar control:** raspberries, thanks to about 8 g of fiber per cup.
* **For vitamin C and iron absorption:** strawberries, for example alongside a meal of legumes or leafy greens.
* **For memory and brain health in older age:** blueberries, a cup a day, as tested in trials.
* **For weight loss:** strawberries (the fewest calories) or raspberries (the most filling).
* **For a low-carb or keto diet:** raspberries, then strawberries.
* **On a budget:** frozen blueberries or raspberries.
* **For children:** strawberries, usually the favorite; cut them up for toddlers to prevent choking.

### Summary

All three berries are a good choice: low in calories, low in sugar compared with other fruit, and rich in polyphenols. Raspberries lead on fiber and low sugar, strawberries on vitamin C and low calories, and blueberries on anthocyanins and memory research. A daily cup of a mix of all three, fresh or frozen, covers the benefits of each.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Which berry is the healthiest?**
Answer: There is no single winner, and that's the point. Blueberries lead on anthocyanins and on research into memory and cognitive function; raspberries lead on fiber by a wide margin (about 8 g per cup) and have the lowest sugar; strawberries lead on vitamin C, with one cup supplying nearly the full daily intake. Combining all three beats choosing one.

**Question: Which berry is lowest in sugar?**
Answer: Raspberries. They contain about 5 g of sugar per cup alongside about 8 g of fiber, an unusual fiber-to-sugar ratio that makes them the best fit for a carb-restricted diet. Strawberries come next, and blueberries are the sweetest of the three.

**Question: What are anthocyanins and why do they matter?**
Answer: Pigments from the flavonoid family that give berries their blue, purple and red colors. In population studies a high intake is linked to a lower risk of heart attack, and in small trials to a modest improvement in memory in older adults. The deeper the color, the higher their concentration, hence the blueberry's advantage.

**Question: Are frozen berries as good as fresh?**
Answer: Yes, and sometimes better. They are picked at peak ripeness and frozen within hours, while fresh berries are often picked before full ripeness for transport. Freezing preserves anthocyanins well, and some vitamin C is lost, but the gap is smaller than the one created by storing fresh fruit for a long time.

**Question: How many berries should I eat a day?**
Answer: One cup (about 150 g) a day is the amount tested in most studies, and it provides only 50–85 kcal. There's no reason to limit beyond that, except for keeping an eye on total sugar if you track it.

**Question: Should berries be washed, and how?**
Answer: Yes, but only just before eating, because wet berries go moldy quickly. A short rinse under running water is enough. Berries, especially strawberries, often appear on lists of pesticide residues, so this is one category where organic growing matters more than for most fruit.
