Leafy greens are the foundation of any healthy diet, rich in vitamins, minerals and phytochemicals. But when it comes to choosing a leaf for a smoothie, a salad or a savory pie – which should you pick?

### 1. [Spinach](/en/food/spinach/)

The familiar, beloved leaf (thanks, Popeye), soft and easy to work into dishes.

* **Composition:** Rich in [vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/), [vitamin A](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-a/), [folic acid](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b9/) and [iron](/en/food/iron/).
* **Benefits:**
    * **Mild flavor:** It blends easily into smoothies and omelets without a strong aftertaste.
    * **Low in calories:** A very high density of nutrients relative to almost zero calories.
    * **Availability:** Easy to get fresh or frozen in any season.
* **Downsides:**
    * **Oxalates:** It contains a high amount of oxalic acid, which interferes with [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) absorption and may cause kidney stones in sensitive people (cooking reduces the amount).

### 2. [Kale](/en/food/kale/)

The trendy star of the last decade, the "king of fiber."

* **Composition:** Rich in [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) (more than [spinach](/en/food/spinach/)), an astronomical amount of [vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/), and especially bioavailable [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/).
* **Benefits:**
    * **[Calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) absorption:** Low in oxalates, which allows its [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) to be absorbed very efficiently in the body.
    * **From the cruciferous family:** It contains sulfur compounds (sulforaphane) known for their anti-cancer potential.
    * **Texture:** Very sturdy, keeps in the fridge for a long time and is suitable for oven-baked "[kale](/en/food/kale/) chips."
* **Downsides:**
    * **Digestion:** The tough fiber may cause stomach discomfort for those who aren't used to it (it's recommended to massage the leaves with oil/[lemon](/en/food/lemon/) before eating them raw).
    * **Taste:** A slight bitterness that not everyone likes.

### 3. Swiss Chard

Leaves from the [beet](/en/food/beets/) family, combining the texture of [spinach](/en/food/spinach/) with the meatiness of [kale](/en/food/kale/).

* **Composition:** Unique for its content of syringic acid, which helps balance blood sugar.
* **Benefits:**
    * **Two vegetables in one:** The crunchy stalk gives a texture like [celery](/en/food/celery/), and the green leaf is soft like [spinach](/en/food/spinach/).
    * **A range of colors:** Rainbow chard contains additional antioxidant pigments in its stalks (red, yellow).
    * **[Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/):** An excellent source of [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/), essential for muscles and nerves.
* **Downsides:**
    * **Oxalates:** Like [spinach](/en/food/spinach/), chard is also rich in oxalates and should be cooked lightly to reduce them.
    * **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/):** It contains slightly more natural [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) than the other greens (still negligible, but it gives a subtle salty taste).

### Nutritional comparison table (per 100 grams of raw leaves)

| Component | [Spinach](/en/food/spinach/) | [Kale](/en/food/kale/) | Chard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **[Vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/)** | ~480% of daily intake | ~600% of daily intake | ~300% of daily intake |
| **[Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/)** | 28 mg | 120 mg (winner!) | 30 mg |
| **[Calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) bioavailability** | Low (because of oxalates) | High and excellent | Medium-low |
| **Taste and use** | Neutral, smoothies and cooking | Bitter, salads and baking | Rich, stews and stuffed dishes |

### Summary and conclusions

If you're looking for an **excellent vegan [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) source**, **[kale](/en/food/kale/)** is the winner thanks to its efficient absorption. For adding **to green smoothies** without ruining the taste, **[spinach](/en/food/spinach/)** is the classic choice. For stews and stuffed dishes that need **body and texture**, **chard** gives the best return. The healthiest recommendation? Vary between them over the course of the week.
