## A Scientific Comparison: Milk, Dark and White Chocolate

**If you eat chocolate with health in mind, pick dark chocolate with 70% cocoa or more: half the sugar of milk chocolate, five times the iron, and flavanols linked to a small drop in blood pressure. Milk and white chocolate are simply sweets; white is the only one without caffeine, so it suits evenings or people sensitive to caffeine better.**

The difference between the three is set by the amount of cocoa solids, the dark part of the cocoa bean that holds the flavanols, minerals and caffeine. Dark chocolate (in Hebrew "שוקולד מריר") is made mostly of cocoa mass, milk chocolate contains less cocoa and more sugar and [milk](/en/food/milk/) powder, and white chocolate is made only from cocoa butter, the fat of the bean, without the dark solids.

### Dark chocolate

[Dark chocolate](/en/food/dark-chocolate/) contains at least 50% cocoa solids, and the "health" versions usually start at 70%. The higher the cocoa percentage, the less sugar and the more bitterness, minerals and caffeine.

**Benefits:**
* **Blood pressure:** a Cochrane review of 35 trials found that flavanol-rich cocoa products lower blood pressure by about 1.8 mmHg on average, and by about 4 mmHg in people with hypertension. A real but small effect. [Cochrane review, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28439881/).
* **What the large trial says:** in the COSMOS trial, about 21,000 older adults took a cocoa-flavanol extract (not chocolate) or placebo. Total cardiovascular events did not fall significantly, but cardiovascular death fell by about 27%. [Sesso et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35294962/). Note that this was an extract, and it does not justify eating a lot of chocolate.
* **Minerals and fiber:** about 11.9 mg of iron and 228 mg of magnesium per 100 g, plus copper and manganese. A 20 g serving gives about 2.4 mg of iron, roughly 13% of a woman's requirement, although plant iron is less well absorbed.
* **Fullness:** the strong, bitter taste makes it easier for many people to stop after a few squares.

**Downsides:**
* **Heavy metals:** testing of 72 dark chocolate and cocoa products in the US found that 43% exceeded California's strict threshold for lead and 35% the threshold for cadmium, though most were below FDA limits. One serving a day is not a concern for most people, but several servings a day or problem brands add up. [Hands et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39144282/).
* **Caffeine and theobromine:** about 80 mg of caffeine per 100 g. 20–30 g in the evening can disturb sleep in sensitive people.
* **Calories and saturated fat:** the highest of the three per 100 g. Much of the saturated fat is stearic acid, which has less effect on cholesterol.
* **Heartburn:** in some people chocolate triggers or worsens heartburn.

### Milk chocolate

[Milk chocolate](/en/food/milk-chocolate/) usually contains 25–40% cocoa, with the rest made up of sugar, milk powder and cocoa butter.

**Benefits:**
* **Taste and texture:** sweet, creamy and melting, and easy for most people to enjoy.
* **Calcium:** about 190 mg per 100 g thanks to the milk powder. In a 20 g serving that's about 40 mg, a small contribution.
* **Less caffeine:** about 20 mg per 100 g, a quarter of dark chocolate.

**Downsides:**
* **Sugar:** about half the weight of the bar. 50 g of milk chocolate supplies about 26 g of sugar, almost the whole recommended daily allowance of free sugar.
* **Few flavanols:** with little cocoa and heavier processing, there's no reason to expect the benefits of dark chocolate.
* **Easy to overeat:** the sweet taste is less satisfying, and a row of squares quickly turns into a bar.

### White chocolate

White chocolate is made from cocoa butter, sugar and milk powder. Without cocoa solids, it has none of cocoa's color, bitter taste or flavanols.

**Benefits:**
* **No caffeine or theobromine:** suitable in the evening, for children in small amounts, and for people sensitive to caffeine.
* **Texture:** the creamiest and smoothest, handy for desserts and coatings.
* **Calcium:** about 200 mg per 100 g, from the milk.

**Downsides:**
* **The most sugar:** about 59 g per 100 g.
* **None of cocoa's benefits:** no flavanols, hardly any iron or magnesium.
* **Saturated fat:** about 19 g per 100 g, without the fiber that partly balances dark chocolate.

### Which chocolate should you choose?

* **For heart health and blood pressure:** 70–85% dark, about 10–20 g a day. The effect is small, so treat it as an addition to a good diet, not a treatment.
* **For weight management:** dark, in a measured amount. The bitterness makes it easier to stop after a few squares.
* **If you're sensitive to caffeine or close to bedtime:** white, or a little milk chocolate. Keep dark for the morning or afternoon.
* **In pregnancy:** all types are fine in small amounts. Dark chocolate counts toward the daily caffeine limit (up to 200 mg).
* **For children:** a small amount of any type as a treat; better not to turn dark chocolate into a "health food" for a child.
* **If you're worried about heavy metals:** vary brands, stay at one serving a day, and prefer brands that publish test results.
* **For dog owners:** all chocolate, dark chocolate especially, is toxic to dogs because of its theobromine.

### Summary

Dark chocolate is the only type with some health advantage to its name: less sugar, more fiber and minerals, and flavanols that lower blood pressure slightly. It is also calorie-dense, contains caffeine and sometimes heavy metals, so it belongs in small portions. Milk and white chocolate are treats for enjoyment, and white is caffeine-free. A 10–20 g serving of dark chocolate with your [coffee](/en/food/coffee/) is a good way to enjoy it.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: From what cocoa percentage is chocolate considered healthy?**
Answer: Most studies used flavanol-rich products, which on most shelves means dark chocolate of 70% or more. The cocoa percentage doesn't guarantee the flavanol content, because heavy processing (such as the "alkalization" of Dutch-process cocoa) reduces it.

**Question: Is white chocolate really chocolate?**
Answer: It is made from cocoa butter, so regulations define it as chocolate, but it contains no cocoa solids. That's why it has no flavanols, no caffeine and none of cocoa's bitter taste.

**Question: How much caffeine is in dark chocolate?**
Answer: About 80 mg per 100 g in 70–85% chocolate, or about 16 mg in a 20 g serving, less than a cup of tea. Milk chocolate has about 20 mg per 100 g, and white chocolate has none.

**Question: Is there heavy metal in dark chocolate?**
Answer: Yes, cocoa takes up lead and cadmium from the soil and the environment. In testing of 72 products in the US, most were below FDA limits, but a substantial share exceeded stricter thresholds. One serving a day and varying brands reduce exposure.

**Question: How much dark chocolate can I eat a day?**
Answer: 10–20 g a day, about two to four squares, is a reasonable amount. It provides about 60–120 kcal, and there is no evidence that more adds benefit.

**Question: Does chocolate make you gain weight?**
Answer: All types have about 535–600 kcal per 100 g, so the amount is what counts. Dark chocolate tends to be more filling because of its taste and fiber, which makes it easier to stop.
