## A Scientific Comparison: Brazil Nuts, Walnuts and Almonds

**For healthy fats and heart health, pick walnuts, the richest nut source of plant omega-3 (ALA). For an everyday snack with the most protein, fiber and vitamin E, pick almonds. Brazil nuts are not a handful-a-day snack: one or two cover your selenium needs, and eating more on a regular basis can push you past the safe upper limit.**

The three nuts are similar in calories, about 580–660 kcal per 100 g, and all of them are linked with better health when they replace processed snacks. The differences lie in one standout nutrient per nut and, in one case, in how much you should eat. In Hebrew they are "אגוזי ברזיל", "אגוזי מלך" and "שקדים".

### Brazil nuts: the strongest food source of selenium

Brazil nuts grow on tall trees in the Amazon rainforest, and most of the crop is gathered from wild trees. Their defining feature is one mineral: [selenium](/en/food/supplements/selenium/), which is part of antioxidant enzymes and of the production of thyroid hormones.

**Benefits:**
* **An unusual amount of selenium:** according to the NIH, 30 g of Brazil nuts (6–8 nuts) supply about 544 µg of selenium, several times the adult daily requirement of 55 µg. One average nut covers the daily need. [Selenium — NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/).
* **Magnesium:** about 376 mg per 100 g, although at the recommended serving of one or two nuts the contribution is modest.

**Downsides:**
* **Too much selenium:** the safe upper limit for adults is 400 µg a day from all sources. A regular handful of Brazil nuts exceeds it easily, and long-term excess is linked with hair loss, brittle nails, garlic breath and digestive symptoms. [Selenium — NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/).
* **Unpredictable content:** the amount of selenium depends on the soil the tree grew in, so individual nuts can differ a great deal. That is another reason not to treat them as a precise "supplement".
* **Saturated fat:** the highest of the three, about a quarter of the fat.

The popular claim that Brazil nuts "raise testosterone and fertility" is not supported by controlled studies, so we have removed it from this page.

### Walnuts: the omega-3 nut

The resemblance to a brain is a coincidence, but the fat profile of [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/) really is different: most of the fat is polyunsaturated, and about 9 g per 100 g is alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), the plant form of [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/).

**Benefits:**
* **Blood lipids:** a meta-analysis of 26 controlled trials (1,059 participants) found that walnut-enriched diets lowered LDL cholesterol by about 5.5 mg/dL and also lowered triglycerides compared with control diets, without weight gain. [Guasch-Ferré et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29931130/).
* **The Mediterranean diet:** in the PREDIMED trial, a Mediterranean diet supplemented with 30 g of mixed nuts a day (half of them walnuts) reduced cardiovascular events compared with a low-fat diet. [Estruch et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29897866/).
* **ALA for vegans:** a 30 g handful supplies about 2.5 g of ALA, more than the recommended daily intake. Keep in mind that the body converts only a small share of ALA into EPA and DHA.

**Downsides:**
* **Oxidation:** the polyunsaturated fats oxidize easily, and the nuts turn bitter and rancid. Store them in the fridge or freezer.
* **Calories:** as with all nuts, a large handful quickly adds up to 300–400 kcal.

### Almonds: the balanced daily snack

[Almonds](/en/food/almonds/) lead on three counts: protein, fiber and [vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/), and their calcium is high for a nut.

**Benefits:**
* **Vitamin E:** 30 g of dry-roasted almonds provide about 6.8 mg of vitamin E, roughly half of the adult recommended intake of 15 mg. [Vitamin E — NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminE-HealthProfessional/).
* **Protein and fiber:** about 6 g of protein and 3.5 g of fiber per handful, the combination that makes them an especially filling snack.
* **Blood sugar:** thanks to their fiber, fat and protein, almonds barely raise blood sugar, and eaten with a starchy meal they slow the rise.

**Downsides:**
* **Phytic acid and oxalates:** the phytic acid in the skin slightly reduces mineral absorption, and almonds are relatively high in oxalates, a consideration for people prone to calcium-oxalate kidney stones. Soaking removes only part of the phytic acid.
* **Allergy:** as with all tree nuts, almond allergy can be severe.

### Which nut should you choose?

* **For heart health and blood lipids:** walnuts, followed by almonds. Both have been tested in trials, and the biggest effect comes when nuts replace snacks, processed meat or pastries.
* **For weight management:** almonds, with the fewest calories and the most protein and fiber. Either way, a measured 30 g portion rather than an open bag.
* **For selenium and the thyroid:** one Brazil nut a day, or a few times a week. If you take a selenium supplement or a multivitamin with selenium, you don't need regular Brazil nuts on top.
* **For vegans:** walnuts for ALA, almonds for calcium and vitamin E.
* **For baking and cooking:** almond flour and almond butter are the most versatile; walnuts suit salads and savory bakes.
* **For long storage:** almonds keep longer outside the fridge; walnuts and Brazil nuts need cooling.

### Summary

There is no single "healthiest nut", only the right nut for the goal. Walnuts are the choice for healthy fats and blood lipids, almonds are the most balanced everyday snack, and Brazil nuts are a selenium source that works only in small portions. A mix of almonds and walnuts in a portion of about 30 g a day, plus the occasional Brazil nut, gives you the benefits of all three without going over the selenium limit.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many Brazil nuts can I eat a day?**
Answer: One or two. An average nut contains about 70–90 µg of selenium, and the safe upper limit for adults is 400 µg a day from all sources. A regular handful of 6–8 nuts supplies about 544 µg and exceeds the limit, so Brazil nuts are not suited to snacking in large amounts every day.

**Question: Which nut is best for the heart?**
Answer: Walnuts are the most studied: a meta-analysis of 26 trials showed lower LDL and triglycerides without weight gain. Almonds show a similar benefit for blood lipids. The difference between them is smaller than the difference between eating nuts and eating processed snacks.

**Question: Which nut has the most protein?**
Answer: Almonds, about 21 g per 100 g and 6 g per 30 g handful. Walnuts and Brazil nuts provide about 4–4.5 g per handful. Peanuts (a legume, not a nut) contain more than any of them.

**Question: Do nuts make you gain weight?**
Answer: They are calorie-dense, but in controlled trials adding nuts to the diet did not cause weight gain, partly because they are filling and part of their fat is not absorbed. The key is a portion of about 30 g that replaces another snack.

**Question: Do almonds need to be soaked?**
Answer: No. Soaking softens almonds and slightly lowers their phytic acid, but the effect on mineral absorption in a varied diet is small. If soaking helps you digest them or you prefer the texture, that is a good enough reason.

**Question: How do I keep walnuts from going rancid?**
Answer: In a sealed container in the fridge for up to a few months, or in the freezer for up to a year. Their polyunsaturated fat oxidizes with heat and light, and a bitter taste or a paint-like smell means the nuts have oxidized.
