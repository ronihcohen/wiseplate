## A Scientific Comparison: Chia, Flax and Hemp Seeds

**For protein, pick hulled hemp seeds: about 32 g per 100 g, nearly twice as much as chia or flax. For plant omega-3 at the lowest price, pick flaxseed, but only ground. For fiber, fullness and pudding, pick chia, which you can eat whole.**

All three are sold as "superfoods", but it is worth dialing the halo down: they are good food, not medicine. In a controlled trial in which overweight adults ate 50 g of chia a day for 12 weeks, there was no change in weight, inflammation or blood lipids. [Nieman et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19628108/). The differences between them are practical: how much protein, how much omega-3, and whether you need to grind them. In Hebrew they are "זרעי צ'יה", "זרעי פשתן" and "זרעי המפ".

### Chia seeds: fiber, gel and fullness

[Chia seeds](/en/food/chia-seeds/) come from a plant of the mint family native to Mexico and Central America.

**Benefits:**
* **The most fiber:** about 34 g per 100 g, and about 10 g in two tablespoons, roughly a third of the daily requirement. Much of the fiber forms a gel on contact with liquid, which is what makes chia pudding possible and slows stomach emptying.
* **No need to grind:** the seed coat is soft enough, and eating whole seeds does raise blood ALA. In the trial mentioned above, 50 g a day raised plasma ALA by about 24%. [Nieman et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19628108/).
* **Calcium:** about 630 mg per 100 g, more than [milk](/en/food/milk/) by weight. But a tablespoon of chia provides about 60 mg, so it is an addition, not a substitute for main calcium sources.

**Downsides:**
* **Drink enough:** a spoonful of dry chia swallowed with little water can swell in the esophagus. Eat it soaked or inside moist food, especially if you have trouble swallowing.
* **No proof of weight loss:** the controlled trial found no effect on weight.
* **Price:** more expensive than flaxseed.

### Flaxseed: omega-3 and lignans, as long as it's ground

[Flaxseed](/en/food/flax-seeds/) is the richest plant source of omega-3 among the common seeds, and also the richest in lignans, polyphenolic compounds that the body converts into enterolignans.

**Benefits:**
* **Blood pressure:** in a controlled trial in patients with peripheral artery disease, 30 g of ground flaxseed a day for six months lowered systolic blood pressure by about 10 mmHg and diastolic by about 7 compared with placebo. [Rodriguez-Leyva et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24126178/).
* **The most ALA:** a tablespoon of whole flaxseed contains about 2.3 g of ALA, above the recommended daily intake. [Omega-3 — NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/).
* **Cheap and easy to find:** usually the least expensive of the three.

**Downsides:**
* **Must be ground:** in a trial comparing different forms, ground flaxseed and flaxseed oil raised blood ALA, but whole seeds did not raise it significantly, because many pass through the gut without being broken open. [Austria et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18689552/).
* **Oxidation:** ground flaxseed oxidizes quickly. Grind a week's worth and keep it in the fridge or freezer.
* **Digestion:** in large portions, especially at first, it can cause gas. Increase gradually and drink enough.

### Hemp seeds: the plant protein

[Hemp seeds](/en/food/hemp-seeds/) come from industrial hemp, and these varieties contain almost no THC. They are usually sold hulled ("hemp hearts"), soft and pleasant to eat.

**Benefits:**
* **The most protein:** about 32 g per 100 g, and about 10 g in three tablespoons. The protein contains all the essential amino acids, though lysine is relatively low, so it pairs well with legumes.
* **Minerals:** very rich in [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) and zinc. Three tablespoons provide about 210 mg of magnesium, roughly half the daily requirement.
* **Convenience:** no grinding, soaking or cooking. Sprinkle on salad, yogurt or porridge.

**Downsides:**
* **Little fiber:** hulled seeds have almost no fiber, because most of it is in the hull.
* **More omega-6 than omega-3:** the ratio is roughly 3:1. That isn't harmful, but as an omega-3 source they are weaker than flax and chia.
* **Price and calories:** the most expensive of the three, and the most calorie-dense per 100 g.

### Which seed should you choose?

* **For extra protein on a vegan diet or for athletes:** hulled hemp. Three tablespoons on a salad or in a shake add about 10 g of protein.
* **For plant omega-3:** ground flaxseed, or chia. Remember that the body converts only a small share of ALA into EPA and DHA, so people who don't eat fish may consider an algae-based [omega-3 supplement](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/).
* **For blood pressure:** ground flaxseed, about 2–3 tablespoons a day, alongside medical treatment and not instead of it.
* **For constipation and fullness:** chia or flax, thanks to the fiber, with enough to drink.
* **On a budget:** flaxseed.
* **For children and texture-averse eaters:** hulled hemp or ground flax that disappear into food; chia in a pudding.
* **If you have trouble swallowing:** avoid dry chia.

### Summary

All three seeds add good fat, minerals, and either fiber or protein, but none of them is a miracle food. Hemp leads on protein and magnesium, flax leads on omega-3, on blood-pressure evidence and on price, and chia leads on fiber and calcium and is easy to use. The one rule that matters: eat flaxseed ground. One to three tablespoons a day of any of them is a sensible amount.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Which is better, chia or flaxseed?**
Answer: For omega-3, blood pressure and price, ground flaxseed. For fiber, calcium and convenience, chia, because it doesn't need grinding. The difference between them is small, and you can combine both.

**Question: Do flaxseeds have to be ground?**
Answer: Yes. In a study comparing different forms, only ground flaxseed and flaxseed oil raised blood omega-3 levels; whole seeds mostly pass through undigested. Grind them in a coffee grinder and keep them in the fridge.

**Question: Do chia seeds need to be ground or soaked?**
Answer: Grinding isn't necessary. It is a good idea to soak them or eat them in moist food, because dry chia absorbs liquid and swells. Soaking for 10–20 minutes is enough.

**Question: How much protein is in hemp seeds?**
Answer: About 32 g per 100 g of hulled seeds, or about 10 g in three tablespoons (30 g). That is almost twice as much as chia or flax for the same amount.

**Question: Do hemp seeds contain THC?**
Answer: Industrial hemp seeds contain almost no THC, and the tiny traces sometimes found on the hull have no effect. People who are drug-tested regularly (athletes, for example) can choose a brand that states it tests its product for THC.

**Question: How many seeds can I eat a day?**
Answer: One to three tablespoons a day (10–30 g) is a common amount. Beyond that the calories add up, and the fiber in chia and flax can cause bloating if you don't increase gradually and drink enough.
