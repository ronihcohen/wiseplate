## Running with the Apple Watch and Advanced Navigation

**You can navigate a trail run with an Apple Watch without pulling out your phone: plan a route on a free site such as gpx.studio, save it as a GPX file, and load it into an app such as WorkOutDoors, which shows an offline map and alerts you when you go off course.** The setup suits runs of up to several hours; for long ultra runs, battery life and the small screen are the main limits. In Hebrew this is "ריצה עם Apple Watch וניווט".

The real value of the watch is not the amount of data but what you do with it — managing intensity by heart rate is covered on the [heart rate zones](/en/run/running-zones/) page.

### The three steps

1. **Planning — [gpx.studio](https://gpx.studio/):** a free, browser-based site for drawing a route on a map. You can snap to trails, see an elevation profile and cumulative distance, and export a GPX file — an open, XML-based format that holds waypoints, the track and elevation data.
2. **Navigation — [WorkOutDoors](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/workoutdoors/id1241909999):** a paid watch app that shows vector maps, supports downloading maps for use without signal, loads GPX files and vibrates when you stray from the route. You send the file from the iPhone via the Files app or the share sheet.
3. **Monitoring — the watch's sensors:** an optical heart rate sensor, an accelerometer for cadence, and a GPS receiver. On the Apple Watch Ultra models the GPS is dual-frequency (L1 and L5), which improves accuracy among tall buildings and under tree cover.

Apple's own Maps app has added offline maps and custom routes in recent versions, and for simple routes it may be enough. WorkOutDoors is more flexible in screen layout, map styles and loading external files.

### Benefits

* **Navigation without a phone in hand:** a quick glance at the watch instead of stopping, pulling out the phone and opening a map.
* **A known route in advance:** the elevation profile lets you plan your effort — easing off before a long climb, not after it.
* **Works without signal:** maps downloaded in advance work off-grid.
* **Off-route alerts:** a vibration when you leave the route saves wrong turns at trail junctions.
* **Real-time heart rate:** in a comparison study, the Apple Watch was the most accurate of the wrist devices tested, though a chest strap was more accurate still. [Gillinov 2017, Med Sci Sports Exerc](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28709155/)
* **Backtrack:** on recent watches, Apple's Compass app can trace the path you took and guide you back to your starting point.

### Downsides and limitations

* **Battery:** continuous GPS, an always-on screen and maps use a lot of power. A standard Apple Watch is usually enough for a run of a few hours; for ultra events, an Ultra or a dedicated sports watch (Garmin, Coros, Suunto) is a better fit.
* **Heart rate accuracy:** an optical sensor responds more slowly to sharp changes and is affected by strap tightness, movement, tattoos and cold. For intervals, use a chest strap.
* **Small screen:** a detailed map on a 41–49 mm screen is hard to read on the move, especially for people with age-related long-sightedness.
* **Complexity:** three tools (website, app, watch) mean three possible points of failure. Check the route on the watch before you set off.
* **GPS in difficult terrain:** in narrow canyons and deep gullies, even dual-frequency GPS can drift.
* **Cost:** WorkOutDoors is paid, and the Ultra costs considerably more than the standard model.

### Practical use: a pre-trail-run checklist

1. **Plan and check:** draw the route, check distance and total climb, and export the GPX.
2. **Download maps:** download the running area for offline use while the watch is charging and on Wi-Fi.
3. **Load and check on the watch:** open the route on the watch before leaving and make sure it displays.
4. **Full charge:** head out with a charged watch. On a long run, lower the brightness or turn off the always-on display.
5. **Backup:** your phone in your pack with the same route, and a power bank.
6. **Safety:** tell someone your route and expected return time. In Israel, stay out of dry riverbeds (wadis) during flash-flood warnings.
7. **Water and salts:** on a long run in the heat, plan water points and [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) replacement.

### Summary

Combining gpx.studio for planning, WorkOutDoors or Apple Maps for navigation, and the Apple Watch for monitoring lets you run unfamiliar terrain without stopping to check your phone. The main limits are battery life, the small screen and heart rate accuracy during intervals. The watch measures — but you make the decisions. ### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How do I transfer a GPX file to the Apple Watch?**
Answer: Save the file on your iPhone (for example from gpx.studio), open it via the share sheet or the Files app in a navigation app such as WorkOutDoors, and the app syncs it to the watch.

**Question: Can I navigate with an Apple Watch without signal?**
Answer: Yes, if you download the maps in advance. WorkOutDoors supports offline maps, and Apple Maps has added this option in recent versions too.

**Question: How long does the battery last on a navigated run?**
Answer: It depends on the model, brightness and settings. A standard Apple Watch is usually good for a few hours; for ultra runs an Ultra or a dedicated sports watch is better, and it is worth testing on a training run.

**Question: Is the Apple Watch heart rate accurate?**
Answer: On steady runs it is fairly good, and in a comparison study it was the most accurate of the wrist devices tested. For intervals and sharp changes, a chest strap is more accurate.

**Question: Do I need an Apple Watch Ultra for trail running?**
Answer: Not necessarily. The Ultra's advantages are a longer battery, dual-frequency GPS and a larger, brighter screen. For runs of an hour or two, a standard model is enough.

**Question: Is gpx.studio free?**
Answer: Yes. It is a free site that runs in the browser and lets you draw a route, see an elevation profile and export a GPX file.
