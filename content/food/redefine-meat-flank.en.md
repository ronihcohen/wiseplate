## Redefine Meat Plant-Based Flank Steak

**Redefine Meat's plant-based flank is a beef substitute made from wheat, soy and potato proteins that, according to the product data, provides about 25 grams of protein per 100 grams, 3 grams of fiber, 1.5 grams of saturated fat and zero cholesterol. It is a good alternative to red meat in terms of saturated fat, but it is an ultra-processed product with gluten, soy and 300 mg of sodium per 100 grams.**

Redefine Meat (in Hebrew "רידיפיין מיט") is an Israeli company that developed a technology it calls **New Meat**: producing plant-based "meat" in layers, inspired by 3D printing, to imitate not only the taste but also the fibrous structure of a beef cut. The flank is one of the cuts in its range and is meant to be seared in a pan or on a grill like a beef steak.

**Main ingredients:** wheat, soy and potato proteins; canola oil; maltodextrin, modified starches and flavorings. **Allergens:** [gluten](/en/food/gluten/) (wheat and [barley](/en/food/barley/)) and soy.

A 150-gram serving would provide, based on these figures, about 37 grams of protein, about 2 grams of saturated fat and about 450 mg of sodium.

### Benefits

* **High protein:** 25 grams per 100 grams — similar to many beef cuts. Combining wheat, soy and potato proteins lets their amino acids complement one another.
* **Little saturated fat and no cholesterol:** 1.5 grams of saturated fat per 100 grams, far less than a fatty beef cut, with the fat coming mainly from canola oil. In a US crossover trial (SWAP-MEAT), adults who replaced animal meat with plant-based meat substitutes for eight weeks, while keeping the rest of their diet similar, had LDL about 10 mg/dL lower, lower levels of TMAO (a marker linked to heart disease) and weighed about a kilogram less. [Crimarco et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32780794/). The trial tested another company's products, was small and was partly funded by that manufacturer, but it is one of the only controlled trials in this area.
* **Fiber:** 3 grams per 100 grams, compared with zero in meat.
* **An alternative to red meat:** the International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies red meat as "probably carcinogenic" and processed meat as carcinogenic. [Bouvard et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26514947/). For people who eat a lot of [red meat](/en/food/red-meat/), replacing some servings with a plant-based substitute can reduce that exposure — although no study has tested whether meat substitutes reduce cancer.
* **Environmental and animal welfare considerations:** plant-based meat substitutes need less land and emit fewer greenhouse gases than beef — the main reason many people choose them.

### Downsides

* **Ultra-processed food:** a long ingredient list, with maltodextrin, modified starches and flavorings. In a controlled NIH trial, people given an ultra-processed diet ate about 500 more calories a day than on an unprocessed diet matched for nutrients, and gained about a kilogram in two weeks. [Hall et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105044/). That trial tested a whole diet, not a meat substitute, but it is a reason not to make products like this the base of your diet.
* **Sodium:** 300 mg per 100 grams — reasonable compared with other meat substitutes, but much more than an unseasoned fresh beef cut. A large serving with sauce can reach a third of the daily limit.
* **Gluten and soy:** the product is not suitable for people with celiac disease, gluten sensitivity or soy allergy.
* **Iron and B12:** beef provides heme iron, which is absorbed well, and vitamin B12. In a plant-based substitute the iron is non-heme, which is absorbed less well, and phytates from plant proteins can reduce its absorption further. [NIH ODS — Iron](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/). B12 does not occur naturally in plant foods; if the label does not list it as added, you need another source. [NIH ODS — Vitamin B12](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/).
* **Price:** advanced meat substitutes usually cost more than simple plant protein sources such as legumes, [tofu](/en/food/tofu/) and [tempeh](/en/food/tempeh/).

### Practical use

* **Cooking:** like a thin beef steak — a very hot pan or grill, a few minutes per side, and a short rest before slicing. Serve it in a sandwich, over rice or with roasted vegetables.
* **Seasoning:** the product already contains salt; add herbs, pepper, lemon and garlic rather than more salt or salty sauces.
* **Balance the plate:** serve it with plenty of vegetables and legumes or whole grains, and include a vitamin C source (bell pepper, tomato, lemon) in the same meal to improve iron absorption.
* **How much and when:** as a replacement for a serving of red meat once or twice a week — not as an everyday staple.
* **Check the label:** nutrition values, allergens and fortification with vitamin B12 and iron vary between product versions.

### Summary

Redefine Meat's plant-based flank is an impressive technological product, high in protein, low in saturated fat and cholesterol-free, and small trials show that replacing meat with plant-based substitutes can improve LDL slightly. On the other hand, it is an ultra-processed food with sodium, gluten and soy, and it does not provide the heme iron and B12 of meat. It suits people who want to cut down on red meat without giving up the steak experience — as part of a diet based mainly on whole foods.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How much protein is in the Redefine Meat flank?**
Answer: According to the product data on this page, about 25 grams of protein per 100 grams — similar to many beef cuts. A 150-gram serving provides about 37 grams of protein.

**Question: Is the plant-based flank healthier than a beef steak?**
Answer: On some measures, yes: much less saturated fat, no cholesterol, and some fiber. In a controlled trial, replacing meat with plant-based substitutes lowered LDL slightly. On the other hand, it is more processed, contains more sodium and provides less available iron and B12.

**Question: Is the product suitable for people with celiac disease?**
Answer: No. It contains gluten from wheat and barley, as well as soy.

**Question: Is the product vegan?**
Answer: It is made from plant-based ingredients and marketed as a plant-based product. If you follow a strict vegan diet or a particular kosher standard, check the current marking on the package.

**Question: Is it an ultra-processed food?**
Answer: Yes. The ingredient list includes maltodextrin, modified starches and flavorings, and the manufacturing process is complex. That doesn't make a serving harmful, but it is better not to make it the base of your diet.

**Question: Do you get as much iron from it as from meat?**
Answer: Not as efficiently. The iron in a plant-based substitute is non-heme iron, which is absorbed less well than the heme iron in meat. Adding vitamin C to the same meal helps absorption.
