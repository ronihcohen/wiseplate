## Cacao and Cacao Nibs (Theobroma cacao)

Cacao is the umbrella name for products made from the beans of the cacao tree. Crushed cacao beans, known in English as **cacao nibs** (in Hebrew "פולי קקאו גרוסים", literally "crushed cacao beans"), are pieces of the roasted or fermented bean with no added sugar. Cocoa powder is made by grinding the beans and removing part of the cocoa butter.

Both forms provide flavanols and minerals, but they are not identical: cacao nibs contain more fat and calories, while the powder is lower in fat and more concentrated in fiber. Alkali processing (Dutch process) reduces acidity and bitterness, but also substantially lowers the flavanol content.

### Cocoa powder, cacao beans and cacao nibs — what's the difference?

| Product | What it is | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| Cacao beans | The whole seeds of the cacao fruit | Require roasting, shelling and grinding before use |
| Cacao nibs | The beans after shelling and breaking into pieces | Sugar-free, rich in fat, fiber and theobromine |
| Natural cocoa powder | Cocoa solids after removing part of the fat | Fewer calories by weight and more fiber |
| Alkalized cocoa powder | Powder processed to reduce acidity | Mild flavor and dark color, but fewer flavanols |

The nutrition facts below refer to **cacao nibs**. Cocoa powder is a different product, so check its label separately.

### Nutrition facts

| Component | Per 30 grams (2 tablespoons) | Per 100 grams |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | 130-160 | 465-540 |
| Fat | 12-13 g | 43 g |
| of which saturated (mostly stearic acid) | 7-8 g | 26 g |
| **Dietary fiber** | **9 g** | **32 g** |
| Protein | 3-4 g | 11-14 g |
| Net carbs | 1-2 g | 4-6 g |
| [Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) | 60-80 mg | 220-270 mg |
| [Iron](/en/food/iron/) | 2-4 mg | 8-14 mg |
| [Copper](/en/food/supplements/copper/) | 0.9 mg | 3.1 mg |
| Theobromine | ~230 mg | ~800 mg |
| [Caffeine](/en/food/supplements/caffeine/) | ~20 mg | ~70 mg |

**Two figures stand out in the table.** The first is **fiber** — 32 grams per 100 grams, one of the highest of any food, so net carbs are very low (4-6 grams) and nibs fit well into a low-carb diet. The second is [copper](/en/food/supplements/copper/): 30 grams provide almost the full daily intake.

Values vary between brands by about 20%, depending on the variety and the amount of cocoa butter left in the bean.

### Rich in polyphenols and flavonoids

Cacao beans are one of the richest natural sources of flavonoids, mainly epicatechin. These compounds improve blood flow, contribute to arterial flexibility and have an especially strong antioxidant effect.

### High mineral content

Cacao is an excellent source of essential minerals:
* **[Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/)** – essential for muscle relaxation, energy production and nervous system function.
* **[Iron](/en/food/iron/)** – from a plant source, contributing to anemia prevention (best eaten with [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) to improve absorption).
* **[Zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) and [copper](/en/food/supplements/copper/)** – important for immune and enzyme function.

### Effects on mood and the brain

Cacao contains unique compounds such as theobromine and phenylethylamine (PEA), which affect neurotransmitters in the brain. These substances may improve mood, increase alertness and contribute to a general sense of well-being.

### Healthy fats and fiber

Cacao nibs contain a good amount of fat (cocoa butter), made up of saturated fat (stearic acid, which does not significantly raise cholesterol) and monounsaturated fat. In addition, they are rich in dietary fiber that contributes to satiety.

### Cardiovascular health

Regular consumption of quality cacao has been linked in studies to lower blood pressure and improved endothelial health (the blood vessel lining), thanks to the vasodilating activity of flavonoids.

### Downsides and side effects

- **Caffeine and theobromine — real stimulants:** Cacao nibs contain about 230 mg of theobromine and about 20 mg of caffeine per 30 grams. Theobromine acts more slowly and for longer than caffeine, so a large serving in the afternoon may disrupt sleep, cause palpitations or restlessness. People sensitive to caffeine should limit intake to the morning hours.
- **Very bitter taste:** Unlike chocolate, raw cacao nibs are not sweetened at all. Many people are unpleasantly surprised by the bitterness and the gritty texture, and add sugar or [honey](/en/food/honey/) — which cancels out part of the benefit.
- **Calorie density:** About 130 calories per 30 grams. A healthy snack, but not a "free" snack.
- **Migraines:** The theobromine and tyramine in cacao are a known migraine trigger for some sufferers.
- **Reflux and heartburn:** Cacao relaxes the lower esophageal sphincter and may worsen reflux.
- **Cadmium and lead:** The cacao tree absorbs cadmium from the soil, and tests of cacao products have sometimes found non-negligible levels of cadmium and sometimes lead. The risk depends on where it was grown. It is advisable to eat moderate amounts and vary between brands and sources.
- **Oxalates:** High oxalate content, relevant for people prone to kidney stones.
- **Toxic to pets:** Theobromine is toxic to dogs and cats, and cacao nibs are especially concentrated. Store them out of their reach — this is a veterinary emergency.

### Summary

Raw cacao nibs provide the health benefits of chocolate in their rawest and most potent form. They are an excellent addition to the diet as a natural energy snack, contributing to heart health, mood and a supply of essential minerals.

The practical caveats: they are **stimulating** because of theobromine and caffeine and so are less suitable for the evening, they are very bitter, and it is wise to eat them in moderation and vary sources because of possible cadmium content. A reasonable serving is about 20–30 grams a day.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: What are the benefits of cacao?**
Answer: Unsweetened cacao provides flavanols, including epicatechin, which have been studied in the context of blood flow and blood pressure. Cacao nibs also provide fiber, magnesium, iron and copper, without the added sugar found in many chocolate products.

**Question: What is the difference between cocoa powder and cacao nibs?**
Answer: Cocoa powder is made after part of the cocoa butter has been removed from the beans, so it is lower in fat and calories by weight. Cacao nibs contain all parts of the bean and are therefore richer in fat and theobromine. Alkali-processed powder usually contains fewer flavanols.

**Question: What is the difference between cacao nibs and dark chocolate?**
Answer: Cacao nibs are the crushed bean itself, without sugar or additives. [Dark chocolate](/en/food/dark-chocolate/) contains the same beans but with sugar, and sometimes extra cocoa butter and an emulsifier. The result: cacao nibs are much more bitter, but provide more flavonoids and fiber by weight and zero sugar.

**Question: How many cacao nibs can you eat a day?**
Answer: 20-30 grams (1-2 tablespoons) is a reasonable serving — about 130-160 calories. Beyond that, the calories, the theobromine and the cadmium exposure all add up, so there is no advantage to a larger amount.

**Question: Do cacao nibs contain caffeine?**
Answer: Yes, about 20 mg per 30 grams — less than a quarter cup of [coffee](/en/food/coffee/). But the more significant stimulant is theobromine, about 230 mg in the same amount. It acts more slowly and for longer than caffeine, so a large serving in the afternoon may disrupt sleep.

**Question: Is there cadmium in cacao nibs?**
Answer: Sometimes, yes. The cacao tree absorbs cadmium from the soil, and levels vary greatly by growing region — cacao from South America tends to be higher than cacao from Africa. The practical rule is moderate intake (20-30 grams) and varying between brands and sources.

**Question: Why are cacao nibs so bitter?**
Answer: Because they are the pure bean with no sugar at all, and the bitterness comes precisely from the flavonoids and theobromine that are the reason to eat them. Combining them with [dates](/en/food/dates/), [Greek yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/) or fruit tempers it without adding added sugar.

**Question: Are cacao nibs dangerous for dogs?**
Answer: Yes, very. Theobromine is toxic to dogs and cats, and cacao nibs are much more concentrated in it than regular chocolate. Ingestion requires immediate contact with a vet, and they should be stored out of their reach.
