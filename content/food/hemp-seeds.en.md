## Hemp Seeds

**Three tablespoons of hulled hemp seeds (hemp hearts, about 30 g) provide about 165 calories, about 9.5 g of protein, about 2.6 g of plant omega-3 and around half the daily magnesium requirement. They do not get you high: food hemp seeds contain almost no THC, and in a study eating 30 g a day did not produce a positive urine screen.** Their protein quality is good for a plant protein, but lower than animal protein.

Hemp seeds (in Hebrew "זרעי קנבוס") come from industrial varieties of *Cannabis sativa* grown for fibre and food, with a very low THC content. They are sold mainly hulled, as "hemp hearts": soft, greenish kernels with a mild, nutty taste. Compared with other common seeds, chia, flax and [sesame](/en/food/sesame-seeds/), they stand out for their protein content.

The recommended magnesium intake for adults is 310–420 mg a day depending on age and sex, so 30 g covers roughly half of it. [NIH ODS: Magnesium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/).

### Benefits

- **Protein.** About a third of the weight of the hulled seed is protein, more than chia, flax or sesame. The protein contains all the essential amino acids, but lysine is present in limiting amounts. On the PDCAAS score, which combines composition and digestibility, hulled seeds scored 63–66% and whole seeds about 50%. These values are similar to lentils and nuts, and lower than egg or milk protein. [House et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20977230/). Pairing them with legumes, which are rich in lysine, fills the gap.
- **Unsaturated fat and the omega ratio.** About 90% of the fat is unsaturated. The omega-6 to omega-3 ratio is about 3:1, lower than in common vegetable oils such as sunflower oil. The ALA is plant omega-3, and its conversion to EPA and DHA is limited, so hemp seeds are not a substitute for [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) from fish. [Nutrients, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32610691/).
- **Magnesium, zinc and iron.** 30 g provides about half the magnesium requirement and about a third of a man's zinc requirement. For vegans this is a meaningful addition. The iron is plant iron and is absorbed less well than iron from meat.
- **Convenience.** No grinding, soaking or cooking needed. Unlike flax, the hulled seed is soft and easy to chew.
- **GLA.** The seeds contain a small amount of gamma-linolenic acid, an omega-6 fatty acid. Claims that it relieves arthritis or PMS symptoms have not been shown for food seeds.

### Downsides

- **Calories.** Three tablespoons are about 165 calories, mostly from fat. "One more spoonful on the salad" adds up.
- **No specific clinical evidence.** Unlike flax, which has been tested in trials on blood pressure and cholesterol, there are no good-quality trials showing a health benefit specific to hemp seeds. Their value is nutritional: protein, unsaturated fat and minerals.
- **THC in products.** The seed itself contains almost no THC, but it can pick some up from other parts of the plant during processing. In a German survey of 511 hemp food products from 2008 to 2021, seeds and beverages typically did not reach problematic levels, but some CBD supplements and teas made from hemp flowers did. [Foods, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35159636/).
- **Drug tests.** In a Korean study, participants ate 30 g of hemp seeds a day for up to 12 weeks. All urine samples were below the positive threshold on the screening test. [Baeck et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31670217/). The risk is low, but people who undergo workplace drug testing, such as athletes, pilots or professional drivers, may choose to avoid them rather than risk complications.
- **Oxidation.** The polyunsaturated fat oxidises quickly. After opening, keep them in the fridge and use them within a few weeks. A bitter taste or paint-like smell signals rancidity.
- **Allergy.** Rare but described, mainly in people sensitive to the cannabis plant.

### Practical use

- **On yogurt, porridge and salad.** Two or three tablespoons add protein and a soft texture.
- **In smoothies.** The seeds blend well and thicken slightly.
- **"Hemp milk".** Hulled seeds blended with water, strained if you like. A homemade drink like this is not fortified with calcium.
- **Pesto and sauces.** In place of some of the pine nuts or other nuts.
- **Hemp seed oil.** For cold use only, not for frying. It contains the fat without the protein.
- **How much?** Two to three tablespoons a day is a reasonable amount.
- **Storage.** In a closed container in the fridge after opening.

### Summary

Hulled hemp seeds are a convenient, nutritious addition: the most protein of the common seeds, unsaturated fat with a favourable omega ratio, and plenty of magnesium and zinc. Their protein quality is good for a plant protein but not "complete" in the way animal protein is, because lysine is limiting. There are no trials showing a specific health benefit, and they have no intoxicating effect. Two to three tablespoons a day, stored in the fridge, is the right way to use them.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Do hemp seeds get you high?**
Answer: No. Food seeds from industrial varieties contain almost no THC, and the amount that sticks to them during processing is very far from an intoxicating dose.

**Question: Can hemp seeds cause a positive drug test?**
Answer: The risk is low. In a study where people ate 30 g a day for up to 12 weeks, all samples were negative on the screening test. People who are tested regularly at work or in sport may choose to avoid them to be safe.

**Question: Is hemp seed protein a complete protein?**
Answer: It contains all the essential amino acids, but lysine is low, so its quality score is lower than that of egg or milk. Combining it with legumes completes it.

**Question: How do hemp seeds differ from chia and flax?**
Answer: Chia and flax are richer in omega-3 and fibre, and flax has evidence for blood pressure. Hemp seeds are richer in protein and magnesium. It is worth varying them.

**Question: How many hemp seeds should you eat a day?**
Answer: Two to three tablespoons, 20–30 g. That provides a good amount of protein and magnesium without excess calories.

**Question: Can you eat hemp seeds during pregnancy?**
Answer: As food seeds in normal amounts they are considered a food rather than a supplement. CBD products and cannabinoid supplements should be avoided in pregnancy. If in doubt, ask your doctor.

**Question: Whole or hulled?**
Answer: Hulled seeds are soft and convenient, with more protein and better protein quality. Whole seeds have more fibre but are hard to chew.
