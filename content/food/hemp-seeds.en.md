## Hemp Seeds

Hemp seeds (*Cannabis sativa*) — also known as "hemp hearts" in their shelled form — are one of the most complete plant sources of protein. It is important to clarify: nutritional hemp seeds contain negligible amounts of THC (less than 0.3%) and have no psychoactive effect at all — they are an entirely legitimate food.

Compared with the other common seeds (chia, flax, [sesame](/en/food/sesame-seeds/)), hemp seeds stand out for their amino acid profile and their rare omega balance.

### Research-based health benefits

* **Complete protein:** hemp seeds contain all 9 essential amino acids in sufficient concentration — a very rare trait among plant sources. 3 tablespoons (30 grams) provide about 10 grams of protein with a bioavailability of about 91% — high for a plant protein.

* **Edestin and albumin:** the seed's two main globulin proteins have a structure similar to proteins in human blood, which makes them easier to digest and absorb.

* **A balanced omega-6 to omega-3 ratio:** the ratio in hemp seeds is about 3:1 in favor of omega-6 — one of the most optimal ratios among all plant foods. The Western diet averages a ratio of 15:1 or even 20:1, so hemp seeds help restore an anti-inflammatory balance.

* **GLA (gamma-linolenic acid):** a rare omega-6 fatty acid, found mainly in evening primrose oil and hemp seeds. GLA is a precursor of anti-inflammatory prostaglandins and may help reduce joint inflammation, PMS symptoms and inflammatory skin conditions.

* **[Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/):** 3 tablespoons provide about 45% of the daily value. Magnesium is essential for ATP production, muscle relaxation, sleep and [insulin](/en/food/supplements/insulin/) sensitivity.

* **[Zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/):** essential for immune function, DNA synthesis and wound healing.

* **[Iron](/en/food/iron/) and vitamin E:** non-heme iron and a fat-soluble antioxidant — both contribute to energy and protecting cell membranes.

### Downsides and health considerations

* **High calorie density:** 3 tablespoons (30 grams) contain about 170 calories — most of them from fat. Plan them within your daily calorie budget.

* **Phytic acid:** like other seeds, they contain phytic acid, which reduces mineral absorption. A short soak (1-2 hours) can reduce its concentration.

* **Storage:** very rich in unsaturated fats and therefore prone to oxidation. Store in the fridge after opening and use within a month.

* **Interaction with drug tests:** in theory, very large amounts could lead to a false positive on certain THC tests — although in practice the chance is low with reasonable intake.

### Culinary and practical use

* **On granola and yogurt:** they add protein and a pleasant, delicate texture
* **In smoothies:** they blend in well and upgrade the nutrient profile
* **On salads:** a light, soft alternative to chopped nuts
* **Making "hemp milk":** soak in water and blend — tasty and nourishing
* **Hemp seed oil:** not suitable for frying (low smoke point) — for cold seasoning only

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Do hemp seeds make you feel "high"?**
Answer: Absolutely not. The THC content of shelled seeds (hemp hearts) is less than 0.3% and is not enough for any psychoactive effect.

**Question: What is the difference between hemp seeds and chia and flax?**
Answer: Chia and flax are richer in [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) (ALA), but hemp seeds win on complete protein and magnesium. It is worth combining all three for variety.

**Question: Can you eat hemp seeds during pregnancy?**
Answer: Yes — they are safe and rich in nutrients. Avoid CBD oil and cannabinoid supplements during pregnancy, but not the seeds.

**Question: Which is better — whole or shelled hemp seeds (hearts)?**
Answer: The hearts (shelled) are softer and more convenient to use; whole seeds contain extra fiber from the shell. Both are excellent.

### Summary

Hemp seeds are an excellent nutritional addition with a complete protein profile and a rare omega balance. They are especially recommended for vegans and anyone looking for quality dietary variety — with attention to quantity because of their calorie density.
