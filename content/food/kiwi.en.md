## Kiwi

Kiwi (*Actinidia deliciosa*; in Hebrew, "קיווי") is a small, nutrient-dense fruit that originated in China (where it was called the "Chinese gooseberry") and was developed commercially in New Zealand. It is one of the fruits with the highest concentration of vitamins relative to calories, and recent studies have revealed surprising properties related to sleep, digestion and heart health.

The common green kiwi differs from the yellow kiwi (SunGold): the yellow one is sweeter and richer in [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/), while the green one is richer in chlorophyll and vitamin K. Both have high nutritional value.

### Research-based health benefits

* **[Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) in exceptional concentration:** One green kiwi (about 70 grams) provides about 65 mg of vitamin C — more than a whole [orange](/en/food/orange/) of the same weight. Yellow kiwi provides up to 160 mg per 100 grams. It supports [collagen](/en/food/collagen/) production, immunity, [iron](/en/food/iron/) absorption and blood vessel health.

* **[Vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/):** 100 grams of kiwi provide about 40 mcg of vitamin K — about 34% of the daily intake. Essential for normal blood clotting and bone mineralization.

* **Actinidin:** A proteolytic enzyme unique to kiwi that helps break down proteins and improves digestion of protein-rich foods such as meat, [eggs](/en/food/eggs/) and milk. Studies have shown that eating kiwi with a protein meal speeds up gastric emptying.

* **Better sleep:** A clinical study (Taipei Medical University, 2011) showed that eating two kiwis about an hour before bed for 4 weeks improved time to fall asleep, sleep efficiency and total sleep time. The mechanism is probably related to its content of serotonin, folate and antioxidants.

* **Anthocyanins and ascorbic acid:** Protect nerve cells from oxidative damage and are linked to cognitive improvement in older age.

* **Potassium and magnesium:** Contribute to blood pressure regulation, muscle function and fluid balance.

* **Fiber and prebiotics:** The soluble fiber in kiwi speeds up bowel transit and supports beneficial gut bacteria.

### Downsides and health considerations

* **Allergy and cross-reaction with latex:** Kiwi is one of the relatively common allergens. There is a known cross-reaction with latex allergy (latex-fruit syndrome), which increases the risk of allergy to kiwi, [banana](/en/food/banana/) and [avocado](/en/food/avocado/).

* **Oral irritation:** The tiny calcium oxalate crystals in kiwi can irritate the tongue and palate — noticeable mainly after eating a large amount.

* **[Vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/) and anticoagulants:** A large daily amount of kiwi may affect INR in patients taking warfarin — keep your intake consistent.

* **Oxalates:** In large amounts they may contribute to calcium oxalate kidney stones in people who are prone to them.

### Green kiwi vs. yellow kiwi

| Feature | Green kiwi | Yellow kiwi (SunGold) |
|---|---|---|
| [Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) (per 100 grams) | ~93 mg | ~161 mg |
| [Vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/) | Higher | Lower |
| Sweetness | Tart-sweet | Very sweet |
| Edible skin | Yes (with rubbing) | Yes |
| Price | Lower | Higher |

### Culinary and practical use

* **With the skin:** Green kiwi skin is edible and rich in fiber and [vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/) — try eating it after a good wash
* **In smoothies:** Combines well with banana, [spinach](/en/food/spinach/) and yogurt
* **As a meat tenderizer:** Actinidin makes kiwi an effective natural marinade for meat
* **In fruit salads and cakes:** Adds refreshing tartness and visual appeal
* **About an hour before bed:** Two small ones as an evening ritual that may support sleep

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Is green or yellow kiwi better?**
Answer: Yellow is sweeter and richer in vitamin C; green contains more vitamin K and fiber. Health-wise both are excellent — it is a matter of preference.

**Question: Is it safe to eat kiwi during pregnancy?**
Answer: Yes — it is rich in folate and vitamin C, both important in pregnancy. Unless there is a known allergy.

**Question: How many kiwis a day are recommended?**
Answer: 1-2 a day are enough to get most of the benefits without consuming excess vitamin K or oxalates.

**Question: Can you eat a kiwi that is still hard?**
Answer: You can, but it is less sweet and less bioavailable. You can place it with an apple for a few days to speed up ripening.

### Summary

Kiwi is a fruit with very high nutritional value relative to its size and calories. Eating 1-2 kiwis a day — even the skin — is a simple way to boost vitamins, improve digestion and perhaps also sleep better.
