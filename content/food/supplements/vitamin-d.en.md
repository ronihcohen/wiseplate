## Introduction: vitamin or hormone?

Despite its name, vitamin D is not an ordinary vitamin but a prohormone (a hormone precursor). The body makes it naturally when the skin is exposed to UV radiation from the sun. However, modern life, with long hours spent indoors and the use of sunscreen, has led to a global epidemic of vitamin D deficiency. It is estimated that about a billion people worldwide have low levels. It directly influences the expression of hundreds of genes in the body.

## Benefits (Pros)

### 1. Bone and muscle health
Vitamin D's classic, best-known role is regulating the body's [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) balance. Without enough vitamin D, the body cannot absorb [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) from food, which leads to bone loss (osteoporosis) and a higher risk of fractures. In addition, normal levels are essential for proper muscle function and preventing falls in old age.

### 2. Mood and mental health
Vitamin D receptors are found in the brain regions responsible for regulating mood. Low blood levels are consistently linked to a higher risk of depression, especially "winter depression" (SAD). Supplementation may significantly improve mood and mental well-being in people who are deficient.

### 3. Hormonal support (testosterone)
Observational and interventional studies have found a direct link between vitamin D levels and male testosterone levels. Men with normal vitamin D levels tend to have higher testosterone levels, and supplementation in deficient men led to a rise in the hormone's levels.

## Downsides and side effects (Cons)

### 1. Toxicity at extreme doses
Vitamin D is fat-soluble, so it accumulates in the body's tissues and is not excreted as easily as water-soluble vitamins (such as [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/)). Taking excessive doses (tens of thousands of units a day over time) can lead to vitamin D toxicity, causing excess [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) in the blood (hypercalcemia), nausea, vomiting and even kidney damage.

### 2. Interaction with [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/)
Activating vitamin D in the body requires [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/). Taking high doses of vitamin D without enough [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) can "drain" the body's [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) stores and lead to symptoms of [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) deficiency (such as muscle cramps and sleep problems), even if you take the vitamin faithfully.

### 3. The need for blood tests
Unlike other supplements that can be taken "blind" with relative safety, the vitamin D dose should be tailored individually to your blood level. Taking it when you don't need it is a waste of money at best and an unnecessary risk at worst. A simple blood test is essential for setting the right dose.

## Summary

Vitamin D is a central cog in the human machine, affecting almost every system in the body. Its effect on bones, mood and hormones makes it one of the most important supplements for the modern "indoor" person. However, because of its potential to accumulate, the right approach is "measure, then take" — get a blood test and fill the gap if needed, preferably together with a fatty meal and [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/).
