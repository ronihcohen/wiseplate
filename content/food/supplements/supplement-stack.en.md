## Combining Supplements

**Most healthy people who eat a varied diet don't need a "stack" of supplements: the US Preventive Services Task Force found insufficient evidence that multivitamins or vitamin combinations prevent heart disease or cancer.** [USPSTF, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35727271/) A supplement makes sense when there is a defined reason — a diagnosed deficiency, pregnancy, a vegan diet or a specific sports goal — and when you take several, timing and the total amount matter. In Hebrew the topic is "שילוב תוספי תזונה".

A "stack" is a nickname for a combination of several supplements taken together. This page is not a recipe for a "perfect stack", because there is no such thing: it explains which combinations have been tested, which interfere with each other, and when it's better to ask a doctor before adding another pill.

### Who actually needs which supplement

| Situation | Supplement with evidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Anyone who could become pregnant | [Folic acid](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b9/) 400–800 mcg a day | Start before conception [USPSTF, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37526713/) |
| Vegans | [Vitamin B12](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b12/) | No reliable plant source |
| Long-term metformin or proton pump inhibitor users | A B12 test, and a supplement based on the result | See the B12 page |
| Little sun exposure, over 75, infants | [Vitamin D](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-d/) | Per the guidelines for each age |
| Diagnosed iron deficiency | [Iron supplement](/en/food/supplements/iron/) | Only with a blood test, not "just in case" |
| Strength trainees | [Creatine](/en/food/supplements/creatine/) 3–5 g a day | Modest gains in strength and muscle mass |
| Little fish in the diet | [Omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) | Limited benefit for healthy people |

Everything else — "energy" supplements, "immune boosters", "anti-aging" — rests on much weaker evidence.

### Timing that actually matters

#### Iron and calcium — keep them apart
Calcium inhibits iron absorption at the same meal: in a Swedish study, 300–600 mg of calcium (and also 165 mg from milk or cheese) reduced iron absorption by 50%–60%. [Hallberg et al., 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1984335/) If you take both — iron in the morning and calcium in the evening, or at least two hours apart. Coffee and tea with the meal also reduce iron absorption; [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) at the same meal improves it.

#### Iron — every other day
In women with low iron stores, a single morning dose every other day was absorbed better than the same dose every day, because a daily dose raises the hormone hepcidin, which blocks absorption the next day. [Stoffel et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29032957/) Your doctor sets the dose and frequency.

#### Fat-soluble vitamins — with a meal
Vitamins A, D, E and K are absorbed better with fat. In a trial where vitamin D3 was taken with breakfast, blood levels were about a third higher with a meal containing fat than with a fat-free meal. [Dawson-Hughes et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25441954/) The same logic applies to omega-3 and curcumin.

#### Medications to keep minerals away from
- **Thyroid medication (levothyroxine):** iron and calcium interfere with its absorption — separate them by several hours. [NIH ODS — iron](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/)
- **Some antibiotics (tetracyclines, quinolones) and osteoporosis drugs (bisphosphonates):** [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/), calcium and iron bind to them — separate them as your pharmacist directs. [NIH ODS — magnesium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-HealthProfessional/)

#### Caffeine and sleep
[Caffeine](/en/food/supplements/caffeine/) before training improves performance, but its effect lasts for hours. If you sleep badly, stop it by the afternoon.

### Combinations worth knowing about

- **Vitamin D and magnesium:** the enzymes that activate vitamin D depend on magnesium, but there's no need to take them in the same capsule — it's enough for your diet to supply magnesium.
- **Vitamin D and K2:** the idea is based on the mechanism; direct evidence that the combination helps is still limited. See [vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/).
- **[Curcumin](/en/food/supplements/turmeric/) and piperine:** piperine greatly increases absorption, but it also changes how drugs are broken down, and it was present in some cases of liver injury.
- **[Zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) and copper:** long-term high-dose zinc causes copper deficiency. The adult upper limit is 40 mg of zinc a day. [NIH ODS — zinc](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/)
- **[Berberine](/en/food/supplements/berberine/):** acts like a blood-sugar-lowering drug and affects how many drugs are broken down. Don't combine it with diabetes medication without a doctor.

### The real danger: the same ingredient from several products

Someone taking a multivitamin, a B-complex, a "hair and nails" supplement and an energy drink may be getting the same ingredient four times. It's worth adding up the amounts from all the labels and comparing them with the upper limit:

| Ingredient | Adult upper limit per day | Where it hides |
|---|---|---|
| [Vitamin A](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-a/) (retinol) | 3,000 mcg | Multivitamins, cod liver oil, skin supplements |
| Vitamin D | 100 mcg (4,000 IU) | Multivitamins, calcium with D, omega-3 with D |
| [Vitamin B6](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b6/) | 100 mg (US); 12 mg (Europe) | B-complex, magnesium with B6, energy drinks |
| Folic acid | 1,000 mcg | Multivitamins, prenatal vitamins, B-complex |
| Zinc | 40 mg | Multivitamins, ZMA, immune supplements |
| Magnesium from supplements | 350 mg | ZMA, sleep supplements, laxatives |

Sources for the limits: the NIH fact sheets on each nutrient (linked from the vitamin pages) and EFSA for B6.

### Who shouldn't combine supplements on their own

- **Pregnant and breastfeeding women** — beyond a prenatal vitamin and what the doctor recommends.
- **Children** — doses differ, and upper limits are lower.
- **People on blood thinners** — omega-3, vitamin E, curcumin, ginkgo and vitamin K affect clotting or the treatment.
- **People taking medication for diabetes, blood pressure or the thyroid, antidepressants, or chemotherapy.**
- **People with kidney or liver disease** — they clear minerals and plant compounds less well.
- **Before surgery** — tell the team about every supplement.

### Practical use

- **Start from the reason, not the product:** what problem is the supplement supposed to solve, and how will you know if it's working?
- **One at a time:** add one supplement, wait a few weeks, and watch for side effects before adding another.
- **Choose third-party-tested products** (USP, NSF or similar), especially sports supplements.
- **Give your doctor and pharmacist** the full list, including herbal products.
- **Review once a year:** is the reason still there? Many supplements stay on out of habit.

### Summary

There is no "perfect stack". For most people, a varied diet does most of the work, and supplements help when they meet a defined need: folic acid before pregnancy, B12 for vegans, vitamin D when there is deficiency or little sun, iron when deficiency is diagnosed, and creatine for strength trainees. When combining, the two things that matter are timing — iron apart from calcium, fat-soluble vitamins with a meal, minerals away from certain drugs — and checking that the same ingredient isn't coming from several products. And anyone on regular medication, pregnant or with a chronic illness should get advice before combining.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Can I take all my supplements together in the morning?**
Answer: Most of them, yes, with breakfast. The exceptions: iron and calcium are best separated by a few hours, and thyroid medication should be taken apart from iron, calcium and magnesium.

**Question: Does a multivitamin replace separate supplements?**
Answer: For most people, a standard-dose multivitamin is enough, if anything is needed at all. It has not been shown to prevent heart disease or cancer, and if you add separate supplements on top, add up the amounts.

**Question: Why not take iron with calcium?**
Answer: Calcium inhibits iron absorption at the same meal, by about 50%–60% in one study. If you take both, take them at different times of day.

**Question: Do I need to take vitamin D with magnesium?**
Answer: Not in the same capsule. Magnesium is needed to activate vitamin D, but it's enough for your diet to supply it. Someone who is low in magnesium can take it at any time.

**Question: When should I take fat-soluble vitamins?**
Answer: With the meal that has the most fat. Vitamin D, for example, was absorbed about a third better with a meal containing fat than with a fat-free meal.

**Question: Which supplement combinations are dangerous?**
Answer: Mainly combinations with drugs — blood thinners with omega-3, vitamin E or curcumin; berberine with diabetes drugs — and duplicates: the same vitamin A, B6 or zinc from several products that together exceed the upper limit.

**Question: How many supplements is too many?**
Answer: There is no magic number. The question is whether each supplement has a defined reason. If you don't know why you're taking a particular supplement, that's the first one to consider stopping.
