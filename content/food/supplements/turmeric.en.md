## Introduction: The Gold of the East

[Turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/) is a vivid yellow spice made from the root of the Curcuma plant, and it is a foundation of many Asian cuisines. But its medicinal secret lies in an active compound called curcumin. Curcumin is considered one of the most-researched natural anti-inflammatories in the world. However, the spice itself contains only about 3% curcumin, so most medical studies focus on concentrated supplements containing a high percentage of the active compound, not on the household spice.

## Benefits (Pros)

### 1. A powerful anti-inflammatory
Silent chronic inflammation underlies many Western diseases. Curcumin blocks the molecule NF-kB, a kind of "master switch" that turns on inflammatory processes in cells. Studies have compared its effectiveness to certain anti-inflammatory drugs, without their accompanying side effects.

### 2. Relief of joint pain (arthritis)
Thanks to its anti-inflammatory properties, curcumin has been found very effective at reducing pain and improving mobility in people with osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis. Many athletes use it to reduce joint pain caused by training load.

### 3. Antioxidant activity
Beyond being an anti-inflammatory, curcumin is a strong antioxidant in its own right, neutralizing free radicals that damage cells. What's more, it encourages the body to produce its own natural antioxidant defense enzymes, providing double protection against oxidative damage.

## Downsides and Side Effects (Cons)

### 1. Very poor absorption
Curcumin's biggest drawback is that it is barely absorbed into the bloodstream in its natural form; it is broken down quickly in the liver and gut. Eating ordinary [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/) powder has almost no systemic effect. The solution is to combine it with "piperine" (black pepper extract), which increases absorption by 2000%, or to use supplements with liposome/phytosome technology.

### 2. Blood thinning
Like [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/), [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/) has a mild blood-thinning effect. People taking anticoagulant drugs (such as aspirin or warfarin) should be careful combining it and consult a doctor, since the combination may increase the risk of bleeding/bruising.

### 3. Stomach discomfort at high doses
Although it is sometimes used to treat digestive problems, excessively high doses of curcumin (or a poor-quality supplement) may have the opposite effect: stomach irritation, nausea and diarrhea. It is always recommended to take it with food (preferably fatty, for a further boost to absorption) and not on an empty stomach.

## Summary

Curcumin is nature's "doomsday weapon" against inflammation and pain. It is an excellent alternative or add-on for treating joint pain and chronic inflammation. However, the condition for success is choosing the right supplement: one that contains a high concentration of curcumin (not just root powder) and includes a mechanism to boost absorption (black pepper or phospholipids); otherwise the health benefit will be marginal at best.
