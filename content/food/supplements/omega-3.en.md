## Introduction: Essential Fatty Acids

Omega-3s are a family of polyunsaturated fatty acids classed as "essential," because the human body cannot make them itself and must get them from food. There are three main types: ALA (found mainly in plants), and EPA and DHA (found mainly in deep-sea fish and algae). Most of the proven health benefits are attributed to EPA and DHA, while the body's efficiency in converting ALA into them is very low.

## Benefits (Pros)

### 1. Heart and blood vessel health
This is the most-researched area of omega-3. Many studies have shown that adequate omega-3 intake helps lower blood triglyceride levels, raise "good" cholesterol (HDL) and slightly lower blood pressure. These factors contribute to a lower risk of heart disease and stroke.

### 2. Brain function and mental health
The brain is very rich in fat, and DHA is a critical structural component of brain cell membranes. Adequate intake has been linked to improved cognitive function, a lower risk of cognitive decline in later life (such as Alzheimer's), and even relief of symptoms of depression and anxiety.

### 3. Anti-inflammatory activity
Omega-3 affects inflammatory mechanisms in the body and can help reduce chronic inflammation, considered a root cause of many diseases. Athletes may benefit from reduced inflammation and improved blood flow to the muscles, while people with autoimmune joint diseases sometimes report pain relief.

## Downsides and Side Effects (Cons)

### 1. Blood thinning
At high doses, omega-3 has a mild blood-thinning effect. People taking anticoagulant drugs (such as Coumadin) or who are about to have surgery should consult a doctor before taking a supplement, to avoid a risk of excessive bleeding.

### 2. Freshness and oxidation
Fish oil is very sensitive to oxidation on contact with air, heat or light. Consuming oxidized (rancid) oil is not only unhelpful but may harm the body through the formation of free radicals. It is important to choose quality, fresh products and refrigerate them if needed.

### 3. Heavy metal contamination
Fish can accumulate toxins from the marine environment, such as mercury. Cheap, unfiltered omega-3 supplements may contain residues of these toxins. Quality brands undergo molecular distillation, which ensures they are clean of heavy metals.

## Summary

Omega-3 is one of the most basic and important supplements for maintaining general health, with a strong emphasis on the heart and brain. For people who don't eat oily sea fish (such as [salmon](/en/food/salmon/), [mackerel](/en/food/mackerel/), [sardines](/en/food/sardines/)) at least twice a week, supplementation is a common recommendation. The key to success is consistency and choosing a quality, clean product with an adequate dose of EPA and DHA.
