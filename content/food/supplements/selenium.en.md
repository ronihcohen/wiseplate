## Selenium

**Selenium is a trace mineral adults need 55 micrograms of a day, and the safe upper limit is 400 micrograms — an unusually narrow margin. Fish, eggs and chicken cover the need, and a single Brazil nut can supply all of it. A supplement is justified only in deficiency: in large trials it did not prevent cancer and may even have raised diabetes risk.** In Hebrew it is "סלניום". [NIH fact sheet on selenium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/).

Selenium is part of about 25 proteins in the body (selenoproteins), including antioxidant enzymes and the enzymes that activate thyroid hormone. How much of it a food contains depends almost entirely on the soil the crop grew in or the animal fed on, so it varies widely between regions.

### How much you need a day

| Age | Recommended intake a day (RDA) | Tolerable upper limit a day (UL) |
|---|---|---|
| Infants 0–6 months | 15 mcg (AI) | 45 mcg |
| Infants 7–12 months | 20 mcg (AI) | 60 mcg |
| 1–3 | 20 mcg | 90 mcg |
| 4–8 | 30 mcg | 150 mcg |
| 9–13 | 40 mcg | 280 mcg |
| 14+ | 55 mcg | 400 mcg |
| Pregnancy | 60 mcg | 400 mcg |
| Breastfeeding | 70 mcg | 400 mcg |

The ratio between the recommended intake and the upper limit is less than 8. For comparison, for [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) it is more than 20.

### Food sources

| Source | Serving | Selenium |
|---|---|---|
| [Brazil nut](/en/food/brazil-nuts/) | 1 nut (about 5 g) | 68–91 mcg on average, highly variable |
| Yellowfin [tuna](/en/food/tuna/), cooked | 85 g | about 92 mcg |
| [Oysters](/en/food/oysters/) | 100 g | about 75 mcg |
| Canned [sardines](/en/food/sardines/) | 85 g | about 45 mcg |
| [Shrimp](/en/food/shrimp/) | 85 g | about 42 mcg |
| [Chicken breast](/en/food/chicken-breast/) | 85 g | about 22 mcg |
| [Egg](/en/food/eggs/) | 1 large | about 15 mcg |

About Brazil nuts: the NIH figure is an average, but content varies several-fold between nuts and orchards. In a New Zealand trial, two Brazil nuts a day that were expected to provide about 100 mcg actually delivered 53 mcg on average (20–84) — and still raised blood selenium as much as a 100 mcg supplement. [Thomson et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18258628/). The takeaway: one or two nuts a day are enough; a daily handful is not needed.

### Benefits

- **Thyroid:** The thyroid holds the highest concentration of selenium in the body. The enzymes that convert T4 into the active hormone T3 depend on selenium, alongside [iodine](/en/food/supplements/iodine/).
- **Antioxidant defence:** Selenium is the core of the glutathione peroxidase enzymes, which neutralise hydrogen peroxide and lipid peroxides and work together with [vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/).
- **Immune function:** Selenium deficiency weakens the immune response. In selenium-poor regions of China, Keshan disease, a heart-muscle disease, was described and was prevented by supplementation.
- **Hashimoto's — antibodies yes, clinical benefit unclear:** A meta-analysis found that selenium supplements lower TPO antibodies in Hashimoto's thyroiditis, mainly in the first three months. [Wichman et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27702392/). But a review by the same group found insufficient evidence of clinical improvement — in thyroid function, gland structure or quality of life. [Winther et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683225/).

### Downsides and side effects

- **The SELECT trial and prostate cancer:** In a trial of about 35,000 men, 200 mcg of selenium a day did not prevent prostate or other cancers, and the trial was stopped early for lack of benefit. [Lippman et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19066370/). Extended follow-up later showed that prostate cancer was 17% more common in the vitamin E arm. [Klein et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21990298/).
- **Type 2 diabetes:** In a randomised trial of 1,202 people, 200 mcg of selenium a day for about 7.7 years did not prevent diabetes and was linked to a higher risk (hazard ratio 1.55), especially in people whose selenium level was already high. [Stranges et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17620655/). Diabetes was a secondary outcome, but the finding reinforces the rule: supplement only when deficient.
- **Selenosis — selenium poisoning:** Long-term excess causes hair loss, brittle nails, rash, fatigue, digestive upset and garlic-smelling breath. Poisonings have been described from supplements manufactured wrongly — in one US case, at roughly 200 times the labelled dose.
- **Medications:** Cisplatin chemotherapy can lower selenium levels. Anyone on regular medication who is considering a supplement should check with their doctor.

### Practical use

- **From food:** A serving of fish, two eggs or a serving of chicken a day covers much of the need.
- **Brazil nuts:** One or two a day — not a habitual handful.
- **Supplements:** Only for proven deficiency or on a doctor's advice. A typical multivitamin contains tens of micrograms, and combining it with Brazil nuts and a separate selenium pill can approach the upper limit.
- **Hashimoto's:** If you're considering selenium, decide with your endocrinologist; it does not replace thyroid hormone treatment.

### Summary

Selenium is essential for the thyroid, antioxidant defence and the immune system, and it is easy to get from food: fish, shellfish, eggs, chicken and Brazil nuts. The window between need and upper limit is narrow, and large trials showed that supplementing people who aren't deficient did not prevent cancer and may have raised diabetes risk. So the rule is simple: food first, one or two nuts rather than a handful, and a supplement only for deficiency.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How much selenium do I need a day?**
Answer: 55 micrograms for adults, 60 in pregnancy and 70 while breastfeeding. The safe upper limit is 400 micrograms a day — less than 8 times the requirement.

**Question: How many Brazil nuts can I eat a day?**
Answer: One or two. According to NIH an average nut contains 68–91 mcg, but content varies widely between orchards. Two nuts a day raised blood selenium as much as a supplement, and a regular daily handful can exceed the upper limit.

**Question: What is selenosis?**
Answer: Selenium poisoning from long-term excess. Symptoms include hair loss, brittle nails, rash, fatigue, digestive upset and garlic breath. Most documented cases came from wrongly dosed supplements.

**Question: Does selenium help the thyroid?**
Answer: It is essential to it. In Hashimoto's, supplements lower TPO antibodies, but they have not been shown to improve thyroid function or how people feel. It is a decision to make with your doctor.

**Question: Does a selenium supplement prevent cancer?**
Answer: No. In the SELECT trial of about 35,000 men, 200 mcg a day did not prevent prostate or other cancers, and the trial was stopped early.

**Question: Is selenium linked to diabetes?**
Answer: In a randomised trial, 200 mcg a day for years did not prevent type 2 diabetes and was linked to a higher risk, mainly in people whose levels were already high. That is another reason not to supplement without a deficiency.

**Question: Which foods are rich in selenium?**
Answer: Brazil nuts, tuna, oysters, sardines, shrimp, chicken and eggs. A serving of fish or two eggs a day covers much of the need.
