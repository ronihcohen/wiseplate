L-Theanine (in Hebrew, "ל-תיאנין"; chemically gamma-glutamylethylamide) is a unique amino acid that is not used to build proteins in the body, but functions instead as a neuromodulator. It was first discovered in 1949 by Japanese scientists in tea leaves, and it is responsible for the delicate, slightly sweet "umami" taste of high-quality [green tea](/en/food/green-tea/) (such as gyokuro or matcha).

### How does it affect the brain?
L-theanine is a small molecule that can cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and directly affect electrical and chemical activity in the brain:
1.  **Alpha waves:** It increases the production of alpha waves in the brain. These waves, at a frequency of 8-14 hertz, are linked to a state of "relaxed alertness" — the state we experience in moments of creativity, deep meditation, or just before falling asleep.
2.  **Neurotransmitters:** It raises levels of GABA (the brain's main calming agent), dopamine (motivation and pleasure) and serotonin (mood).

### The winning combination (the stack): [caffeine](/en/food/supplements/caffeine/) + theanine
This is probably the most famous and safest supplement combination for cognitive enhancement (a nootropic stack).
The ideal ratio is usually 1:2 in favor of theanine (for example: 100 mg of [caffeine](/en/food/supplements/caffeine/) + 200 mg of theanine).
*   **[Caffeine](/en/food/supplements/caffeine/):** Stimulating, sharpening, energizing. The downside: it causes jitters, anxiety, a rise in blood pressure and a "crash".
*   **Theanine:** Calms overstimulated glutamate receptors, lowers blood pressure, and smooths out the "edge" of [caffeine](/en/food/supplements/caffeine/).
**The result:** Clean, sharp and calm focus for hours, without the jittery feeling.

### Frequently asked questions about L-Theanine

**Question: Does it make you sleepy?**
Answer: **Not exactly.** Unlike sleeping pills or sedating supplements (such as melatonin), theanine does not cause forced drowsiness (sedation). It causes relaxation. You can take it during the day and stay sharp and focused at work. When used before bed, it helps "quiet" intrusive thoughts and eases the transition into sleep, but it does not "knock you out".

**Question: Are there side effects?**
Answer: Theanine is considered one of the safest supplements there are (GRAS status from the FDA). Studies at very high doses have shown no toxicity. However, some people may experience mild headaches or dizziness, and sometimes (paradoxically) overstimulation. In addition, it lowers blood pressure slightly, so people taking blood pressure medication should consult a doctor.

**Question: How long does the effect last?**
Answer: The effect is usually felt within 30-45 minutes and lasts about 4 to 6 hours.

**Question: Is it addictive like [caffeine](/en/food/supplements/caffeine/)?**
Answer: **No.** There is no known tolerance to theanine and no risk of physical addiction. There is no need to increase the dose over time to get the same effect.

**Question: Can you get enough from drinking tea alone?**
Answer: A regular cup of green tea contains only about 8 to 20 mg of theanine. The effective dose in studies (for calm and focus) is usually 100-200 mg. So to reach a noticeable therapeutic effect, you usually need a concentrated supplement or high-quality matcha (which contains more theanine because it is grown in the shade).
