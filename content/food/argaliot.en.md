## Argaliot (Filled Cookies)

**Argaliot are small shortcrust cookies filled with fruit jam or chocolate: one cookie weighs about 8 g and provides about 35–40 calories, and 100 g usually contains about 25–35 g of sugar.** They are an industrial baked product high in sugar and fat and low in fibre, so they suit an occasional snack rather than a daily one. In Hebrew they are called "ערגליות" (singular "ערגלית", argalit).

In English they are usually described as filled cookies. In Israel they are sold under many brands and with various fillings — strawberry, plum, date and chocolate — and often end up in the shopping basket of families with children.

### Nutrition facts

Fruit-filled argaliot (approximate; values vary between brands — check the label):

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Per 5 cookies (about 40 g) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calories | about 430–470 | about 175–190 |
| Carbohydrate | about 65–70 g | about 26–28 g |
| of which sugars | about 25–35 g | about 10–14 g |
| Fat | about 15–20 g | about 6–8 g |
| Protein | about 5 g | about 2 g |
| Fibre | about 1–2 g | under 1 g |

Five cookies provide about half of the World Health Organization's stricter target of about 25 g of free sugar a day. [WHO — Guideline: sugars intake](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549028)

### What they are made of

The dough is based on white wheat flour, sugar and vegetable fat (usually palm oil or margarine). The filling is mostly sugar, glucose syrup, fruit purée, starch and stabilisers that keep its shape during baking. Because of these industrial ingredients, argaliot count as ultra-processed food under the NOVA system. [Monteiro 2019, Public Health Nutr](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30744710/)

### Benefits

* **Quick energy:** a ready source of carbohydrate before activity or on a long hike.
* **Long shelf life:** no refrigeration needed, and easy to carry.
* **A small, defined portion:** each cookie is small, and it is easy to count how many you eat.
* **Most types are pareve:** suitable after a meat meal under kosher rules and usually for vegans — check the label.
* **Enjoyment:** an occasional cookie with coffee or tea is a legitimate part of a balanced diet.

### Downsides

* **A lot of sugar in a small volume:** it is easy to eat a handful without noticing, and several teaspoons of sugar with it.
* **Low nutrient density:** little fibre, protein, vitamins or minerals relative to the calories.
* **Low satiety:** with little fibre and protein they are not very filling, and it is easy to keep eating. In a controlled trial, an ultra-processed diet led people to eat about 500 extra calories a day. [Hall 2019, Cell Metab](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105044/)
* **Saturated fat:** palm oil and margarine raise the saturated fat content. Older recipes also used partially hydrogenated fat, which contains trans fats — look for "hydrogenated fat" on the ingredient list.
* **Red label:** because of their sugar content, most products of this type carry Israel's red "high in sugar" symbol.
* **Teeth:** the sticky filling stays on the teeth, as with other sweets.

### Practical use

* **Set a portion:** 2–3 cookies on a plate, not straight from the pack.
* **Pair them with something filling:** a glass of milk, a yogurt or a handful of [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/) — the protein and fat slow eating down.
* **Not a daily school snack:** fruit, a sandwich or cut vegetables are better choices.
* **Compare labels:** the differences between brands in sugar and fat are significant.
* **A homemade alternative:** home-baked cookies with part wholemeal flour, less sugar and a filling of ground [dates](/en/food/dates/).

### Summary

Argaliot are an industrial cookie high in sugar and fat, with little fibre or protein. One cookie is not a problem, but it is easy to eat five or ten in one sitting, and that approaches the daily sugar target. The way to enjoy them is in a fixed amount, alongside something filling, and not as a daily snack.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in one argalit?**
Answer: A fruit-filled argalit weighing about 8 g has about 35–40 calories.

**Question: How much sugar is in argaliot?**
Answer: Usually about 25–35 g per 100 g, or about 2–3 g per cookie. Five cookies provide about half of the WHO's stricter daily target.

**Question: Are argaliot vegan?**
Answer: Most standard types are pareve and contain no animal ingredients, but some fillings or brands include milk or eggs. Check the current label.

**Question: What are the main ingredients in argaliot?**
Answer: Wheat flour, sugar, vegetable fat, glucose syrup, fruit purée (in fruit fillings), starch, raising agents and flavourings.

**Question: Are argaliot suitable for children?**
Answer: As an occasional treat, yes. As a daily snack they deliver a lot of sugar and little nutrition, and fruit, yogurt or a sandwich is a better swap.

**Question: Are chocolate argaliot less healthy than fruit-filled ones?**
Answer: The difference is small. Chocolate filling usually has a little more fat, and fruit filling a little more sugar; either way, it is a sweet.
