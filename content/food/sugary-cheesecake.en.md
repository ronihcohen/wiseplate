## Sugar-Rich Cheesecake

Cheesecake (in Hebrew, "עוגת גבינה") is one of the few desserts that contains a non-trivial amount of protein — 5-6 grams per 100 grams — and yet a standard slice easily tops 400 calories. That isn't a contradiction: it simply contains **everything** — protein, fat and sugar together.

On this page, two things worth knowing: **exactly where the calories are** (hint: not only in the filling), and **the nutritional difference between baked cheesecake and the no-bake cheesecake** common in Israel — a difference that is bigger than it seems.

### Nutrition facts per 100 grams

| Nutrient | Baked (New York style) | No-bake (instant pudding and cream base) |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | 321 | 300-340 |
| Protein | 5.5 g | 3-4 g |
| Carbohydrates | 25.5 g | 30-34 g |
| **of which sugars** | **22 g** | **26-30 g** |
| Fat | 22.5 g | 20-23 g |
| **of which saturated** | **10 g (50%)** | **13-17 g (65-85%)** |
| [Calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) | ~50 mg | ~40 mg |
| [Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) | 438 mg | 300-400 mg |

**A realistic slice** is 120-150 grams: that is, **385-480 calories and 26-33 grams of sugar**. The maximum daily recommendation for added sugar for an adult is 25-50 grams — **one slice uses it up**.

### No-bake or baked — the surprising difference

In Israel, "cheesecake" usually refers to the **no-bake** version: soft white [cheese](/en/food/cheese/), sweet cream or [non-dairy whipped topping](/en/food/plant-whipped-cream/), and instant vanilla pudding powder as a stabilizer. The baked version, New York style, is based on **cream cheese and eggs** that set in the oven.

The essential differences:

* **Protein:** baked wins — the eggs and full-fat cheese provide 5.5 grams per 100 grams, compared with 3-4 in no-bake.
* **Sugar:** no-bake contains more. Instant pudding powder is mostly sugar and starch, and a significant amount is needed to stabilize it.
* **Saturated fat:** depends entirely on the whipped topping. **A no-bake cake stabilized with non-dairy (pareve) plant-based whipped topping can contain 65-85% of the daily saturated fat allowance per 100 grams** — more than baked — because coconut oil and palm kernel oil are 85-92% saturated.
* **Ingredient list:** baked — 5-6 kitchen ingredients. Commercial no-bake — usually many more.

**The conclusion:** nutritionally, **a baked cake made from cheese and eggs is better than a no-bake cake stabilized with instant pudding and non-dairy topping** — contrary to intuition, which tends to see "no-bake" as the lighter one.

### Where the calories are — a breakdown

| Cake component | Share of calories |
|---|---|
| Cookie crust + butter | 25-30% |
| Fat from the cheese / cream | 40-45% |
| Sugar | 25-30% |
| Topping / fruit sauce | 5-10% |

**The crust** surprises many people: cookie crumbs mixed with melted butter have a calorie density of about 500 calories per 100 grams, higher than the filling itself. **A thinner crust** is one of the simplest ways to cut 60-80 calories from a slice.

### Benefits

* **Protein in a dessert.** 5.5 grams per 100 grams in the baked version — more than almost any cake. The protein also slows sugar absorption, so the glycemic response is more moderate than that of a cake based on flour and sugar alone.
* **[Calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/)** from the cheese, in a modest amount.
* **A cultural role.** Cheesecake is the dessert most associated with **the holiday of Shavuot**, and a dessert in a festive context has a completely legitimate place in the diet.
* **Easy to control portions** — it's cut into defined slices, unlike snacks.
* **A base fully controlled at home.** Unlike industrial pastries, almost every variable in a homemade cake is a choice — the type of cheese, the amount of sugar, the thickness of the crust.

### Downsides and considerations

* **Sugar.** 22-30 grams per 100 grams, and a whole slice uses up the daily recommendation.
* **Saturated fat.** 10 grams per 100 grams in baked, and up to 17 in no-bake with plant-based topping — 50-85% of the daily allowance.
* **High energy density.** 321 calories per 100 grams, with relatively low satiety — the sugar and fat create a dish that's easy to keep eating. A slice contains about as much sugar as a serving of [industrial ice cream](/en/food/industrial-ice-cream/) or [chocolate-coated cookies](/en/food/chocolate-coated-cookies/).
* **Industrial cakes.** They usually contain vegetable fat, stabilizers, flavorings and more sugar, and sometimes a "cheese substitute" instead of cheese.
* **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/).** 438 mg per 100 grams — not something you'd expect in a dessert, and it comes from the cheese and the crust.
* **Toppings and sauces.** Commercial red fruit sauce is mainly sugar and corn starch thickener; it adds 40-70 calories to a slice with no value at all.
* **Protein and calcium are not a justification.** You can get both from [cottage cheese](/en/food/cottage-cheese/) or [Greek yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/) for a fraction of the calories.

### Practical use

* **Bake it, don't stabilize with instant pudding.** Cheese, eggs, a little sugar and an oven — more protein, less sugar and a short ingredient list.
* **Dilute the cheese.** Half cream cheese and half **quark, strained [cottage cheese](/en/food/cottage-cheese/) or strained [Greek yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/)** — saves 25-35% of the calories and adds protein, without a significant change in texture.
* **A thin crust.** Half the amount of cookie crumbs in the recipe is almost always enough, and saves 60-80 calories per slice.
* **Cut a third of the sugar.** In most cheesecake recipes you can cut a third of the sugar without hurting the texture — unlike other baked cakes, where sugar is critical to the structure.
* **Fresh fruit instead of sauce.** [Strawberries](/en/food/strawberry/), [berries](/en/food/blueberries/) or [pomegranate](/en/food/pomegranate/) on the cake — color, tartness and fiber instead of syrup.
* **One slice, not two.** Slice the whole cake in advance and freeze some — cheesecake keeps very well frozen.
* **When buying ready-made:** look for "cheese" on the ingredient list, not "cheese substitute" or "vegetable fat".
* **Eat it after a meal** rather than as a standalone snack — the glycemic response is much more moderate.

### Summary

Cheesecake is a dessert with 321 calories and 22 grams of sugar per 100 grams, meaning **a slice that uses up the daily sugar allowance**. Two things are worth knowing: **the crust accounts for 25-30% of the calories** — more than it seems, and trimming it is the easiest improvement; and **no-bake cheesecake stabilized with instant pudding and non-dairy topping is not "lighter" than baked** — it usually contains more sugar, less protein and sometimes more saturated fat. The homemade baked version, with half the cheese replaced by strained Greek yogurt and a thin crust, is the same dessert — at two thirds of the load.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a slice of cheesecake?**
Answer: About 321 calories per 100 grams, and a realistic slice of 120-150 grams comes to 385-480 calories and 26-33 grams of sugar. That uses up the maximum daily recommendation for added sugar for an adult (25-50 grams) in a single item.

**Question: Which is better — no-bake or baked cheesecake?**
Answer: Nutritionally, baked — contrary to intuition. It contains more protein (5.5 grams vs. 3-4 per 100 grams) thanks to the eggs and cheese, and less sugar, because it doesn't need instant pudding powder, which is mostly sugar and starch. A no-bake cake stabilized with non-dairy plant-based topping may also contain more saturated fat, because coconut and palm oils are 85-92% saturated.

**Question: Where are the calories in cheesecake?**
Answer: About 40-45% from the fat in the cheese and cream, 25-30% from the sugar, and 25-30% from the crust — cookie crumbs in butter, at about 500 calories per 100 grams, denser than the filling itself. Halving the crust is the easiest saving: 60-80 calories per slice.

**Question: How do you make a healthier cheesecake?**
Answer: Four changes that work together. Replace half the cream cheese with quark, strained cottage cheese or strained Greek yogurt — saves 25-35% of the calories and adds protein. Thin the crust by half. Cut a third of the sugar, which is possible in cheesecake without hurting the texture. And serve with fresh fruit instead of commercial fruit sauce.

**Question: Does cheesecake contain protein?**
Answer: Yes, and that is unusual in the world of desserts — 5.5 grams per 100 grams in the baked version, or about 7-8 grams per slice. The protein also slows sugar absorption, so the glycemic response is more moderate than that of a cake based on flour and sugar. That's no reason to eat cheesecake instead of protein — Greek yogurt provides twice as much in a sixth of the calories.

**Question: How much sugar is in cheesecake?**
Answer: 22 grams per 100 grams in the baked version and 26-30 in no-bake, meaning 26-45 grams in a whole slice. The maximum daily recommendation for an adult is 25-50 grams of added sugar. In a homemade cake you can cut a third of the sugar without hurting the texture, because unlike other baked cakes it isn't critical to the structure.

**Question: How do you choose a store-bought cheesecake?**
Answer: Read the ingredient list and look for "cheese", not "cheese substitute" or "vegetable-fat-based product". A short list, without stabilizers and flavorings, usually indicates a product closer to a homemade cake. It's also worth comparing sugar and saturated fat per 100 grams between products — the differences in the category are large.

**Question: Can you freeze cheesecake?**
Answer: Yes, and it is one of the cakes that does especially well. Cut it into slices, wrap each one separately and freeze. This is also a practical solution for portion control: one slice comes out of the freezer, and the rest stay there. Thawing in the fridge takes about two hours.
