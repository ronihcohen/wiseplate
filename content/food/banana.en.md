## Banana

**A medium banana (about 118 g peeled) provides roughly 105 calories, 27 g of carbohydrate, 3 g of fiber and about 420 mg of potassium. It is not fattening in itself; a green banana contains more resistant starch and less sugar, and during long endurance exercise bananas fueled cyclists as well as a sports drink did.**

The banana (*Musa*, in Hebrew "בננה") is one of the most widely eaten fruits in the world: cheap, naturally packaged, and with no need to wash or cut it. It is a good source of available carbohydrate, [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) and [vitamin B6](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b6/), and its composition changes a great deal as it ripens.

### Nutrition facts

Per 100 g of ripe banana (peeled), and for a medium banana of about 118 g. Values are rounded.

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Medium banana (118 g) |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | 89 | about 105 |
| Carbohydrate | 22.8 g | about 27 g |
| Sugars | 12.2 g | about 14 g |
| Dietary fiber | 2.6 g | about 3.1 g |
| Protein | 1.1 g | about 1.3 g |
| Fat | 0.3 g | about 0.4 g |
| [Potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) | 358 mg | about 420 mg |
| [Vitamin B6](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b6/) | 0.37 mg | about 0.43 mg |
| [Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) | 27 mg | about 32 mg |
| [Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) | 8.7 mg | about 10 mg |

As a banana ripens, starch turns into sugar: in a green banana most of the carbohydrate is starch, some of it resistant to digestion, while in a spotted banana most of it is sugar. The calories barely change; what changes is how fast the sugar reaches the blood.

### Benefits

* **Potassium and blood pressure.** A medium banana provides about 420 mg of potassium, roughly 12-16% of the adequate intake for adults (3,400 mg for men and 2,600 mg for women). Adequate potassium intake is associated with lower blood pressure, particularly when sodium intake is high. [NIH ODS: Potassium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Potassium-HealthProfessional/). Bananas are a convenient source but not the only one: [sweet potato](/en/food/sweet-potato/), legumes and [beets](/en/food/beets/) provide similar or larger amounts.

* **Fuel for endurance exercise.** In a crossover trial, trained cyclists rode 75 km once with bananas and once with a carbohydrate drink, at the same carbohydrate dose (0.2 g per kg of body weight every 15 minutes). Performance, blood glucose and inflammation markers were similar in both conditions. [Nieman et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22616015/). In practice, a banana is a cheap, natural alternative to a gel or drink, provided your stomach tolerates it during effort.

* **Whole fruit and diabetes risk.** In an analysis of three large cohort studies, every three servings of banana a week was associated with a 5% lower risk of type 2 diabetes. [Muraki et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990623/). This is an observational association, not proof, but it does contradict the idea that bananas are "forbidden" because of their sugar.

* **Resistant starch in green bananas.** Resistant starch is not digested in the small intestine and reaches the colon, where bacteria ferment it much as they ferment fiber. That is why a green banana raises blood sugar more slowly than a ripe one.

* **Vitamin B6.** A medium banana provides about a quarter of the recommended daily intake of vitamin B6, which takes part in protein metabolism and in making neurotransmitters. That does not mean bananas "improve your mood"; it is simply a useful nutritional contribution.

### Downsides

* **Kidney disease and certain medications.** People with advanced kidney disease, or who take drugs that raise potassium levels (such as ACE inhibitors or potassium-sparing diuretics), can build up potassium in the blood. For them, how many bananas to eat should be decided with a doctor or dietitian. [NIH ODS: Potassium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Potassium-HealthProfessional/).

* **Sugar in very ripe bananas.** A spotted banana contains mostly simple sugars and raises blood glucose faster. For people with diabetes, a yellow-greenish banana in a smaller portion, eaten with protein or fat, is the better choice.

* **Bloating in some people.** Resistant starch in green bananas can cause gas at first. Firm, unripe bananas are considered low-FODMAP, while very ripe bananas contain more fructans.

* **Allergy.** Banana allergy is rare but real, and is sometimes linked to latex allergy.

* **No need to overdo it.** Bananas are a good fruit, but nothing in them justifies eating five a day at the expense of other fruit. Variety is better.

### Practical use

* **Before training:** a banana 30-60 minutes before a workout supplies available carbohydrate without weighing on the stomach.
* **During long sessions:** half a banana every 30-45 minutes is an easy way to refuel, depending on personal tolerance.
* **Breakfast:** a sliced banana on [oatmeal](/en/food/oatmeal/) replaces added sugar.
* **Overripe bananas:** freeze the slices and use them in smoothies or blended into "banana ice cream".
* **Storage:** at room temperature. In the fridge the peel turns black, but the fruit itself keeps longer.
* **Smart pairing:** a banana with yogurt, nuts or [peanut butter](/en/food/peanut-butter/) slows the rise in blood sugar and keeps you full for longer.
* **Baking:** mashed ripe banana can replace part of the sugar and fat in muffins and pancakes, since it adds sweetness and moisture.

### Summary

The banana is a cheap, convenient source of carbohydrate, potassium and vitamin B6. It suits the time before and during exercise especially well, and there is no reason to avoid it because of its sugar when you eat the whole fruit in reasonable portions. Be careful with quantities if you have kidney disease or take medication that raises potassium.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a banana?**
Answer: About 89 calories per 100 g, so roughly 105 calories for a medium banana (118 g peeled). A large banana reaches about 120 calories.

**Question: What is the difference between green and yellow bananas?**
Answer: A green banana has more resistant starch and less sugar, and raises blood glucose more slowly. In a yellow, spotted banana the starch has turned into sugar; it is sweeter and easier to digest. The calories are almost the same.

**Question: Are bananas fattening?**
Answer: Not in themselves. About 105 calories per banana is less than most processed snacks. As with any food, total daily calories are what count.

**Question: Are bananas good for heartburn?**
Answer: Many people find a ripe banana gentle on the stomach because it is soft and not acidic. There is no strong research evidence for this, and responses vary from person to person.

**Question: Do bananas help with muscle cramps?**
Answer: The link between dietary potassium and exercise cramps is not well established. Cramps are mainly related to muscle fatigue and load, not only to minerals.

**Question: Can people with diabetes eat bananas?**
Answer: Yes, in a portion that fits their carbohydrate counting. A less ripe banana, half a banana, or a banana with protein works better.

**Question: Can you eat too many bananas?**
Answer: For a healthy person with normal kidney function, a few bananas a day are not dangerous. The risk from high potassium is relevant mainly in kidney disease or with certain medications.
