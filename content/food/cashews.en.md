## Cashews

The cashew (*Anacardium occidentale*; in Hebrew "קשיו") is a tropical nut native to Brazil and now widely grown in India, Vietnam and Africa. Botanically it is not a true nut but a "botanical nut" — a seed that grows outside the fruit known as the "cashew apple." The cashew is one of the best-selling nuts in the world, both as a snack and as a base for vegan alternatives to [milk](/en/food/milk/) and [cheese](/en/food/cheese/).

### Research-based health benefits

* **Monounsaturated fats:** About 60% of the cashew's fat content is MUFA — mainly oleic acid (as in [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)). These fats are linked to lower LDL, an improved cholesterol profile and support for heart health.

* **[Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/):** 28 grams of cashews provide about 20% of daily intake. Supports energy production, muscle function and sleep quality.

* **Copper:** Cashews are one of the best plant sources of [copper](/en/food/supplements/copper/) — 28 grams provide about 70% of daily intake. Copper is essential for the activity of the enzyme superoxide dismutase (SOD) — a key cellular antioxidant — and for iron absorption.

* **[Zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) and [iron](/en/food/iron/):** Contribute to immunity and anemia prevention, especially for vegans.

* **[Vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/):** One of the nuts relatively rich in vitamin K, essential for blood clotting and bone health.

* **Insulin sensitivity:** Preliminary studies found that cashew extract may support [insulin](/en/food/supplements/insulin/) sensitivity and glucose regulation, possibly due to its unique [polyphenol](/en/food/polyphenols/) content.

* **Protein:** 28 grams provide about 5 grams of protein — less than peanuts and almonds, but still a significant contribution in a plant-based diet.

### Downsides and health considerations

* **Urushiol:** The truly raw cashew contains urushiol in its shell — the same toxin that causes the itch from poison ivy. "Raw" cashews sold in stores have always undergone heat processing. Even so, a small amount may remain and irritate sensitive people.

* **High calorie density:** 28 grams = about 160 calories. Uncontrolled snacking can lead to a significant calorie surplus.

* **Allergy:** Cashew is one of the most severe allergens among nuts — an anaphylactic reaction is possible. It cross-reacts with pistachio (both from the Anacardiaceae family).

* **Phytic acid:** Reduces mineral absorption. Soaking and roasting reduce its concentration.

* **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) in salted versions:** Roasted, salted cashews may contain a high amount of sodium — prefer a natural version without added salt.

### Culinary and practical use

* **As a straight snack:** Natural with no salt — the healthiest way
* **"Cashew cheese":** Soaked and blended — a base for vegan cheeses and creams
* **"Cashew milk":** A smooth, rich drink, creamier than almond milk
* **In creamy sauces:** In Asian cooking — replaces cream in stews
* **In a tossed salad:** Adds crunch and protein
* **Cashew butter:** An interesting alternative to peanut butter, with a milder taste

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Are "raw" cashews really raw?**
Answer: No — the "raw" cashews sold in stores have always undergone heat processing to remove the urushiol. Truly raw cashews are not safe to eat.

**Question: Are cashews suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: Yes — in a controlled amount. Despite their calorie density, cashews have a relatively low GI and don't cause a sharp spike in blood sugar.

**Question: How many cashews a day are recommended?**
Answer: 28-30 grams (about a handful) — enough for nutritional benefit without overdoing the calories.

**Question: Is cashew butter as healthy as peanut butter?**
Answer: Similar — cashew butter contains less protein but more copper and magnesium. Both are good options, preferably in versions without added sugar and salt.

### Summary

The cashew is a nutritious, versatile nut with an exceptional concentration of copper and magnesium. As a daily snack in a controlled amount, and as a base for vegan alternatives, it is a worthwhile nutritional addition — as long as you choose a natural version without additives.
