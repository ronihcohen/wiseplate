## Cashews

**Cashews are a soft, slightly sweet nut: a 28 g handful provides about 155 calories, 5 g of protein, about a fifth of the daily magnesium requirement and around 70% of the daily copper requirement. In a randomised trial in people with diabetes, 30 g of cashews a day modestly lowered systolic blood pressure without raising weight or blood sugar.** They contain more carbohydrate and less fibre than most nuts.

The cashew (*Anacardium occidentale*, in Hebrew "קשיו") is native to Brazil and is now grown mainly in India, Vietnam and West Africa. Botanically it is a seed that grows inside a hard shell at the bottom end of a fleshy fruit, the "cashew apple". The shell contains a caustic oil, so every cashew sold has been heated and shelled. It is one of the best-selling nuts in the world, both as a snack and as a base for vegan alternatives to [milk](/en/food/milk/) and [cheese](/en/food/cheese/).

### Benefits

- **Blood pressure and HDL.** In a randomised trial in India, 300 adults with type 2 diabetes received standard dietary advice, and half of them also received 30 g of cashews a day for 12 weeks. In the cashew group systolic blood pressure fell by 4.9 mmHg versus 1.7 in the control group, and HDL cholesterol rose slightly. There was no change in weight, LDL or blood sugar. [Mohan et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29378038/).
- **Unsaturated fat.** About 60% of the fat is monounsaturated, mainly oleic acid, as in [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/). In observational studies, eating nuts is associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular disease and death. [Aune et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27916000/).
- **Copper.** Cashews are one of the best sources of copper, which is needed for antioxidant enzymes, for making red blood cells and for using iron.
- **[Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/), [iron](/en/food/iron/) and [zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/).** A handful makes a fair contribution, which matters especially for vegans. Plant iron is absorbed less well than iron from meat, and pairing it with vitamin C improves absorption.
- **Fewer calories absorbed.** A systematic review found that the body extracts less energy from nuts, cashews included, than the food tables calculate, because some of the fat is not released from the cells. [Adv Nutr, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36934832/).

### Downsides

- **Allergy.** Cashew is one of the more severe tree nut allergens, and anaphylaxis is possible even from a small amount. Cross-reactivity with pistachio is common, as both belong to the same plant family. People who are allergic need to check sauces, pesto and vegan products too.
- **Urushiol.** The shell contains urushiol, the same compound that causes the poison ivy rash. Cashews on sale, even those labelled "raw", have been heated to remove most of it, but workers who shell them by hand get burns on their hands. A truly raw cashew, straight from the shell, is not safe to eat.
- **Calories.** Cashews are soft and pleasant, and it is easy to eat several handfuls. 100 g is about 555 calories by the tables.
- **More carbohydrate, less fibre.** Compared with almonds or walnuts they have two to three times as much carbohydrate and little fibre. Even so, the effect on blood sugar is small.
- **Salt and oil.** Roasted salted cashews add [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/), and honey-coated or fried cashews are a sweet snack.
- **Oxalates and phytic acid.** As with most nuts, cashews contain oxalate and phytic acid. In normal amounts this is not significant, but people prone to kidney stones should not build their diet around cashew "cheeses" and "creams".

### Practical use

- **How many a day?** A handful of 28–30 g, about 15–20 nuts, which is also the amount tested in the trial.
- **Cashew "cream" and "cheese".** Cashews soaked for a few hours and blended with water give a smooth cream that replaces dairy cream in sauces and soups. The nutrition differs from dairy: less protein and calcium, more unsaturated fat.
- **Cashew milk.** Creamier than almond milk, but it also contains few nuts and little protein. Choose a calcium-fortified, unsweetened version.
- **In Asian and Indian cooking.** Ground cashews thicken curries and stews, and roasted cashews add crunch to stir-fries and salads.
- **Cashew butter.** A milder alternative to peanut butter, with slightly less protein.
- **Storage.** In a sealed jar in a cool place, or in the fridge for longer storage.

### Summary

Cashews are a nutritious nut with a particularly high copper content and a fair amount of magnesium, iron and zinc. A randomised trial showed a small fall in blood pressure and a small rise in HDL without weight gain. They have more carbohydrate and less fibre than other nuts, and they are among the more severe allergens. A handful a day, unsalted, is the way to enjoy them.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a handful of cashews?**
Answer: About 155 calories in 28 g, around 18 nuts. In practice the body absorbs a little less, because some of the fat is not released from the cells.

**Question: Are "raw" cashews really raw?**
Answer: No. Every cashew sold has been heated to remove the urushiol in the shell. "Raw" on the pack only means it was not roasted after shelling.

**Question: Are cashews suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: Yes, in controlled amounts. In a randomised trial in people with diabetes, 30 g a day did not raise blood sugar or weight and slightly lowered blood pressure.

**Question: How many cashews should you eat a day?**
Answer: A handful of 28–30 g, about 15–20 nuts. That is enough for the nutritional benefit without excess calories.

**Question: Do cashews make you gain weight?**
Answer: No more than other nuts. In trials, a handful a day did not change body weight. What causes weight gain is several handfuls a day on top of your usual diet.

**Question: Is cashew butter as healthy as peanut butter?**
Answer: About the same. Cashew butter has slightly less protein and more copper. For both, choose a version without added sugar and salt.

**Question: If I am allergic to cashews, should I avoid pistachios?**
Answer: Often yes, because cross-reactivity between them is common. The decision should be made by an allergist, based on testing.
