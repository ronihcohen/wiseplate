## Cucumber

**100 g of cucumber with the peel contain about 15 calories and about 95 g of water, with a little vitamin K and potassium. It is not a vitamin-rich food; its real value is a large volume for almost no calories, and in a controlled trial a low-calorie salad at the start of a meal reduced the total calories eaten at that meal. A very bitter cucumber is best thrown away.**

The cucumber (*Cucumis sativus*, in Hebrew "מלפפון") belongs to the gourd family, together with [zucchini](/en/food/zucchini/), [pumpkin](/en/food/pumpkin/), melon and [watermelon](/en/food/watermelon/). In Israel the most common type is the small cucumber (often called "Israeli" or "Persian"), with thin skin and few seeds; there are also long English cucumbers and small pickling cucumbers.

### Nutrition facts

Per 100 g of raw cucumber with the peel, and for three small cucumbers (about 300 g), the amount in a large bowl of salad. Values are rounded.

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | 3 small cucumbers (300 g) |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | about 15 | about 45 |
| Water | 95.2 g | about 285 g |
| Carbohydrate | 3.6 g | about 11 g |
| of which sugar | 1.7 g | about 5 g |
| Dietary fiber | 0.5 g | about 1.5 g |
| Protein | 0.65 g | about 2 g |
| [Vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/) | 16.4 mcg | about 49 mcg |
| [Potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) | 147 mg | about 440 mg |
| [Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) | 13 mg | about 39 mg |

Most of the fiber and vitamin K sit in the peel and seeds. A peeled cucumber is mostly water.

### Benefits

* **Volume and fullness without calories.** In a controlled trial, 42 women ate a first-course salad before pasta at lunch, or no salad. A low-calorie salad cut total meal calories by 7% (small portion) and 12% (large portion), while a salad with lots of dressing and cheese actually increased them. [Rolls et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15389416/). Cucumber is one of the natural ingredients for such a salad.

* **Contribution to daily fluids.** About 95% of a cucumber is water. Water-rich foods contribute to total fluid intake, especially in summer. That does not make it "better hydration than water", just another source.

* **Vitamin K and potassium.** A salad bowl with three cucumbers supplies about half the recommended daily intake of vitamin K and roughly 10-15% of the potassium.

* **Part of your vegetable intake.** In a meta-analysis of cohort studies, every extra 200 g a day of fruit and vegetables was associated with a lower risk of heart disease and death. [Aune et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28338764/). Cucumber is not a "healing" food, but it makes reaching your daily vegetable target easier.

* **A snack with almost nothing to count.** A whole cucumber is a crunchy snack of under 20 calories, suitable for children, the office and anyone counting calories.

### Downsides

* **Bitter cucumber: don't eat it.** The bitterness comes from cucurbitacins, natural compounds of the gourd family. In high amounts they cause diarrhea, vomiting and abdominal pain. In a series of 353 cases of poisoning from bitter squash in France, most were mild, but 14 were of moderate severity. [Le Roux et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29323540/). Commercial cucumbers are rarely bitter, but if a bite tastes markedly bitter, throw the cucumber out.

* **Vitamin K and warfarin.** The amount in one cucumber is small, but big salad bowls every day add up. Anyone taking warfarin (Coumadin) should keep their intake steady and avoid sudden changes. [NIH ODS: Vitamin K](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/).

* **Burping and gas.** Some people burp after cucumber, especially varieties with large seeds. It is harmless; seedless varieties or peeling help.

* **Pesticide residues and wax.** Long English cucumbers are sometimes coated in wax. Wash well; if the cucumber is waxed or you are worried about residues, peeling is a reasonable compromise despite losing some fiber.

* **Salty pickles.** Pickled cucumbers contain a lot of sodium. Pickles fermented in brine (rather than vinegar) contain lactic acid bacteria, but they too are best eaten in moderation.

### Practical use

* **Israeli salad:** cucumber, tomato, [onion](/en/food/onion/), lemon and [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/). Add [cheese](/en/food/cheese/) or legumes to turn it into a meal.
* **Salad before the meal:** a bowl of vegetable salad with a little dressing before the main course, as in the Rolls trial.
* **Tzatziki:** grated cucumber with yogurt, garlic and dill.
* **Green gazpacho:** cucumber, avocado, [ginger](/en/food/ginger/) and lemon.
* **Storage:** cucumbers dislike extreme cold; keep them in the vegetable drawer and eat within about a week.
* **Cucumber water:** it adds flavor and helps you drink more, but not "minerals" in any meaningful amount.
* **Quick pickles:** thin cucumber slices in vinegar, water, a pinch of salt and dill keep in the fridge for several days and add crunch with far less salt than store-bought pickles.

### Summary

The cucumber is not a superfood, nor is it "empty": it provides volume, water, a little vitamin K and potassium, with almost no calories. Its big practical advantage is that it makes it easier to eat more vegetables and to cut calories at a meal. Eat it with the peel when you can, and throw out a markedly bitter one.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a cucumber?**
Answer: About 15 calories per 100 g. A small cucumber (about 100 g) provides about 15 calories, and a long English cucumber about 45.

**Question: Is it harmful to eat cucumber and tomato together?**
Answer: No. The claim that the combination "cancels vitamins" has no research basis. An Israeli salad is a good combination.

**Question: Is cucumber with the peel healthier?**
Answer: Yes. The peel holds most of the fiber and vitamin K. Wash it well and eat it.

**Question: Does cucumber help with weight loss?**
Answer: Not directly, but it lets you eat a large volume for few calories. A low-calorie salad at the start of a meal reduced total calories in a controlled trial.

**Question: Why are cucumbers sometimes bitter?**
Answer: Because of cucurbitacins, which build up especially under heat or water stress during growing. A very bitter cucumber is best discarded, because in high amounts these compounds cause diarrhea and vomiting.

**Question: What is the difference between a regular and an English cucumber?**
Answer: The English cucumber is long, with thin skin and few seeds, and is sometimes wrapped in plastic or waxed. Nutritionally they are similar.

**Question: Are pickles as healthy as fresh cucumbers?**
Answer: They contain much more sodium. Fermented pickles add beneficial bacteria, but they are best eaten in moderation.
