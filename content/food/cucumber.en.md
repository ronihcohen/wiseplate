## Cucumber

The cucumber (*Cucumis sativus*; in Hebrew "מלפפון") is about 96% water, which makes it one of the lowest-calorie vegetables there is. Contrary to the perception that it is "nutritionally empty", the cucumber contains active phytochemicals, vitamins and minerals — and above all it is an ideal tool for eating a large volume with minimal calories.

It belongs to the gourd family (Cucurbitaceae) together with [zucchini](/en/food/zucchini/), [pumpkin](/en/food/pumpkin/) and melon, and comes in many varieties: the small Persian cucumber, the long English cucumber, pickling cucumbers (cornichons) and more.

### Research-based health benefits

* **Hydration and electrolyte balance:** 96% water combined with potassium, [magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) and [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) — the cucumber helps keep the body hydrated more efficiently than water alone (because of the minerals). Important in hot weather, sports and conditions that drain fluids.

* **Cucurbitacins:** terpenoids that cause a slight bitterness in the skin and in damaged fruit. They are being studied for anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor and anti-diabetic activity in lab studies — with potential for treating fatty liver disease.

* **[Vitamin K](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-k/):** a cucumber with its skin provides about 16 mcg of vitamin K per 100 grams — about 13% of the daily intake. Essential for blood clotting and bone mineralization.

* **Low in calories — high in volume:** 100 grams of cucumber contain only about 16 calories. As a high-volume vegetable, it lets you "fill" the plate while staying in a calorie deficit — a central principle of the volume-eating approach (Volumetrics).

* **Lignans:** cucumbers contain lariciresinol, pinoresinol and secoisolariciresinol — plant lignans studied in connection with a lower risk of breast, uterine and ovarian cancer.

* **Kaempferol and epicatechin:** flavonoids in moderate concentration with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity.

* **[Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) and [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/):** contribute to blood pressure regulation and to muscle and nerve function.

### Downsides and health considerations

* **Limited nutritional value without the skin:** most of the fiber, the vitamin K and the phytochemicals (including cucurbitacins) are concentrated in the skin. A peeled cucumber is left as mostly water and a little starch. Always eat it with the skin when possible.

* **Pesticides:** cucumbers frequently make the "Dirty Dozen" list — buy organic or wash thoroughly. If not organic — it may be worth peeling.

* **Gas in sensitive people:** cucurbitacins may irritate the digestive system and cause gas in some people — usually in large amounts.

* **Wax coating:** long English cucumbers are sometimes coated with a food-grade wax to keep them fresh — another reason to wash them carefully.

### Culinary and practical use

* **Raw in salads:** the most common way — cucumber, tomato, [onion](/en/food/onion/), Bulgarian [cheese](/en/food/cheese/) and [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/)
* **With cream cheese or tzatziki:** as a dip — a refreshing, balanced snack
* **Pickled cucumber:** friendly probiotics (when pickled by natural fermentation, not just in vinegar)
* **Green gazpacho:** with avocado, [ginger](/en/food/ginger/) and lemon — a rich cold dish
* **As a "cup" for bites:** a large cucumber halved and topped with spreads — a healthy alternative to snacks
* **Cucumber water (infused water):** refreshing and interesting — adds subtle minerals

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Should you eat cucumber and tomato in the same meal?**
Answer: The claim that the combination is harmful has no scientific basis. The body digests a variety of foods at the same time without any problem. You can, and should, combine them.

**Question: Is a cucumber with its skin really healthier?**
Answer: Yes — the skin contains most of the fiber, the vitamin K and the phytochemicals. The difference is nutritionally significant.

**Question: Does cucumber help with weight loss?**
Answer: Directly — no. But because of its very low calorie density, it lets you eat a large volume while consuming few calories, which helps with satiety during weight loss.

**Question: What is the difference between a regular cucumber and a Persian cucumber?**
Answer: The Persian cucumber is smaller, with fewer seeds and thinner skin. Nutritionally they are similar — it is a matter of texture and preference.

**Question: Are dried (crisped) cucumbers healthy?**
Answer: Dried cucumber snacks are more concentrated in nutrients, but also in sugar and calories. Salt is usually added — check the label.

### Summary

The cucumber is an excellent vegetable for filling the plate, for low-calorie snacking and for supporting hydration. It is not a "superfood" — but its role as an everyday staple vegetable, with the skin on and organic when possible, is valuable in a balanced diet.
