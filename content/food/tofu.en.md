## Tofu

**Tofu (in Hebrew "טופו") is made from just three ingredients: soybeans, water and a coagulant.** It is produced in a process reminiscent of [cheese](/en/food/cheese/) making: soybeans are soaked and ground into a drink, which is curdled with a coagulant, and the curds are pressed into molds. It originated in China about two thousand years ago, and it is a major source of protein in East Asian cuisines.

100 grams of firm tofu provide about 144 calories, 15-17 grams of protein, 8 grams of fat and 2-3 grams of carbohydrates. Silken tofu contains less protein (about 5-8 grams) and more water.

### The coagulant determines the calcium content

This is the most important practical point about tofu, and it is almost always missing from the discussion.

There are two main coagulants:

* **Calcium sulfate (gypsum)** — used in most Western industrial tofu. It adds **a large amount of [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/)** — 200-350 mg per 100 grams, an amount comparable to [cheese](/en/food/cheese/) and even higher than [milk](/en/food/milk/) by weight.
* **Nigari (magnesium chloride)** — used in traditional Japanese tofu. It gives a more delicate texture and flavor, but **contributes almost no calcium** — usually less than 50 mg per 100 grams.

The difference is five- to sevenfold. **Tofu is not automatically a "calcium source"** — it depends entirely on the ingredient list. If you rely on tofu as a calcium source on a vegan diet, you must check that calcium sulfate is listed.

Another advantage: the calcium in tofu is well absorbed — about 30%, a rate similar to [milk](/en/food/milk/) — because the oxalate content of soy is low, unlike [spinach](/en/food/spinach/).

### Health benefits

* **High-quality complete protein:** soy protein is one of the few plant proteins that supplies all nine essential amino acids in sufficient amounts. Its PDCAAS score reaches about 0.9-1.0 — on par with [egg](/en/food/eggs/) and [milk](/en/food/milk/) protein, and significantly higher than most plant protein sources. For vegetarians and vegans it is one of the most important sources.

* **Protein for building muscle:** trials comparing soy protein with [whey protein](/en/food/supplements/whey-protein/) found that both support muscle building, although milk protein has a slight edge because of its higher leucine content. The gap closes almost completely when total protein intake is sufficient.

* **Fat profile:** most of the fat in tofu is unsaturated, and it is cholesterol-free. The FDA has approved a health claim that 25 grams of soy protein a day, in a diet low in saturated fat, may reduce the risk of heart disease.

* **Isoflavones:** as described under [soy milk](/en/food/soy-milk/) — phytoestrogens that bind mainly to the estrogen receptor beta, with a potency thousands of times lower than human estrogen. Meta-analyses of controlled trials **found no effect on testosterone or estrogen in men** at dietary amounts. Regarding breast cancer, cohort studies lean toward a protective or neutral effect, and leading organizations do not recommend avoiding it.

* **[Iron](/en/food/iron/) and minerals:** a good source of iron (though it is non-heme and less well absorbed — pairing it with [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) improves absorption), manganese, phosphorus and selenium.

* **Moderate calorie density:** 144 calories per 100 grams with 15-17 grams of protein — a very good protein-to-calorie ratio, suitable for weight management.

* **Minimally processed food:** unlike industrial "meat substitutes," traditional tofu contains just three ingredients: soy, water and a coagulant.

### Downsides and considerations

* **Soy allergy:** one of the leading allergens in children, and it requires complete avoidance.

* **Thyroid and medications:** soy may interfere with the absorption of thyroxine medication. Leave at least 4 hours between taking the medication and eating soy products. In people with normal thyroid function and adequate [iodine](/en/food/supplements/iodine/) intake there is no effect.

* **Phytate:** reduces the absorption of [iron](/en/food/iron/) and [zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/). The production process removes some of it, and including [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) in the meal offsets it.

* **Neutral texture and flavor:** tofu on its own is almost tasteless — a culinary advantage (it absorbs flavors) that is often experienced as a downside. Most failures with tofu come from poor preparation, not from the product.

* **Processed tofu products:** tofu sausages, schnitzels and "flavored" products usually contain significant amounts of [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/), oils and additives. These are entirely different products from plain tofu.

* **Unpredictable calcium content:** as described above — it depends entirely on the coagulant.

### Types of tofu

| Type | Protein (per 100 g) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Silken | 5-8 g | Sauces, desserts, smoothies, miso soups |
| Soft | 8-10 g | Soups, delicate dishes |
| Firm | 15-17 g | Stir-fries, baking |
| Extra firm | 17 g or more | Roasting, frying, grilling |

### Practical preparation — what changes the result

**Pressing:** firm tofu holds a lot of water. Pressing it — between two plates with a weight on top for 20-30 minutes, or in a tofu press — concentrates the protein, improves the texture, and allows much better marinade absorption. It is the single step that makes the biggest difference.

**Freezing and thawing:** a lesser-known and very effective trick. Freezing creates ice crystals that tear the tofu's structure; after thawing you get a much spongier, chewier texture that soaks up marinade beautifully and is reminiscent of a meaty texture.

**Starch for crispness:** coating pressed cubes with a tablespoon of cornstarch before frying or baking creates a crisp crust.

**Marinade:** because it is so neutral, tofu needs aggressive seasoning. [Coconut aminos](/en/food/coconut-aminos/) or soy sauce, garlic, ginger and sesame oil are a classic base. Give it at least 30 minutes.

**Silken tofu is not interchangeable with firm** — it is meant for smooth textures, not for roasting.

### Summary

Tofu is one of the best plant protein sources there is — complete protein of a quality comparable to egg and milk, at a reasonable calorie density and with minimal processing. Concerns about isoflavones are not supported by the evidence. Two practical points to take away: check the **coagulant** in the ingredient list if calcium matters to you, and press or freeze it before cooking — that is where the difference between good and failed tofu lies.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: What is tofu made of?**
Answer: Three ingredients: soybeans, water and a coagulant (calcium sulfate or nigari). The beans are soaked and ground with water into soy milk, the milk is curdled, and the curds are pressed into a block — similar to making cheese.

**Question: How much protein is in tofu?**
Answer: Firm tofu contains 15-17 grams of protein per 100 grams at about 144 calories, and silken tofu only 5-8 grams because of its high water content. It is a complete protein that includes all the essential amino acids.

**Question: Is tofu a good source of calcium?**
Answer: It depends entirely on the coagulant. Tofu coagulated with calcium sulfate contains 200-350 mg per 100 grams — excellent. Tofu coagulated with nigari (magnesium chloride) usually contains less than 50 mg. Check the ingredient list.

**Question: Does soy affect hormones in men?**
Answer: No, according to the evidence. Meta-analyses of dozens of controlled trials found no effect on testosterone, estrogen or sperm quality at dietary amounts.

**Question: Is tofu protein as good as animal protein?**
Answer: Almost. Soy protein is a complete protein with a PDCAAS score of about 0.9-1.0, on par with egg and milk. Milk protein has a slight edge in leucine for muscle building, but the gap is negligible when total protein intake is sufficient.

**Question: How do you make tofu taste good?**
Answer: Press it for 20-30 minutes before cooking, and season aggressively. Another trick: freeze and thaw it before use — this creates a spongy, chewy texture that absorbs marinade much better. A cornstarch coating gives crispness.

**Question: What is the difference between silken tofu and firm tofu?**
Answer: Water content. Silken tofu contains 5-8 grams of protein per 100 grams and suits sauces, desserts and soups. Firm tofu contains 15-17 grams and suits stir-fries, roasting and baking. They are not interchangeable.

**Question: Can you eat tofu while taking thyroid medication?**
Answer: Yes, but leave at least 4 hours between taking thyroxine and eating soy, since it may interfere with absorption of the medication. Coordinate with your doctor.
