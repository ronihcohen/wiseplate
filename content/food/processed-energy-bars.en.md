## Processed Energy Bars

This category hides three completely different products behind very similar packaging:

* **Protein bar** — 20-30 grams of protein, low sugar, high in sugar alcohols. Meant to top up protein.
* **Energy / granola bar** — 5-8 grams of protein, 25-35 grams of sugar. **In practice, it is usually candy in a sports wrapper.**
* **Gels and carbohydrate bars for endurance sports** — deliberately fast carbohydrate, for prolonged effort. **It is supposed to be high in sugar** — that is its purpose.

**Most of the "healthy bars" sold at the checkout belong to the second category.** Below is how to tell them apart in ten seconds, and when each one is really justified.

### Nutrition facts — ranges for the category

| Per 100 grams | Protein bar | Energy/granola bar | Date and nut bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | 350-420 | 420-480 | 400-450 |
| **Protein** | **25-35 g** | 5-8 g | 6-10 g |
| Carbohydrates | 30-45 g | 60-70 g | 50-60 g |
| **of which sugars** | **3-15 g** | **25-35 g** | 40-50 g (from fruit) |
| Sugar alcohols | 10-25 g | 0-3 g | 0 |
| Fiber | 5-15 g | 3-5 g | 6-10 g |
| Fat | 10-20 g | 15-20 g | 15-25 g |
| [Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) | 200-400 mg | 150-300 mg | 5-50 mg |

**A typical bar weighs 40-60 grams**, so roughly half to two-thirds of the values in the table.

### Four numbers on every wrapper

This is the only practical tool that really works in this category. **Calculate everything per 100 grams**, not per "serving":

1. **Sugar.** Under 10 grams per 100 grams — good. Over 25 — it's candy, and there is nothing wrong with that as long as you call it what it is.
2. **Protein.** Over 20 grams per 100 grams — it is a real protein bar. Under 10 — it is not a source of protein, no matter what the front says.
3. **Fiber.** Over 6 grams per 100 grams.
4. **The first ingredient on the list.** Nuts, oats, dates or protein — good. Glucose syrup, sugar or [corn syrup](/en/food/corn-syrup/) — less so.

### Sugar alcohols — the reason for the stomach aches

Many protein bars contain 10-25 grams of **sugar alcohols** per 100 grams — mainly **maltitol, sorbitol, erythritol and xylitol**. They provide sweetness with fewer calories and without raising blood sugar, which is why they are popular in the category.

**The price:** they are only partly absorbed in the small intestine, and continue on to the large intestine — where they draw in water and are fermented by bacteria. The result is **bloating, gas, stomach pain and sometimes diarrhea**, especially with more than one bar a day.

* **Maltitol** is the most problematic in the category.
* **Erythritol** is better tolerated, because it is absorbed and excreted in the urine.
* **For people with irritable bowel syndrome** — sugar alcohols are FODMAPs in every sense of the word, and they are often the real reason for symptoms after a "health bar".

**And the formal note:** sugar alcohols provide about 2.4 calories per gram, not zero, but in some countries they may be left out of the "sugars" count — so "3 grams of sugar" on a bar is not necessarily the whole picture.

### When a bar is really justified

There are contexts in which a bar is the right solution, not a compromise:

* **Endurance sports over 90 minutes** — long runs, cycling, triathlon. Here a fast, compact carbohydrate is exactly what you need, and a high-sugar bar is better than a "health bar" rich in fiber and fat that is hard to digest mid-effort.
* **Hiking, treks and military reserve duty** — calories per weight, no refrigeration.
* **A no-choice window** — a long drive, a busy day with no kitchen. **A bar is better than skipping a meal or stopping for fast food.**
* **Difficulty reaching a protein target** — for people doing resistance training, recovering from surgery, or older adults with a poor appetite, a protein bar is an entirely legitimate tool.

**When it is not justified:** when you have access to a kitchen, when it replaces a meal on a regular basis, or when you buy it believing it is "healthy" in its own right.

### Benefits

* **Real convenience.** Compact, keeps for months, needs no refrigeration or preparation.
* **A significant amount of protein** in protein bars — 20-30 grams, usually [whey protein](/en/food/supplements/whey-protein/), soy protein or [pea](/en/food/peas/) protein.
* **Nutritional transparency.** Every bar has a full label — you can compare and choose based on numbers.
* **Good products exist in the category.** Bars based on [dates](/en/food/dates/), [nuts](/en/food/walnuts/) and [oats](/en/food/oatmeal/) with 3-5 ingredients and no added sugar are a completely different product.
* **A solution for endurance sports** — as detailed above.

### Downsides and considerations

* **[Ultra-processed food](/en/food/ultra-processed-foods/).** Isolated proteins, syrups, stabilizers, sweeteners and flavorings.
* **Hidden sugar.** "Brown rice syrup", "date syrup", "evaporated cane juice", "apple juice concentrate" — all of them are sugar, and all are split up on the list so none appears first.
* **Sugar alcohols** — as detailed above.
* **Calorie density.** 400-480 calories per 100 grams — close to a chocolate bar, which will almost always cost a third of the price.
* **Price.** Very expensive relative to their value. A hard-boiled [egg](/en/food/eggs/), [Greek yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/) or a handful of [almonds](/en/food/almonds/) provides protein at a much lower cost.
* **Relatively low satiety.** Dense food is eaten quickly and gives less fullness per calorie than whole food with the same amount of energy.
* **The "sports" halo.** The packaging design, the name and the shelf placement create a perception of health that is not backed by the label.
* **Protein claims on the front.** "Source of protein" is a regulated term, but the bar for it is low; a bar with 8 grams of protein and 30 grams of sugar can carry it.

### Practical use

* **Calculate per 100 grams** and check the four numbers above.
* **Read the ingredient list to the end**, and look for sugars split up under different names.
* **Count sugar alcohols** if you are prone to bloating — over 10 grams per serving is a noticeable amount.
* **Prefer whole food when possible:** [dates](/en/food/dates/) with [almonds](/en/food/almonds/), [Greek yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/) with fruit, [peanut butter](/en/food/peanut-butter/) on [sourdough bread](/en/food/sourdough/), or a hard-boiled [egg](/en/food/eggs/) — all cheaper, more filling and with a one-item ingredient list.
* **Keep one bar in your bag as a safety net** — not as a planned meal.
* **Choose by context:** for endurance effort — a carbohydrate bar. To top up protein — a protein bar. For snacking — whole food is better.
* **Homemade version:** dates, oats, nuts and a little cocoa in a food processor, rolled into balls and chilled — 15 minutes of work, one to four ingredients, at a third of the price.

### Summary

"Energy bar" is one name for three different products, and most of what is sold at the checkout is **candy in a sports wrapper** — 25-35 grams of sugar per 100 grams and less than 8 grams of protein. The only tool that works is four numbers per 100 grams: **sugar under 10, protein over 20 (in a protein bar), fiber over 6, and a first ingredient that is not sugar.** If you get bloated after a protein bar, the cause is almost always **the sugar alcohols**, led by maltitol. And to be fair: there are contexts in which a bar is the right choice — a long endurance effort, a hike, or a day without a kitchen — and in every other case, [dates](/en/food/dates/) with [almonds](/en/food/almonds/) do the same job at a third of the price.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: What is the difference between an energy bar and a protein bar?**
Answer: A protein bar contains 25-35 grams of protein and 3-15 grams of sugar per 100 grams, and usually 10-25 grams of sugar alcohols as well. An energy or granola bar contains only 5-8 grams of protein and 25-35 grams of sugar — nutritionally, that is close to candy. The two categories look alike on the shelf, and the difference shows up only in the nutrition table.

**Question: What should you check on a bar's wrapper?**
Answer: Four numbers, all per 100 grams and not per "serving". Sugar — under 10 grams. Protein — over 20 grams if it is supposed to be a protein bar. Fiber — over 6 grams. And the first ingredient on the list should be nuts, oats, dates or protein, not glucose syrup or sugar.

**Question: Why does my stomach hurt after a protein bar?**
Answer: Almost always because of sugar alcohols — maltitol, sorbitol, xylitol and erythritol — which protein bars contain at 10-25 grams per 100 grams. They are only partly absorbed in the small intestine, continue on to the large intestine, draw in water and are fermented. The result: bloating, gas and sometimes diarrhea. Maltitol is the most problematic, and erythritol is better tolerated.

**Question: Are energy bars good for athletes?**
Answer: It depends on the sport and the timing. In endurance efforts over 90 minutes — long runs, cycling, triathlon — a high-sugar carbohydrate bar is exactly the right tool, and better than a "health bar" rich in fiber and fat that will weigh on digestion. To top up protein after resistance training, a protein bar is a legitimate tool. For everyday snacking — whole food is better.

**Question: How do you spot hidden sugar in the ingredient list?**
Answer: Look for alternative names — brown rice syrup, date syrup, apple juice concentrate, evaporated cane juice, maltodextrin, fructose, honey. The common technique is to split the sugar into several different sources so that none of them appears first on the list. The simple solution is to look at the "of which sugars" line in the table instead of the list.

**Question: Can a bar replace a meal?**
Answer: When there is no choice — yes, and a bar is better than skipping a meal or stopping for fast food. As a regular solution — no. A bar provides 200-280 calories at high density, is eaten in a minute, and gives less fullness than whole food with the same amount of energy. It is also low in vegetables, micronutrients and volume.

**Question: What are good alternatives to a processed bar?**
Answer: Dates with almonds, Greek yogurt with fruit, peanut butter on a slice of sourdough bread, or a hard-boiled egg — all more filling, significantly cheaper, and with a one-item ingredient list. A homemade version that works: dates, oats, nuts and a little cocoa in a food processor, rolled into balls and chilled.

**Question: Are "sugar-free" bars really sugar-free?**
Answer: They usually contain sugar alcohols instead of sugar, and sugar alcohols provide about 2.4 calories per gram, not zero. In some countries they may be left out of the "sugars" count in the table, so "3 grams of sugar" is not necessarily the full picture. They are also the common cause of gut symptoms after such a bar.
