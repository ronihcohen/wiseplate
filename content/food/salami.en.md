## Salami

Salami is a **dried and fermented** sausage — not a cooked one. Ground meat is mixed with salt, spices and nitrite, stuffed into a casing and hung to dry for weeks to months. In the process it loses 30%-40% of its weight in water, and everything else — fat, salt and protein — becomes concentrated.

That is the reason for everything that characterizes it nutritionally: **it is one of the saltiest and fattiest foods on the shelf**, and in the same breath also rich in protein.

### Nutrition facts per 100 grams

| Nutrient | Amount | % of daily value |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | 400-420 | — |
| Protein | 22 g | 44% |
| Fat | 32-35 g | — |
| of which saturated | 12-13 g | 60%+ |
| [Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) | 1,700-1,900 mg | **75%-83%** |
| Carbohydrates | 1-3 g | — |
| [Vitamin B12](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b12/) | 2.8 mcg | 117% |
| [Zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) | 3.0 mg | 27% |
| Heme [iron](/en/food/iron/) | 1.3 mg | 7% |

**The number worth remembering:** 100 grams of salami provide about **80% of the entire recommended daily sodium intake** and about 60% of the saturated fat limit. Five or six thin slices (about 30 grams) already provide about 550 mg of sodium.

### The risks

* **Classified as processed meat.** The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classifies processed meat in Group 1 — carcinogenic to humans, based on the evidence for colorectal cancer. Every 50 grams a day raises the relative risk by about 18%. Important for context: the classification refers to the certainty of the evidence, not the size of the risk, and the link is dose-dependent — meaning frequency is the decisive variable. By comparison, [fresh red meat](/en/food/red-meat/) is classified only in Group 2A — "probably carcinogenic", a less severe rating.
* **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) — the more immediate problem.** The link between high sodium intake and high blood pressure and cardiovascular risk is one of the best established in nutrition. With salami this is not a theoretical long-term risk but an immediate overshoot of the daily allowance.
* **Saturated fat:** 12-13 grams per 100 grams — more than half the recommended daily limit.
* **Nitrites and nitrosamines:** nitrite (E250) prevents botulism and preserves the color, but under certain conditions in the gut it forms N-nitroso compounds with genotoxic activity.
* **Listeria in pregnancy.** This is a point many people miss: salami is not cooked, only dried and fermented, so it is on the list of foods to avoid during pregnancy. Thorough heating (for example on a pizza in the oven) neutralizes the risk.
* **Tyramine.** A byproduct of fermentation and aging. It is a known migraine trigger in sensitive people, and is off-limits for anyone taking MAO inhibitors.
* **Toxoplasma:** an additional risk in uncooked [dried meat](/en/food/beef-jerky/), relevant mainly in pregnancy and for the immunocompromised.

### Benefits

* **High-density protein:** 22 grams per 100 grams, thanks to the water lost during aging.
* **[Vitamin B12](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b12/):** more than 100% of the daily value per 100 grams.
* **[Zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) and heme [iron](/en/food/iron/)** with high bioavailability.
* **Long shelf life without refrigeration** — aging and salting are an ancient and effective preservation method.
* **Intense flavor:** a small amount provides a lot of flavor, and that is actually the right way to use it — as a seasoning, not as a main dish.

### Practical use

* **As a seasoning, not a main ingredient.** 15-20 grams on a pizza, in pasta or in a salad provide intense flavor at a reasonable nutritional cost. This is the traditional Mediterranean approach, as opposed to a salami sandwich.
* **Read the label:** sodium can vary twofold between brands.
* **Good-quality aged salami** (for example traditional Italian salami) is made from meat and salt only, without fillers or added coloring — a real difference in quality, but not in sodium or fat.
* **Serve with vegetables and [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/)** — this inhibits the formation of nitrosamines in the gut.
* **In pregnancy:** only after thorough heating.

### Summary

Salami provides 22 grams of protein per 100 grams, but along with it 400 calories, 13 grams of saturated fat and about 80% of the daily sodium intake — and it is classified as processed meat. There is no way to turn it into a healthy food, but there is a right way to use it: a small amount, in the role of a seasoning, alongside vegetables. The difference between 20 grams a week and a daily sandwich is the difference that counts.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: What is the nutritional value of salami?**
Answer: 100 grams provide 400-420 calories, 22 grams of protein, 32-35 grams of fat (12-13 of them saturated) and 1,700-1,900 mg of sodium — that is, about 80% of the recommended daily sodium intake. It also provides more than 100% of the daily value of vitamin B12.

**Question: How much salami can you eat?**
Answer: There is no official threshold, but the practical logic is to treat it as a seasoning and not a serving. 15-20 grams (3-4 thin slices) provide intense flavor at a reasonable nutritional cost. The link between processed meat and colorectal cancer is dose-dependent, so frequency matters more than the amount in a single sitting.

**Question: Why is salami off-limits in pregnancy?**
Answer: Salami is not cooked, only dried and fermented, so it may carry listeria and toxoplasma. Both are dangerous in pregnancy. Thorough heating — for example on a pizza in the oven — neutralizes the risk and makes it safe.

**Question: How much sodium is in salami?**
Answer: 1,700-1,900 mg per 100 grams, among the highest on the shelf. Five or six thin slices (about 30 grams) already provide about 550 mg — about a quarter of the daily allowance. Sodium can vary twofold between brands, so it is worth comparing labels.

**Question: Does salami cause migraines?**
Answer: In sensitive people, yes. The fermentation and aging process produces tyramine, a known migraine trigger. Salami is also off-limits for anyone taking MAO inhibitors, since the combination can cause a sharp rise in blood pressure.
