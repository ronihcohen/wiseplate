## Cottage Cheese

**Cottage cheese is one of the most efficient protein sources there is: 100 grams of 5% cottage cheese provide 11 grams of protein for about 98 calories, and almost all of that protein is slow-digesting casein. On the other hand, it contains about 360 mg of sodium per 100 grams and little calcium — less than milk — so it does not replace other dairy as a calcium source.**

Cottage cheese (in Hebrew "גבינת קוטג'", or simply "קוטג'") is a fresh, unaged cheese made by souring [milk](/en/food/milk/) and separating the curd into granules, which are rinsed and mixed with a little cream or milk. Unlike hard cheeses, it is not pressed or aged — hence its granular texture and its high water content.

### What cottage cheese is made of — the process in brief

This question comes up often, and the answer is simple: **cottage cheese is made from two basic ingredients — milk and a bacterial culture** (and in some products a little cream at the end of the process).

The process in four steps:

1. **Souring:** a culture of lactic acid bacteria is added to the milk, lowering its pH. Sometimes a small amount of coagulating enzyme is added as well.
2. **Curdling:** at the low pH, the **casein** protein loses its stability and curdles, separating from the liquid — the whey.
3. **Cutting and rinsing:** the curd is cut into granules and rinsed with water. **Rinsing is the step that sets cottage cheese apart** — it removes excess acidity and lactose and creates the mild flavor and granular texture.
4. **Adding the dressing:** finally the granules are mixed with a little milk or cream, which determines the final fat percentage (3%, 5%, 9%).

What doesn't happen in the process: **no pressing and no aging**. That is why cottage cheese contains a lot of water, keeps only a short time, and is considered a "fresh cheese" rather than an aged cheese. On the label of a quality cottage cheese the ingredient list is short — milk, cream, culture and salt.

### Nutrition facts

100 grams of 5% cottage cheese provide about 98 calories, 11 grams of protein, 4.3 grams of fat and 3.4 grams of carbohydrates. In the 3% version, calories drop to about 80 and fat to about 3 grams. A typical 150-gram serving (half a large tub) provides about 16 grams of protein, about 150 calories and about 540 mg of sodium. A comparison table with other dairy foods appears further down.

### Casein — the slow-release protein

Milk protein is divided into two types: **whey**, which makes up about 20%, and **casein**, about 80%. In cottage cheese the ratio is even more extreme — most of the whey is drained off during production, so the remaining protein is **almost entirely casein**.

The difference between the two is practical: [whey protein](/en/food/supplements/whey-protein/) is absorbed quickly and produces a sharp, short rise in blood amino acids. Casein, on the other hand, forms a clot in the acidic environment of the stomach and breaks down slowly — producing a sustained release of amino acids over hours.

That is the logic behind the familiar practice of **eating cottage cheese before bed**. In a controlled trial, young men who drank 40 grams of casein 30 minutes before bed, after an evening strength workout, reached a positive whole-body protein balance overnight — compared with a negative balance on placebo. [Res et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22330017/). The evidence is not conclusive about the size of the effect on muscle mass over months. In another trial in active women, cottage cheese before bed did not change resting metabolic rate or morning hunger compared with a casein shake or a placebo — in other words, it does not "burn fat overnight," but it does no harm either. [Leyh et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30249314/).

### Benefits

* **One of the best protein-to-calorie ratios on the market:** 11 grams of protein in 98 calories in the 5% version, and about 11 grams in 80 calories in the 3% version. Compared with hard cheeses (25 grams of protein but 400 calories), cottage cheese provides protein at a much lower calorie cost — a clear advantage in weight management.

* **A complete protein with high biological value:** provides all the essential amino acids, and is rich in leucine — the amino acid that activates the mTOR pathway responsible for muscle protein synthesis.

* **Satiety:** the combination of high protein and high water content produces significant satiety at a low calorie cost. That is why cottage cheese is such a staple of weight-loss menus.

* **[Phosphorus](/en/food/supplements/phosphorus/) and [selenium](/en/food/supplements/selenium/):** 100 grams provide about 160 mg of phosphorus and about 9 mcg of selenium.

* **[Vitamin B12](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b12/) and riboflavin (B2):** in significant amounts — about 0.4 mcg of B12 per 100 grams.

* **Relatively low in lactose:** most of the lactose drains off with the whey, so its content is relatively low compared with [milk](/en/food/milk/) — about 2-3 grams per 100 grams. Some people with mild intolerance tolerate it better than milk.

### Downsides

* **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) — the main downside:** 100 grams of cottage cheese contain about 330-400 mg of sodium. A whole container (250 grams) may provide about 900 mg — close to half the daily recommendation. High salt intake is linked to high blood pressure and, in cohort studies, to a higher risk of stomach cancer. [D'Elia et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22296873/). The salt is not only for flavor: it is part of the production process and of preservation. Reduced-sodium versions exist, and they are preferable for people with high blood pressure.

* **Surprisingly low [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/):** this is the most surprising point, and it runs against intuition. Cottage cheese contains only about 80-90 mg of calcium per 100 grams — less than milk (120 mg) and much less than yellow [cheese](/en/food/cheese/) (700-900 mg). The reason: a considerable part of the calcium in milk is bound to the whey and the soluble casein, and drains out during production. **Cottage cheese is an excellent source of protein — but not a major source of calcium**, contrary to popular belief. Adults need about 1,000 mg of calcium a day. [NIH ODS — Calcium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Calcium-HealthProfessional/).

* **Flavored versions:** cottage cheese with sweet add-ins (fruit, vanilla) usually contains 8-12 grams of sugar per 100 grams — that is, triple the carbohydrate content. Read the labels.

* **Milk protein allergy:** unlike lactose intolerance, this is an immune reaction to casein that requires complete avoidance. Cottage cheese is especially rich in casein.

* **Short shelf life:** as a fresh, unaged cheese, it is sensitive and spoils relatively quickly. Eat it within a few days of opening and keep it consistently refrigerated.

* **Additives:** some products contain stabilizers, thickeners or preservatives. A short ingredient list is preferable.

### Practical use

**A reasonable amount:** 150-250 grams per serving — provides 16-27 grams of protein. Pay attention to the cumulative sodium.

**Before bed:** 150-200 grams provide about 20 grams of casein. A mechanistically grounded practice for athletes and for anyone doing resistance training.

**Balance the calcium:** because cottage cheese is relatively low in calcium, anyone relying on it as a main protein source should make up calcium from another source — [yogurt](/en/food/greek-yogurt/), hard cheese, [whole tahini](/en/food/tahini/), [tofu](/en/food/tofu/) set with calcium sulfate, or [kale](/en/food/kale/).

**Prefer plain over "flavored":** and add fresh fruit, [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/) or [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/) — full control over the sugar.

**A satisfying combination:** cottage cheese with vegetables, [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/) and whole-grain bread is a meal balanced in macronutrients and highly filling.

**Reduced sodium:** for people with high blood pressure — this is a more significant consideration than the fat percentage.

### Summary

Cottage cheese is one of the most efficient protein sources in terms of calorie cost — 11 grams of protein in about 98 calories — and its protein is almost entirely slow-release casein, which makes it especially suitable for eating before bed. Two points worth correcting in the popular perception: it is **low in calcium** relative to other dairy products, so it doesn't replace them for that purpose; and it is **high in sodium** — its main practical downside, which you can reduce by choosing a reduced-salt version.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: What is cottage cheese made of?**
Answer: Milk and a culture of lactic acid bacteria, and usually also a little cream at the end of the process. The bacteria sour the milk, the acidity curdles the casein protein, and the curd is cut into granules and rinsed with water — the step that sets cottage cheese apart and creates its mild flavor. Finally it is mixed with a little milk or cream, which determines the fat percentage. There is no pressing and no aging, which is why it is a "fresh cheese".

**Question: What is casein?**
Answer: The main milk protein — about 80% of milk protein (the rest is whey). In cottage cheese the ratio is even more extreme: most of the whey is drained off during production, so the remaining protein is almost entirely casein. Casein forms a clot in the acidic environment of the stomach and breaks down slowly, so it provides a sustained release of amino acids over hours — unlike whey, which is absorbed quickly. That is why cottage cheese is considered suitable for long-lasting satiety and for eating before bed.

**Question: How much protein is in cottage cheese?**
Answer: About 11 grams per 100 grams, that is, about 27 grams in a 250-gram container. This is one of the best protein-to-calorie ratios on the market — 11 grams of protein for just 98 calories in the 5% version.

**Question: Is cottage cheese a good source of calcium?**
Answer: Less than people tend to think. It contains about 83 mg per 100 grams — less than milk and much less than yellow cheese, because a considerable part of the calcium drains off with the whey during production. It is an excellent source of protein, not of calcium.

**Question: Why is it recommended to eat cottage cheese before bed?**
Answer: Its protein is almost entirely casein, which forms a clot in the stomach and breaks down slowly — producing a sustained release of amino acids through the night. In a controlled trial, 40 grams of casein before bed improved overnight protein balance after strength training. This matters mainly for people who train; if you don't, the timing matters less than your total daily protein.

**Question: What are the downsides of cottage cheese?**
Answer: Mainly a high sodium content (330-400 mg per 100 grams), relatively low calcium compared with other dairy products, a short shelf life, and added sugar in flavored versions.

**Question: Cottage cheese or Greek yogurt?**
Answer: Similar in protein (11 vs. 10 grams). Greek yogurt has the advantage of significantly lower sodium and the presence of live cultures; cottage cheese has the advantage of slow-release casein. Both are excellent.

**Question: Is cottage cheese suitable for lactose intolerance?**
Answer: It is usually better tolerated than milk, since most of the lactose drains off with the whey — about 2-3 grams per 100 grams remain. That said, aged hard cheeses contain even less.
