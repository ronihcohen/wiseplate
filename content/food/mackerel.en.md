## Mackerel

Mackerel (in Hebrew, "מקרל") is a small-to-medium oily fish that ranks among the healthiest fish sources. Unlike large fish such as [tuna](/en/food/tuna/) or swordfish, which accumulate mercury up the food chain, mackerel — mainly Atlantic mackerel (*Scomber scombrus*) — offers a high concentration of [omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) with a relatively low load of heavy metals.

Mackerel belongs to the Scombridae family and comes in different sizes: Atlantic mackerel (small-to-medium, a good mercury balance), Spanish mackerel (medium, also good), and king mackerel — a large fish with a very high mercury concentration that should be avoided.

### Research-based health benefits

* **Omega-3 (EPA and DHA) — an especially high concentration:** 100 grams of mackerel contain about 2.5-3 grams of EPA+DHA — among the highest concentrations of any fish, higher on average even than [salmon](/en/food/salmon/). EPA supports reducing inflammation and triglycerides; DHA is essential for brain function, the retina and the nervous system.

* **[Vitamin D](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-d/) — one of the best natural sources:** 100 grams of mackerel provide about 16 mcg of vitamin D — over 100% of the daily intake. Vitamin D is essential for [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) absorption, immunity, mood and the prevention of chronic disease. Most of the Western population is deficient.

* **Vitamin B12:** 100 grams of mackerel provide about 19 mcg — several times the recommended daily intake. B12 is essential for forming blood cells, for myelin function (nerve insulation) and for DNA synthesis.

* **[Selenium](/en/food/supplements/selenium/):** An essential antioxidant that supports thyroid function. 100 grams of mackerel cover about 100% of the daily intake.

* **High-quality protein:** About 19-20 grams of protein per 100 grams — highly bioavailable, with all the essential amino acids.

* **Coenzyme Q10:** Mackerel is one of the best natural sources of CoQ10, essential for cellular energy production and heart health.

* **Niacin and potassium:** Vitamin B3 for metabolism and [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) for regulating blood pressure and heart muscle function.

### Downsides and health considerations

* **King mackerel — avoid it:** King mackerel (*Scomberomorus cavalla*) is a large predatory fish with a very high mercury concentration. The FDA designates it as a fish to "avoid" for pregnant women, breastfeeding women and children. Atlantic mackerel is completely different.

* **High [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) in canned fish:** Mackerel canned in brine can contain 300-500 mg of sodium per 100 grams — choose "no salt added" cans or rinse before eating.

* **Histamine:** Mackerel, like tuna and sardines, can accumulate histamine if it is not stored properly. People with histamine sensitivity should eat it right after purchase.

* **Refrigeration and storage:** Because of its high fat content, fresh mackerel spoils very quickly — eat it on the day of purchase or store it frozen.

### Culinary and practical use

* **Fresh grilled mackerel:** The best way — it preserves the vitamins and adds a delicate smoky flavor
* **Whole mackerel in the oven:** With [lemon](/en/food/lemon/), [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) and herbs — simple and elegant
* **Canned in olive oil:** Convenient for quick meals; prefer [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/) over sunflower oil
* **Smoked mackerel:** Tasty as a spread on bread with sour cream and mustard — keep an eye on the sodium
* **In a Niçoise salad:** An excellent alternative to tuna with higher nutritional value

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many times a week is it recommended to eat mackerel?**
Answer: 2-3 times a week — enough for maximum benefit from omega-3 without concern about heavy metals (Atlantic mackerel).

**Question: Does canned mackerel provide as much omega-3 as fresh?**
Answer: Yes — most of the omega-3 survives the canning process. Prefer it in olive oil rather than sunflower oil.

**Question: Is mackerel good for children?**
Answer: Yes (Atlantic mackerel) — an excellent source of DHA for brain development. Watch out for bones and avoid king mackerel.

**Question: What is the difference between fresh mackerel and smoked mackerel?**
Answer: Smoked mackerel is higher in sodium and may contain compounds from the smoking; fresh is nutritionally better. Eat smoked in moderation and occasionally.

### Summary

Atlantic mackerel is one of the most worthwhile fish nutritionally: relatively inexpensive, rich in omega-3 and vitamin D, and with fewer concerns about mercury than large fish. It is recommended to include it 2-3 times a week as part of a healthy diet.
