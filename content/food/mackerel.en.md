## Mackerel

**Atlantic mackerel is an oily, relatively cheap fish that provides about 20 grams of protein per 100 grams along with omega-3 (EPA and DHA), vitamin D and B12. Unlike king mackerel, which is large and high in mercury, Atlantic mackerel is on the FDA's list of best choices, and two servings of oily fish a week have been linked to lower death rates from heart disease.**

Mackerel (in Hebrew "מקרל") belongs to the tuna family (Scombridae). In Israel it is sold mainly frozen, smoked and canned, and a close relative, Atlantic chub mackerel (*Scomber colias*), is also caught in the Mediterranean. It is important to tell the species apart: Atlantic mackerel (*Scomber scombrus*) and Spanish mackerel are relatively small and low in mercury, while king mackerel (*Scomberomorus cavalla*) is a large predator that accumulates mercury.

### Nutrition facts

Approximate values for Atlantic mackerel. The fat content — and with it the omega-3 — varies a lot by season and fishing area:

| Nutrient | Per 100 g (raw) | Cooked serving (about 100 g) |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | about 205 | about 260 |
| Protein | about 19 g | about 24 g |
| Fat | about 14 g | about 18 g |
| [Omega-3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) (EPA+DHA) | about 1.2 g | about 1.2-2.5 g |
| [Vitamin D](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-d/) | about 16 mcg | varies |
| [Vitamin B12](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b12/) | about 9 mcg | about 15-19 mcg |
| [Selenium](/en/food/supplements/selenium/) | about 44 mcg | about 50 mcg |

According to the NIH, 85 grams of cooked Atlantic mackerel provide about 1 gram of EPA+DHA — a little less than farmed salmon, and more than most white fish. [NIH ODS — Omega-3](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/).

### Benefits

* **Omega-3 and heart health:** in a comprehensive review of the research, eating 1-2 servings of fish a week, especially oily fish rich in EPA and DHA, was linked to about a 36% lower risk of death from coronary heart disease. The authors concluded that the benefits far outweigh the risks from contaminants when low-mercury fish are chosen. [Mozaffarian & Rimm, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17047219/).

* **Vitamin D:** oily fish are among the few natural food sources of vitamin D. 100 grams of mackerel can provide roughly the full recommended daily intake (15 mcg), although the amount varies between fish. [NIH ODS — Vitamin D](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/).

* **Vitamin B12:** a serving provides several times the recommended daily intake (2.4 mcg), which is especially useful for older adults, whose B12 absorption declines.

* **Selenium and protein:** a serving covers almost the full daily intake of [selenium](/en/food/supplements/selenium/) and provides highly digestible complete protein.

* **Price and convenience:** canned mackerel is one of the cheapest and easiest ways to get two servings of oily fish a week.

### Downsides

* **King mackerel — avoid:** the FDA advises pregnant and breastfeeding women and children to avoid king mackerel because of its high mercury content, and places Atlantic mackerel on its "best choices" list. [FDA — Advice about Eating Fish](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish).

* **Histamine (scombroid poisoning):** in fish of the tuna and mackerel family that were not kept cold enough, bacteria convert the amino acid histidine into histamine. Eating such fish causes flushing, headache, a peppery or metallic taste and sometimes diarrhea within minutes to an hour — a reaction that looks like an allergy. Cooking does not destroy the histamine. [Feng et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25876709/).

* **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) in canned and smoked fish:** canned mackerel usually contains 300-500 mg of sodium per 100 grams, and smoked mackerel much more. Choose reduced-salt cans, or eat smoked mackerel only occasionally.

* **Spoils quickly:** because of its high fat content, fresh mackerel oxidizes and spoils fast. Buy it fresh only when it looks shiny and smells clean, and cook it the same day or freeze it.

* **Fish allergy:** people allergic to fish must avoid it.

### Practical use

* **How much:** 2 servings of fish a week, at least one of them oily fish such as mackerel, salmon or sardines — that is the standard recommendation. Atlantic mackerel needs no special limit beyond that, including during pregnancy.
* **Fresh, grilled or baked:** whole mackerel or fillets with [lemon](/en/food/lemon/), [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) and herbs, 12-15 minutes in a hot oven.
* **Canned:** preferably in olive oil or water, and reduced-salt. Good in salads, on [rye bread](/en/food/rye-bread/) or in pasta.
* **Smoked:** as a spread with yogurt, mustard and lemon — in small portions because of the salt.
* **Storage:** fresh — the same day or in the freezer. Don't leave fresh fish out of the fridge, to prevent histamine from forming.

### Summary

Atlantic mackerel is an oily, cheap and accessible fish that provides omega-3, vitamin D, B12 and selenium, with little mercury. Eating oily fish regularly is linked to fewer deaths from heart disease. Tell it apart from king mackerel, which should be avoided in pregnancy and childhood, keep it cold to prevent histamine poisoning, and watch the sodium in canned and smoked mackerel.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How often should you eat mackerel?**
Answer: The standard recommendation is two servings of fish a week, at least one of them oily. Atlantic mackerel is low in mercury, so it works as one or both of those servings.

**Question: Is mackerel safe during pregnancy?**
Answer: Atlantic mackerel — yes, the FDA places it among the best choices. King mackerel — no, because of the mercury. Cook it thoroughly and avoid cold-smoked mackerel that has not been cooked.

**Question: Does canned mackerel provide as much omega-3 as fresh?**
Answer: Mostly yes — most of the omega-3 survives canning. Watch the sodium, and choose cans in olive oil or water.

**Question: Mackerel or salmon — which is better?**
Answer: Both are oily, low-mercury fish. Farmed salmon has a little more omega-3 on average, and mackerel is usually cheaper. The best choice is to alternate.

**Question: Why did I get flushing and a headache after eating mackerel?**
Answer: It may have been histamine (scombroid) poisoning, caused by fish that was not kept cold enough. Symptoms appear within minutes to an hour and usually pass within hours. If you have trouble breathing, seek medical care immediately.

**Question: Is mackerel good for children?**
Answer: Yes, Atlantic mackerel is a good source of DHA and protein. Remove the bones and avoid king mackerel.
