## Strawberry

The strawberry (*Fragaria × ananassa*; in Hebrew, "תות שדה") is one of the most popular fruits in the world, and turns out to be a nutritionally rich food with a unique polyphenol profile. Unlike many sweet fruits, the strawberry offers a very high concentration of vitamins and antioxidants relative to its sugar content.

Botanically, what we call the "fruit" is actually an "accessory fruit" — a fleshy, swollen structure of the receptacle — and not a true fruit. The small seeds on the surface are the true fruits. Interesting from a botanical point of view, but nutritionally what matters is the strawberry's rich profile.

### Research-based health benefits

* **[Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) in high concentration:** 100 grams of strawberries provide about 59 mg of vitamin C — about 65% of the recommended daily intake — more than an [orange](/en/food/orange/) by weight. Vitamin C supports [collagen](/en/food/collagen/) production, immunity and skin health, and increases [iron](/en/food/iron/) absorption from plant sources.

* **Anthocyanins:** the purple-red pigments (mainly pelargonidin) are powerful antioxidants. Studies link them to improved heart health, reduced inflammation, improved short-term memory and anticancer effects.

* **Low glycemic index:** despite their perceived sweetness, strawberries are rated at only GI≈40 — lower than [banana](/en/food/banana/), grapes and [mango](/en/food/mango/). Also suitable for people managing their blood sugar levels.

* **Ellagic acid:** a polyphenol with antimutagenic and antiproliferative properties, studied in the context of preventing skin, lung and breast cancer. It is also present in high concentration in the seeds and the stem.

* **Manganese:** a mineral essential for antioxidant enzymes, bone formation and metabolism. A serving of strawberries (150 grams) covers about 25% of the daily intake.

* **Folate:** especially important for women of childbearing age and during pregnancy, to support normal neural development of the fetus.

* **Potassium and fiber:** contribute to blood pressure regulation and lasting satiety.

### Downsides and health considerations

* **Allergens:** strawberries are among the more common fruits that cause allergies, usually as a mild oral reaction — itching or swelling in the mouth and lips. More rarely — a systemic reaction.

* **Salicylates:** strawberries contain a relatively high amount of salicylic acid; people with aspirin sensitivity may experience a reaction.

* **Pesticide exposure:** strawberries often rank on the "Dirty Dozen" — the list of fruits with the highest pesticide residues. Choosing organic or a long wash in water plus vinegar is worthwhile.

* **Oxalates:** they contain a moderate amount of oxalates — people prone to calcium oxalate kidney stones should be aware of this.

### Culinary and practical use

* **Fresh in season (April–June in Israel):** nutritional value at its peak, natural sweetness high
* **Frozen:** retain most of the nutrients and suit smoothies and sauces
* **Homemade jam with less sugar:** limit added sugar and use the natural pectin for thickness
* **With Greek yogurt:** anthocyanins, fiber and protein together — an excellent balanced meal
* **In a salad with balsamic:** increases the availability of [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) with the olive oil

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Are frozen strawberries as healthy as fresh ones?**
Answer: Almost — freezing right after harvest preserves most of the vitamins and antioxidants. Sometimes frozen strawberries picked at their peak are better than "fresh" strawberries that have traveled for a week.

**Question: How many strawberries a day are enough?**
Answer: A serving of 150-200 grams (a cup) is enough to get a significant benefit — vitamin C, anthocyanins and fiber — without excess sugar.

**Question: Are strawberries suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: Usually yes — thanks to their low GI and fiber content. Consult a dietitian and monitor your personal blood sugar response.

**Question: Which is better — organic or conventional strawberries?**
Answer: Organic is clearly better for strawberries — one of the fruits with the highest pesticide residues. If it isn't available, a thorough wash with water and a little [apple cider vinegar](/en/food/apple-cider-vinegar/) helps.

### Summary

The strawberry is a sweet, rich and accessible fruit that combines great taste with high nutritional value. It's recommended to eat them in season and freely as part of a varied diet, preferring organic because of pesticide levels.
