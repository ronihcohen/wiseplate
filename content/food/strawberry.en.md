## Strawberry

**100 g of strawberries provide only about 32 calories, about 5 g of sugar and about 59 mg of vitamin C, more than an orange by weight. A cup of strawberries covers the whole daily vitamin C requirement. Controlled trials give mixed results: blood sugar markers improved in people with prediabetes, but LDL did not change in people with high cholesterol.**

The strawberry (*Fragaria × ananassa*, in Hebrew "תות שדה") is one of the world's favorite fruits. Botanically, the fleshy part is the swollen base of the flower, and the small seeds on the surface are the true fruits. In Israel the main strawberry season is winter and spring. Nutritionally, the strawberry stands out for its vitamin C density, its low sugar and the anthocyanins (mainly pelargonidin) that give it its color.

### Benefits

* **Plenty of vitamin C.** A cup of strawberries supplies about 88 mg, the full recommended intake for women (75 mg) and nearly all of it for men (90 mg). Vitamin C is needed to make [collagen](/en/food/collagen/) and improves the absorption of [iron](/en/food/iron/) from plant foods. [NIH ODS: Vitamin C](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/).

* **Blood sugar in people with prediabetes.** In a crossover trial with 12-week periods, adults with prediabetes received 32 g of freeze-dried strawberry powder a day (about 2.5 servings of fresh strawberries). During the strawberry period insulin, insulin resistance, fasting glucose and HbA1c fell, as did total cholesterol, compared with a period without strawberries. [Basu et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40250566/). The trial was small (25 participants) and participants knew what they were eating.

* **Cholesterol: a negative result worth knowing.** In a 4-week, double-blind controlled trial, 34 adults with moderately high cholesterol drank a beverage with 50 g of strawberry powder a day. LDL, total cholesterol, glucose and blood pressure did not change compared with a control drink. [Huang et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33758944/). In other words, strawberries are not "cholesterol-lowering".

* **Memory.** In the Nurses' Health Study, women who ate more strawberries and blueberries showed slower cognitive decline. [Devore et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22535616/). That is an observational association only.

* **Sweet without much sugar.** A cup of strawberries tastes sweet but contains only about 7 g of sugar, less than one banana. That makes them a good dessert for people managing blood sugar.

### Downsides

* **Pesticide residues.** Strawberries often top pesticide-residue lists, because they are soft, eaten with the skin and heavily sprayed. Rinsing under running water before eating removes part of the residue. No soap or cleaning products are needed.

* **Allergy and allergy-like reactions.** Strawberries can cause an itchy mouth in people with pollen allergy, and sometimes a rash around the mouth in young children, which is not always a true allergy. A reaction with swelling or shortness of breath needs medical assessment.

* **Fragility and mold.** Strawberries are soft and mold quickly. Do not wash them before storing.

* **Oxalates.** Strawberries contain a moderate amount of oxalate. People who form calcium oxalate kidney stones need not avoid them, but should not eat especially large amounts.

* **"Strawberry-flavored" products.** Yogurts, strawberry milk and jams usually contain a lot of sugar and little fruit. In the Muraki analysis, strawberries were not associated with a lower risk of diabetes, unlike blueberries. [Muraki et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23990623/). A reminder that strawberries are good, but not a medicine.

### Practical use

* **Washing:** only before eating, under running water, with the leaves on, and remove the leaves afterwards so water does not get into the fruit.
* **Storage:** in the fridge, in a ventilated container on a paper towel, and eat within 2-3 days.
* **Frozen:** frozen strawberries are cheap and suit smoothies, sauces and desserts; vitamin C survives freezing better than cooking.
* **In salads:** strawberries with leafy greens, nuts and cheese, plus a little [apple cider vinegar](/en/food/apple-cider-vinegar/) or balsamic.
* **Dessert:** strawberries with plain yogurt instead of strawberry-flavored yogurt.
* **With a plant-based meal:** strawberries after a legume meal improve iron absorption.
* **Choosing:** look for evenly red, glossy berries with fresh green caps; strawberries do not ripen further after picking, so white shoulders stay sour.

### Summary

The strawberry has one of the best vitamin-to-calorie ratios of any fruit: a cup supplies a full day's vitamin C for under 50 calories. The clinical evidence is mixed: one trial showed improved blood sugar markers in prediabetes, and another found no effect on cholesterol. Wash them well before eating, and prefer real strawberries to "strawberry-flavored" products. Fresh in season or frozen the rest of the year, they are one of the easiest ways to cover your daily vitamin C.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in strawberries?**
Answer: About 32 calories per 100 g, so roughly 48 calories per 150 g cup.

**Question: Do strawberries have more vitamin C than oranges?**
Answer: By weight, yes: about 59 mg per 100 g in strawberries versus about 53 mg in oranges.

**Question: Are strawberries suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: Yes. A cup contains only about 7 g of sugar and the glycemic index is low. A small trial showed improved blood sugar markers in people with prediabetes.

**Question: Do strawberries lower cholesterol?**
Answer: In a controlled trial with 50 g of strawberry powder a day for 4 weeks, LDL did not change. There is no basis for saying they lower cholesterol.

**Question: How do I wash pesticides off strawberries?**
Answer: Under running water, with the leaves on, just before eating. No soap or special products are needed.

**Question: Are frozen strawberries as nutritious as fresh?**
Answer: Mostly, yes. Freezing preserves the fiber and most of the vitamin C well; the texture is softer after thawing.

**Question: Why does my child get a rash around the mouth after strawberries?**
Answer: It is common in young children and is sometimes irritation rather than allergy. If there is swelling, vomiting or shortness of breath, see a doctor.
