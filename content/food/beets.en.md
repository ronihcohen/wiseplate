## Beets

**100 g of raw beetroot provide about 43 calories, 2.8 g of fiber and a large amount of nitrate (roughly 250 mg, highly variable). The body turns nitrate into nitric oxide: in meta-analyses beet juice lowered systolic blood pressure by about 4-5 mmHg, mainly in clinic readings and in people with hypertension, and slightly improved endurance performance. Antiseptic mouthwash weakens the process.**

The beet (*Beta vulgaris*, also called beetroot, in Hebrew "סלק") is a root vegetable that has become one of the most studied foods in exercise physiology. The reason is not its vitamins but its **inorganic nitrate** and the pathway it triggers in the body. The red-purple color comes from **betanin**, a pigment in the betalain family found almost only in beets and a few other plants.

### How nitrate works, and why mouthwash matters

1. Nitrate from beets is absorbed in the gut and enters the blood.
2. A substantial part of it (roughly a quarter) is secreted back into saliva and concentrated there.
3. **Bacteria on the tongue** reduce it to nitrite. Humans lack an efficient enzyme for this step, so we depend on oral bacteria.
4. The nitrite is swallowed, and in the stomach and in low-oxygen tissues it becomes **nitric oxide (NO)**, which widens blood vessels.

The practical consequence: antiseptic mouthwash harms these bacteria. In a trial of 19 healthy volunteers, a week of chlorhexidine mouthwash cut oral nitrite production by about 90% and blood nitrite by about 25%, and blood pressure rose by 2-3.5 mmHg. [Kapil et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23183324/). Anyone eating beets or drinking beet juice for this effect should avoid antiseptic mouthwash on those days, and should not spit out saliva right afterwards.

### Benefits

* **Blood pressure.** In a meta-analysis of 13 trials (325 participants) lasting one to six weeks, nitrate from food or beet juice lowered systolic pressure by about 4.1 mmHg and diastolic by about 2 mmHg. But the drop appeared only in clinic measurements; with 24-hour monitoring and home readings there was no significant fall. [Ashor et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28319596/). In a meta-analysis of 7 trials in people with hypertension, beet juice (70-250 ml a day) lowered systolic pressure by about 5 mmHg and did not change diastolic. [Benjamim et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35369064/). It is a real but modest effect, and no substitute for drug treatment.

* **Endurance performance.** A review of 23 studies in athletes found that beet juice can improve efficiency during exercise and extend time to exhaustion at submaximal intensity, though the data are not uniform. [Domínguez et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28067808/). A recent meta-analysis of 33 studies found a small-to-moderate improvement in high-intensity performance, peak power and VO₂max, and the authors note that athlete level, type of sport and timing of intake may influence the result. [Cai et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42445795/). The benefit appears to be smaller in elite athletes.

* **Folate.** 100 g of beets supply about a quarter of the recommended intake, a substantial amount for a vegetable.

* **Betalains.** Betanin has been studied for antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity, mostly in the lab. It is sensitive to heat and light, and long cooking reduces it.

* **The greens.** Beet greens, usually thrown away, are rich in vitamin K, vitamin A and lutein. Cook them like [spinach](/en/food/spinach/).

### Downsides

* **Beeturia.** In some people (by most estimates about 10-14%) urine or stool turns red after beets. It is harmless. But red urine with no beets in your diet needs medical assessment.

* **Oxalates and kidney stones.** Beets, and especially the greens, are high in oxalates. People prone to [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/) oxalate stones should be careful with daily concentrated beet juice. Boiling in water removes some of the oxalate.

* **Sugar in juice.** Beets are sweet for a vegetable, but whole beets have a low glycemic load. Beet juice concentrates the sugar and removes most of the fiber.

* **Medications.** Anyone taking nitrates for angina or PDE-5 inhibitors (such as sildenafil) should check before regular use of concentrated beet juice, because of a possible added blood-vessel-widening effect. People on blood-pressure medication may notice a small additional drop.

* **Infants.** High-nitrate vegetables, including beets, are not recommended for very young infants, because of a risk of methemoglobinemia. In children and adults there is no such concern.

* **Nitrate in vegetables vs. nitrite in meat.** In vegetables, nitrate comes with [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) and [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/), which inhibit nitrosamine formation. The nitrites in [sausages and processed meat](/en/food/sausages/) come with protein and are exposed to high heat, which is a completely different context.

### Practical use

* **Before training or a race:** studies usually used a concentrated "shot" or one to two glasses of juice, about 2-3 hours before exercise, sometimes for several days in a row. Try it in training before a race, because beet juice can upset the stomach.
* **No antiseptic mouthwash** on those days.
* **To keep nitrate and betanin:** roasting in the skin, brief steaming or raw grated beet in a salad beat long boiling, which leaches both into the water.
* **Pickled beets:** tasty, but usually high in [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/).
* **Don't throw out the greens:** they suit omelets, stews and warm salads.
* **Stains:** gloves and a plastic cutting board spare you pink hands.
* **Ready-cooked beets:** vacuum-packed cooked beets are a convenient option and keep most of their nitrate; check that they contain no added sugar or vinegar brine if you want them plain.

### Summary

Beets have a defined mechanism tested in controlled trials: nitrate, nitrite and nitric oxide. The result is a modest drop in blood pressure, mainly in people with hypertension and in clinic readings, and a small improvement in endurance performance. Two practical rules: consume beets 2-3 hours before exercise, and avoid antiseptic mouthwash. The main cautions are oxalates in daily concentrated juice and combining it with vasodilating drugs.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Why is my urine red after eating beets?**
Answer: That is beeturia, caused by the pigment betanin, and it is harmless. If your urine is red and you have not eaten beets, get it checked.

**Question: How much does beet juice lower blood pressure?**
Answer: In meta-analyses the drop was around 4-5 mmHg in systolic pressure, mainly in people with hypertension and in clinic readings. With 24-hour monitoring the effect was not significant.

**Question: When should I drink beet juice before training?**
Answer: About 2-3 hours before exercise, when blood nitrite peaks. Several days in a row may work better than a single dose.

**Question: Does mouthwash cancel the effect of beets?**
Answer: It weakens it considerably. In a trial, a week of chlorhexidine mouthwash cut oral nitrite production by about 90% and blood pressure rose slightly.

**Question: Are beets suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: Whole beets in a normal portion, yes; the glycemic load is low. In juice the sugar is more concentrated.

**Question: Are the nitrates in beets as dangerous as those in processed meat?**
Answer: No. In vegetables they come with vitamin C and polyphenols and have been linked to beneficial effects. The concern with processed meat stems from a different context.

**Question: Are cooked or raw beets better?**
Answer: Raw or roasted. Long boiling draws nitrate and betanin into the cooking water.
