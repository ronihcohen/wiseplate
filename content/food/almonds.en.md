## Almonds

**Almonds are one of the most nutrient-dense nuts: a 30 g handful, about 23 almonds, provides around 175 calories, 6 g of protein, 3.5 g of fibre and roughly half the daily requirement for vitamin E. In a meta-analysis of randomised trials, eating almonds modestly lowered "bad" LDL cholesterol without causing weight gain.** A sensible amount is a handful a day.

The almond (*Prunus dulcis*, in Hebrew "שקדים") is botanically the seed of a stone fruit, a relative of the plum and the peach, but in the kitchen and in nutrition it is treated as a nut. Israel has grown almonds for a long time, and green almonds are eaten there in season before the shell hardens. This article is about the common dried almond.

### Nutrition facts

Approximate values for natural, unroasted almonds with the brown skin. Ten almonds weigh about 12 g.

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Per 10 almonds (12 g) | Per handful (30 g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | about 580 | about 70 | about 175 |
| Protein | about 21 g | about 2.5 g | about 6.3 g |
| Fat | about 50 g | about 6 g | about 15 g |
| of which saturated | about 3.8 g | about 0.5 g | about 1.1 g |
| Carbohydrates | about 22 g | about 2.6 g | about 6.5 g |
| Fibre | about 12.5 g | about 1.5 g | about 3.7 g |
| Vitamin E | about 25 mg | about 3 mg | about 7.5 mg |
| Magnesium | about 270 mg | about 32 mg | about 80 mg |
| Calcium | about 270 mg | about 32 mg | about 80 mg |

Most of the fat in almonds is monounsaturated (oleic acid), as in [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/). Salted roasted almonds add sodium, and almonds roasted in oil add a few calories.

**The calories you actually absorb are fewer than the label says.** In a controlled feeding study in which all excretions were collected, the body extracted about 4.6 calories per gram from almonds, not the 6 calculated with the standard method. A 28 g serving therefore supplied about 129 calories rather than about 170. [Novotny et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22760558/). Part of the fat stays locked inside the cell walls and leaves the body unabsorbed. A systematic review found the same pattern for other nuts, and ground almonds or almond butter are absorbed more completely than whole almonds. [Adv Nutr, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36934832/).

### Benefits

- **Cholesterol.** A meta-analysis of 15 randomised trials found that almonds lowered total cholesterol by about 10.7 mg/dL and LDL cholesterol by about 5.8 mg/dL, along with apolipoprotein B. The reduction is modest, and the trials varied in dose and duration. [Lee-Bravatti et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31243439/).
- **Weight and satiety.** Despite their calorie density, a meta-analysis of 37 randomised trials found that eating almonds did not raise body weight and was even associated with a small loss of about half a kilogram and with lower hunger ratings. [Obes Rev, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38351580/). That fits with part of the calories going unabsorbed and with the satiety from protein, fibre and fat.
- **Long-term heart health.** In observational studies, every 28 g of nuts a day was associated with about a 21% lower risk of cardiovascular disease. These are observational studies of nuts in general, not trials of almonds, and people who eat nuts tend to live healthier lives in other ways too. [Aune et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27916000/).
- **[Vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/).** Almonds are among the best sources of alpha-tocopherol, a fat-soluble antioxidant. A handful provides about half the recommended intake of 15 mg a day. [NIH ODS: Vitamin E](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminE-HealthProfessional/).
- **[Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) and calcium.** A handful covers about a fifth of the magnesium requirement and a fair amount of calcium for a plant food, although it is absorbed less well than calcium from milk.
- **Blood sugar.** Almonds contain almost no available carbohydrate, so when they replace a sweet snack or a pastry, the rise in blood sugar after eating is smaller.

### Downsides

- **Allergy.** Almonds are tree nuts, and an allergy to them can be severe, up to anaphylaxis. People who are allergic need to avoid almonds, almond milk and almond flour.
- **Calories.** Even if some is not absorbed, handful after handful adds up. It is easy to eat 100 g in front of the TV, about 450–580 calories.
- **Oxalates.** Almonds are relatively high in oxalate. People who have had calcium oxalate kidney stones should ask about amounts, especially for large quantities of almond milk and almond flour.
- **Phytic acid.** It binds iron, zinc and calcium and slightly reduces their absorption. In a varied diet this is not significant.
- **Bitter almonds.** Wild bitter almonds contain amygdalin, which releases cyanide during digestion. The sweet almonds sold for eating are not dangerous, but raw bitter almonds or bitter apricot kernels should not be eaten.
- **Choking.** Whole almonds are dangerous for toddlers. Serve young children almond butter or ground almonds.

### Practical use

- **How many a day?** A handful of 20–30 g, about 15–25 almonds, suits most people. This is also the amount tested in most studies.
- **Skin on or off?** Most of the polyphenols are in the brown skin, so whole almonds are preferable. Blanched almonds work well for baking.
- **Soaking.** Soaking is not necessary. Soaking for a few hours softens the almond, and some people find it easier to digest that way. The effect on phytic acid is small.
- **Products.** [Almond milk](/en/food/almond-milk/) contains very few almonds and therefore little protein. Almond flour and almond butter keep the nutritional value of the nut, and almond butter is absorbed better than whole almonds.
- **Storage.** In a sealed jar in a cool place. In the fridge they keep for months.

### Summary

Almonds are a nutritious snack: protein, fibre, vitamin E and magnesium in a single handful. Randomised trials show a small reduction in LDL cholesterol and apoB and no weight gain, partly because about a quarter of the calories are not absorbed. The main downsides are allergy, oxalates for people prone to kidney stones, and how easy it is to eat more than a handful. About 30 g a day is the amount that has been tested and suits most people.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in 10 almonds?**
Answer: About 70 calories by the food tables. In practice the body absorbs less, because some of the fat is not released from the cells, so the real figure is closer to 55.

**Question: How much protein is in almonds?**
Answer: About 21 g per 100 g, which is about 6 g in a 30 g handful and 2.5 g in 10 almonds.

**Question: How many almonds should you eat a day?**
Answer: A handful of 20–30 g, roughly 15–25 almonds. That is the amount tested in most trials, and it did not cause weight gain.

**Question: Do almonds need to be soaked before eating?**
Answer: No. Soaking softens the almond and some people digest it more easily that way, but the nutritional value barely changes.

**Question: Do almonds lower cholesterol?**
Answer: Modestly. In a meta-analysis of randomised trials, LDL cholesterol fell by about 6 mg/dL. That helps as part of an overall diet, not as a substitute for treatment.

**Question: Roasted or raw almonds?**
Answer: The nutritional value is similar. Dry roasting without salt is fine. Salted almonds add sodium, and sugar-coated almonds are a sweet.

**Question: Are almonds suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: Yes, in moderate amounts. They barely raise blood sugar and are a good replacement for sweet snacks.
