## White Flour Crackers

Crackers have an aura of being "light." They are thin, crisp, dry and sound modest — and that is exactly why they show up in so many diets as a bread substitute. **That assumption is wrong, and it is wrong in the opposite direction from what most people assume.**

The reason is simple: **a cracker is bread with the water taken out.** Bread contains about 35-40% water; a cracker contains 3-5%. When you remove the water, everything else — the flour, the fat, the salt — becomes concentrated. That is why **100 grams of crackers contain more calories and several times more sodium than 100 grams of bread.**

### Nutrition facts — crackers vs. bread

| Per 100 g | White flour crackers | [Sourdough bread](/en/food/sourdough/) | Whole [rye bread](/en/food/rye-bread/) | Rye crispbread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | 418 | ~265 | ~250 | ~330 |
| Carbohydrates | 71 g | 51 g | 48 g | 66 g |
| **Fiber** | **2.8 g** | 2.4 g | 6-8 g | **16-20 g** |
| Protein | 9.5 g | 9 g | 8 g | 10 g |
| Fat | 10 g | 1.5 g | 1.5 g | 1.5 g |
| **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/)** | **1,100 mg** | 500 mg | 550 mg | 400-500 mg |

**The standout number:** 1,100 mg of sodium per 100 grams — **twice as much as bread, and more than most potato chips**. A large part of the salt sits on the surface of the cracker itself, where the palate detects it immediately; that is part of what makes it so moreish.

**And here is the fair comparison:** in the serving actually eaten, bread usually wins. A slice of bread (30 grams) is ~80 calories; a handful of crackers (30 grams, about 8-10 pieces) is ~125 calories and 330 mg of sodium. And it is much easier to eat 30 grams of crackers without noticing.

### The glycemic index — a surprising number

The glycemic index of salted white flour crackers has been measured at around **74**, meaning **higher than table sugar (65)**.

The explanation: the starch in a cracker underwent **gelatinization** during baking and then drying, in a porous, open structure. It is highly accessible to digestive enzymes. Without fiber, accompanying fat or protein to slow absorption — glucose reaches the blood quickly.

That is why white crackers as a snack between meals provide especially short-lived satiety, and often bring on renewed hunger within an hour.

### Benefits

* **Shelf life and portability.** Dry, light, keep for months — a real advantage for your bag, for travel and for an emergency kit.
* **Easy to digest.** Low in fiber and fat, so they are used during nausea, stomach illness and pregnancy. The classic dry cracker in the morning against pregnancy nausea works precisely because of what it lacks.
* **A neutral base.** They carry nutritious spreads well — [tahini](/en/food/tahini/), [hummus](/en/food/chickpeas/), [avocado](/en/food/avocado/), soft white cheese, hard-boiled [egg](/en/food/eggs/).
* **An easy-to-measure portion.** Unlike snacks in a bag, crackers are counted in pieces.

### Downsides and considerations

* **Very high [sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/).** 1,100 mg per 100 grams — among the highest in the snack category.
* **Calorie density.** 418 calories per 100 grams, mainly from refined flour and added fat.
* **High glycemic index** — as described above.
* **Almost zero fiber.** 2.8 grams per 100 grams, and in a realistic 30-gram serving less than one gram.
* **Fats in cheap crackers.** Palm oil or interesterified vegetable fat is a common ingredient, contributing saturated fat with no accompanying value.
* **A "diet cracker" is almost always misleading.** Products marketed for dieting show values for a "serving" of 3-4 crackers — 40-60 calories that look great, and are not stuck to in practice.
* **Low satiety.** The combination of zero fiber, salt that encourages you to keep eating, and a texture that vanishes in your mouth makes them a snack that is hard to stop.
* **Closer to a snack than to bread.** In terms of composition, a white flour cracker is closer to [processed energy bars](/en/food/processed-energy-bars/) than to [sourdough bread](/en/food/sourdough/).

### Practical use

* **A slice of bread is almost always better.** [Sourdough bread](/en/food/sourdough/) or [rye bread](/en/food/rye-bread/) — fewer calories per serving, less sodium, and more fiber.
* **If crackers — then rye crispbread.** Whole rye crispbreads (the Scandinavian kind) provide 16-20 grams of fiber per 100 grams — six times as much as a white cracker — and less sodium. This is the simplest and most meaningful upgrade in the category.
* **Never on their own.** A cracker with [tahini](/en/food/tahini/), [avocado](/en/food/avocado/), cheese or [egg](/en/food/eggs/) is a small meal; a cracker on its own is a glycemic load without satiety.
* **Count in advance.** Put 5-6 crackers on a plate and close the package.
* **Read the sodium per 100 grams**, not per "serving." Products in the same category differ twofold.
* **Check the type of fat.** If the ingredient list includes "palm oil" or "hydrogenated vegetable fat" — there is a better alternative on the same shelf.
* **Choose crackers made from whole wheat or rye flour** even when it isn't a Scandinavian crispbread.

### Summary

A white flour cracker isn't "light bread" — it is **dried, concentrated bread**: 418 calories and 1,100 mg of sodium per 100 grams, compared with 265 calories and 500 mg in sourdough bread. Its glycemic index (74) is higher than that of table sugar, and it has almost no fiber — a combination that produces especially short-lived satiety. It has real uses: portability, shelf life, and easy digestion during nausea. But as an everyday snack, **a whole rye crispbread or a slice of whole-grain bread is better on almost every measure** — especially fiber and sodium.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Are crackers easier on a diet than bread?**
Answer: No, the opposite. 100 grams of white flour crackers are 418 calories and 1,100 mg of sodium, compared with about 265 calories and 500 mg in sourdough bread. A cracker is essentially bread with the water taken out, so everything else is concentrated. Even in a realistic serving — 30 grams of crackers is about 125 calories versus 80 in a slice of bread.

**Question: How much salt is in crackers?**
Answer: About 1,100 mg of sodium per 100 grams — twice as much as bread and more than most potato chips. A large part of the salt is sprinkled on the surface of the cracker, where the palate detects it immediately, and that is part of what makes it hard to stop eating. A 30-gram handful provides about 330 mg of sodium.

**Question: What is the glycemic index of crackers?**
Answer: Around 74, meaning higher than table sugar (65). The reason is that the starch underwent gelatinization during baking and then drying, in a porous structure that is highly accessible to digestive enzymes, and the cracker has no fiber or protein to slow absorption. Hence the short satiety and renewed hunger about an hour later.

**Question: Which crackers are better?**
Answer: Whole-grain rye crispbreads, of the Scandinavian kind. They provide 16-20 grams of fiber per 100 grams — six times as much as a white cracker — with fewer calories and less sodium. This is the simplest and most meaningful upgrade in the category, and they are available in every supermarket.

**Question: Is a "diet cracker" really low in calories?**
Answer: Usually it is a misleading presentation. The values are shown for a "serving" of 3-4 crackers, meaning 40-60 calories that look great — but almost no one stops there. The value per 100 grams is the relevant comparison, and by that measure many "diet" products are not substantially different from regular crackers.

**Question: Why are dry crackers given for nausea?**
Answer: Precisely because of what they lack. They are low in fiber, fat and smell, so they are especially easy to digest and don't trigger nausea. In pregnancy and stomach illness this is a legitimate and useful use of the product — it simply isn't suitable as an everyday snack.

**Question: What should you eat crackers with?**
Answer: Never on their own. Tahini, avocado, soft white cheese, hummus spread or hard-boiled egg add protein and fat that slow carbohydrate absorption and prolong satiety. A cracker on its own is a fast glycemic load without satiety; a cracker with a spread is a small meal.

**Question: What is the fat in crackers and why is it there?**
Answer: 10 grams per 100 grams, usually palm oil or hydrogenated vegetable fat, meant to create the crisp texture and extend shelf life. Palm oil is about 50% saturated and adds no nutritional value. It is worth checking the ingredient list — on the same shelf there is usually an alternative with olive oil or no added fat at all.
