## Brazil Nuts

**One Brazil nut contains roughly 50–90 micrograms of selenium, close to an adult's entire daily requirement of 55 mcg. One or two nuts a day is therefore enough, and the safe upper limit of 400 mcg a day can be crossed with as few as five or six nuts if you eat them day after day.** No other ordinary food covers a whole mineral in such a small portion.

Brazil nuts (in Hebrew "אגוזי ברזיל") are the seeds of *Bertholletia excelsa*, a tree that grows mainly in the Amazon rainforest of Brazil, Bolivia and Peru, and most of the crop is still gathered from wild trees. Nutritionally they resemble other nuts: high in fat, with some protein, fibre and magnesium. What sets them apart is [selenium](/en/food/supplements/selenium/), so the most useful thing to know about them is how many to eat, not just why to eat them.

### Nutrition facts

Approximate values for dried, unroasted nuts. An average nut weighs about 5 g.

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Per nut (about 5 g) | Per 2 nuts (about 10 g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | about 660 | about 33 | about 66 |
| Fat | about 67 g | about 3.4 g | about 6.7 g |
| of which saturated | about 15 g | about 0.8 g | about 1.5 g |
| Protein | about 14 g | about 0.7 g | about 1.4 g |
| Carbohydrates | about 12 g | about 0.6 g | about 1.2 g |
| Fibre | about 7.5 g | about 0.4 g | about 0.8 g |
| Magnesium | about 375 mg | about 19 mg | about 38 mg |
| Selenium | highly variable, hundreds to thousands of mcg | roughly 50–90 mcg | roughly 100–180 mcg |

According to the US National Institutes of Health fact sheet, 1 ounce (28 g, 6–8 nuts) of Brazil nuts supplies 544 mcg of selenium, more than the adult tolerable upper intake level of 400 mcg a day. The recommended dietary allowance (RDA) for adults is 55 mcg a day. [NIH ODS: Selenium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Selenium-HealthProfessional/).

### How much selenium is in one Brazil nut?

The figure in the table is an average, and the real spread is wide. Selenium content depends on the soil the tree grew in, which varies from region to region and even from tree to tree. In a controlled trial in New Zealand, researchers planned for two nuts a day to deliver about 100 mcg, but when they analysed the nuts actually eaten, two nuts supplied only 53 mcg on average, with a range of 20 to 84 mcg. [Thomson et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18258628/).

The practical consequence: you cannot know how much selenium is in a particular bag. The safe approach is to plan with the high figure, about 90 mcg per nut, and keep to a small number of nuts a day.

### How many Brazil nuts can you eat per day?

| Regular daily amount | Estimated selenium (at 50–90 mcg per nut) | Against the RDA (55) and upper limit (400) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 nut | 50–90 mcg | roughly covers the daily need |
| 2 nuts | 100–180 mcg | above the need, well below the limit |
| 3–4 nuts | 150–360 mcg | approaching the limit, together with selenium from the rest of the diet |
| 5–6 nuts or more | 250–540 mcg | may exceed 400 mcg |

The upper limit (UL) refers to a regular daily intake over time, not a threshold for a single meal. An occasional handful of Brazil nuts will not poison you. The problem is a daily handful as a habit, particularly for someone who already takes a selenium supplement or a multivitamin containing selenium, and who also gets selenium from fish, eggs, meat and grains.

**The practical recommendation: 1–2 nuts a day as a habit, or a small handful once or twice a week.** If you take a selenium supplement, you do not also need Brazil nuts every day.

### Benefits

- **Better selenium status.** In the New Zealand trial, two nuts a day for 12 weeks raised plasma selenium by 64% and increased the activity of the antioxidant enzyme glutathione peroxidase, as effectively as a 100 mcg selenium supplement. [Thomson et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18258628/). This matters most in regions with selenium-poor soil and for vegans, who miss out on selenium from fish and meat.
- **Blood markers.** A systematic review of 24 clinical trials (most of them non-randomised) found consistent improvements in antioxidant markers and, in some studies, improvements in blood lipids and fasting glucose. Most of the studies were small, and the findings do not guarantee a clinical benefit. [da Silva et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36141050/).
- **The thyroid.** Selenium is built into the enzymes that activate thyroid hormone. In a meta-analysis of selenium supplements in people with Hashimoto's thyroiditis, TPO antibodies fell, and TSH fell in those not taking thyroid hormone. These were studies of supplements, not nuts, and they do not show that Brazil nuts prevent thyroid disease. [Thyroid, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38243784/).
- **A nut like other nuts.** Mostly unsaturated fat, magnesium and fibre. In observational studies, eating nuts is linked to a lower risk of cardiovascular disease, although Brazil nuts were not studied separately. [Aune et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27916000/).

### Downsides

- **Selenosis (too much selenium).** The typical signs are hair loss, brittle or discoloured nails, diarrhoea, nausea, fatigue, joint pain and a garlic odour on the breath. In a US outbreak caused by a liquid supplement that contained 200 times the labelled amount of selenium, these were the most common symptoms, and some lasted more than 90 days. [MacFarquhar et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20142570/). The doses there were far higher than anything you would get from nuts, but the clinical picture is the same.
- **More is not better.** In a long randomised trial, a 200 mcg daily selenium supplement did not prevent type 2 diabetes and was associated with a higher risk of developing it. [Stranges et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17620655/). People whose selenium status is already adequate gain nothing from more.
- **Radium.** Brazil nuts contain more radium than other nuts, with wide variation. A 2024 review calculated that one or two nuts a day add an exposure of about 88–220 microsieverts a year, and described this level as "neither clearly harmful nor clearly harmless". [Koeder & Keller, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39489716/). That is one more reason not to eat large amounts every day.
- **Tree nut allergy.** Brazil nuts are tree nuts, and anyone allergic to them must avoid them.
- **Calories.** Like all nuts, a 30 g handful supplies about 200 calories.
- **Mould and rancidity.** Old nuts or nuts kept in damp conditions can go mouldy. A nut that tastes bitter or musty, or has uneven colouring, belongs in the bin.

### Practical use

- One nut with breakfast is the simplest way to cover selenium without a supplement.
- In cakes, granola or pesto, spread the nuts over several days rather than putting a whole handful into a dish you eat every day.
- Because of their polyunsaturated fat, Brazil nuts go rancid relatively quickly. Store them in a sealed jar in the fridge or freezer.
- Children need less selenium than adults and have a lower upper limit, so for a young child half a nut to one nut is enough, and not every day.

### Summary

The Brazil nut is the most concentrated source of selenium in the diet. One or two nuts a day are enough to improve selenium status, as effectively as a supplement. Beyond that there is no extra benefit and there is a cumulative risk: at five or six nuts a day over time, you can exceed the 400 mcg upper limit and develop selenosis. There is also a small radium exposure. Treat Brazil nuts as a natural supplement with a fixed dose, not as a snack to eat by the handful.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How much selenium is in one Brazil nut?**
Answer: Roughly 50–90 micrograms according to food tables, but the value varies a great deal with the soil the tree grew in. When the nuts were actually measured in one study, two nuts supplied between 20 and 84 mcg.

**Question: How many Brazil nuts can you eat a day?**
Answer: One or two nuts a day is a safe habit. The adult upper limit is 400 mcg of selenium a day, and five or six nuts daily over time may exceed it.

**Question: What happens if you eat too many Brazil nuts?**
Answer: A one-off handful is not dangerous. A high, regular intake can cause selenosis: hair loss, brittle nails, diarrhoea, fatigue and garlic breath. Symptoms usually resolve once intake stops.

**Question: Can Brazil nuts replace a selenium supplement?**
Answer: Yes. In a controlled trial, two nuts a day raised blood selenium as much as a 100 mcg supplement. Just do not take both together.

**Question: Are Brazil nuts good for the thyroid?**
Answer: Selenium is needed for thyroid function, and selenium supplements lowered antibodies in people with Hashimoto's. There is no evidence that the nuts prevent or treat thyroid disease. If you are being treated, ask your doctor before adding selenium.

**Question: Are Brazil nuts radioactive?**
Answer: They contain a little radium, more than other nuts. At one or two nuts a day the exposure is small, but it is another reason not to eat a large handful every day.

**Question: Can children eat Brazil nuts?**
Answer: Yes, in small amounts: half a nut to one nut, and not every day, because their upper limit is lower than an adult's. For toddlers, grind or slice the nut, as a whole nut is a choking hazard.
