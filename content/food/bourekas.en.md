## Bourekas

Bourekas (in Hebrew "בורקס", related to the Turkish börek) are filled pastries that are a staple of Israeli bakeries. A boureka looks like a small pastry, and that is exactly the problem: **a single bakery boureka sometimes weighs 120-150 grams and reaches 450-600 calories** — like a meal, not like a snack you eat on the way to work.

The reason is in the dough, not the filling. **Puff pastry is made of paper-thin layers of flour with layers of fat between them**, and that is what creates the flakiness. In a finished pastry, fat makes up **about half of the calories** — even before adding cheese or a hard-boiled egg on the side.

| Type | Calories | Protein | Fat | of which saturated | [Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) |

| Potato bourekas | 340-380 | 6-7 g | 22-26 g | 10-13 g | 500-650 mg |
| [Mushroom](/en/food/mushrooms/) bourekas | 350-390 | 6-8 g | 24-28 g | 11-14 g | 500-650 mg |

| Size | Weight | Calories |

**The gap between the first and last rows of the second table is sixfold** — and that is the gap most people don't measure.

### The shape reveals the filling

In bakeries and delis in Israel a convention has taken hold that was originally meant to separate **dairy from pareve** for kosher reasons — and it is also useful for anyone counting calories or checking for allergens:

* **Triangle** — usually **cheese** (dairy).
* **Square** — usually **potato** (pareve).
* **Circle or crescent** — usually **mushrooms or spinach** (pareve).
* **Sesame, poppy seeds or cheese sprinkled on top** — serve as an extra marker.

**The convention is not uniform across bakeries**, so it is a hint, not a guarantee — especially for anyone with an allergy. But it holds true in most places.

### What has changed — and what hasn't

**Trans fat:** In the past, industrial puff pastry was made from partially hydrogenated margarine that contained trans fat. **Regulation changed this**, as happened with [margarine](/en/food/margarine/) in general, and today's products are almost free of it. It is still worth looking for "hydrogenated" in the ingredient list.

**What hasn't changed:** The fat that replaced hydrogenation is usually **palm fat**, which is about 50% saturated. In other words, **the saturated fat content of the dough did not go down** — it just changed source. And that is why 100 grams of bourekas provide 60-80% of the recommended daily limit for saturated fat.

### Benefits

* **Protein and calcium in the cheese filling.** Cheese bourekas provide 9-11 grams of protein per 100 grams — not negligible, and it comes with [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/).
* **A base to build on.** The traditional way bourekas are served in Israel — with a hard-boiled [egg](/en/food/eggs/), [tomato](/en/food/tomato/), pickles and zhug — **turns them from dough into a meal**: protein, vegetables and fiber alongside the carbohydrate.
* **A [spinach](/en/food/spinach/) or [mushroom](/en/food/mushrooms/) filling** adds fiber and micronutrients.
* **Cultural value.** A central pastry in Balkan, Turkish and Sephardic Jewish cuisines, and part of family meals and holidays.
* **Transparency in the frozen product.** Industrial packages carry a full label — you can compare products before buying, which you can't do at a bakery.

### Downsides and considerations

* **Calorie density and, above all, portion size** — as detailed above.
* **Saturated fat.** 12-16 grams per 100 grams in the cheese version — 60-80% of the daily limit.
* **[Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/).** 550-700 mg per 100 grams, and a large boureka reaches 800 mg or more — before the pickles and the zhug.
* **Refined white flour.** Almost zero fiber, and therefore relatively low satiety for the calories.
* **Palm fat** — as detailed above, including the environmental aspect.
* **"Diet bourekas" and "baked, not fried."** Industrial bourekas are **baked, not fried, anyway** — the fat is inside the dough, not from frying. This label says nothing about fat content.
* **A daily habit.** One large boureka a day, as a habit, is 450-600 calories and 15 grams of saturated fat out of your diet — an amount that is hard to balance.

### Practical use

* **Weigh one once.** Bring a bakery boureka home and put it on a kitchen scale. Most people are surprised by the 130 grams.
* **Mini instead of large.** Two mini bourekas are 180 calories; one bakery boureka is 500.
* **Choose phyllo over puff pastry** — almost half the fat and calories, in the same role.
* **Make it a meal, not a snack.** A hard-boiled [egg](/en/food/eggs/), a large vegetable salad and zhug alongside half a boureka — more filling than a whole boureka on its own, with fewer calories.
* **A homemade version with [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/):** Phyllo sheets brushed with olive oil instead of industrial puff pastry — cuts saturated fat dramatically.
* **A [cottage cheese](/en/food/cottage-cheese/) or [spinach](/en/food/spinach/) filling** instead of full-fat cheese — more protein, less fat.
* **Read the ingredients on frozen products:** Look for butter or non-hydrogenated oil, and compare saturated fat and sodium per 100 grams between brands. The differences in this category are large.
* **Don't eat from the box.** Cocktail bourekas at an event — put a set amount on your plate.

### Summary

Bourekas are not a snack — **a single bakery boureka is 450-600 calories and 15 grams of saturated fat**, in other words a whole meal in a small wrapper. The fat is in the dough, not the filling, so "baked, not fried" means nothing. Two practical changes transform the picture: **choose phyllo instead of puff pastry**, which saves almost half the calories; and **serve it as a meal** — with egg, vegetables and salad — rather than something you eat alone on the go. As for the shape: **a triangle is usually cheese, a square potato** — a useful convention, but not a guarantee for anyone with an allergy.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in bourekas?**
Answer: It depends entirely on the size. A mini boureka (20-25 grams) is 80-100 calories, a medium one (50-60 grams) 190-250, and a large bakery boureka — which sometimes weighs 120-150 grams — reaches 450-600 calories and 15 grams of saturated fat. The gap between small and large is sixfold, and that is the gap most people don't measure.

**Question: Why is puff pastry so fatty?**
Answer: Because that is exactly the technique. Puff pastry is made of paper-thin layers of dough with layers of fat between them, and the steam created from the moisture during heating is what separates them and creates the flakiness. Without the fat there are no layers. The result is that about half of the calories in the pastry come from fat, even before the filling.

**Question: What does the shape of a boureka tell you?**
Answer: In Israeli bakeries a convention has taken hold that was originally meant to separate dairy from pareve: a triangle is usually cheese (dairy), a square potato, and a circle or crescent mushrooms or spinach. Sesame or poppy seeds on top serve as an extra marker. The convention is not uniform across bakeries, so it is a useful hint but not a guarantee — especially for anyone with an allergy.

**Question: Is there trans fat in bourekas?**
Answer: Almost none, today. In the past, industrial puff pastry was made from partially hydrogenated margarine that contained trans fat, but regulation changed this. The caveat: the fat that replaced it is usually palm fat, which is about 50% saturated — meaning the saturated fat content did not go down, it just changed source. It is worth looking for "hydrogenated" in the ingredient list.

**Question: Which filling is best?**
Answer: A cheese filling provides 9-11 grams of protein per 100 grams plus calcium, and a spinach or mushroom filling adds fiber and micronutrients. A potato filling mainly adds more carbohydrate. But the difference between fillings is much smaller than the difference between types of dough and portion sizes — that is where the real impact lies.

**Question: Are "baked bourekas" healthier?**
Answer: The label is misleading, because industrial bourekas are baked, not fried, anyway. Their fat is inside the dough, not in frying oil, so "baked, not fried" says nothing about the fat content. What does matter is the type of dough: phyllo contains almost half the fat and calories of puff pastry.

**Question: How do you turn bourekas into a reasonable meal?**
Answer: Serve them the traditional way — with a hard-boiled egg, a large vegetable salad, pickles and zhug. The egg adds protein that prolongs satiety and the vegetables add fiber and volume, so half a boureka with the sides is more filling than a whole boureka on its own, and with fewer calories.

**Question: How do you choose better frozen bourekas?**
Answer: Compare two numbers between brands — saturated fat and sodium per 100 grams. The differences in this category are large. It is also worth reading the ingredient list and looking for butter or non-hydrogenated vegetable oil instead of palm fat, and preferring phyllo versions over puff pastry.
