## Flax Seeds

**One tablespoon of ground flax seeds (about 7 g) provides about 37 calories, about 1.6 g of plant omega-3 (ALA) and about 2 g of fibre. In meta-analyses of controlled trials, flax modestly lowered blood pressure and LDL cholesterol, but only when ground: a whole seed passes through the body almost unabsorbed.** The usual amount is one to two tablespoons a day.

Flax seeds (*Linum usitatissimum*; "זרעי פשתן" in Hebrew) hold two nutritional records at once: they are the densest plant source of **alpha-linolenic acid (ALA)**, and also the richest dietary source in the world of **lignans** — phytoestrogens from the [polyphenol](/en/food/polyphenols/) group.

### Nutrition facts

Values for flax seeds. A tablespoon of ground flax weighs about 7 g.

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Per ground tablespoon (7 g) | Per 2 tablespoons (14 g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | 534 | about 37 | about 75 |
| Total fat | 42 g | about 3 g | about 5.9 g |
| of which ALA (omega 3) | 22.8 g | about 1.6 g | about 3.2 g |
| Protein | 18.3 g | about 1.3 g | about 2.6 g |
| Carbohydrates | 29 g | about 2 g | about 4 g |
| Dietary fibre | 27.3 g | about 1.9 g | about 3.8 g |
| [Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) | 392 mg | about 27 mg | about 55 mg |
| Lignans (SDG) | 300-370 mg | about 21-26 mg | about 42-52 mg |

One tablespoon provides roughly the recommended daily intake of ALA: 1.6 g for men and 1.1 g for women. [NIH ODS: Omega-3](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/).

### You have to grind them — the most important point

This is the advice that determines whether eating them is beneficial at all.

The flax seed has a hard, smooth outer hull that the human digestive system cannot break down. **A whole seed passes through the digestive tract and comes out the way it went in** — without absorption of the ALA, the lignans or the minerals inside it. In a trial in which participants ate 30 g of flax a day baked into muffins, blood ALA rose in those who ate milled flax or flax oil, and did not rise significantly in those who ate whole seeds. [Austria et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18689552/).

So: **always grind them** (in a [coffee](/en/food/coffee/) grinder or a blender), or buy them ground and store them refrigerated. The whole seed is useful only as a source of fiber and for bowel movement.

The caveat: ground seed oxidizes quickly. So the preference is to grind a small amount every few days and keep it in the fridge in an airtight container — or buy vacuum-packed ground flax and use it within a few weeks.

### Benefits

* **Lignans — the highest density in the world:** flax seeds contain SDG (secoisolariciresinol diglucoside) at a concentration far higher than any other food. Gut bacteria convert them to enterodiol and enterolactone — phytoestrogens structurally similar to estrogen that bind to estrogen receptors very weakly. The result is a modulating effect: in a low-estrogen environment they act as weak agonists, and in a high-estrogen environment they compete for the receptor and reduce the overall effect.

* **Blood pressure:** this is one of the strongest findings. A meta-analysis of 14 controlled trials found a fall of about 1.8 mmHg in systolic and about 1.6 mmHg in diastolic pressure, and the fall was larger when the seed itself was eaten (rather than oil or an extract) and for more than 12 weeks. [Khalesi et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25740909/). In a randomised trial in patients with peripheral artery disease who received 30 g of milled flax a day, those who started with a systolic pressure of 140 or higher saw a fall of about 15 mmHg systolic and 7 mmHg diastolic. [Rodriguez-Leyva et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24126178/).

* **Fiber and mucilage:** 27 g of fiber per 100 g, in a unique combination of insoluble fiber and mucilage — a soluble fiber that forms a thick gel on contact with water. The combination helps with constipation (bulk and motility) and glycemic regulation. A meta-analysis of 62 randomised trials found that flax lowered total cholesterol by about 5.4 mg/dL, LDL by about 4.2 mg/dL and triglycerides by about 9.4 mg/dL. [Hadi et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31899314/).

* **ALA and heart health:** despite the limited conversion to EPA/DHA (see below), in observational studies ALA intake is associated with lower cardiovascular risk. Observational studies do not prove cause and effect.

* **Breast cancer — reassuring evidence:** many people worry about phytoestrogens in the context of hormone-dependent cancer. The evidence does not point to harm: in a meta-analysis of 21 observational studies, lignan intake was not associated with overall breast cancer risk, and in postmenopausal women high intake was associated with about a 14% lower risk. [Buck et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20463043/). However, women in active hormonal treatment should consult their oncologist.

* **Menopausal symptoms:** the evidence here is weak and mixed. Some trials showed a reduction in hot flashes, others found no difference from placebo. This should not be presented as an established benefit.

### The ALA conversion limit

It is important to be precise, as with [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/) and [chia seeds](/en/food/chia-seeds/): the body's conversion of ALA to EPA and DHA is limited, and in the trial mentioned above, 30 g of flax a day did not raise blood EPA or DHA at all. Flax seeds **are not a substitute** for [oily fish](/en/food/salmon/) or an [omega 3](/en/food/supplements/omega-3/) supplement for anyone looking for EPA/DHA. For vegans who want DHA, an algae-based supplement is the direct solution.

### Downsides

* **Rapid oxidation:** the high polyunsaturated fat content makes ground seed and the oil very sensitive to oxidation. Flaxseed oil especially — it must not be heated at all, and should be stored refrigerated in a dark bottle. A bitter or sharp taste indicates oxidation.

* **Cyanogenic glycosides:** flax seeds contain linustatin and similar compounds that release a tiny amount of cyanide. In normal dietary amounts (up to about 2-3 tablespoons a day) there is no danger, and the body easily neutralizes the amount. Heating reduces the content. Caution is relevant only for extreme, prolonged consumption.

* **You must drink water:** mucilage absorbs fluids. Eating a significant amount of ground flax without enough to drink can worsen constipation instead of relieving it.

* **Drug absorption:** the gel may slow or reduce the absorption of medications taken at the same time. Separating them by at least an hour is recommended.

* **Effect on clotting:** ALA in large amounts has a moderate antiplatelet effect. People taking blood thinners should keep their intake consistent and consult their doctor.

* **Pregnancy:** because of the hormonal activity of lignans, the accepted recommendation is caution and moderation during pregnancy, and to consult a doctor.

### Practical use

**How much:** 1-2 tablespoons of ground flax a day (7-14 g) for everyday use. Blood pressure trials sometimes tested larger amounts, about 30 g (about 4 tablespoons).

**How to grind and store:** an electric coffee grinder does the job in seconds. Grind a week's worth and keep it in an airtight container in the fridge, or freeze it. Whole seeds keep well for many months at room temperature — so it is better to buy them whole and grind them at home.

**Combinations:** in yogurt, [oatmeal](/en/food/oatmeal/), smoothies, bread dough or cookie dough. The taste is mild and nutty and goes with almost anything.

**"Flax egg":** a tablespoon of ground flax + 3 tablespoons of water, wait 5-10 minutes until a gel forms — a substitute for an [egg](/en/food/eggs/) in vegan baking.

**Flaxseed oil:** provides concentrated ALA but **without** the lignans and fiber, which stay in the pressed seed cake. For cold use only.

### Summary

Flax seeds are a food with two truly unique advantages: the highest density of lignans in the world, and a measurable, if modest, effect on blood pressure and cholesterol. They are not a substitute for fish as a source of EPA/DHA, and it is worth knowing about the conversion limit. But the practical point that decides everything is simple: **a whole seed passes through the body without being absorbed**. Grind them, store them refrigerated, and eat a tablespoon or two a day.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Do you have to grind flax seeds?**
Answer: Yes, this is the most important point. The hull is too hard for the digestive system to break down, and a whole seed passes through the body and comes out the way it went in — without absorption of ALA or lignans. The whole seed is useful only as fiber.

**Question: Flax or chia — which is better?**
Answer: Flax is richer in ALA (22.8 vs. 17.8 g per 100 g) and in lignans, and has stronger research support for blood pressure. [Chia](/en/food/chia-seeds/) is richer in fiber and [calcium](/en/food/supplements/calcium/), and does not need to be ground. Both are excellent and complement each other.

**Question: How much flax seed a day?**
Answer: 1-2 tablespoons ground (7-14 g) for everyday use. Blood pressure trials sometimes tested up to 30 g. There is no need for larger amounts, and extreme intake raises the issue of cyanogenic glycosides.

**Question: Is flax dangerous for women because of phytoestrogens?**
Answer: The evidence does not point to harm. In a meta-analysis of observational studies, high lignan intake was associated with a lower risk of postmenopausal breast cancer. Women in active hormonal treatment should consult their treating physician.

**Question: Can you cook with flaxseed oil?**
Answer: No. The oil is very sensitive to heat and oxidation and is intended for cold use only. Store it refrigerated in a dark bottle, and throw it out if it tastes bitter.

**Question: How do you store ground flax?**
Answer: In an airtight container in the fridge for up to about two to three weeks, or in the freezer. Whole seeds keep for months at room temperature — so it is better to buy them whole and grind a small amount at home.
