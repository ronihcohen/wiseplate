## Nutritional and health benefits of brown lentils

Brown lentils (in Hebrew, "עדשים חומות") are a nutrient-dense legume, characterized by a significant content of plant protein, dietary fiber and essential micronutrients. Because of their composition, they are considered a functional food that contributes to metabolic, cardiovascular and digestive health.

### Plant protein and amino acid profile

Brown lentils contain about 24–26% protein (by dry weight) and are a major source of protein in a plant-based diet. Although they do not contain all the essential amino acids in optimal amounts, combining them with grains creates a complementary amino acid profile of high biological value.

### Dietary fiber and digestive health

Lentils are rich in soluble and insoluble fiber.
Soluble fiber helps slow glucose absorption and lower LDL cholesterol levels, while insoluble fiber supports normal bowel activity. In addition, lentils contain resistant starch, which serves as a substrate for gut bacteria and contributes to a balanced microbiome.

### Glycemic balance and metabolic effects

Brown lentils have a low glycemic index, which makes them a suitable food for balancing blood sugar levels and preventing [insulin](/en/food/supplements/insulin/) resistance. Regular intake is associated with an improved glycemic profile and a prolonged feeling of fullness.

### Essential micronutrients

Brown lentils are a significant source of:

* Non-heme [iron](/en/food/iron/) – essential for forming hemoglobin and transporting oxygen
* [Zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) – supports enzymatic activity and the immune system
* [Magnesium](/en/food/supplements/magnesium/) – involved in muscle, nerve and enzyme function
* [Potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) – contributes to electrolyte balance and blood pressure regulation
* [Folate](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-b9/) (B9) – important for cell division and nervous system function

### Bioactive compounds and antioxidants

Lentils contain [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) and antioxidants with anti-inflammatory activity, which may reduce oxidative stress and contribute to the dietary prevention of chronic disease.

### Contribution to heart and blood vessel health

The combination of soluble fiber, [potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) and [polyphenols](/en/food/polyphenols/) is associated with an improved blood lipid profile, lower cholesterol and support for blood pressure regulation, thereby reducing cardiovascular risk.

### Bioavailability and preparation

Brown lentils contain phytates, which can reduce the absorption of minerals such as [iron](/en/food/iron/) and [zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/). Soaking and cooking reduce the phytate content and improve the bioavailability of nutrients.

### Summary

Brown lentils are a legume that makes a significant contribution to metabolic, heart and digestive health. The combination of high-quality plant protein, dietary fiber and essential micronutrients makes them a central component of a balanced, plant-based diet.
