## Gummy Candies

Gummy candies are sweet snack products usually based on sugar, gelatin or pectin, plus flavorings and colorings. They are a popular snack and a treat for children and adults alike, but from a health perspective they should be considered with caution.

### Benefits
- A quick energy source: the high sugar content provides fast glucose, useful for readily available energy, especially during short bouts of exertion.
- No fat: most gummy candies are low in fat, so they do not directly add to saturated fat intake.
- The psycho-social benefit: an accessible snack associated with social enjoyment and rituals (birthdays, celebrations).

### Downsides and risks
- High sugar and teeth: gummy candies are rich in sugar that sticks to the teeth — causing a high risk of cavities and gum inflammation.
- Sugar and poor nutrition: almost no nutritional value beyond empty calories — linked to obesity, type 2 diabetes risk and metabolic disorders when eaten in excess.
- Additives and colorings: food colorings and flavorings, depending on concentrations and use, may be linked to sensitivity reactions in children (hyperactivity in certain groups), although the evidence is mixed.
- Choking hazard: especially in young children — the sticky texture and consistency of gummy candies can create a choking hazard.

### Important biological mechanisms
- Teeth and acidity: dissolved sugars feed acid-producing mouth bacteria, and the acid damages enamel and causes cavities.
- Sugar metabolism: high intake of simple sugar raises the repeated glycemic and insulin load, contributing to [insulin](/en/food/supplements/insulin/) resistance and belly fat over time.

### Practical findings and evidence-based recommendations
- Limit frequency and portions: limit intake and treat gummy candies as an occasional treat rather than a routine source of nutrition.
- Oral hygiene: after eating them — brushing your teeth is recommended, or at least rinsing your mouth with water to reduce the dental impact.
- Reduced-sugar options: consider versions with non-caloric sweeteners with caution (some sweeteners cause gut side effects) or versions with reduced sugar content.
- Child safety: avoid giving them to young children and supervise to reduce the risk of choking.

### Limits of knowledge and research
Long-term studies on the specific effects of gummy candies compared with other sugar sources are limited; however, the harmful effects of excessive sugar intake are well accepted in the literature.

### Summary
Gummy candies provide brief enjoyment and energy but make no nutritional contribution and sometimes carry dental, metabolic and child-safety risks. It is recommended to eat them in moderation, keep up oral hygiene and consider healthier alternatives when appropriate.
