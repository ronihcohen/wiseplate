## Gummy Candies

**Gummy candies are mostly sugar and glucose syrup set with gelatin or pectin: 100 g provides about 340 calories and about 46 g of sugar, with almost no vitamins, minerals or fibre.** Because of their sticky texture they stay on the teeth for a long time, which makes them one of the more problematic sweets for tooth decay — and a choking hazard for toddlers. In Hebrew they are called "ממתקי גומי".

They include gummy bears, worms, "jelly sweets" and fruit snacks in all kinds of shapes. Most products are based on gelatin from beef or pork; vegan or kosher versions use pectin, starch or agar.

### Nutrition facts

Gelatin-based gummy candies (approximate; varies between brands):

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Per small bag (25 g) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calories | about 340 | about 85 |
| Carbohydrate | about 77 g | about 19 g |
| of which sugars | about 46 g | about 11.5 g |
| Protein (from gelatin) | about 7 g | about 1.7 g |
| Fat | under 0.5 g | negligible |
| Fibre | 0 | 0 |

One small bag provides almost half of the World Health Organization's stricter target of about 25 g of free sugar a day. [WHO — Guideline: sugars intake](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549028)

### Benefits

* **Fast energy for sport:** on a long run or ride, gummies are a convenient, quickly available carbohydrate source, and some athletes use them instead of gels.
* **Almost fat-free:** they contain no saturated or trans fat.
* **A defined portion:** it is easy to hand out a set number of pieces, which helps control the amount.
* **Enjoyment:** an occasional sweet is a legitimate part of a balanced diet.

### Downsides

* **Tooth decay:** sugar left on the teeth feeds bacteria that produce acid. Sticky sweets lodge in the grooves of the teeth and prolong exposure. A systematic review found that caries is lower when free sugars make up less than 10% of calories. [Moynihan 2014, J Dent Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24323509/)
* **Empty calories:** lots of sugar, with almost no satiety or nutritional value.
* **Choking hazard:** small, round, sticky sweets are dangerous for toddlers. It is best not to give them to children under about 4, and to supervise young children while they eat.
* **Food colours:** in a controlled trial in the UK, mixtures of synthetic food colours with the preservative sodium benzoate increased hyperactivity in some children aged 3 and 8–9. [McCann 2007, Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17825405/) As a result, in the European Union products containing six specific colours carry a warning. The effect is small and not seen in every child.
* **"Sugar-free" versions:** these are usually sweetened with sugar alcohols such as maltitol, which in large amounts cause gas and diarrhoea.

### Gummies with "vitamins" or "real fruit"

Gummies with added vitamins or "fruit juice" are still sweets as far as sugar goes. Gummy vitamins for children also contain sugar, and they should be kept out of reach so that children do not eat them like candy.

### Practical use

* **With a meal, not throughout the day:** eating them in one go after a meal is less harmful to teeth than continuous snacking.
* **Water afterwards:** rinsing your mouth with water reduces leftover sugar. Brushing before bed is recommended.
* **A small portion:** put a set amount in a bowl instead of eating from the bag.
* **Toddlers:** under about 4 — don't give them at all.
* **For sport:** a small bag during an effort of more than about 90 minutes is reasonable fuel.
* **Read the ingredients:** look for gelatin or pectin according to your preference, and check colours and preservatives.

### Summary

Gummy candies are almost pure sugar in a sticky form: they provide quick energy, but they are harder on teeth than many sweets and are a choking hazard for toddlers. There is no need to ban them — a small portion after a meal, followed by a rinse with water, is a sensible way to enjoy them.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How much sugar is in gummy candies?**
Answer: About 46 g per 100 g, and about 11.5 g in a small 25 g bag — nearly half of the WHO's stricter daily target.

**Question: Why are gummies worse for teeth than other sweets?**
Answer: They stick in the grooves of the teeth and leave sugar available to bacteria for a long time. The longer sugar stays in the mouth, the more acid the bacteria produce.

**Question: At what age can children have gummy candies?**
Answer: It is best to avoid them until about age 4 because of the choking risk, and to supervise young children after that too.

**Question: Do gummy candies contain pork gelatin?**
Answer: Some do. There are also products with beef or fish gelatin, and versions made with pectin or starch. Check the ingredient list and any kosher or halal marking.

**Question: Do food colours in sweets cause hyperactivity?**
Answer: A controlled trial found that mixtures of synthetic colours and sodium benzoate increased hyperactivity in some children. The effect is small, but parents who notice it in their child can choose products with natural colours.

**Question: Are gummies good for energy while running?**
Answer: Yes — in efforts longer than about 90 minutes they are a convenient source of fast carbohydrate. For a short workout you don't need them.
