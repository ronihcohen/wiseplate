## Raspberry

**100 g of red raspberries provide about 52 calories, 6.5 g of fiber and only about 4.4 g of sugar, one of the best fiber-to-sugar ratios among fruits. In a controlled trial, one to two cups of raspberries with breakfast reduced the insulin rise in people with prediabetes. By contrast, "raspberry ketone" weight-loss supplements have not been tested in controlled human trials.**

The raspberry (*Rubus idaeus*, in Hebrew "פטל") is a berry from the rose family. Botanically it is a cluster of tiny fruits (drupelets) around a central cavity, which is why it is hollow, delicate and spoils quickly. Each tiny fruit holds a small seed, which explains the high fiber content: most of it is insoluble fiber in the seeds and skin.

### Nutrition facts

Per 100 g of fresh red raspberries, and per cup (about 125 g). Values are rounded.

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Cup (125 g) |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | 52 | about 65 |
| Carbohydrate | 11.9 g | about 15 g |
| of which sugar | 4.4 g | about 5.5 g |
| Dietary fiber | 6.5 g | about 8 g |
| Protein | 1.2 g | about 1.5 g |
| Fat | 0.65 g | about 0.8 g |
| [Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) | 26.2 mg | about 33 mg |

| Glycemic index | low (about 25-32) | — |

For comparison: a [banana](/en/food/banana/) has about 12 g of sugar against 2.6 g of fiber per 100 g, and [blueberries](/en/food/blueberries/) about 10 g of sugar against 2.4 g of fiber.

### Benefits

* **Blood sugar and insulin after a meal.** In a controlled crossover trial, 32 adults (21 of them with overweight, prediabetes and insulin resistance) ate a high-carbohydrate breakfast with no raspberries, with about 125 g, or with about 250 g of frozen raspberries. In those with prediabetes, both amounts reduced total insulin over the two hours after the meal, and the larger amount also lowered peak glucose. [Xiao et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30767409/). This was a short trial of a single meal, not a long-term effect.

* **Vegetable-level fiber.** A cup of raspberries provides about 8 g of fiber, roughly a third of the recommended intake for women (about 25 g a day) and somewhat less of the target for men. The insoluble fiber adds bulk to stool and helps bowel regularity.

* **Ellagitannins and urolithins.** Raspberries are a good source of ellagitannins, as are [pomegranate](/en/food/pomegranate/) and [walnuts](/en/food/walnuts/). Gut bacteria turn them into urolithins, but the ability to do this varies greatly between people, depending on the microbiome. Research on what urolithins do in humans is still at an early stage.

* **Manganese and vitamin C.** A cup of raspberries supplies roughly a third to half of the recommended intake of manganese (1.8 mg for women and 2.3 mg for men) and about a third of the vitamin C. [NIH ODS: Manganese](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Manganese-HealthProfessional/).

* **Lots of volume for few calories.** A cup of raspberries at about 65 calories, with plenty of fiber, is a dessert or breakfast topping that fills you more than its size suggests.

### Raspberry ketone: what's the problem

"Raspberry ketone" is the compound responsible for the raspberry's smell. It was marketed as a weight-loss supplement on the back of lab and rodent studies. There are no controlled trials in humans showing that it reduces weight. Even in a study of mice on a high-fat diet, the weight loss came mainly from the mice eating less; mice given the same amount of food without the supplement lost weight similarly. [Cotten et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28378858/). The doses in such experiments are far higher than anything you could get from fruit, and supplements usually use synthetic ketone. Raspberries are useful as food, not as a "fat burner".

### Downsides

* **Short shelf life.** Raspberries are very prone to mold and sometimes spoil within a day or two. Do not wash before storing; wash only before eating.
* **Price.** One of the more expensive fruits, because of hand-picking and delicate transport. Frozen raspberries are much cheaper.
* **Seeds.** The tiny seeds get stuck between teeth. During a flare of inflammatory bowel disease or after bowel surgery, some people are told to avoid seeded fruit.
* **FODMAPs in large portions.** Small amounts are well tolerated even on a low-FODMAP diet; a large serving can cause symptoms in sensitive people.
* **Allergy.** Rare, sometimes as part of oral allergy syndrome in people sensitive to pollen.
* **"Raspberry-flavored" products.** Jams, syrups and baked goods usually contain a lot of sugar and little fruit, cancelling the fiber-to-sugar ratio that is the whole point here.

### Practical use

* **Frozen is the smart choice:** it is frozen soon after harvest, costs less and is available all year.
* **Storing fresh berries:** in the fridge, in a ventilated container on a paper towel, unwashed.
* **When buying:** check the bottom of the box; damp stains or mush indicate fruit that is starting to spoil.
* **Breakfast:** raspberries on yogurt or [oatmeal](/en/food/oatmeal/). The Xiao trial suggests this is exactly when the addition is most useful.
* **Quick sauce:** frozen raspberries warmed with a little water and mashed with a fork replace jam, with no added sugar.
* **Black raspberries:** richer in anthocyanins than red, with similar values for the other nutrients.
* **In savory dishes:** their tartness and low sugar work well in salads with leafy greens, goat cheese and nuts, or in a vinaigrette blended with a little vinegar and oil.

### Summary

Raspberries stand out for their high fiber and low sugar, and the trial available shows they reduce the insulin rise after a high-carbohydrate meal in people with prediabetes. They also provide manganese and vitamin C. Raspberry ketone as a weight-loss supplement is unfounded. The main downside is a short shelf life, so frozen raspberries are usually the practical choice.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in raspberries?**
Answer: About 52 calories per 100 g, so roughly 65 calories per cup of about 125 g.

**Question: Are raspberries suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: Yes. They contain about 4.4 g of sugar and 6.5 g of fiber per 100 g, and in a short trial they reduced the post-meal insulin rise in people with prediabetes.

**Question: Does raspberry ketone help weight loss?**
Answer: No controlled human trials show this, and in a mouse study the weight loss came mainly from eating less.

**Question: Why do raspberries mold so quickly?**
Answer: They are hollow, delicate and moist. Do not wash them before storing, keep them in the fridge in a ventilated container and eat them within a day or two.

**Question: Frozen or fresh raspberries?**
Answer: Frozen is usually the practical choice: cheaper, available all year, and it keeps the nutrients well.

**Question: How much fiber is in raspberries?**
Answer: 6.5 g per 100 g. A cup provides about 8 g, more than most fruits.

**Question: Raspberries or blueberries?**
Answer: Raspberries have more fiber and less sugar; blueberries have more research on blood vessels and memory. Both are good choices, and you can combine them.
