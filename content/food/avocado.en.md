## Avocado

**Avocado is a fruit rich in unsaturated fat: half a medium avocado (about 70 g) provides about 115 calories, about 10 g of mostly monounsaturated fat, about 5 g of fibre and a good amount of potassium. In a large observational study, two or more servings a week were associated with about a 16% lower risk of cardiovascular disease, but an avocado a day did not reduce belly fat in a randomised trial.** It is a good food, not a weight-loss remedy.

The avocado (*Persea americana*, in Hebrew "אבוקדו") originates in Mexico and Central America, and Israel is a long-standing grower and exporter. Varieties common in Israel include Hass, Ettinger and Pinkerton, which differ in size and fat content. Unlike most fruit, avocado contains very little sugar and a lot of fat.

### Nutrition facts

Approximate values for fresh Hass avocado, edible part. Other varieties are slightly lower in fat.

| Calories | about 165 | about 115 | about 230 |
| Fat | about 15 g | about 10.5 g | about 21 g |
| of which monounsaturated | about 10 g | about 7 g | about 14 g |
| of which saturated | about 2 g | about 1.5 g | about 3 g |
| Carbohydrates | about 8.6 g | about 6 g | about 12 g |

| Sugars | about 0.3 g | under 0.5 g | under 0.5 g |
| Protein | about 2 g | about 1.4 g | about 2.8 g |
| [Potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) | about 500 mg | about 350 mg | about 700 mg |

A large Ettinger or Pinkerton avocado can weigh 250 g or more of edible flesh, and then provides about 350–400 calories.

### Benefits

- **Heart and blood vessels.** In a 30-year follow-up of about 110,000 US nurses and health professionals, people who ate avocado twice a week or more had about a 16% lower risk of cardiovascular disease and about a 21% lower risk of coronary heart disease than non-eaters. Replacing half a serving a day of butter, cheese or processed meat with avocado was associated with lower risk. [Pacheco et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35352568/). This is an observational study, and avocado eaters tend to eat more healthily overall.
- **LDL cholesterol.** In a controlled crossover feeding trial in 45 people with overweight, a diet including one Hass avocado a day lowered LDL by about 13.5 mg/dL, more than a diet in which the same fatty acids came from oils. [Wang et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25567051/). A larger trial found only a small fall in LDL (see below).
- **Fibre.** Half an avocado provides about 5 g of fibre, about a fifth of the daily need, with almost no sugar. That helps with satiety and bowel function.
- **[Potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/).** A medium avocado provides about 700 mg of potassium, more than a medium [banana](/en/food/banana/). A diet rich in potassium and low in sodium helps blood pressure.
- **Carotenoid absorption.** Adding avocado to salad or salsa greatly increased absorption of carotenoids such as beta-carotene and lutein from the vegetables. [Unlu et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15735074/). In other words, avocado in a salad improves the value of the vegetables next to it.
- **[Vitamin E](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-e/), vitamin K and folate** in decent amounts, plus lutein and zeaxanthin.

### Downsides

- **Not a slimming food.** In the HAT trial, 1,008 adults with a large waist circumference either ate one large avocado a day for six months or continued their usual diet. There was no difference in belly fat, liver fat or body weight, and only a small fall in LDL cholesterol. [Lichtenstein et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35861827/). Avocado did not cause weight gain, but it did not reduce fat either.
- **Calories.** A large avocado is like a small meal in calories. When you add it to every meal on top of your usual diet, rather than in place of another fat, the calories add up.
- **Latex-fruit syndrome.** People allergic to latex often react to avocado, banana, kiwi and chestnut as well, because of similar proteins. Reactions can be severe. [J Clin Med, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39064262/).
- **Anticoagulants.** Avocado contains vitamin K. People taking warfarin do not need to avoid it, but should keep their intake steady rather than change it suddenly.
- **FODMAP.** Avocado is high in polyols (sorbitol), and in large amounts it can cause bloating in people with irritable bowel syndrome. A small serving is usually better tolerated.
- **Potassium in kidney disease.** People on a potassium restriction because of kidney disease should ask about amounts.

### Practical use

- **How much?** Half an avocado a day is a reasonable amount. Ideally it replaces another fat, such as butter on bread or mayonnaise in a sandwich.
- **In salad.** Avocado or olive oil in a salad improves the absorption of vitamins and carotenoids from the vegetables.
- **Instead of saturated fat.** Mashed avocado on bread instead of butter or full-fat cheese is the swap behind the research findings.
- **Ripening.** Avocados ripen after picking. In a closed paper bag with an [apple](/en/food/apple/) or a [banana](/en/food/banana/), which give off ethylene, they ripen faster. A ripe avocado goes into the fridge to slow further ripening.
- **Browning.** Cut avocado darkens through oxidation. Lemon juice and cling film pressed onto the surface slow this down.

### Summary

Avocado is a nutritious fruit with monounsaturated fat, fibre, potassium and folate, and almost no sugar. Observational studies link it to a lower risk of heart disease, especially when it replaces butter, cheese and processed meat, and trials show a small to moderate fall in LDL. In the largest trial it did not reduce belly fat or weight. Half an avocado a day, in place of another fat, is the way to benefit from it.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a whole avocado?**
Answer: A medium Hass avocado (about 140 g of flesh) provides about 230 calories. A large avocado of other varieties can reach 350–400 calories.

**Question: How much avocado can you eat a day?**
Answer: Half an avocado a day is a reasonable amount for most people. Even a whole avocado a day did not cause weight gain in a trial, but it is best if it replaces another fat in your diet.

**Question: Does avocado make you gain weight?**
Answer: Not necessarily. In a six-month trial, an avocado a day did not change weight or belly fat. It does not make you lose weight either. Total calories are what count.

**Question: Does avocado lower cholesterol?**
Answer: To a small to moderate degree. In a controlled trial, an avocado a day lowered LDL by about 13 mg/dL, and in a larger trial the fall was small. The benefit is greater when it replaces saturated fat.

**Question: How do you speed up ripening?**
Answer: Put it in a closed paper bag with an apple or banana, which release ethylene gas. At room temperature it takes a day or two.

**Question: Is it safe to eat the brown parts of an avocado?**
Answer: Light browning on the surface is oxidation and is safe to eat, though it tastes less good. If there is a bad smell, mould or deep black streaks running through the fruit, it is better to throw it away.

**Question: I am allergic to latex. Can I eat avocado?**
Answer: Not necessarily. Latex allergy is often linked to reactions to avocado, banana and kiwi. Ask an allergist before eating it.
