## Deep-Fried Sweet Potato Fries

Sweet potato fries — "צ'יפס בטטה" in Hebrew — are usually sold as the healthy alternative to regular [fries](/en/food/fries/). That assumption is only partly true: **the [sweet potato](/en/food/sweet-potato/) is indeed better than the potato, but deep frying flattens most of the difference.**

In terms of calories, deep-fried sweet potato fries are very close to regular fries — and sometimes even higher, because sweet potato absorbs more oil thanks to its sugar content and softer texture.

### How many calories — the comparison that explains everything

| Preparation | Calories per 100 g | Fat |
|---|---|---|
| [Sweet potato](/en/food/sweet-potato/) baked in its skin | 90 | 0.2 g |
| Oven sweet potato fries (a teaspoon of oil) | 150-170 | 5-6 g |
| **Deep-fried sweet potato fries** | **280-330** | **15-18 g** |
| Deep-fried potato [fries](/en/food/fries/) | 290-320 | 15-17 g |
| Frozen sweet potato fries, fried (commercial) | 310-350 | 17-20 g |

**The bottom line in one sentence:** frying triples the calories of the sweet potato, and the gap between it and fried potato shrinks almost to zero. Anyone choosing sweet potato fries for health reasons mostly gets a different taste.

### What does remain of the sweet potato

* **Beta-carotene — and this time frying actually helps.** Orange sweet potato is very rich in beta-carotene (about 8,500 mcg per 100 g), and it is **fat-soluble**. The presence of fat significantly improves its absorption, and bioavailability studies have shown this consistently. Some of the carotenoids are indeed destroyed at high heat, but the availability of what remains goes up. This is the only real advantage of the fried version.
* **[Potassium](/en/food/supplements/potassium/) and fiber** are mostly preserved.
* **[Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/)** is almost completely destroyed at frying temperatures.

### Downsides

* **Calorie density:** 280-330 calories per 100 g. A restaurant serving weighs 150-200 g — that is, 450-650 calories for the side dish alone.
* **Acrylamide.** It forms in starchy foods above 120°C (248°F), in a reaction between asparagine and reducing sugars. **Sweet potato is richer in sugar than potato**, so it browns faster and also produces acrylamide faster at a given temperature. Acrylamide is classified as probably carcinogenic to humans (IARC Group 2A). The practical rule: **"golden, not brown"** — dark browning is exactly where the amount jumps.
* **Oil quality and oxidation products.** Oil that has been used for frying again and again, as is common in restaurants and food stands, accumulates aldehydes and lipid oxidation products. This is the hardest downside to control outside the home.
* **Trans fats** — rare today in commercial oils, but still formed in small amounts during prolonged frying at high temperature.
* **Sodium:** commercial fries are generously salted, sometimes 400-600 mg per 100 g.
* **Starch coating in frozen products:** most frozen sweet potato fries are coated in starch or flour to make them crisp — an added processed carbohydrate that does not appear in the product name.
* **Glycemic index:** fried sweet potato has a higher glycemic index than baked or boiled sweet potato, because of changes to the starch structure at high heat.

### How to make a significantly better version

1. **Oven at 200-220°C (390-430°F)** with a tablespoon of [olive oil](/en/food/olive-oil/) on a baking sheet — 150-170 calories per 100 g instead of 300.
2. **Air frying:** achieves similar crispness with 1-2 teaspoons of oil. A reduction in acrylamide compared with deep frying has been documented in studies.
3. **Soak in water for 30 minutes** before baking — this removes excess starch and sugars from the surface, reducing both rapid browning and acrylamide.
4. **Dry thoroughly** before adding oil — water is the enemy of crispness.
5. **Take them out when they are golden**, not brown.
6. **Leave the skin on** — a considerable part of the fiber and polyphenols is concentrated there.
7. **Season with smoked paprika, [turmeric](/en/food/turmeric/) or [cinnamon](/en/food/cinnamon/)** instead of adding salt.

### Summary

Deep-fried sweet potato fries contain 280-330 calories per 100 g — three times as much as baked sweet potato, and practically the same as potato fries. Their healthy image is largely unjustified: frying cancels most of the advantage of the raw ingredient. What does remain is the beta-carotene, whose absorption even improves in the presence of fat. As an occasional side this is no problem; anyone who wants the real advantage of sweet potato should bake it in the oven with a tablespoon of oil — and get the same taste for about half the calories.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in sweet potato fries?**
Answer: 280-330 calories per 100 g when deep fried, and 310-350 in the commercial frozen version. For comparison: sweet potato baked in its skin provides only 90 calories, and oven sweet potato fries with a teaspoon of oil — 150-170.

**Question: Are sweet potato fries healthier than regular fries?**
Answer: Less than people tend to think. The raw ingredient is indeed better — sweet potato is rich in beta-carotene and potassium — but when deep fried the calories are almost identical (280-330 versus 290-320), and the sweet potato even absorbs more oil because of its sugar content. The meaningful difference is the cooking method, not the type of tuber.

**Question: Is beta-carotene preserved during frying?**
Answer: Some of it is destroyed by heat, but absorption of what remains actually improves — beta-carotene is fat-soluble, and the presence of oil significantly raises its bioavailability. This is the only real advantage of the fried version.

**Question: What is acrylamide and why is it relevant to sweet potato fries?**
Answer: A compound that forms in starchy foods above 120 degrees Celsius, from a reaction between asparagine and sugars. Sweet potato is richer in sugar than potato, so it browns and produces acrylamide faster. The practical rule is "golden, not brown" — with dark browning the amount jumps.

**Question: How do you make crispy sweet potato fries without deep frying?**
Answer: Soak in water for 30 minutes (to remove excess starch), dry thoroughly, toss with a tablespoon of olive oil, and bake at 200-220 degrees Celsius until golden. Air frying achieves a similar result with 1-2 teaspoons of oil. The result: about half the calories and less acrylamide.
