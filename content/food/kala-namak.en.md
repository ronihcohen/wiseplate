## Indian Black Salt (Kala Namak)

**Kala namak ("black salt" in Hindi, in Hebrew "מלח הודי שחור") is salt that has been heated with plant or chemical additives, creating sulphur compounds that give it the taste and smell of hard-boiled egg. That makes it very useful in vegan cooking. Health-wise it is salt first and foremost: mostly sodium chloride, and there is no evidence that it is healthier than table salt.** Count its sodium like any other salt.

The name is misleading: the crystals are dark, grey to purplish brown, but ground into powder they turn pinkish grey. It is common in Indian and Pakistani cooking, in chaat masala, chutneys and salty yogurt drinks, and Ayurvedic tradition credits it with digestive benefits. In Western kitchens it became known mainly as the "egg" seasoning for vegans.

### How it is made and what is in it

Traditionally, rock salt is heated in a furnace with wood charcoal and dried fruits or seeds, such as harad (*Terminalia chebula*). In the process some of the sulphates in the salt are converted to sulphides, which is where the smell comes from. Today most commercial black salt is made industrially: sodium chloride is mixed with sulphur salts such as sodium sulphate and iron sulphate, and heated.

The main components:

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| Sodium chloride | most of the weight, the salty taste |
| Sodium sulphate and sulphides | the sulphurous taste and smell |
| Hydrogen sulphide (H₂S), in tiny amounts | the egg smell |
| Iron compounds | the dark and pinkish colour |

### Nutrition facts

Salt provides no calories, protein or fat. The only nutritionally significant value is sodium. According to manufacturers' labels, the sodium content is close to that of table salt, roughly 35–39 g of sodium per 100 g. Approximate values:

| Amount | Weight | Sodium (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| A pinch | about 0.3 g | about 110 mg |
| A quarter teaspoon | about 1.5 g | about 550 mg |
| A teaspoon | about 6 g | about 2,200 mg |

The World Health Organization recommends that adults consume less than 2 g of sodium a day, that is less than 5 g of salt. [WHO: Sodium reduction](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/sodium-reduction). A teaspoon of black salt, just like a teaspoon of ordinary salt, already exceeds that.

### Benefits

- **Egg flavour without egg.** This is the real benefit. People who avoid [eggs](/en/food/eggs/) because they are vegan or allergic to egg can get a familiar flavour in scrambled tofu, chickpea-flour omelettes or vegan "egg" salad.
- **Less salt for the same flavour?** Because the flavour is strong, a dish seasoned with black salt does not always need extra salt. If it replaces salt rather than being added on top, it does not raise total sodium.
- **Digestion.** In Ayurveda it is used to relieve gas and heartburn. We found no clinical studies that have tested this, so it should not be treated as a proven benefit.

### Downsides

- **It is still sodium.** The claim that black salt "contains less sodium" and is therefore healthier is not well founded. Any difference from table salt is small, and the amount of sodium is decided mainly by how much you sprinkle. In a meta-analysis of randomised trials, cutting salt by about 4.4 g a day lowered systolic blood pressure by about 4 mmHg on average. [He et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23558162/).
- **Usually not iodised.** In many countries table salt is fortified with iodine, and it is a main source of [iodine](/en/food/supplements/iodine/) in the diet. [NIH ODS: Iodine](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/). Anyone who replaces all the salt at home with black salt, or with Himalayan salt, gives up that source. This matters especially in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- **No "detox".** Claims that the sulphur in the salt "helps the liver flush out toxins" are unfounded. The liver does not need sulphur from salt, and the amounts are tiny.
- **Strong taste and smell.** In the wrong dish, such as a green salad, fruit or a dessert, the sulphurous smell can be unpleasant.
- **Source and quality.** Black salt is usually imported, and quality varies between producers. Buy a brand with a proper label.

### Practical use

- **Add it at the end.** The sulphur compounds are volatile and dissipate with heat. Sprinkle the salt on scrambled tofu or a vegan omelette after you take it off the heat, or straight onto the plate.
- **Scrambled tofu.** Crumbled tofu, turmeric for colour, [nutritional yeast](/en/food/nutritional-yeast/) for depth, and a pinch to a quarter teaspoon of black salt at the end.
- **Chickpea-flour omelette.** A batter of [chickpea](/en/food/chickpeas/) flour and water, cooked like an omelette, with black salt at the end.
- **Vegan "egg" salad.** Crumbled tofu with vegan mayonnaise, mustard, black pepper and half a teaspoon of black salt.
- **In Indian cooking.** In chaat masala, on cut fruit, and in salty lassi.
- **How much?** A quarter teaspoon per serving gives a clear flavour. It contains about 550 mg of sodium, about a quarter of the daily recommendation, so do not add ordinary salt as well.
- **Storage.** In a tightly closed jar, or the smell spreads through the pantry and the salt loses strength.

### Summary

Kala namak is an excellent kitchen tool: its sulphur compounds give it the taste and smell of egg, which makes it especially useful for vegans and people allergic to eggs. Health-wise it is salt in every respect: mostly sodium chloride, with a sodium content close to table salt, and usually not iodised. Claims about digestion and "detox" are unfounded. Use it in pinches, at the end of cooking, and in place of ordinary salt, not on top of it.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Is Indian black salt healthier than ordinary salt?**
Answer: Not meaningfully. It is mostly sodium chloride, and its sodium content is close to that of table salt. The main difference is the flavour. It is also usually not iodised.

**Question: Why does it taste and smell of egg?**
Answer: Because of sulphur compounds formed during heating, including a tiny amount of hydrogen sulphide. The same compounds are responsible for the smell of cooked egg yolk.

**Question: Why is it called black if it is pink?**
Answer: The whole crystals are dark, grey to purplish brown, because of iron and sulphur compounds. Ground into powder, the colour lightens to pinkish grey.

**Question: How much black salt should you add to a dish?**
Answer: A pinch to a quarter teaspoon per serving of scrambled tofu gives a clear egg flavour. Add a little, taste, and add more if needed.

**Question: Can you use it instead of ordinary salt in all your cooking?**
Answer: You can, but the sulphurous flavour does not suit every dish. Anyone who replaces all the salt at home should make sure of another iodine source, such as iodised salt in some dishes.

**Question: Is black salt suitable for people with high blood pressure?**
Answer: Only with the same caution as ordinary salt. It contains a similar amount of sodium, so it is not a "low-sodium salt".
