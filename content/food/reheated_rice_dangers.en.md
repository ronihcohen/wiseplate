## The Dangers of Eating Reheated Rice

**Reheated rice is not dangerous because it is reheated — the danger is cooked rice left at room temperature for several hours, in which the bacterium Bacillus cereus can multiply and produce a heat-stable toxin that causes vomiting.** Cooling it quickly in the fridge and eating it within a day or two removes almost all of the risk. The condition is known as "fried rice syndrome"; in Hebrew, reheated rice is "אורז שחומם מחדש".

[Rice](/en/food/rice/) is a staple and safe in most cases. The problem is fairly specific to rice and other starchy foods, such as cooked pasta and potatoes, because the bacterium is common on grains and survives cooking.

### How the bacterium works

1. **Spores survive cooking:** Bacillus cereus lives in soil, so it is also found on uncooked rice. It forms spores — a dormant, resistant form — that are not destroyed by boiling water.
2. **Germination at room temperature:** when cooked rice cools slowly or is left on the counter, the spores germinate into active bacteria that multiply quickly.
3. **Toxin production:** the bacteria produce a toxin called cereulide, which causes vomiting. The toxin is extremely stable and unlikely to be inactivated by food processing or reheating. [Yang 2023, Foods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36832907/)

So strong reheating in the microwave or a pan will kill the bacteria, but it will not neutralise toxin that has already formed. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) notes that the risk arises mainly when cooked food is held at unsuitable temperatures and cooled slowly. [EFSA 2016 — Bacillus cereus](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2016.4524)

### Two types of food poisoning

| Type | Cause | Time to symptoms | Symptoms | Typical foods |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emetic (vomiting) | Cereulide, heat-stable | 30 minutes to 6 hours | Nausea and vomiting | Rice, pasta, starchy foods |
| Diarrhoeal | Enterotoxins formed in the gut | 6–15 hours | Abdominal pain and diarrhoea | Meat, vegetables, sauces, rice too |

A review of outbreaks between 1984 and 2019 notes that the severity of illness varies widely depending on the bacterial strain and the person's immune system, and that cereulide can be deadly in rare cases. [Leong 2023, Microb Pathog](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37866551/) Most people recover within 24 hours.

### Benefits of cooled rice

Cooled rice also has an upside: as it cools, some of the starch turns into resistant starch, which behaves like fibre. In a small trial, rice cooled for 24 hours in the fridge and then reheated contained more resistant starch and caused a smaller rise in blood sugar than freshly cooked rice. [Sonia 2015, Asia Pac J Clin Nutr](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26693746/) Note the condition: cooling in the fridge, not leaving it on the counter.

### Downsides and people at risk

* **Rice left out:** the main risk. Rice that has sat at room temperature for several hours is better thrown away.
* **Vulnerable groups:** babies, older adults, pregnant women and people with weakened immune systems may suffer more from dehydration.
* **Buffets and restaurants:** rice held lukewarm for hours is a classic outbreak scenario.
* **False reassurance:** "I'll heat it really hot and it'll be fine" is not true once the toxin has formed.

### Practical use

1. **Cool it fast:** do not leave cooked rice at room temperature for more than an hour or two.
2. **Spread it in a shallow dish:** a thin layer cools much faster than a deep mass in the pot.
3. **Refrigerate:** at 5°C or below, in a closed container.
4. **Eat within a day or two:** the sooner, the safer.
5. **Reheat once, until steaming hot throughout:** stir halfway through in the microwave.
6. **Don't keep it lukewarm:** if serving rice over a long period, keep it very hot (above 60°C) or cold.
7. **Batch cooking:** if you cook a large amount, freeze portions as soon as they have cooled.

### Summary

Reheated rice is safe when it has been cooled quickly and kept in the fridge. The danger is rice left at room temperature for hours, in which Bacillus cereus produces a heat-stable toxin that causes vomiting within hours. Cooling in a shallow dish, refrigerating, eating within a day or two and reheating thoroughly once are enough to enjoy leftover rice — with a little extra resistant starch as a bonus.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Can I reheat rice in the microwave?**
Answer: Yes, if the rice was cooled quickly and kept in the fridge. Heat it until it is steaming throughout and stir halfway. Reheating does not fix rice that has sat out for hours.

**Question: How long can cooked rice be left out?**
Answer: Ideally no more than an hour or two. Rice that has stood at room temperature for several hours, especially on a hot day, is best thrown away.

**Question: How long does cooked rice keep in the fridge?**
Answer: It is best eaten within a day or two. For longer storage, freeze portions as soon as they have cooled.

**Question: What are the symptoms of food poisoning from rice?**
Answer: Nausea and vomiting from 30 minutes to 6 hours after eating, or abdominal pain and diarrhoea after 6–15 hours. In most cases symptoms pass within a day; see a doctor if there are signs of dehydration.

**Question: Can I reheat rice twice?**
Answer: It is better to reheat only the amount you will eat, once. Each extra round of cooling and reheating gives bacteria another chance to multiply.

**Question: Is cooled rice healthier?**
Answer: Cooling in the fridge increases resistant starch, and in a small trial it caused a smaller rise in blood sugar. The difference is modest and does not justify leaving rice out of the fridge.
