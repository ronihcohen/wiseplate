## Sufganiyah (Jelly Doughnut)

**A standard jam-filled sufganiyah weighing about 80–90 g provides about 300 calories, about 16 g of fat and about 18 g of sugar — roughly a small meal's worth of energy, but with little protein or fibre.** "Gourmet" doughnuts with cream, glazes and toppings provide a lot more. One or two over Hanukkah are not a problem; one a day throughout the holiday adds up. In Hebrew it is called "סופגניה".

A sufganiyah is a yeast dough fried in deep oil, filled with jam or custard and dusted with powdered sugar. In English it is a jelly doughnut, and in Jewish tradition it is eaten at Hanukkah to commemorate the miracle of the cruse of oil.

### Nutrition facts

A classic jam sufganiyah (approximate; values vary with size, filling and frying oil):

| Nutrient | Per 100 g | Per doughnut (about 85 g) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calories | about 340 | about 290 |
| Carbohydrate | about 39 g | about 33 g |
| of which sugars | about 21 g | about 18 g |
| Fat | about 19 g | about 16 g |
| Protein | about 6 g | about 5 g |
| Fibre | about 1 g | about 1 g |

One doughnut provides about 70% of the World Health Organization's stricter target of about 25 g of free sugar a day. [WHO — Guideline: sugars intake](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549028)

### Benefits

* **Cultural and social value:** a holiday food that connects generations and families. Pleasure and tradition have a legitimate place in a diet.
* **Available energy:** concentrated carbohydrate and fat, before a long activity or on a day of high energy expenditure.
* **Home cooking gives control:** at home you can choose fresh oil, make them smaller and reduce the sugar in the filling.

### Downsides

* **Energy density:** about 300 calories in a single pastry, with relatively short-lived satiety.
* **Sugar:** in the dough, the filling and the powdered sugar; two doughnuts exceed the stricter daily target.
* **Fat from frying:** the dough absorbs oil, and the quality of the oil matters. Oil that is reused for frying again and again oxidises and breaks down.
* **Acrylamide:** a compound that forms when starchy food is fried or baked at high temperature. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) concluded that it may raise cancer risk and advises reducing exposure — for example, by not frying to a dark brown. [EFSA 2015 — acrylamide in food](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4104) The amount in one doughnut is small; what matters is the total from all sources.
* **People with diabetes:** a fast rise in blood sugar; plan the portion and have it after a meal.

### Alternatives and cooking tips

* **Bake instead of fry:** baked or air-fried doughnuts contain less fat, although the texture is different.
* **Fresh, heat-stable oil:** canola or sunflower oil at about 170–180°C, without heavy reuse.
* **Golden, not dark brown:** less acrylamide.
* **Small size:** "mini doughnuts" let you enjoy them with half the calories.
* **Lower-sugar filling:** jam with more fruit, or a [date](/en/food/dates/) purée.
* **Less powdered sugar and glaze:** that is where much of the sugar in gourmet doughnuts hides.

### Practical use

* **Plan the holiday:** decide in advance on which days and at which events you will have a doughnut.
* **Half a doughnut is still a doughnut:** sharing one saves half the calories.
* **After a meal:** eating it after a balanced meal makes you less likely to reach for another.
* **Drinks:** water or unsweetened tea, not a [sugary soft drink](/en/food/sugary-soft-drinks/) on top.
* **Children:** a small doughnut at the holiday party; not a daily extra for all eight days.

### Summary

A sufganiyah is a festive pastry of about 300 calories, with plenty of sugar and fat and little nutritional value. There is no reason to skip it at Hanukkah — the issue is the amount over eight days. Choosing a small size, frying in fresh oil to a golden colour and planning ahead let you enjoy the tradition without overdoing it.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How many calories are in a sufganiyah?**
Answer: A standard jam doughnut of about 85 g provides about 290–300 calories. Gourmet doughnuts with cream and glaze usually provide a lot more.

**Question: How much sugar is in a sufganiyah?**
Answer: About 18 g in a standard jam doughnut — about 70% of the WHO's stricter daily target. Glazed and cream-filled versions contain more.

**Question: Is a baked sufganiyah healthier?**
Answer: It contains less fat and therefore fewer calories, but the sugar is about the same. It is a good alternative for people who eat doughnuts several times over the holiday.

**Question: What oil is best for frying sufganiyot?**
Answer: A heat-stable oil such as canola or sunflower, at about 170–180°C. Change the oil rather than reusing it again and again.

**Question: Do sufganiyot contain acrylamide?**
Answer: Like any fried starchy pastry, yes — in small amounts. Frying to golden rather than dark brown reduces it.

**Question: Can people with diabetes eat a sufganiyah?**
Answer: Many can, with planning: a small portion, after a meal, counting the carbohydrate (about 33 g in a standard doughnut) and consulting their care team.
