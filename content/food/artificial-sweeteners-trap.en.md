### Introduction: the promise of zero calories
The dream of "sweetness without paying for it" turned artificial sweeteners (aspartame, sucralose, saccharin and others) into the Western world's magic solution to the obesity epidemic. "Diet" and "zero" drinks are sold as a healthy, responsible alternative, yet in practice, alongside the rise in their consumption, obesity rates just keep climbing. Could the solution actually be part of the problem?

### The biological mechanism: metabolic confusion
When we taste something sweet, our brain prepares to receive energy (sugar). With an artificial sweetener, the signal is sent but the energy never arrives. This gap creates "metabolic confusion":
*   **Cephalic insulin response:** studies show that the sweet taste itself may trigger an initial release of [insulin](/en/food/supplements/insulin/), even without a rise in blood sugar, which encourages fat storage and suppresses fat breakdown.
*   **Calorie compensation:** the body, having been "tricked", keeps looking for the energy it was promised, which often leads to increased hunger and eating more calories at the next meal.

### Damage to the microbiome: the hidden danger
One of the most troubling discoveries of recent years, including groundbreaking research from the Weizmann Institute of Science, concerns the effect of sweeteners on the population of gut bacteria (the microbiome):
*   **Changing the bacterial composition:** sweeteners such as saccharin and sucralose alter the composition of gut bacteria in a way that promotes inflammation.
*   **Impaired glucose tolerance:** the change in the microbiome may, ironically, lead to glucose intolerance – the stage that precedes diabetes. In other words, the substance meant to prevent diabetes may in some cases accelerate it through an indirect mechanism.

### The industry and the "halo effect"
Food manufacturers use artificial sweeteners to create a "health halo" around processed products:
1.  **Legitimizing consumption:** labeling a product "sugar-free" makes consumers perceive it as healthy and eat larger amounts of it, sometimes without restraint.
2.  **Preserving the sweetness habit:** using sweeteners (which are sometimes 200-600 times sweeter than sugar) keeps the consumer's sweetness threshold high and prevents genuine weaning from the need for an intensely sweet taste at every meal.

### Summary
Artificial sweeteners are not as neutral as we thought. They are biologically active substances that affect our brain, our hormones and our gut bacteria. For anyone seeking real health, the solution is not replacing sugar with chemicals, but restoring the sense of taste and reducing dependence on sweet taste, whatever its source.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Do zero-sugar drinks raise insulin?**
Answer: Some studies show that the sweet taste alone can trigger a "cephalic" (initial) insulin response, even without sugar, but the effect varies between people.

**Question: Do artificial sweeteners harm gut bacteria?**
Answer: Yes, studies (including from the Weizmann Institute) have shown that sweeteners such as saccharin and sucralose may change the composition of the microbiome in a way that promotes glucose intolerance.

**Question: Is stevia considered a dangerous artificial sweetener?**
Answer: Stevia is considered a more natural sweetener and is usually safer than synthetic sweeteners, but it too may sustain the craving for an intensely sweet taste.
