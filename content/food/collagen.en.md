## Collagen

**Collagen is the most abundant protein in the body, and hydrolyzed collagen supplements provide about 9 grams of protein per 10 grams of powder. Meta-analyses of controlled trials found a modest improvement in skin hydration and elasticity after about three months, and hints of short-term relief of joint pain — but most of the trials were funded by manufacturers, and for building muscle collagen is inferior to whey protein.**

Collagen (in Hebrew "קולגן") makes up about a third of the protein in the body, and it is the main structural protein in skin, tendons, ligaments, cartilage and bone. The name comes from the Greek "kolla" — glue. The body makes collagen itself from amino acids (mainly glycine, proline and hydroxyproline), and it needs [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) to do so. Production gradually declines with age, and sun, smoking and a high-sugar diet speed up its breakdown in the skin.

### Nutrition facts and doses

Eating collagen does not turn directly into collagen in your skin: the digestive system breaks it down into amino acids and short peptides, some of which are absorbed into the blood. "Hydrolyzed collagen" (collagen peptides) is collagen that has been broken down in advance, which is why it dissolves in both hot and cold drinks.

| Product | Amount | Protein | Calories |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrolyzed collagen powder | 10 g (a heaped tablespoon) | about 9 g | about 36 |
| Dry gelatin | 10 g | about 8.5 g | about 34 |
| [Bone broth](/en/food/bone-broth/) | one cup (240 ml) | about 6-10 g (highly variable) | about 40-50 |

Doses tested in trials (not medical advice):

| Goal | Dose in trials | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Skin (hydration, elasticity) | 2.5-10 g a day | 8-12 weeks |
| Joint pain | about 10 g a day | 3-6 months |
| Combined with strength training | 15 g a day | 12 weeks |

Worth knowing: collagen protein is incomplete — it lacks the amino acid tryptophan and is low in leucine. It does not replace other protein sources.

### Benefits

* **Skin — a modest improvement:** a meta-analysis of 19 controlled trials with about 1,100 participants (95% women) found that hydrolyzed collagen improved skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkles compared with placebo, mainly after about 90 days. [de Miranda et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33742704/). A meta-analysis of 26 trials reached a similar finding for hydration and elasticity, but noted biases in the included studies and called for larger trials. [Pu et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37432180/). Most of the trials are small and funded by supplement makers, so the true size of the effect is unclear.

* **Joint pain — hints:** in a meta-analysis of supplements for osteoarthritis, hydrolyzed collagen was among the supplements that showed a large short-term effect on pain, but no supplement showed a meaningful long-term effect, and the quality of the evidence was low. [Liu et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29018060/).

* **Tendons and ligaments — mechanism:** in a small trial of eight men, 15 grams of vitamin C-enriched gelatin an hour before short exercise raised blood markers of collagen synthesis. [Shaw et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27852613/). That is interesting for sports injury rehabilitation, but it has not been shown to prevent injuries.

* **Muscle mass combined with training:** in a trial of older men with sarcopenia, 15 grams of collagen peptides a day alongside strength training added more lean mass and strength than placebo. [Zdzieblik et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26353786/). But the placebo contained no protein at all, so it is hard to know whether the advantage came from collagen itself or simply from extra protein.

### Downsides

* **Inferior for building muscle:** in a trial in older women, whey protein raised muscle protein synthesis more than collagen, both in the short term and over several days. [Oikawa et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919527/). If your goal is muscle, [whey protein](/en/food/supplements/whey-protein/) or another complete protein is better.

* **Industry-funded evidence:** most skin and joint trials are small, short and funded by supplement makers. That does not mean the results are wrong, but the size of the effect should be treated with caution.

* **Price:** a collagen supplement costs far more per serving of protein than ordinary protein sources.

* **Source and allergens:** collagen comes from the skin and bones of cattle, pigs, chickens or fish. People allergic to fish should avoid marine collagen, and anyone who keeps kosher or is vegetarian should check the source. There is no plant collagen.

* **Regulation:** dietary supplements are not tested like drugs. Choose a product with third-party testing.

* **What is not proven:** hair and nails, "gut healing," weight loss and better bone density — there is no good evidence for these claims. Collagen creams also do not deliver collagen deep into the skin; they mainly moisturize.

### Practical use

* **If you want to try it:** 2.5-10 grams a day, in a hot or cold drink, for at least three months — then judge whether you see a difference. There is no proven best time of day.
* **With vitamin C:** no pill needed — a meal with citrus, bell pepper or kiwi supplies the vitamin C needed to make collagen.
* **Food:** chicken and fish skin, meat cuts with connective tissue (shank, oxtail), jelly and [bone broth](/en/food/bone-broth/) contain collagen or gelatin, but in unknown amounts.
* **Protect the collagen you have:** sunscreen, not smoking and little added sugar affect the skin as much as any supplement.
* **For vegans:** there is no plant collagen. A diet with varied protein, vitamin C, [zinc](/en/food/supplements/zinc/) and [copper](/en/food/supplements/copper/) gives the body the raw materials to make its own.

### Summary

Collagen supplements are an incomplete protein that is cheap to produce and sold at a high price. Meta-analyses show a modest improvement in skin hydration and elasticity after about three months, and hints of short-term relief of joint pain — but most trials are small and funded by manufacturers. For building muscle, collagen is inferior to whey protein. If you want to try it, take 2.5-10 grams a day for three months; if you want healthy skin, sunscreen and not smoking will do more.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: Do collagen supplements really work for skin?**
Answer: There is evidence of a modest improvement. Meta-analyses of controlled trials found better skin hydration and elasticity after about three months, but most trials are small and funded by manufacturers.

**Question: Does applying collagen cream help?**
Answer: Generally not for the collagen deep in the skin. The collagen molecule is too large to penetrate the outer layer of the skin. These creams moisturize the skin, and that is all.

**Question: When is the best time to take a collagen supplement?**
Answer: There is no proven best time. In trials, people took it at different times of day. What matters is consistency over several months.

**Question: Collagen or whey protein for building muscle?**
Answer: Whey protein. In a trial in older women, whey raised muscle protein synthesis more than collagen. Collagen lacks tryptophan and is low in leucine.

**Question: Is there collagen for vegans?**
Answer: No. Collagen is an animal protein only. Vegan "collagen boosters" are blends of vitamin C, zinc and amino acids, and there is no evidence that they work better than a varied diet.

**Question: Does collagen help hair and nails?**
Answer: There is no good evidence for it. Hair and nails are made of keratin, a different protein, and the studies on this are few and weak.

**Question: Is bone broth a good source of collagen?**
Answer: Not a reliable one. A lab analysis found that the amount of collagen amino acids in bone broth is far lower than in a supplement dose, and varies widely between broths.
