## Coconut Aminos

**Coconut aminos is a dark sauce made from fermented coconut blossom sap and salt, and a soy sauce substitute without soy or gluten; a tablespoon contains about 270 mg of sodium, roughly a third of regular soy sauce (about 900 mg), but it also contains sugar and costs several times more.** It is most useful for people with a soy allergy or celiac disease, and for those trying to cut down on salt. In Hebrew it is called "אמינו קוקוס".

The sap is collected from the flower stalks of the [coconut](/en/food/coconut/) palm, fermented, mixed with salt and reduced to a dark liquid. The taste is salty-sweet, closer to a mild teriyaki than to soy sauce, with almost no coconut flavor. The name "aminos" is marketing: it contains tiny amounts of amino acids with no nutritional significance.

### Nutrition facts

Values vary between brands. The table shows a typical range based on product labels, compared with soy sauce:

| Nutrient (per tablespoon, 15 ml) | Coconut aminos | Regular soy sauce | Reduced-sodium soy sauce |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calories | about 15 | about 10 | about 10 |
| [Sodium](/en/food/supplements/sodium/) | about 270–390 mg | about 900–1,000 mg | about 500–600 mg |
| Sugar | about 3 g | under 1 g | under 1 g |
| Protein | almost none | about 1–1.5 g | about 1 g |
| Gluten | none | yes (most types) | yes (most types) |
| Soy | none | yes | yes |

So compared with regular soy sauce, coconut aminos contains roughly 60–70% less sodium; compared with reduced-sodium soy sauce the gap is smaller, about 40–50%.

### Benefits

**Less sodium.** Cutting salt lowers blood pressure: in a meta-analysis of trials, reducing salt by about 4.4 g a day lowered systolic blood pressure by about 4 mmHg on average, and more in people with high blood pressure [(He et al., BMJ 2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23558162/). The World Health Organization recommends less than 2,000 mg of sodium a day (5 g of salt) [(WHO: salt reduction)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/salt-reduction). If you use soy sauce generously, several tablespoons a meal, switching can save several hundred milligrams of sodium.

**Soy-free and gluten-free.** Most soy sauces are made from soy and wheat, so they are unsuitable for people with celiac disease or a soy allergy. Coconut aminos suits both (check for a "gluten-free" label). Gluten-free tamari is another option for celiac disease, but it contains soy.

**Rich flavor.** The sweet-salty taste suits stir-fries, marinades and sauces, and in some recipes it can replace both soy sauce and sugar.

**What about the amino acids and the "low glycemic index"?** The amount of amino acids in a tablespoon is negligible compared with any protein serving, and no studies have tested the effect of coconut aminos on blood sugar. These are marketing claims, not proven benefits.

### Coconut aminos and other soy sauce alternatives

| Sauce | Soy | Gluten | Sodium (per tablespoon, approx.) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coconut aminos | no | no | 270–390 mg | sweetish, expensive |
| Regular soy sauce | yes | yes | 900–1,000 mg | the cheapest |
| Reduced-sodium soy sauce | yes | yes | 500–600 mg | similar taste, less salt |
| Gluten-free tamari | yes | no | 900–1,000 mg | suits celiac disease, not soy allergy |
| Fish sauce | no | usually no | over 1,000 mg | very salty, used in drops |
| [Miso](/en/food/miso/) diluted in water | yes | depends on type | depends on dilution | adds depth to soups and sauces |

The right choice depends on the reason for switching. For a soy allergy, coconut aminos is almost the only option with a similar taste. For celiac disease, gluten-free tamari is cheaper and closer to soy sauce in flavor. To cut sodium without an allergy, reduced-sodium soy sauce or simply using less soy sauce, with more ginger, garlic, lemon and herbs for flavor, works just as well.

### Downsides

**It still contains sodium.** 270–390 mg per tablespoon is about 15–20% of the daily limit. "Lower" is not "low": swapping one tablespoon of soy sauce for three of coconut aminos cancels the advantage.

**Sugar.** About 3 g of sugar per tablespoon. At normal amounts that is little, but in sauces and marinades used generously it adds up, and anyone on a strict ketogenic diet needs to count it.

**Price.** Coconut aminos costs several times as much as soy sauce. If you have no allergy or celiac disease and mainly want less sodium, reduced-sodium soy sauce is a cheaper alternative with similar savings in some cases.

**Little research.** There are almost no human studies on the product itself. Its advantages come from its composition, meaning its sodium content and what it lacks, not from any special effect.

**Coconut allergy.** Rare, but it exists. People allergic to coconut should avoid it.

### Practical use

- **Swap 1:1:** use the same amount as soy sauce. Because it is less salty, some people add a little more, which reduces the sodium saving.
- **Stir-fries:** add it in the last half-minute so the sugar does not burn.
- **Marinades:** coconut aminos with [ginger](/en/food/ginger/), [garlic](/en/food/garlic/) and [lemon](/en/food/lemon/) juice suits [tofu](/en/food/tofu/), chicken and fish.
- **Asian salad dressing:** with a teaspoon of [peanut butter](/en/food/peanut-butter/), lemon juice and a little water.
- **Storage:** refrigerate after opening. It keeps for many months; check the maker's advice.

### Summary

Coconut aminos is a good soy sauce substitute for people with a soy allergy or celiac disease, or anyone cutting sodium: about a third of the sodium of regular soy sauce, with no soy or gluten. It is not a "health food": it contains sugar, still contains sodium and costs much more. The claims about amino acids and glycemic index have no research behind them.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: How much sodium is in coconut aminos compared with soy sauce?**
Answer: A tablespoon of coconut aminos has about 270–390 mg of sodium, depending on the brand, compared with about 900–1,000 mg in regular soy sauce and about 500–600 mg in reduced-sodium soy sauce.

**Question: Is coconut aminos safe for people with celiac disease?**
Answer: Yes, it is naturally gluten-free. Choose a product labeled "gluten-free" to be sure about cross-contamination.

**Question: Does it taste of coconut?**
Answer: Hardly. The taste is salty-sweet, close to a mild teriyaki, and suits stir-fries and marinades without any coconut flavor.

**Question: Is coconut aminos keto-friendly?**
Answer: In small amounts, yes. A tablespoon contains about 3 g of sugar, so on a strict keto diet measure it rather than pour it.

**Question: Is it healthier than soy sauce?**
Answer: It contains less sodium and no soy or gluten, but more sugar and less protein. If you have no allergy, reduced-sodium soy sauce in moderate amounts is an equally reasonable choice.

**Question: How long does it keep after opening?**
Answer: Many months in the fridge, thanks to the salt and fermentation. Most makers recommend refrigerating it after opening.
