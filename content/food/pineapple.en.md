## Pineapple (*Ananas comosus*)

The pineapple (in Hebrew "אננס") is one of the few fruits that contain an active protein-digesting enzyme — **bromelain** — and quite a few health claims have been built around it. Some are well founded, most are exaggerated. That said, pineapple is good even without the story: 100 grams provides more than half the daily value of [vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) and about 40% of manganese, for just 50 calories.

This page also has a practical fact that saves money: **pineapple does not ripen after picking.** It is not a climacteric fruit, meaning it does not convert starch to sugar after it has been picked. A pineapple picked green will stay sour forever — it will only get softer.

Manganese is needed for antioxidant enzymes (mitochondrial SOD), for carbohydrate metabolism and for building connective tissue and bone.

### Bromelain — what it really does

**Bromelain** is not a single enzyme but a mixture of protein-digesting enzymes (proteases). It is concentrated mainly **in the stem and core** of the fruit, less in the flesh we eat. Commercial bromelain supplements are almost always extracted from the stem.

What is well founded:
* **It really does break down protein.** That's why it is used to tenderize meat, and why jelly (gelatin) won't set with fresh pineapple — the bromelain breaks down the gelatin.
* **It is deactivated by heat.** Canned pineapple is pasteurized, so its bromelain is no longer active. That's why canned pineapple does work in jelly.
* **The burning on the tongue** is caused by bromelain starting to break down proteins in the lining of the mouth. It goes away on its own, and the lining regenerates.
* **At supplement doses** (hundreds of mg a day, concentrated from the stem) there is reasonable evidence of benefit for swelling after surgery and for sinusitis.

What is not well founded:
* **The bromelain in a slice of pineapple won't "digest your meal for you."** The amount in the flesh is small, and a considerable part of it is neutralized by stomach acid. Eating pineapple is not equivalent to taking a bromelain supplement.
* **"Pineapple burns fat"** — there is no basis for this. Bromelain breaks down protein, not fat.

### Benefits

* **[Vitamin C](/en/food/supplements/vitamin-c/) for 50 calories:** 53% of the daily value per 100 grams — supporting collagen production, immune function and absorption of plant [iron](/en/food/iron/).
* **[Manganese](/en/food/supplements/manganese/):** 40% of the daily value — one of the best sources of it in the fruit world.
* **High water content and low calorie load:** 86% water, a fruit that is filling relative to its calories.
* **Polyphenols and phenolic acids** that contribute to its antioxidant capacity.
* **Medium glycemic index with low load:** despite its sweetness, a normal serving of pineapple (100-150 grams) does not create a high glycemic load.
* **Bromelain at supplement doses** — reasonable evidence for swelling and for upper respiratory inflammation, in the context of a supplement and not of the fruit.

### Downsides and considerations

* **Relatively high sugar, relatively low fiber.** 9.9 grams of sugar versus only 1.4 grams of fiber — a less favorable ratio than that of a [pear](/en/food/pear/) or an [apple](/en/food/apple/). Anyone monitoring sugar should measure their portion.
* **Burning and sores in the mouth.** Bromelain irritates the lining of the mouth, especially in unripe fruit or when eating a large amount. It goes away on its own.
* **Skin irritation when handling fresh fruit.** Peeling large quantities can irritate the skin by the same mechanism.
* **Canned pineapple in syrup.** "Heavy syrup" versions add 10-15 grams of sugar per 100 grams. In light syrup or "natural juice" it's better, but still not like fresh, and without active bromelain.
* **Interaction with anticoagulants.** Bromelain has a mild effect on platelet aggregation. At food amounts this is not practical, but people taking Coumadin or aspirin who also take a bromelain supplement should consult a doctor.
* **Acidity.** pH around 3.3 — may worsen heartburn in sensitive people and erode enamel with very frequent consumption.
* **The core is hard but edible.** It is richer in fiber and bromelain than the flesh; anyone willing to chew it benefits.

### Practical use

* **Buy a ripe pineapple, don't expect it to ripen.** Because pineapple is not climacteric, it won't get sweeter at home. Signs of a good fruit: a sweet smell at the base, a yellow-golden rather than entirely green skin, and a heavy weight for its size. A central leaf that pulls out easily is not a reliable sign.
* **Short marinades only.** Fresh pineapple tenderizes meat — but soaking it too long will turn it to mush. 20-30 minutes is enough.
* **Jelly:** use canned (pasteurized) pineapple or boil the fresh fruit for a minute — otherwise the dessert won't set.
* **Eat it with a plant source of [iron](/en/food/iron/)** — [lentils](/en/food/lentil/), [tofu](/en/food/tofu/) or [spinach](/en/food/spinach/) — the vitamin C significantly improves iron absorption.
* **A reasonable serving:** 100-150 grams. One thick slice, not a quarter of the fruit.
* **Keep it cut in the refrigerator for up to 3 days** in an airtight container. Commercial pineapple juice is another story — sugar without fiber, and without active bromelain.

### Summary

Pineapple is an excellent source of vitamin C and manganese at 50 calories per 100 grams, and that alone justifies it. Bromelain is an interesting story but overhyped: it really does break down protein — enough to tenderize meat, ruin jelly and burn your tongue — but eating a slice of pineapple is not the same as taking a supplement. And the most practical thing to remember: **pineapple doesn't ripen at home.** What you buy is what you get.

### Frequently asked questions

**Question: What are the nutrition facts of pineapple?**
Answer: 100 grams of fresh pineapple provides 50 calories, 13.1 grams of carbohydrates (of which 9.9 grams are sugar), 1.4 grams of fiber, 47.8 mg of vitamin C (53% of the daily value) and 0.93 mg of manganese (40%). It is one of the best fruit sources of manganese.

**Question: What is bromelain and what does it do?**
Answer: A mixture of protein-digesting enzymes concentrated mainly in the stem and core of the pineapple. It tenderizes meat, prevents gelatin from setting and causes a burning sensation on the tongue. At supplement doses (hundreds of mg, concentrated) there is evidence of benefit for swelling after surgery and for sinusitis, but a slice of pineapple does not provide such a dose.

**Question: Why does my tongue burn after eating pineapple?**
Answer: Bromelain starts breaking down proteins in the lining of the mouth. This is a local, temporary reaction, not an allergy, and the lining regenerates within hours. The effect is stronger with unripe pineapple and in large amounts; canned pineapple, which has been heated, doesn't cause it at all.

**Question: Does pineapple ripen at home?**
Answer: No. Pineapple is not a climacteric fruit — it does not convert starch to sugar after harvest. A pineapple picked green will soften but won't get sweeter. That's why it's important to choose a ripe fruit at the store: a sweet smell at the base, a golden skin and a heavy weight for its size.

**Question: Why won't jelly set with pineapple?**
Answer: Bromelain breaks down the gelatin protein and prevents it from forming a network. The solution is canned pineapple, in which the heat of pasteurization has deactivated the enzyme, or briefly boiling the fresh fruit before adding it.

**Question: Is pineapple suitable for people with diabetes?**
Answer: In a measured portion, yes. Although its glycemic index is medium, a serving of 100-150 grams creates a moderate glycemic load. Its fiber is relatively low (only 1.4 grams), so it's worth combining it with protein or fat — yogurt, cheese or nuts — and avoiding pineapple juice and pineapple in syrup.

**Question: Does pineapple burn fat?**
Answer: No. The claim rests on the fact that bromelain is a digestive enzyme — but it breaks down protein, not fat, and its activity is in the lab and the stomach, not in fat tissue. Pineapple helps with weight loss in only one way: it is a relatively filling fruit at 50 calories per 100 grams that replaces a high-calorie snack.

**Question: Is canned pineapple as healthy as fresh?**
Answer: It keeps some of the minerals but loses vitamin C to heating, and its bromelain is completely deactivated. The main problem is the syrup: "heavy syrup" versions add 10-15 grams of sugar per 100 grams. If you choose canned, choose "in natural juice" and drain it.
